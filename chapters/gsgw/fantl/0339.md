# Chapter 338 - Sekwang Special City Disaster, IV

A mountain path in Sekwang Special City.

Ryu Jaekwan panted as he looked at the figure racing ahead of him.

The person riding the bicycle, no, the agent, was guiding him with astonishing speed and decisiveness.

“Huff...”

They were literally riding through the mountain.

More precisely, they were using a well-maintained walking trail as a shortcut to avoid the densely populated residential areas.

The path was just well-maintained enough that a mountain bike wasn’t strictly necessary, though it was a close call.

Behind the leading agent, the Counselor was perched almost as if laid across the bike, while Ryu Jaekwan struggled to keep up on a bicycle he wasn’t used to riding.

The agent occasionally checked on their condition but never slowed down. His determination to escape danger as quickly as possible was palpable.

Even in this lie-filled, chilling, and urgent situation straight out of a disaster movie, he remained calm and resolute.

And yet—

“Jaekwan, be careful!”

How did he even know his name?

He was just an orphan with no family or connections.

Then why was he trying to save him?

Something between hope and fear churned in his chest. Amid the terror of this extreme, surreal situation where death felt imminent, a strange thrill crept in.

It was the admiration of a teenager.

The excitement of being given a special role.

But the situation was far too urgent for such sentiments to fully surface.

“...!”

“We’re turning!”

The moment another disaster text alert blared, the bike ahead sharply swerved.

Ryu Jaekwan hurriedly followed.

But a chill ran through him as he realized he’d almost let go of the handlebars to grab his phone and check the message.

That impulse was growing stronger...

After racing for some time, they finally reached a stopping point.

“How long had they been riding?”

“Let’s... huff... rest for a moment over there.”

They stopped in front of a rest-area sign.

Ryu Jaekwan reflexively scanned the area.

The rest area halfway up the mountain was a cabin, with a small clearing around it enclosed by a fence.

In one corner, a faucet shaped like a wooden sculpture protruded from between the rocks, trickling water into a small pool like a spring.

Following the agent, Ryu Jaekwan dismounted and pushed his bike toward the cabin entrance—

Then he heard the Counselor whisper softly.

“There are other people inside the cabin.”

...!

“They’re watching us from the window. ...I don’t think their intentions are good. Don’t look.”

Ryu Jaekwan almost turned his head toward the window, but stopped himself. Cold sweat slid down his temples.

...It felt as though leering eyes were scanning their bikes, their builds, and their belongings.

Then, beside him, a calm voice spoke as its owner caught his breath.

“We weren’t planning to go inside anyway... If we need to move quickly, indoors isn’t ideal. Let’s sit outside briefly and then go. There’s a spring here.”

The agent’s calm voice soothed the teenager’s bristling nerves.

“Are you okay, Student Jaekwan?”

“...Yes.”

After checking on Ryu Jaekwan with a faint smile, the adults soon began speaking with hardened expressions.

“It’s only been an hour since the disaster started, and people are already thinking that way...”

“When death is right in front of them, there’s no reason to expect bravery. Anyone could immediately tell that this wasn’t a normal event. It’s only natural.”

They were clearly talking about the “people with bad intentions” inside the cabin.

Ryu Jaekwan swallowed hard.

But the agent, when he looked back at him, seemed unfazed. He simply patted the space beside him, gesturing for the boy to relax.

His gaze, framed by black hair, remained steady.

As though he’d experienced things like this countless times before.

Unaware of the inner turmoil the agent might have been feeling, Ryu Jaekwan felt a small sense of relief and sat beside him.

Catching their breath, they began discussing the situation.

“The alert earlier probably wasn’t on the mountain trail. It was likely somewhere down below. We should be safe for a while, but we don’t know when we’ll get another chance, so let’s hydrate and get some nutrients now.”

The agent pulled an empty plastic bottle from his backpack, filled it with water from the spring, and offered it to the Counselor.

The Counselor initially refused, but at the agent’s firm insistence, he eventually took a few sips and popped a piece of candy into his mouth.

Then the agent asked him,

“You said you were heading for the station.”

“Yes.”

“You mentioned there’s a way to end this Disaster... so exactly where are you trying to go by subway?”

“To Sekwang Station.”

At those words, the agent’s calm facade wavered.

Why?

“Sir. The epicenter of this Disaster is Sekwang City Hall. That’s right next to Sekwang Station.”

Ah.

“What you’re saying is that you’re going to walk straight into the heart of a fire, without a manual or basic equipment, in the middle of a newly erupted large-scale Disaster.”

The agent paused for a moment, swallowing hard before speaking again.

Ryu Jaekwan had the strange impression that he was deliberately trying to make the situation sound even more dangerous.

“And this isn’t an ordinary Disaster. At minimum, I suspect it’s Annihilation-Sanctioned, and in the worst-case scenario...”

“I know.”

The Counselor’s voice sounded slightly anxious.

“That’s exactly why I have to hurry. And don’t worry. I have my own methods.”

Then, as if to show them something, the Counselor suddenly held out an empty candy wrapper.

He clenched his hand tightly over it so the contents could not be seen, then slowly opened his palm.

And then...

“...!!”

“Looks like magic, doesn’t it?”

They were small, ripe red berries.

“These are the fruits of a plant called the Fox Bead, Jaekwan-ah.”

The Counselor spoke gently, holding the berries and smiling with a hint of playfulness.

“If you eat this, you’ll be fine for a while. You won’t be tempted by disaster alerts. ...I can’t promise complete safety, so we still need to leave this place as quickly as possible.”

As he spoke, his eyes briefly blurred as if gazing at the empty space where his right hand, melted away and severed, had been.

But he soon smiled again and offered the berries.

“Go on.”

Ryu Jaekwan glanced at the agent. When the agent nodded, he took the berries.

He remembered the moment the Counselor had shoved his hand into the guard-turned-monster, and when that hand had been severed.

This Counselor wasn’t an ordinary human...

......

Wait, had there even been a Counselor at his school before?

...Had they always been like this?

A faint sense of unease rose, but his young, restless mind quickly brushed it aside.

Because the Counselor continued speaking.

“So you two eat these and head somewhere safe.”

“......”

“Just taking me as far as the station is already more than enough. Agent... you should rescue those who want to be rescued.”

The agent fell silent.

Ryu Jaekwan watched his face.

He sensed intense conflict and struggle flashing across it—then—

“Thank you.”

A calm voice spoke.

“Jaekwan-ah.”

“...Yes?”

“We’ll take him to the station, then continue along the mountain path. There should be a river ahead—fewer people, relatively safer.”

The agent looked him in the eye and spoke gently.

“We’ll use that route to leave the city. I’ll stay with you until you get out of this disaster safely, so don’t worry.”

“......”

“For now, that’s the plan.”

“That’s a lie, isn’t it?”

“......!”

“It looks like you’ll get me out, then come back here... Didn’t you say there were others you needed to rescue?”

“......”

“I want to come with you.”

“What?”

Ryu Jaekwan spoke firmly.

“You don’t need to send only me outside. Isn’t that a huge waste of time?”

“...!!”

Clearly, that struck a nerve.

As the agent’s expression changed, Ryu Jaekwan oddly felt relieved.

His willpower was starting to outweigh his fear.

“This is an urgent situation. You don’t need to look after me separately while rescuing others. I’m... strong, and I’ll do whatever you tell me without complaint.”

“No—”

The agent’s face hardened, then he let out a sigh. The high schooler’s heart skipped, but the agent looked at him with eyes that said, ‘I knew it’.

...How?

“First—”

Just as the agent began to speak—

“Hey!”

...!

They turned toward the unfamiliar voice.

...A pair of eyes appeared through the half-open cabin door.

As Ryu Jaekwan sprang to his feet in fright—

“Aren’t you coming in?”

The door opened wider, revealing a middle-aged man in ordinary hiking clothes.

“Things outside don’t look normal. It’s better to be somewhere safer, even if just a little.”

Ah.

He was trying to help.

The high schooler felt a momentary sense of relief—

—but the two adults subtly moved him behind them, taking a guarded stance.

‘...!’

“It’s alright. We’re heading out again.”

“Heading out? Didn’t something happen at City Hall? Are you going to a shelter?”

He stole a glance at them.

“Those bikes would be fast, but still, shouldn’t you eat something first? Take a cup noodle or two. We’ve got some inside.”

But though he spoke kindly, his eyes lingered on the bicycles with greed.

Ryu Jaekwan realized it then.

They were after the bikes.

With the roads blocked, there was no better means of transportation.

Just as that realization struck and he was about to speak—

“Agent...”

“Agent?”

The middle-aged man’s eyes swept over the agent’s jacket. When he realized it was a uniform, his expression changed.

“Hey, are you a civil servant?”

Crap.

“Police? Military?”

Inside the cabin, people stirred and murmured.

Ryu Jaekwan clenched his teeth—but the agent simply smiled.

“Hmm. Something like that, but... we’re not on duty. We don’t have any official backing, either.”

He patted Ryu Jaekwan’s back.

“I’m just traveling with my younger brother.”

...!

“We’re not heading to a shelter. We’re leaving the city.”

“Wait a second—”

“The situation isn’t good. You’d be better off leaving the city too, heading somewhere with fewer people. We’ll be going now.”

“Me too.”

The two adults slowly backed away, then mounted their bikes.

“Jaekwan-ah!”

Without hesitation, Ryu Jaekwan grabbed his bike and started pedaling like mad.

“Hey!”

He glanced back—people were rushing out of the hut, clutching hiking sticks and what looked like wire cables.

A chill ran through him, and he pedaled even harder.

Soon, the footsteps behind them faded into the distance.

“Jaekwan-ah, the berries!”

“Ah—!”

His heart pounding, Ryu Jaekwan hurriedly shoved the nearly crushed berries into his mouth.

Strangely, amid the fear and revulsion, he felt a faint sense of exhilaration.

It was a sense of belonging.
