# Chapter 338 - Sekwang Special City Disaster, IV

A mountain path in Sekwang Special City.

Ryu Jaekwan panted as he looked at the figure racing ahead of him.

The person on the bicycle... no, the ‘agent,’ was leading him, making decisions with astonishing speed and clarity.

“Huff...”

They were actually riding over the mountain.

More precisely, they were taking a shortcut out of the residential area along a well-maintained mountain walking trail, avoiding densely populated areas.

The trail was just well maintained enough to be passable without a mountain bike.

The Counselor was perched behind the agent ahead, almost as if he’d been plopped onto the bike, while Ryu Jaekwan fought to keep up on a bicycle he wasn’t used to riding.

The agent checked on him from time to time without slowing down. Ryu Jaekwan could sense his determination to get out of danger even a little sooner.

Even in this chilling, urgent situation, so unreal it felt like something out of a disaster movie, he remained calm and resolute.

And yet—

“Student Jaekwan, be careful!”

How on earth did the agent know him?

He was just an orphan with no family or connections.

Then why was he trying to save him?

Something stirred in his chest, though he couldn’t tell if it was anticipation or fear. Even amid the terror of this bizarre, life-threatening ordeal, a strange thrill welled up.

It was a teenager’s admiration.

The thrill of being entrusted with a special role.

But the situation was too urgent for those feelings to come to the fore.

“...!”

“We’re turning!”

The instant another disaster text alert sounded, the bicycle ahead veered sharply.

Ryu Jaekwan hurriedly followed.

But a sudden chill ran through him as he realized he’d almost let go of the handlebars to grab his phone and check the message.

That impulse was growing stronger....

After they’d been riding for a while...

“Let’s... huff... rest for a moment over there.”

They stopped in front of a rest-area sign.

Ryu Jaekwan reflexively scanned the rest area.

About halfway along the mountain trail, the rest area consisted of a cabin surrounded by a small, level clearing enclosed by a fence.

In one corner, he could see a faucet shaped like a wooden sculpture emerging from a crevice between the rocks, with water pooled beneath it like a little spring.

...!

“They’re watching us from the window. ...I don’t think they mean well. Don’t look.”

Ryu Jaekwan nearly turned to look at the window. Cold sweat trickled down his temples.

...He could almost feel those predatory eyes sizing up their bicycles, their builds, and their belongings.

Then the agent spoke beside him in a steady voice as he caught his breath.

“We weren’t going inside anyway.... Being indoors isn’t ideal if we need to move at once. Let’s sit outside the cabin for a moment before we go. There’s a spring here.”

The agent’s calm voice eased the tension that had set the teenager on edge.

“Are you okay, Student Jaekwan?”

“......Yes.”

The adults checked on Ryu Jaekwan with a faint smile, then began talking, their faces grave.

“It’s only been an hour since the disaster began, and some people are already thinking of taking extreme measures....”

“No one is fearless when their life is on the line. They must have realized right away that this was no ordinary incident, so it’s only natural.”

They must have been talking about the ‘people who didn’t mean well’ inside the cabin.

Ryu Jaekwan swallowed hard.

But when the agent looked back at him, he seemed unfazed. He merely tapped the spot beside him, as if to reassure him.

His gaze, framed by black hair, remained steady.

As though he’d experienced things like this countless times before.

Unaware of the screams going on inside the agent’s head, Ryu Jaekwan simply felt a little relieved and sat down beside him.

Catching their breath, they began discussing the situation.

“The alert earlier... was probably for somewhere down the mountain, not here on the trail. We should be safe here for a while. We don’t know when we’ll get another chance like this, so let’s get some water and nutrients into us now.”

The agent took an empty plastic bottle from his backpack, filled it with spring water, and held it out to the Counselor.

The Counselor refused at first, but at the agent’s insistence, he eventually took a sip or two and put a piece of candy in his mouth.

Then the agent asked him.

“You said you were heading for the station, didn’t you?”

“Yes.”

“You mentioned there’s a way to end this Disaster... so exactly where are you trying to go by subway?”

“I’m heading for Sekwang Station.”

At that, a flicker of unease crossed the agent’s previously impassive face.

Why?

“Sir. This Disaster began at Sekwang City Hall, right near Sekwang Station.”

Ah.

“You’re telling me you intend to go right into the middle of a raging fire. Without a manual or even basic equipment, in the middle of a major Disaster that’s only just begun.”

After a pause, the agent swallowed and continued.

For some reason, Ryu Jaekwan had the feeling that the agent had done it partly on purpose, to heighten the sense of danger.

“And this isn’t an ordinary Disaster. I suspect... it’s at least Fracture-Sanctioned, and in the worst case....”

“I know.”

The Counselor’s voice sounded slightly anxious.

“That’s why I need to hurry even more. And don’t worry. I have my own ways of dealing with it.”

Then, to their surprise, the Counselor suddenly held out an empty candy wrapper as if to show them something.

He clenched his hand tightly to hide the contents, then opened it.

And then...

“...!!”

“Looks like magic, doesn’t it?”

They were small, ripe red berries.

“These are the berries of a plant called the Fox bead, Student Jaekwan.”

The Counselor spoke with a touch of warmth, the little berries in his hand and a faintly mischievous smile on his face.

“If you eat these, you’ll be all right for a while. You won’t be bewitched by the disaster texts. ...I can’t promise complete safety, though, so we’ll still have to get out of here as quickly as possible.”

As he spoke, the Counselor’s gaze clouded slightly, as though he were looking at the spot where his right hand had been before it melted into a limp mass and was cut off.

But he soon smiled again and offered the berries.

“Go on.”

Ryu Jaekwan looked to the agent for approval, then took the berries when he nodded.

He’d already thought as much when the Counselor had thrust his hand into the monster the guard had become, and again when that hand had been cut off.

The Counselor didn’t seem to be an ordinary human....

.......

Wait, had there even been a Counselor at his school before?

...Had he been someone like this?

A faint feeling that something was amiss surfaced, but his young, lively mind soon swept it away.

Because the Counselor continued speaking.

“So you two eat these and head somewhere safe.”

“.......”

“I’m deeply grateful just to have you take me as far as the station. Agent... you should rescue those who want to be rescued.”

The agent fell silent.

Ryu Jaekwan watched his face.

Just as he thought he saw a fierce inner struggle flash across the agent’s face...

“Thank you.”

The voice was calm.

“Student Jaekwan.”

“...Yes?”

“We’ll just take him as far as the station. Then if we keep following this mountain trail, we should come to a river. There won’t be many people there, so it should be relatively safe.”

When their eyes met, the agent spoke gently.

“We’ll use that route to leave the city. Student Jaekwan, I’ll stay with you until you make it safely out of this Disaster, so don’t worry.”

“.......”

“There’s no need to take me out of the city separately. Wouldn’t that be a huge waste of time?”

If anything, the change in the agent’s expression came as a relief to Ryu Jaekwan.

His resolve was stronger than his fear.

“Every second counts. You don’t need to go out of your way to look after me until you’ve found the others you need to rescue. I’m... in good shape, and I can do whatever you tell me without complaint.”

“No—”

The agent’s face hardened, then he sighed. The high schooler’s heart lurched, yet the agent looked at him as though to say, ‘I knew you’d say that.’

...How?

“Hey!”

Ryu Jaekwan turned toward the unfamiliar voice.

...A pair of eyes appeared through the half-open cabin door.

As Ryu Jaekwan sprang to his feet in fright—

“Aren’t you coming in?”

The door opened wider, revealing a middle-aged man in ordinary hiking clothes.

“Things outside don’t look normal, so come inside for now! Better to be somewhere a little safer.”

Ah.

‘He was trying to help.’

For a moment, the high schooler felt relieved—

—but the two adults pushed the high schooler behind them and assumed a strangely guarded stance.

‘...!’

“It’s alright. We’re heading out again.”

“Leaving? I heard there was a terrorist attack at City Hall. Are you heading to a shelter or something?”

He cast a sidelong glance.

“You’d get there quickly on those, but wouldn’t it be better to eat something before you get back on? At least take a cup of instant noodles with you. We’ve got some inside.”

But even as he feigned concern, his greedy gaze drifted to the bicycles.

Ryu Jaekwan realized it too.

They were after the bikes.

With the roads blocked, there was no better means of transportation.

A sudden flash of alarm made him open his mouth before he could think...

“Agent...”

“Agent?”

The middle-aged man in the cabin looked over the agent’s jacket, and his expression changed as though he’d recognized it as a uniform.

“Oh, wait, are you a civil servant?”

Crap.

“Police? Military?”

Inside the cabin, he heard the others getting to their feet and stirring in agitation.

Ryu Jaekwan clenched his teeth—but the agent simply smiled.

“Hmm. Something along those lines, but... we’re not on official duty. We don’t have any support from our organization, either.”

He patted Ryu Jaekwan’s back.

“I’m just traveling with my younger brother.”

...!

“We’re not heading to a shelter. We’re leaving the city.”

“Wait a second—”

“The situation isn’t good, so you’d be better off leaving the city too, heading where there’s no one around. We’ll be on our way, then.”

“I’ll be going too.”

The two adults edged backward before mounting their bikes.

“Jaekwan-ah!”

Without hesitation, Ryu Jaekwan grabbed his bike and started pedaling like mad.

“Hey!”

He glanced back to see people bursting out of the cabin door with trekking poles and what looked like lengths of wire.

A chill ran through him, and he pedaled even harder.

Soon the footsteps of those pursuing them faded into the distance.

“Student Jaekwan, the berries!”

“Ah—!”

His heart still pounding, Ryu Jaekwan hastily tipped the berries into his mouth, having nearly crushed them in his fist.

Oddly enough, even amid the fear and revulsion, he felt a flicker of lightheartedness.

It was a sense of belonging.
