Agent Choi!

“What are you doing?”

“Quiet.”

Agent Choi’s gaze swept over my heart on the scale as though he wanted to retrieve it. Then he looked away, seemingly weighing the danger, and spoke.

“...Hey, Selection Officer speaking through that speaker, come out and talk to us! Looks like the Scales of Malice is being misused. Not that I ever thought much of the thing to begin with.”

Agent Choi grinned.

“In an emergency like this, do you honestly think you can make a sound judgment by grilling people until they reach their breaking point? Don’t you know information extracted through torture is unreliable?”

The speaker remained silent, unnervingly so.

But Agent Choi shouted even louder.

“Even with the Silver Heart appearing, the result was ‘unsuitable.’ We’d consider something like this an exceptional incident even when dealing with Supernatural Disasters!”

“Indeed.”

As Agent Bronze agreed, I saw no cold sweat on the back of his neck—only the pallor of dead flesh.

“As a concerned party, I urge you to reconsider. ...This is no way to determine civilians’ eligibility for rescue.”

Why are they doing this?

Even if I died by ‘humane execution,’ there was a good chance I’d simply end up outside Sekwang Special City. I could prepare and go back in. Shouldn’t they worry more about their own position?

Are they concerned that I might die *permanently* in this ghost story? Like the heap of corpses beneath that glass floor.

‘I don’t think so.’

They seemed to have thought of a way to overcome this ghost story and were trying it, but it would be safer to wait until *after* my trial.

I spoke very quietly, so only the agents could hear.

“Um... I really am okay.”

“I said, shut your mouth.”

“Really. My original plan was just to destroy the scales.”

“......!”

The same approach I’d taken at Noon Station—simply setting the residential district on fire.

‘If the means of judgment is gone, they’ll have no choice but to end the ghost story.’

And since it wouldn’t break easily, I planned to unleash all sorts of contamination and wreak havoc.

I’d figured there was a good chance I’d die in the attempt.

“I went into this expecting to die anyway. So it’s fi—”

“No. You are not fine.” Agent Bronze cut me off. “You’re the one at greatest risk of becoming bound to this Supernatural Disaster.”

What?

“Nothing makes a person easier prey for a Supernatural Disaster than being mentally backed into a corner and panicking. So....”

“I’m not panicking.”

“Why is that so precious to you?”

Why?

Was it safe to tell him?

For a moment, I grew wary, but then remembered that Agent Choi beside me already knew everything. There was no point in hiding it.

He would share it anyway.

So....

“...It’s a way to return home.”

“.......”

“No, it’s more like a symbol of the way home.”

The more I spoke, the more jumbled my words seemed to get, and I struggled to straighten them out.

“Because I only just discovered it, and I never thought I’d find another way home....”
