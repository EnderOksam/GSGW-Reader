# Chapter 348 - Sekwang Sealing Ritual, II

I wanted to blurt out, “What do you mean?”

But in truth, my mind had already understood what Agent Haegeum was saying.

It felt like something within my brain had burst from the shock.

...The sealing ritual is collapsing?

So the disaster of Sekwang Special City... will be completely unleashed?

And that—

In one week...

An overwhelming sense of terror surged up reflexively.

Having just experienced the reality of that “disaster” first-hand, my mind erupted into a near-physical panic, sharp and prickling.

Then...

It’s the end.

What happens now?

Even though I knew the horrific truth behind the sealing ritual, even though I had seen the suffering of Sekwang’s citizens with my own eyes, my face must have gone deathly pale.

Because in a few days, that scene would befall the entire country.

Helplessly—no, wait.

I turned to Agent Haegeum.

There was no way I was the only one feeling this fear.

“Does the Bureau... not know?”

If they had known, there was no way they would’ve wasted the past 300 days. They would have devised countermeasures somehow.

The problem was that, if they had a countermeasure, these two agents wouldn’t be talking about an Annihilation-Sanctioned Disaster breaking out in one week with this kind of tone.

"...Are they keeping it secret?"

Agent Haegeum frowned slightly.

“You know this. There are barely any agents or officials in the Bureau who even know about that Special City.”

......!

That’s right. Everyone’s memories had been wiped, the city rendered cognitively unrecognizable.

In the process, the Azure Dragon Team had been erased entirely, the organization reorganized as if it had always been that way.

But—

“Still, someone must know.”

“......”

Looking into Haegeum’s eyes, I realized who she meant.

Someone who led the sealing ritual.

Someone who pushed the horrifying idea of endlessly repeating May 4th—the day of the disaster—using the deaths of countless people as fuel to sustain the sealing ritual.

That person, the higher-up, would definitely know.

“...But from what Choi and I have been able to find out, there hasn’t been any unusual movement inside the Bureau over the past few months. If they were taking action, it should’ve affected the sealing ritual—but there’s been nothing.”

“That’s...”

Wait.

A horrifying possibility flashed through my mind.

If they weren’t acting now, then—

“...Are they waiting until the seal disappears completely?”

“......”

“And after it vanishes... are they planning to perform the ritual again?”

“Yes.”

“...!”

“If they have to seal it again anyway, it’s a proven method. They’re more likely to choose that than some uncertain reinforcement.”

Damn.

“The problem is... where they’ll get the ‘sacrifices’ this time.”

Haegeum seemed tense just thinking about that unknown.

But I already knew the answer.

'...They could easily solve it by sacrificing all the citizens of Sekwang Special City once they were freed.'

This is insane.

The hair on my head stood on end.

I spoke urgently.

I had to say this—

“Agent, have you... talked to the other agents about the sealing ritual?”

About the human sacrifices.

I tried to keep it vague, but the moment I met Agent Choi’s eyes, I knew.

“Why beat around the bush, Grapes-ie? I already heard.”

......!

His bitter smile came with a dry, exhausted voice.

But there was little anger or confusion.

...He had already turned it over countless times.

“Oh. Worried about being tracked? Then it’s fine to talk here. This place is right below the Tiger General’s resting place—they can’t follow us.”

Then he added quietly,

“...And this is a place even the Bureau doesn’t know exists.”

“......”

“We tried to somehow get the living citizens out while maintaining the existing sealing ritual—but we just couldn’t interfere with the ritual itself.”

His blue eyes met mine.

“Right up to this point, with seven days left until the seal ends.”

“......”

His mind must have been on the verge of breaking.

A missing junior and a former spy still unconscious after an Annihilation-Sanctioned Disaster.

Someone in the agency he’d dedicated his life to was hiding a grotesque secret.

Director Ho, unpredictable as ever.

Looking into those tired eyes, which had clearly never rested despite his leave, my chest went cold.

The crisis was right in front of us.

I’m losing it.

A storm of unresolved matters crashed through my head: Director Ho, the Security Team, the researchers at Twilight Station, Blood Broadcasting Station’s rent, the lizard body stored at Section Chief Lee Jaheon’s residence...

The suffocating void of 293 lost days.

But none of it would matter if we couldn’t stop the Annihilation-Sanctioned Disaster coming in one week.

And yet... re-sealing Sekwang Special City—

I couldn’t bring myself to accept that again.

If it ends up one way, it’s hell for some people; if it ends up another way, it’s hell for others.

...How much does Director Ho know by now?

No. Focus.

If we couldn’t change what was coming, we had to move faster.

We had to deal with this—somehow.

“Agent Grapes, be careful—”

“I’m fine.”

I stood up without support. My legs shook.

My mind cleared.

The first thing we needed—

“I’d like to meet Yeongeun-ssi... that is, Agent Mint.”

The incident where the sealing ritual began to unravel.

We needed details from the person who had been at the center of the train shelter escape.

Fast.

“Of course that’s fine! I mean, you just woke up—you must be exhausted from losing your muscles and all that...”

She noticed that I was in better shape than expected, and a strange light passed through her eyes. But instead of asking outright, she stayed quiet.

...Even there, I felt a strange distortion of time.

“Ms. Yeongeun, how have you been? I heard you’ve been entering the Special City subway again.”

“I’m... better than before. I’m living with my aunt now. My aunt... has forgotten everything that happened.”

Her voice darkened briefly, then steadied.

“Honestly, part of why I want to return as an agent is so that I don’t lose my memories.”

Ah.

“You might call it calculating, but... losing my memories feels like losing myself.”

She explained that she’d deliberately expressed her desire to return, implicitly delaying memory deletion.

“And I brushed off getting my body parts back as an item effect. ...Sounds excessive, right? It’s the Disaster Management Bureau, not some Daydream.”

“No. I understand completely.”

Especially from the perspective of someone who had tried to get a Wish Ticket for her family in Sekwang without even knowing why she had forgotten them.

And then—

Thinking that far, I felt even more reluctant to open my mouth.

I’d planned to say this with both agents present so I wouldn’t have to repeat it again.

Damn it.

But with so little time left. Ruin was coming.

“Ms. Yeongeun.”

“Yes?”

“There’s... good news, and bad news.”

Agent Choi’s bitter smile appeared—then vanished as I continued.

“The good news is that the others may also wake up safely.”

“That’s—that’s wonderful!”

Joy sparkled in her voice, then quickly settled.

Agent Choi was watching me too.

“Assistant Manager Haje, Mr. Ryu Jaekwan... and Section Chief Lee Jaheon—are they alive? They disappeared from the train, too. We thought they... fell together.”

I nodded slowly. A flash of cold fury crossed her face.

“...That conductor pushed them, didn’t he?”

“......”

“I knew it. That insane bastard...”

She said she didn’t regret hitting him at all, then steadied her breath.

“Still... I’m glad they’re alive. We couldn’t find any trace of the four of you in the subway.”

“......”

“Then you were on the surface?”

“Yes. ...Unexpectedly, people were living aboveground in Sekwang, too.”

I met her eyes.

“And they were dying every day.”

“...What?”

The air turned cold.

“...What?”

I told them.

About Sekwang’s endlessly repeating May 4th.

“...!!”

“And... that’s how the disaster is being contained?”

“......”

“By repeating a single day over and over, so that no matter how much time passes, the disaster never leaves the city—people are dying every day?”

Her stunned voice slowly filled with emotion.

“Then my parents...?”

“Ms. Yeongeun—”

“It’s been years now... n—”

She gasped. I moved to support her, but she pushed my hand away.

...!

She bowed her head, silent for a long while.

Then a whisper.

“...Is this real? Is this reality? Did it really... happen?”

“It might only be my deduction—”

“You’ve always been right! Every deduction you made was right! Why say that now...!”

She took another breath.

A stabbing silence sank into the cave.

Her fists were pale and shaking.

Agent Choi rose quietly and moved beside her. Both he and Agent Haegeum had cold, pale faces. However, they didn’t show their shock in front of the person directly involved in the situation—the family member.

I barely caught my breath and asked softly, “...Do you know?”

That she knew the seal would break in seven days.

“Do I know that the seal will break in seven days?”

......!

“I know. My ability to hear ‘rumors about the Special City’ didn’t disappear after I got out. In fact, those rumors were why I kept trying to enter the subway.”

Her voice cracked.

“But then I thought—”

“...Yes.”

“If we just let the seal break... maybe it could break while my parents are still alive?”

...!

“May 4th keeps repeating. If the timing’s right—if it breaks while lots of people are still alive—wouldn’t they be restored in a living state?”

Suppressed fury, like that of a Counselor pushed too far.
