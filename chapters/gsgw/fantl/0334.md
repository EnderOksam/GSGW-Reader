# Chapter 333 - Hanbit Department Store, II

Today, the weather in Sekwang Special City was exceptionally good.

“Whew.”

Stepping outside Hanbit Department Store, I took a deep breath in and let it out.

There was no yellow dust, and it wasn’t as hot as I’d expected. The weather was wonderfully pleasant.

The people passing by seemed cheerful, too. I smiled as I walked along the sidewalk, keeping clear of a broadcasting station vehicle nearby.

‘Why do I feel so good today?’

My mind felt calm, like I’d just returned home after a long trip and finally gotten some proper rest.

Maybe it was because I’d just come out of a department store.

Or maybe there was less fine dust here than in Seoul....

‘Hm. Maybe it’s because it’s the day before Children’s Day.’

I checked the date on my smartphone.

@c@#*Monday, May 4*#@c@

It was Monday after the weekend, but knowing I could rest again tomorrow made me happy.

Maybe that was why I felt so good.

...Even though I still had to go to work now.

‘Business lunch....’

Well, come to think of it, it was better than a dinner engagement. Ugh.

But then, why did I take a day off?

It wasn’t as if I’d taken leave to enjoy a long break after the weekend. Taking a day off just to visit a department store made no sense, no matter how I looked at it....

‘.......’

That... doesn’t seem important!

The client meeting was probably scheduled suddenly after I’d already planned my day off.

Yeah. What matters is what I need to do now.

‘Let’s go entertain the client....’

Holding back a sigh, I started walking.

I had some time, and the weather was nice, so instead of taking the subway, I decided to walk to the lunch meeting spot.
p$

“Mr. Kim Soleum, I always say this, but you’re really a decent person.”

“Haha, no, Section Chief Park, it’s just that you’re kind enough to see me in a good light.”

The business lunch itself wasn’t particularly difficult.

Since it wasn’t a dinner meeting, there was no drinking involved, which made it easier, and we were already acquainted.

Platform companies had the upper hand everywhere, but as long as your company wasn’t too far behind in size, both sides tended to maintain a certain level of courtesy.

Moments like this made me grateful I’d landed a job at a major corporation.

Of course, the client still had the upper hand.

“So even if you study hard and get into a good place, you still end up doing such unproductive things. This country, really—”

“Haha....”

Making money isn’t easy....

He probably just liked talking to polite young people, which was why someone as junior as me got sent out.

‘Just don’t step on a landmine... whew.’

Making desperate conversational survival maneuvers, I steered things toward safer, more personal topics.

Thankfully, once we finished eating and stepped outside, we found something else to talk about.

“My kid goes to school here.”

“Oh, is your child’s school right there?”

I smiled as I looked at the high school building he pointed to.

Sekwang Technical High School.

It seemed close to a Meister high school, one that received extensive city-level support for technical education. *Editor’s Note: In South Korea, Meister high schools specialize in practical technical and vocational training, often working with industry to prepare students for skilled careers.*

Then the man beside me pointed past the school, toward a massive glass building.

“You see that? That’s a library. I think it really helps the academic atmosphere, so it makes me happy.”

“An environment for studying really matters. You sent them somewhere good.”

When a boss or someone important starts bragging about their kids, you’re supposed to nod along enthusiastically.

I looked at the enormous library beyond the school and did my best to sound suitably impressed.

Usually, after sweating through that kind of response, I’d get a reaction in return—

#f# – Excellent response, *Friend*! #f#
p$

‘......?’

Had someone said something like that?

A pleasant voice seemed to stir in my memory. I was sure I’d heard it often, somewhere....

‘...Maybe on TV?’

“.......”

“Mr. Kim Soleum?”

“...Yes! Section Chief!”

Oops, business meeting.

I quickly dropped the thought and smiled. I could look it up later.

“So your child must be in class right now.”

“That’s right! The school’s quiet as a mouse. Proper atmosphere, don’t you think?”

“Haha.”

That was when it happened.

Someone came sprinting out through the school’s front gate.

“Hm?”

The person rushing out wore neat casual clothes, not a uniform.

“Good-looking fellow. A student teacher, perhaps?”

“That could be.”

But as the person ran past on the other side of the crosswalk, I caught a glimpse of a cute name tag on their chest.

‘Counselor’

Probably something a student had made.

I’d heard that schools these days had professional School Counselors. He must be one of them.

“He’s heading to the subway. Doesn’t he have a car?”

“Haha....”

He sure did love talking about other people....

“Oh, that’s the taxi I called. Well then, I’ll head off now, Mr. Soleum.”

“Yes! Take care!”

I saw him off as he got into the taxi.

As I saw him off, my gaze happened to drift toward the subway station again. The Counselor was still running that way.

...He looked familiar.

I had the odd feeling I’d seen that retreating figure somewhere before—

$p
A sharp pain jolted through my hand.
p$

“...!”

Startled, I pulled my hand out of my pocket.

‘Static electricity?’

But it hurt so badly my hand tingled—far too much for mere static.

I pulled out the only metal object in my pocket.

A silver coin.

The coin had been tucked away on me for who knew how long, and by now it was black with grime.

‘What is this?’

I tried wiping the grime off with my fingers, but it made no difference.

‘...Is the material bad or something?’

I considered throwing it away. Just then, the silver surface reflected sunlight.

It was blinding.

I looked up instinctively and saw sunlight glinting off a window.

The windows of Sekwang Technical High School.

“.......”

Feeling strange, I walked closer to the school.

As if possessed, I approached the wall.

$p
“Excuse me!”
p$

I looked up.

Someone was calling to me from a window at the far end of the school’s third floor.

A student with dyed hair and piercings, wearing a loosely worn uniform, waved and called down to me.

What was he saying?

$p
“Down there!”
p$

I looked down.

There was a note on the ground near the wall.

It looked like the student had dropped it by accident.

“Could you pick it up? Just crumple it and throw it back.”

“Oh, sure.”

Was it a note he’d been passing to a friend during class? I picked it up and glanced at the message without thinking before tossing it back.

“.......”

I folded it up small enough that it wouldn’t blow away, then tossed it back up through the window.

“Thank you.”

Despite his appearance, the student with dyed hair thanked me with unexpected politeness, snatched the note, and disappeared back through the window.

I stood there blankly in front of the school wall until I finished my Americano.

I felt strange.
p$

I went home.

“Whew!”

My apartment had a neat two-room layout, with the living room and bedroom clearly separated.

Even after taking the bus home, I still had time.

‘Let’s enjoy my day off.’

I ordered chicken, sat by the living room window, cracked it open slightly, and enjoyed the familiar comfort.

“Ha.”

The breeze was cool.

‘Living alone isn’t so bad.’

My parents lived in Bundang, and my younger sibling ran a dessert shop nearby that was popular with the locals.

I lived alone in Seoul—

......No, I’d been assigned to ‘Sekwang Special City,’ living here.

‘Well, I go down to see them on weekends.’

Sometimes, when my sibling got swamped making some trendy dessert, I’d even get roped in as hired help on weekends.

$p

And whenever I thumped them, they’d yell, ‘Canceled! Canceled! Hyung!’

It felt like it was just yesterday. How heartwarming.

Come to think of it, it really was yesterday. I must have gone down over the weekend.

‘It’s spacious. Easier to breathe.’

Now that I thought about it, my apartment—

Sekwang wasn’t as insane as Seoul when it came to housing prices. I could’ve rented something bigger. Why did I sign a lease on this place?

...Probably to save money.

I shrugged, took off my suit jacket, and started getting ready for a shower.

But with the very first movement, my hand brushed against something soft, and I grabbed it.

“.......”

I reached into my pants pocket and pulled it out.

A pink rabbit plush.

The silver coin was one thing, but I had no idea where this came from.

If I had to guess, I probably bought it at the department store.

‘Why did I even go there today and take leave?’

No idea. Impulse?

Looking at it now, the plush seemed remarkably high-end—soft and cushiony, almost frighteningly intricate, yet strangely endearing.

I turned the plush around, examining it more closely.

The tag on its body bore intricate golden lettering. I couldn’t read it; perhaps it wasn’t Korean.

I pressed the rabbit’s belly.

No sound.

“...Seriously.”

What was I expecting? Dolls that made sounds when you squeezed their bellies went out of fashion ages ago. What on earth was I thinking?

“Sorry. That was stupid.”

I pressed the rabbit’s nose. It felt smooth.

After thinking it over, I washed my hands again, arranged a cushion and blanket into a little resting place on the side table, and set the rabbit there.

Under the soft lamp light, its black nose gleamed.

“...Comfortable?”

Of course, there was no answer.

Feeling oddly awkward, I fled to the shower.
p$

After showering and collecting my chicken delivery, I settled in front of the TV—

Well, I was streaming something rather than watching regular TV.

“Alright.”

It was a mystery-thriller drama.

As long as I skipped the more brutal scenes, it was watchable. No ghosts, after all.

But for some reason, watching it made me more uncomfortable than before.

Why?

Every time limbs were severed, or someone died in another’s place, my mood soured rapidly. 

It wasn’t as if they even showed where the limbs had been cut.

What’s wrong with me?

‘I thought I felt great today.’

Maybe entertaining the client had drained me, and the boost I’d gotten from the nice weather had worn off.

Holding back a sigh, I turned off the streaming service.

Then, a little earlier than usual, before the day was over, I lay in bed and turned on my phone.

“.......”

It felt like there was something I always looked at whenever I did this... but I couldn’t remember what. What was it?

‘Shorts?’

But it didn’t feel familiar.

I kept browsing, but nothing brought me any comfort or even a dopamine hit, so I turned my phone off.

Then I turned my head and nearly jumped out of my skin.

A giant human-shaped black shadow loomed on the wall.

“Aaak!”

...But on second look, it was just the rabbit plush’s shadow on the side table.

“Ha.”

Embarrassing. There’s no one else here. This is like jumping at the sight of a pot lid after being startled by a turtle. *Editor’s Note: This Korean proverb describes how a past fright can make even something harmless seem frightening. A pot lid resembles a turtle’s shell.*

Calming my pounding heart, I looked at the shadow again.

Streetlights and headlights from outside made it flicker, almost as if it were moving—

But that was all.

“.......”

Let’s sleep.

I shrugged, buried my head in the pillow, and strangely found myself savoring the lingering feeling, almost as though I were disappointed.

And fell asleep....

What... was I waiting for?

Not important. Let’s go home.

“Whew.”

Something dropped from my front pocket.

A rabbit plush.

Did I buy this?

‘...Merch?’

But it looked too worn for that.

‘Don’t really know.’

I shrugged and put the coin back.

I must’ve gotten it somewhere.

I tucked the plush back against my chest for now and headed toward the department store’s lobby doors.

Out of Hanbit Department Store,

in Sekwang Special City.
