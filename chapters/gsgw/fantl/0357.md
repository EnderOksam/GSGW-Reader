# Chapter 356 - Sekwang Sealing Ritual, X

Agent Choi, standing beneath the tracks, spoke in a flat, explanatory tone.

But the facts were staggering.

“The re-sealing ritual for Sekwang Special City is already underway.”

......!

“A ritual this massive takes at least three days. It’s already begun.”

My head grew cold as I understood.

There was no way the obliteration of an entire metropolis could happen in a short span of time...

Especially not when a countdown of 300 days was given.

It was no surprise that it had already begun and was in full swing.

“Of course, it’s a different approach from a typical gut or devotional rite, but the underlying principle of ‘devotion’ is similar. Everything works out better when you put in the effort, right?”

“......”

“They timed this so everything would align perfectly when the seal is lifted two days from now. The specialist agents, having completed their seven-day anban-gut[1]{Editor’s Note: Anban-gut (안반굿): A preparatory shamanic ritual conducted prior to a main gut to cleanse the space and invoke spiritual presence.} and ritual purification, are already wielding their shinkal[2]{Editor’s Note: Shinkal (신칼): Ritual divine swords used by Korean shamans to channel spirit energy and dispel malevolent forces.}, divine swords used in shamanic rituals.”

Agent Choi describes it as if he can see it.

“The ritual implement is a myeongdu (明斗)[3]{Editor’s Note: Myeongdu (명두): A sacred brass mirror used in Korean shamanic rites, associated with the sun, moon, and stars and used as a medium for the divine.}, basically a brass mirror. The sun-and-moon myeongdu, engraved with gugyeol[4]{Editor’s Note: Gugyeol (구결): The term traditionally refers to annotations used to aid the reading of Classical Chinese texts.} inscriptions symbolizing Sekwang Special City, will be covered with fresh blood symbolizing the offering and turned upside down on the floor of the ritual shrine...”

Somewhere, I almost hear the clang of gongs, shouted chants, drums pounding, bells ringing madly...

And Agent Choi looks at me.

“I planned to switch the blood of the sacrifice that was to be poured on the myeongdo.”

My hair stands on end.

“...You don’t mean, your own blood?”

“That’s right.”

“Are you out of your mi—”

“Listen to the rest.”

He cuts me off and continues calmly.

“Then the direction of the sacrifice changes. Originally, they would have prepared the blood of a spiritually potent animal whose saju had been matched to represent the citizens of Sekwang Special City... but now, that sal[5]{Editor’s Note: Sal (살): A malevolent spiritual aura or destructive energy in Korean shamanism associated with misfortune, sudden illness, and bad luck.} will be coming to me.”

“......”

“Of course, that alone wouldn’t be enough. So from the moment the seal lifts, I’d perform a separate ritual. I guess in modern terms, you’d call it drawing aggro.”

“How were you planning to substitute for an entire city’s population by yourself?”

“It’s simple. Just make the numbers fit.”

“......”

A sense of foreboding hung in the air.

“What matters is the moment a life is severed. That’s the crucial part of this offering. So I would...”

“Die that many times?”

“......”

“As many times as there are people?”

“That’s right.”

Agent Choi says it nonchalantly.

‘Wait a minute.’

What kind of number are we talking about?

‘Hundreds of thousands of people are dying every day... so he’s going to repeat that by himself, every single day...’

The scale is so astronomical it borders on cosmic horror.

The hair on the top of my head stands on end.

“Of course, it’s not like just anyone could complete the ritual that way. I’ve got a fair bit of supernatural pull myself, which is why I can even try something like this.”

“A-are you crazy?”

Did he go insane?

Could this even be described as insanity?

“No. It’s actually bearable. Since I won’t remember each time I die, I won’t go mad either.”

He let out a sigh.

“Of course, I’m not planning to endure this forever or become some kind of living jangseung of suffering.[6]{Editor’s Note: Jangseung (장승): Traditional Korean guardian poles, typically placed at village entrances to ward off evil and protect the community.} I planned to leave hints behind, just like Team Leader Hong did at Sekwang High School.”

“...!”

“Then someday you two would come save me, right? Bronze-ie and Grapes.”

“......”

“Not something as reckless as this. You’ll be able to try after more research. It’ll be different if Bronze-ie is there.”

A faint smile flickers across Agent Choi’s face, then fades back into weary calm.

His gaze shifts to the body lying between us.

Specifically, the corpse of the dead Agent Bronze.

“...Agent.”

“Don’t worry. Do you know how many times I’ve seen the bodies of people I know while out on dispatch? I know he’s sleeping soundly outside, too.”

Yet Agent Choi looks at the motionless Bronze as if he were a younger brother or son who had fallen in battle. He reaches out, strokes his hair, and quietly puts his hand down.

“...Seeing as the body hasn’t disappeared, he probably hasn’t woken up outside. His consciousness is probably still caught on the surface of Sekwang Special City.”

“......”

“When the seal holding the citizens breaks, Bronze will come out, too.”

He says it like a vow.

Then he glances briefly toward the track exit—which probably looks like nothing to him—and looks back at me.

“Shall we continue our talk, then? You were also curious about what I did to get you out of Daydream’s basement.”

“......”

“It’s simple. Think of it like a collateral loan.”

He rubs the back of his neck.

“If I don’t offer a suitable gift within a few years to... well, ‘the person’ who found you for me, a few parts of me are going to disappear.”

This is insane...

“It’s a bit tricky to find a gift that satisfies them. If nothing else works, I’ll have to try to make sure the parts I lose are internal organs. Then I could coexist with a dokkaebi flame, or perhaps become like the Elder.”

“......”

“That’s it.”

I stare at him, feeling like I’ve just been doused with ice water.

“Anything else you want to know? If so, say it now.”

Having nonchalantly dropped a bombshell that made my brain reel, Agent Choi watches me without a change in expression.

“The reason I never told you any of this before is simple. Hearing it would only worry you, and there wasn’t much to learn from it.”

“......”

“Right? It’s not like you’re going to learn gut like I did.”

A trace of his usual banter finally returns to his voice.

I look him straight in the eye.

“Please don’t lie.”

“......”

“You just didn’t want to deal with my concern or resistance. Because you never intended to regret or change your decision anyway.”

“...If you want to put it that way, sure.”

“Does Agent Haegeum know about this? Did she agree?”

“Whether she agrees or not doesn’t matter. I’m the only one among us who can interfere with the sealing ritual.”

His voice roughens.

“This is a method I’m fairly confident I can pull off.”

“......”

“But you decided to do as you pleased, trusted Lee Kangheon’s note on your own, and said you were going to haul the Potion Maker to City Hall.”

“Because persuasion doesn’t work.”

“You’re the same.”

“No. It’s different.”

I grit my teeth.

“You don’t believe a word I say, so there’s no way persuasion can work.”

“Yeah. Blindly trusting someone corrupted by a Supernatural Disaster is basically suicide!”

“......”

“That could put both of us in danger. There’s more than just a few cases where someone tried to follow a missing Agent and went missing themselves! You...”

He pauses to breathe.

“...Right. You said you came from another world.”

“......”

“You know what? It’s not like I didn’t try to believe you.”

His voice drops.

“You were gone for 293 days. That’s long enough to build a road. Do you really think I wouldn’t have tried to verify whether you were telling the truth?”

...!!

“...You checked?”

“Of course.”

He gives a bitter smile, then sobers.

“The ritual site I set up doesn’t just let me peek at the sealing ritual. I chose an auspicious time and performed spirit divination. To check whether what you said was true.”

I inhale.

Tension clamps down on my body.

“I chose my sentences carefully. I crammed in everything I could, and structured them properly, too...”

‘So that was it.’

So... that’s why he was being so decisive.

Relief, futility, frustration, and a surge of indignation swirl together, kneading into a strange mixture before dissipating in my mind.

Then clarity settles in.

“I understand.”

My voice is calm.

A faint spark of hope flickers in his eyes, but there’s also a much greater sense of guardedness.

Of course.

That’s expected.

“You’re right. I’ve been influenced by too many ghost stories in too short a time, and I only recently managed to steady myself. My fixation on going home is excessive.”

But.

“But even those people still have a home to return to.”

“And so do I, Agent.”

I look at him again and smile.

“You chose the wrong wording for your divination.”

“...!”

“I don’t fall under that category. If anything, I was invited into this body. I’m not a lost child; I’m a guest.”

“...A guest?”

“I guess this also sounds like a cult. In any case, one thing is certain.”

His pupils tremble.

I speak gently.

“Whether Agent-nim believes me or not, you can’t stop my choice. My choice is mine to make, based on my own judgment, and I’ll do what I believe is right.”

“But—”

“Just as you have already done, Agent.”

Right?

“You only told me all this now because everything for the resealing ritual is already prepared, and I can’t interfere anymore.”

“...!”

“I’m not angry.”

I really am not.

After hearing so much, so honestly, from an exhausted Agent Choi...

Even if there were no handcuffs, I don’t think I would grab him by the collar and shout at him, asking him if he was crazy.

A calm resolve settles over me.

“From your perspective, that is the most certain answer. A mistake could get me or someone else killed, and you can’t choose another method just for fun, or simply because you’re drawn to it.”

“......”

“My method seems like an unverified gamble, and my desire to go home probably seems distorted, too.”

“...Still.”

His voice cracks slightly.

“I’m still always ready to listen to you. About your home, or anything else.”

“......”

For a moment, I’m at a loss for words.

“...Thank you.”

I know.

You’re passionate, have a firm judgment, choose self-sacrifice over fear, and are intellectually curious. If something isn’t rational, you won’t be easily persuaded.

...A good person.

This was exactly what an early Bureau agent looked like.

He had always been that kind of person.

“Even so, your judgment probably won’t change.”

Saying it only reinforces my certainty.

“Yes. We can’t persuade each other.”

Not right now.

“Grapes-ie.”

“But...”

I inhale and give a wry smile.

“I still think we can cooperate.”

“......”

“I think you’re an incredible person. And until now... well. I can’t say I never lied. I was a spy, after all.”

“......”

“I know I’ve said a lot of things that can’t be verified. So I won’t demand your trust.”

His eyes tremble slightly.

“I just thought, what if we made a promise?”

With my free hand, I gesture as if making one.

“For this Sekwang Special City incident, let’s cooperate so each of us can attempt what we want. No interference. Genuine help.”

“......”

“If even one of us succeeds, it’s a good thing for the citizens. And if we don’t cooperate, the probability of both attempts failing only increases.”

I deliberately hold his gaze.

“Even if everything looks like a suicide mission because you can’t trust me, you can at least increase the survival rate, right?”

“You...”

“I’m just joking. Still, I’d appreciate your cooperation.”

“......”

“If your answer is no, there’s nothing for it. I’ll just have to try without cooperation.”
