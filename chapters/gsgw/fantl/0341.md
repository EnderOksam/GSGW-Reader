# Chapter 340 - Sekwang Special City Disaster, VI

We managed to board the train heading for Sekwang Station about twenty minutes later.

$Brd
[Good grief, at this rate we’ll be trampled to death! What a filthy spectacle!]
Brd$

We had to let several trains go by.

‘I almost got crushed to death trying to get on.’

It was the point where people who had suffered serious injuries while fleeing began desperately fighting to stay on the trains, refusing to get off.

‘Everyone had realized that their bodies would stay intact as long as they remained on the train.’

Between those trying to board and those refusing to get off, the platform descended into chaos as people shoved each other to squeeze inside.

...In fact, I even saw someone get trampled to death.

“...Hoo.”

Watching the horrific scene unfold beyond any chance of intervention, I clenched my teeth and focused on keeping the two of them safe.

“Please come this way.”

Instead of forcing our way through, we moved calmly to the very end of the platform, where there were relatively fewer people, and attempted to board from there.

<br>

“As expected.”

People in panic rushed along the same routes like lemmings, but the very last car, which they bypassed, remained comparatively orderly.

...For now.

I pushed the two of them inside and managed to stand directly in front of the doors.

@c@***The screen doors are now closing.***@c@

<br>

Done.

“Hoo.”

The relief and tension twisted my insides to the point it almost hurt.

But Reporter Eun Haje raised an eyebrow as she looked at me.

“You’re pretty capable. Calm, too.”

“Thank you.”

“There’s mayhem outside, but people here aren’t in terrible shape. From what I overheard on the way, it sounds like some kind of zombie virus outbreak.”

I replied evenly.

“The underground is relatively safe.”

“Is it? That’s strange. Normally, with biochemical terrorism, toxic substances settle downward, so underground should be more dangerous, shouldn’t it?”

Sharp.

“And... how does that beanpole like you know things like that?”

“......”

I checked our surroundings.

With the noise this loud, no one seemed to have the spare attention to eavesdrop. Everyone was lost in their own pain and desperation, trying to contact someone, clinging to acquaintances in fear, or staring outside.

I spoke.

“...Think of me as someone involved in disaster situations like this, someone familiar with them.”

“Does that have anything to do with being called an ‘agent’?”

“......”

So she heard.

“Yes.”

I briefly explained the Disaster Management Bureau to the reporter. Her eyes sparkled as she listened.

“Even if it sounds far-fetched, it fits the situation well enough.”

When I finished explaining that there were people to rescue and evacuate and that the operation was already underway, she laughed heartily.

“Alright. ‘Agent,’ then you’ll need to write a report too, won’t you?”

“Possibly.”

“You’ll need someone to record what you did, right?”

She then pointed to herself with a grin.

“I’d be perfect.”

“......”

“Few people are better than me at tracking conspiracies and incidents. I’ll write it very objectively, with proper analysis and interpretation.”

It really just sounds like you want to publish something...

“...Well, fine.”

I suppressed a sigh and nodded.

“I’d appreciate that.”

“Okay. Good choice.”

Reporter Eun Haje winked.

But in truth, she wouldn’t be able to write that article as a reporter anyway.

What was here wasn’t the real reporter, but only an assistant manager, contaminated and fixed into a form suitable for the Disaster Day of Sekwang Special City.

...Actually, I had already tried using the resort contract, invoking an ‘employer’s order’ to revert her to her original form.

It didn’t work.

All I had done was add a single line about the resort contract to the existing agreement. I couldn’t exercise any more authority than that.

If it had worked, she would’ve changed into her resort uniform, and her injuries would’ve healed, but it didn’t work at all.

That was the limit of my abilities as the resort manager.

‘Does this mean Sekwang Special City is blocking the influence of other ghost stories after all?’

I’d assumed it was because the city was isolated, but perhaps it was a property of the disaster itself, which had started at City Hall...

.......

Wait.

The opposite side of where we were standing.

To the right.

“Aaaaah!”

My blood ran cold.

I immediately hooked my arm around the pole and reached for the other two. Reporter Eun Haje, already clutching the other pole, and student Ryu Jaekwan, gripping the same rail, reached out and grabbed my arm and shoulder.

We were nearly suspended in midair, dangling crookedly against the left-side doors.

“What is it?! What’s happening?!”

“H-hhkk!”

The train was still moving, but it was terrifyingly unstable.

“Agent... there’s a strange sound...”

It wasn’t the friction noise of a curve.

It was...

The sound of something breaking.

“Ah.”

The train was running while packed far beyond its capacity.

Finally, unable to bear the weight any longer, it derailed.

It began to overturn.

$p
#^# Screeeeeech! #^#
p$

“Aaaagh!”

“Help us!”

Screams came from the front cars. The train continued to tilt and barrel forward without slowing, rocking violently.

Then it began to topple completely with a massive thud.

“Aaaaaah!”

The pole we were holding rose toward the ceiling. People collapsed downward like dumped cargo, piling up with the sound of bones snapping.

Those crushed at the very bottom must have already been reduced to pulp.

We were being buried.

The train kept sliding, its body bouncing along.

“No—!”

If I let go, I’d be swept away.

“Calm down, calm down—fuck!”

Gritting my teeth, I prepared to land as carefully as possible.

“Before it flips completely, I need to slide down to the floor.”

I’d slide down the pole.

The hands of the two beside me were slick with tension and cold sweat. I sent them down first, then drew a breath and pulled on the pole to prepare for my own descent.

And then I saw it.

$p
Outside the window, a blue light flickered.
p$

“...!”

A dokkaebi.

For a split second I blanked out, then snapped back to my senses and turned toward the device mounted beside the door.

“Emergency release...!”

I operated the manual mechanism, setting the door to open.

“Hurry!”

I climbed the pole again and reached for the door.

“Agent, step on me...!”

“...!”

Using high schooler Ryu Jaekwan’s shoulder as a foothold, I launched myself forward and grabbed the door.

Then I pried it open slightly.

$p
*Whoosh.*
p$

An eerie light slipped through the gap in the door.

The blue traces of dokkaebi flame lingered near the door.

It looked like a rescue attempt to anyone else...

“...A trace?”

There was no expression or personality in the dokkaebi flame.

Just a mass of energy that had lost its purpose.

“Then...!”

I released my grip on the pole.

“...!”

Barely hanging onto the ceiling-mounted door with one hand, I thrust my other arm through the gap—

And grabbed the trace of the dokkaebi flame.

Then hurled it with all my might beneath the train.

$p
*Whoom.*

The sacred flame of a Cooperative Spirit, recognizing me as an agent, flowed through my hand and connected with the subway wheels below.

A flash of blue light—

$p

“...!!”

“Urrgh—!”

The train rotated ninety degrees back in the opposite direction, but not too violently. The door I was holding shut on its own. The sound of wheels spinning in place echoed.

$p

The sound of rails and wheels locking together.

“......”

Outside the windows, the scenery began to move smoothly once more...

“...Ha.”

Clinging to the door, I let out a long breath.

“Are you okay?”

“Huh... more or less?”

“Yes—yes!”

We were safe. Relief made my heartbeat thunder in my head.

“Damn... that beanpole can fly.”

I gave a weak smile at Reporter Eun Haje’s amazement.

But the inside of the car had already turned to hell.

“Hhngh...”

“Minjeong? Minjeong!”

I could see the corpses of those crushed to death and hear the cries of people with broken bones.

Bodies were piled right beside us, close enough to brush against.

The train, once packed with people, was now filled with corpses too.

“Damn it.”

I reflexively pulled the high schooler away, closer to the door.

The reporter, on the other hand, was carefully observing the carnage.

My hair stood on end, but I eventually forced myself up on trembling legs and looked at the mass of people.

It was horrifying... but after seeing so much death all day, I felt a strange, desolate, dry exhaustion instead.

I staggered forward and began pulling survivors out of the pile. Once the interior was somewhat cleared, I withdrew my hands—

“Wow, thank you, fellow agent. We’re alive because you were here.”

...!!

I whipped my head around.

Beyond the crowd in the front cars, several new figures had appeared inside the train.

Figures tending to the crushed and injured.

They wore blue jackets very similar to mine.

Binding Ropes, Glass Hand-Cannon...

...and traces of blue dokkaebi flame.

Then—

“If this hadn’t been handled before the next train came, it would’ve been a real disaster.”

Beside me, a person wearing a traditional Lion Mask appeared and began babbling away.

On the blue jacket beneath the mask, letters were visible.

$p
Supernatural Disaster Management Bureau.

They were agents from the Disaster Management Bureau.

“...!!”

The Disaster Day of Sekwang Special City.

The agents dispatched that day had appeared right before my eyes.

The one wearing the Lion Mask looked at me, the person who’d helped reconnect the train to the rails.

Then, sounding slightly anxious yet cheerful, they asked.

“You even cleaned up this car? You handled that dokkaebi flame really well earlier. Which branch are you from, Agent?”

.......

“I’m not from the Sekwang branch.”

“Ohhh, I see.”

The eyes inside the playful yet fierce Lion Mask seemed to glow yellow as they looked at my jacket.

“...But that uniform doesn’t look official. Seems like you’re winging it rather than wearing the regulation gear.”

“That’s right.”

I answered deliberately awkwardly.

“I’m on leave, so I didn’t have my uniform. I borrowed this from a local Cooperative Spirit in a hurry.”

“Hah. Living with gums when you’ve got no teeth, now that’s a real field agent answer...”

“Haha.”

They laughed heartily at my wry smile.

I quickly looked them over...

“...Male? Female?”

I couldn’t even tell.

Like the masks of Daydream, the Lion Mask obscured perception, making the agent’s appearance and traits hard to grasp.

Even the voice, high or low, I couldn’t tell. The playful aura of the Lion Mask flattened all individuality.

“...Or maybe it’s the dokkaebi’s power.”

Sure enough, they brought up the dokkaebi with a slightly troubled tone.

“Do you also travel with a dokkaebi flame? One of my partner dokkaebi flames suddenly vanished this morning. It left nothing but an afterimage.”

Of course it did.

All the real dokkaebi flames would have been removed before the sealing ritual was performed.

There wouldn’t be any left in Sekwang Special City on May 4th, trapped in the day of the disaster.

But I didn’t let it show.

“...I’m on leave, so I don’t have any dokkaebi flame or equipment. But if that’s true, it’s no ordinary situation.”

“Yeah... Normally, that thing sticks to me like glutinous rice cake.”

The Lion Mask agent scratched at the mask.

“What’s weird is that there are quite a few traces left. Its ego vanished, but something like a backup battery remains.”

“......”

That meant—

‘Could the power of the dokkaebi flames used on the day of the disaster in Sekwang Special City still be trapped in here?’

Just like at Sekwang Technical High School.

“Did you hear anything else when you borrowed that uniform from the Cooperative Spirit?”

“Nothing in particular.”

Instead, I relayed what the Counselor had said, that they were heading to Sekwang Station to end the disaster.

“I see. Hmm...”

The Lion Mask agent turned away.

I swallowed as I watched agents in the front cars calming people and separating bodies as best they could.

And my companions...

“So you’re both from the same Disaster Management Bureau, but you don’t know each other? I suppose the departments are quite divided, with separate branches for each region?”

“Hahaha. Yes, that’s right.”

The Lion Mask agent brushed off the reporter’s question easily, then suddenly patted Ryu Jaekwan on the head and said with a smile.

“I take it you’re the ones requesting rescue? Are you with the Azure Dragon Team?”

I smiled bitterly.

And before I realized it, I answered.

“We were actually from the Black Tortoise Team. But we have no equipment... so for now, we’re rescuing whoever we can.”

“Haha! Impressive, fellow agent.”

A shimmering jade-colored weapon made of glass.

A Glass Hand-Cannon.

“I’ll give this to you. I brought a spare.”

“So you’re getting off at Sekwang Station. You’ll check on the condition of the allied Cooperative Spirit and re-establish a rescue route.”
