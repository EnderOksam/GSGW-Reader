# Chapter 330 - The Rabbit Doll Cult, IV

Inside the driver’s cab.

The conductor, his face ashen, glanced back and forth between my agent uniform and the commotion outside, then began stammering out everything he knew.

About this train.

“You know how it was... how it was before, don’t you? I mean, the subway trains were strangely safe to begin with...”

Almost everyone seemed to have figured out the gimmick on the very first day of the disaster. Even injured people recovered once they got aboard a train.

“People shoved to get on, fought over seats... fights broke out, people died, it was absolute chaos.”

“So had people already claimed the spots on this train too?”

“No! This one came out of nowhere!”

...!

“The next day, we heard another train would be running. Then it pulled into the station and opened its doors, just like that!”

I could picture the scene clearly.

People who’d barely survived without getting aboard were gathered on the relatively safe platform. Then another train pulled in, seemingly empty, and its doors opened.

“...And Agent Chogae and the other agents must have been on board.”

“T-T-That’s right.”

The conductor’s eyes darted about as he tried to compose himself and sound convincing.

“But I recognized this train. It was a broken-down train!”

“...!”

“You know how it is. When a train breaks down during operation, you don’t always haul it back to the depot. Sometimes you shunt it off onto a side line. Once subway service ends for the day, they usually park trains on sidings hidden away in the stations. It was probably on one of those.”

“......”

“I don’t know how they repaired it, but they came driving it right into the station and started taking aboard the people still there...”

They likely used supernatural equipment or abilities to make the broken train functional again.

Since they had already been swallowed by a Supernatural Phenomenon, there would’ve been no reason to hesitate.

“As for me... yes. They asked if anyone had experience driving trains, and... yes, I helped Agent Chogae a lot. Hahaha...”

So this man hadn’t even been this train’s driver in the first place.

‘Is that why they call him the conductor?’

“Subways are mostly automated these days, sure, but still, having a professional makes a huge difference, doesn’t it?”

“Yes. I understand Agent Chogae’s judgment.”

But I looked back at the sheet beside the controls. Dozens of names, all crossed out with X’s.

“But I imagine he expected that ‘professional’ to behave differently after he was gone.”

“No, Agent, Agent! You really have to understand this. Do you have any idea how hard it is to look after this many people for years?”

The conductor spoke desperately.

“The agents, you see, they’d go out to the station to get supplies, and then one by one they wouldn’t come back! It happened because they kept trying to take aboard every refugee who came to the train. They were short on food and everything else, and they still went looking for enough for everyone. That’s how it got this bad!”

“...Is that how you see it?”

“Of course! You know how it is, when people are all stuck together, you have to make a few cuts here and there, you know? Throw away what needs throwing away, or the system won’t run!”

“......”

“It was all to keep this shelter going, you know? To honor Agent Chogae’s wishes! I tried to keep it going, and I got in over my head and made mistakes!”

I didn’t feel anger surge up.

Just a strange feeling.

The outside world had thrown him away under that very same logic of ‘throw away what needs throwing away.’

It was strange to hear him defend the logic that had left him stranded here.

The agents had pushed themselves too far trying to save someone like him. One by one, they’d all died. The thought left me strangely drained.

‘......I probably shouldn’t be the one to judge what happens from here.’

That was for the people who’d been living in the train shelter all along to decide for themselves.

So instead of showing hostility, I resumed gathering information.

“Tell me more about the time Agent Chogae went missing. I heard he went out to get food.”

“What? No, there’s some misunderstanding here. I absolutely did not kick him out, Agent!”

Hmm?

I stayed silent on purpose. The conductor grew more nervous, watching for my reaction as he poured out excuse after excuse.

“You’re asking why he picked that moment to go out when we weren’t short on food, aren’t you?”

“...Well, he tried to give the people on the train hope. Stuff like, one day we’d be able to escape, that he was looking for a way...”

I could see him roll his eyes at what he took for the usual platitudes.

That alone told me enough.

‘...He must have been through so much.’

The Disaster Management Bureau agents would have understood, too. If anything, being agents made it all the clearer to them.

Countless people had died on the surface, and down here in the city’s subway ghost story, there was no telling how much longer they’d be trapped with the survivors.

‘At some point, he must have realized they were sealed in.’

An agent familiar with shamanic rites or other rituals might even have picked up on signs of human sacrifice.

They would’ve had to keep that suspicion to themselves.

Few survivors were left, but there were still too many for one person to look after. Reassuring them all and keeping the shelter from descending into chaos again and again would have been no small task...

“Oh, r-right! Agent Chogae left something behind! A personal item. You could say it’s the only keepsake we have of him now. Over there...”

“Don’t move.”

The conductor watched me warily and started to rise, then froze again when I grabbed his shoulder.

Keeping one hand pressed firmly on his shoulder, I opened the drawer he’d pointed to.

A small drawer beside the controls.

When I opened it, dust stirred around the single small item in the otherwise empty drawer.

“......”

It was a five-colored shoelace.

Agents were issued these to help them escape, though they didn’t work here.

Dust had settled over the neatly tied lace.

...The ends were slightly worn.

As if someone had often handled it for comfort.

“......”

The wish to get out.

And... it felt like a thread of hope. Something that said he meant to get out someday.

That’s when I realized.

‘He must have done something.’

“Yes.”

Under the circumstances, her laughter didn’t last.

“Ah, there he is.”

He’d spent the last few days visiting Jang Heoun—that is, Agent Hwagak—under the pretext of gathering food, and now couldn’t adjust to the sudden, chaotic transformation of the train.

The sight was enough to make him forget the bitterness of going outside to tend to the dead body and keep it from rotting.

And now he’d finally found someone he knew.
