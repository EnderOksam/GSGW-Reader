“What, do you think any good people are working at this company? Ah, is it because you’re an agent—you have to save anyone you see? That’s not true, is it? You don’t save people when the situation doesn’t allow it.”

‘It’s surprising, though—he did seem like he was trying to help me just now.’

Still, I didn’t understand why he was acting as if he’d been betrayed. It was excessively intense, like a trauma response...

‘...Hmm.’

Was it because I’m an agent?

Thinking back to what happened in Jisan Village, I chose my words carefully.

I felt chilled to the bone and let out a sigh.

“Sir.”

I spoke as calmly as I could.

“I’m not doing this because I’m an agent. ...It’s just simple logic.”

I looked at the rescued person, who was still crouched in the corner, trembling as he fumbled with his flayed skin.

“If we can all live, there’s no need to let someone die.”

“......”

Instead of waiting for Baek Saheon’s reply, I lowered myself toward the person I’d brought along.

And asked quietly,

“Would you prefer to stay somewhere safe?”

“Hik—hng...”

He nodded frantically.

Then he glanced fearfully at Baek Saheon. Baek Saheon stared back at him with a cold, calculating face, as if assessing a potential threat to his own survival...

But I didn’t react to either of them. I simply declared, slowly, “Alright. Then we’ll move to a safe place.”

I supported the rescued person and went up one more floor via the emergency stairs.

I didn’t call for Baek Saheon.

He seemed to stand there blankly for a moment, but instead of running off with the camcorders, I heard him follow after me.

I entered the sixth floor.

‘Definitely less brutal than the fifth floor.’

The sealed ward’s corridor was dark, but instead of the heavy stench of rot, the sharp smell of disinfectant dominated.

It still felt ominous and uncanny, like something out of a horror attraction—but at least it looked more normal.
