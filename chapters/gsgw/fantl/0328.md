# Chapter 327 - The Rabbit Doll Cult, I

A few days after Kim Soleum’s ‘Grand Plan to Dominate the Train Shelter’ was launched.

<br>

“Damn it.”

A train resident was irritably tossing a magazine aside in Car No. 2.

The cover featured a celebrity striking a pose, but the magazine was already ragged and torn.

‘So damn bored.’

Today was another unbearably dull day.

Before ‘the incident,’ he had been in his final year of university. Job-hunting stress had pushed him to the brink, and he’d even wished the world would just end.

But after the world really ended, he missed that ordinary life like crazy.

At least back then, he could watch short videos.

‘This sucks.’

He got up and looked around, trying to convince himself that he was still living in the top 1% among the people in this city.

The comfortable Car No. 2.

Cars 3 and 4, a step down.

Even worse conditions in the rear cars.

And outside the train, monsters roamed freely.

‘I’m a winner.’

If you stayed on this train long enough, a way out would eventually appear. He’d heard that the person who built this shelter had said, ‘There will be a way to leave later.’

And since he was in the front cars, he’d surely be included in the priority group when that time came.

Safe.

The person who’d said that had been expelled, even back when there was still room in the shelter.

He’d been such an obnoxious ass that of course they’d thrown him out. The college student had found it pretty satisfying, too.

‘If you’re going to cling to a train someone else claimed first, you should at least be polite, right? We’re the ones being generous here.’

This former ‘college student,’ quickly forgetting that he’d only gotten in because one of his seniors was already there, left Car No. 2 and wandered off.

He was so bored that he just wanted to find something to look at.

That was when he saw it.

Between Cars No. 4 and 5.

In the narrow passage between the front and rear cars, people were gathered, laughing and whispering.

‘Huh?’

Usually, people in the front cars subtly looked down on those in the rear.

He didn’t realize that, trapped aboard the train in fear and boredom, the front-car residents were using their sense of superiority to squeeze out a little dopamine.

But today, that air of condescension seemed strangely subdued.

Why?

‘That...’

A high school student from the rear cars was chatting cheerfully with a middle-aged woman from the front.

After showing each other something, the student gave a small bow and headed back to the rear cars.

The woman, meanwhile, put on a deliberately nonchalant expression, as though hiding something, and walked away...

‘What did they exchange?’

The college student immediately followed her and spoke up.

“Ma’am, what were you talking about with someone from the back?”

“Huh? Oh, nothing, just asking how they were doing...”

As she tried to brush it off, he clearly caught the fleeting emotions in her eyes.

$p
Superiority.

The pride of knowing something.

Pleasure.
p$

Those feelings showed through for an instant before she forced them back down.

‘What?’

His mood soured instantly.

At the same time, anxiety crept in.

‘Something seems to be making the rounds...’

If the hierarchy on the train was about to change, shouldn’t he get involved fast?

If he missed out and fell behind, he might have to do dangerous day labor himself, going outside to scavenge for food.

Or worse, pushed aside during an escape.

“Excuse me, ma’am.”

In the end, he humbled himself more than ever before, hanging around the woman, flattering her, and relentlessly prying for details.

The woman kept insisting, ‘There’s nothing like that,’ but eventually, with a look on her face as if she were doing him a great favor, she whispered.

$p
“Go check Car No. 7.”
p$

And so, the college student stepped into the rear cars.

$p
*Clatter.*
p$

The door slid open, revealing Car No. 7.

It was a place for trading supplies with outsiders, so people usually avoided it unless necessary. Uproars had broken out here before, and several people had even died.

But today, it was chaos for a different reason.

“Line up!”

“Hey, move it already!”

More than ten people crowded in front of a cramped, unremarkable nook where goods were usually piled up.

Among them were several familiar faces from the front cars.

‘What the hell...?’

Rear-car residents were all but forbidden from going forward by an unspoken rule, but front-car residents came to the back whenever they felt like it, making all sorts of excuses.

Still, he’d never seen this many before.

‘What are they selling?’

He tried to push forward, but wary of the front-car people’s reactions, he ended up lining up instead.

After several dozen minutes in line, he finally caught sight of the ‘goods,’ almost beside himself with impatience.

The sign was a shabby piece of cardboard cut from an old box, but the goods on display were glossy and intricately detailed.

Chocolate bars, cookie sets, popcorn... even salads with fresh green vegetables and hot dogs.

‘Huh...??’

Every surface was decorated with colorful, theme-park-like illustrations.

“Where did all this come from?”

When the college student demanded an answer, the vendor hunched their shoulders.

The vendor wore glasses, and their messy hair made their face hard to see.

“Th-the station with the home-shopping place... something new opened there... and I happened to go...”

Apparently, there was a new place to get food at Afternoon Station.

Of course, since being accepted into the train shelter, the college student had never gone down to a station to do ‘that kind of thing.’

Anyway, the vendor claimed they’d been the 100th customer and received a massive pile of snacks.

Even items that weren’t sold to regular buyers.

‘Lucky bastard.’

The college student frowned and glanced at them. The vendor bowed even deeper.

‘Can’t even make eye contact?’

It was amazing someone this pathetic had survived this long. He could only imagine what a sorry state they’d be in if they stepped off the train.

‘Trying to worm your way into being a train resident with this stuff?’

Then they should’ve donated everything ‘for the sake of the train shelter,’ not tried to do business with it.

But judging by the timid wording on the sign, they hadn’t thought that far.

‘Total pushover.’

“What’s your name?”

“Uh, I... please call me Grapes.”

Hiding his real name, too? Who does he think he is?

Anyway, eyeing the snacks greedily, he said, “I’ll pick some out and bring you a lodging pass later. Got it?”

“Huh...? Th-that’s a bit...”

“I’m saying I’ll give you one, so why not? What, don’t trust me? If you don’t trust train residents, why are you selling here?”

The vendor fidgeted, but eventually nodded.

“Two bags of snacks for one lodging pass...”

“Yeah, yeah.”

Brushing it off, the college student quickly picked his snacks.

Even though food supplies weren’t scarce in the front cars, flavor was a luxury.

There were limits to what could be brought back.

Not to mention the weird, barely edible stuff or rough, raw vegetables people often dragged in.

For people used to that, new snacks were more stimulating than food itself.

There were keychains and magnets too, but he didn’t even glance at them.

Chocolate bars and popcorn.

He hurriedly stuffed the snacks into an inner pocket of his padded jacket, then went to get a lodging pass.

Getting one for free from the rear cars, where people avoided conflict with those in the front, was easy.

He hurried back to the stall run by that idiot, ‘Grapes,’ and held out the pass.

“Here.”

“Th-thank you...”

As ‘Grapes’ bowed his head and reached out with both hands to accept the pass—

“No, if you’re grateful, give me some freebies.”

“Huh?”

The college student snatched the pass back and grabbed a handful of candy from the stall.

“One of these should do.”

“N-no!”

As he smiled in triumph at their weak resistance—

“Stop it.”

“Yeah. What is he doing?”

...People farther back in line murmured their objections, putting a stop to it.

Some were even from the front cars.

‘...Shit.’

What’s with this atmosphere?

Everyone used to get away with this much, didn’t they?

But in the end, reading the room, he put the candy back.

Just as he was about to resent Grapes out of embarrassment—

“Um... if you buy snacks right now, we’re giving out raffle tickets...”

“Raffle?”

“Yes. Here’s the list...”

Grapes flipped the sign to reveal the ‘prize list.’
