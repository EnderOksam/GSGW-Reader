# Chapter 337 - Sekwang Special City Disaster, III

Why was Agent Bronze standing in front of me as a Sekwang High teenager?

“...”

The situation was so absurd that my thoughts almost stalled. I forced them back into order and examined what I was seeing.

He looked even younger than the student version I had encountered inside the ‘Within the Shadow of Darkness’ ghost story.

The uniform was only the most obvious change.

His face and the atmosphere around him both carried an unmistakably adolescent quality.

“Um...”

He didn’t recognize me.

‘...Was he absorbed into the ghost story of Sekwang Special City?’

Like me, he appeared to have been assigned a role as an ordinary citizen living through May 4th.

...Wait a minute.

If everyone who had fallen here with me had been rewritten this way, how could I possibly take them back out?

Where would I even begin convincing them that this world was false?

Finding an ally brought me relief for only a moment before a more pressing problem took its place.

I searched frantically for an answer. Beside me, the high school version of Ryu Jaekwan recovered his breath and spoke with hesitation.

“That bicycle incident… are you okay?”

“I’m fine.”

I studied his young face.

He looked confused and worried, yet he remained remarkably composed for a student his age who had just watched a ghost-story nightmare unfold in front of him.

‘…Agent Bronze once told me that the Bureau rescued him from his first ghost story when he was still a student.’

‘Could this be a point in his life even earlier than that?’ No. First, I had to figure out how to get him to leave with me.

I glanced at his uniform, which had no name tag, as though I were only trying to identify his school.

“You’re a student, right?”

“Yes.”

“Are you heading to Sekwang Technical High School? Your uniform looks like theirs.”

“It is, but…”

“Good. I’m going in the same direction.”

I went to the shared-bike rack and raised a chunk of stone against one of the locks.

“…!”

“The app probably won’t be useful anymore. Breaking this will be quicker than trying to rent one.”

“…”

“Did you see anyone’s body start to swell on the way here? Did your phone receive an emergency alert?”

“…Yes.”

“Listen for the warning tone, but don’t read the message itself. And if you see a large group of people walking together, stay far away from them.”

The first lock gave way. Without stopping, I immediately began working on the next.

I freed a bicycle and pushed it toward him.

“Do you know how to ride a bike?”

Ryu Jaekwan looked at me with unsteady eyes, then gave a firm nod.

***

I took the lead on my bicycle, pedaling hard.

The young Ryu Jaekwan followed close behind on the bike I had taken for him.

He held on despite the strain, which suggested that Agent Bronze had always been a capable rider...

I threaded through the streets toward Sekwang Technical High, avoiding the constant screams and warning tones by taking one detour after another.

Those warnings would not continue forever.

...When no living people remained nearby, there would be no phones left to ring.

‘I have to move fast.’

My thoughts raced as I mapped out our route. Then I noticed something.

A convenience store stood ahead.

“Hold on.”

“What is it?”

I checked that Ryu Jaekwan’s movements were still steady, then braked outside the store.

I rushed in and gathered whatever looked useful.

‘Food first.’

I kept the load light. We needed to stay mobile.

After a moment’s consideration, I took two of the sturdiest-looking umbrellas and a toolkit.

The part-timer at the register had been reading with earphones in throughout the entire time I was in the store. I addressed him before leaving.

“You need to get out of here. Now.”

“Huh?”

“A biochemical attack began near City Hall and it’s spreading. Reach the subway as fast as you can.”

“!!”

Only then did the part-timer reach for his phone. His face drained of color as he checked the news, scrolling through his screen frantically...

As I stepped out, I caught sight of him starting to pack supplies for himself, just as I had.

I turned to Ryu Jaekwan, who was gripping his bicycle and watching me uncertainly.

“Give me your bag.”

“Huh?”

I placed two small bottles of water and several energy bars inside his schoolbag, then passed him an umbrella to secure to the bicycle.

“We don’t know how things will escalate, so it’s best to be prepared. Use the umbrella to push things away if you get cornered.”

“Th-thank you.”

Ryu Jaekwan took the bag, hesitating before speaking.

“Um, let me pay you for this.”

“I’m not taking money from a student during a national emergency. Get on your bike. We need to reach Sekwang High at high speed.”

“But... okay.”

Ryu Jaekwan clearly had a thousand questions, but sensing the seriousness of the situation, he wiped his sweat and took hold of the handlebars again.

Eventually, he couldn’t help but ask,

“How... how do you know all this so well?”

“...I’m just used to it.”

“Excuse me?”

I quickly changed the subject.

“More importantly, school has already started. Why are you only heading there now?”

“I... I had to stop by the bank, so I’m a bit late.”

“Have you contacted your family?”

“...It’s fine.”

That vague response made me realize that this version of Ryu Jaekwan likely carried the same history as the real one.

“...Hoo.”

I checked the opening first, then stepped aside and let Ryu Jaekwan take my place.

He gave me a questioning glance. As soon as he looked through the gap, the color drained from his face.

“...Why?”

Just as I thought.

Students lay unconscious in rows along the corridor.

It was the same peaceful end-credit scene I had seen after clearing the ‘Within the Shadow of Darkness’ ghost story.

Talismans filled the cracks around every classroom door and window, stirring an old, complicated feeling in me.

‘So this was the form Team Leader Hong’s barrier took...’

The children were being kept asleep while the disaster day looped around them.

That suspended state was likely counted as ‘death,’ much like the Snow White Mountain Apples I had consumed. It separated them from the Annihilation-sanctioned Disaster spreading through the alerts.

So the school doors had to remain closed.

They probably wouldn’t have opened easily anyway.

Ryu Jaekwan, who belonged to this school, looked horrified by his classmates lying motionless in rows along the corridor.

“What is this? What on earth...?”

I quietly looked at Ryu Jaekwan.

“Does any of this look familiar?”

“...Remind me?”

“When you see them, do you feel anything? In particular...”

I pointed toward one of the talismans.

It was the design Agent Bronze himself had once made.

“That talisman.”

“...”

For an instant, an unfamiliar glimmer passed through Ryu Jaekwan’s eyes as he followed the design.

Then worry and confusion covered it, leaving only the face of an ordinary teenager.

When he faced me again, suspicion and tension tightened his expression.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about.”

“...”

Of course, a few words would not be enough to wake him. I had needed more than six hundred repetitions to escape this place myself.

From his perspective, I must have looked like some suspicious stranger pointing at talismans and talking nonsense in the middle of a crisis.

‘I can’t afford to be impatient.’

In that case...

“There’s no need to worry.”

I smiled at the teenage Ryu Jaekwan.

“They’re protective talismans. As long as they remain attached, the danger outside cannot reach this school.”

An expression that seemed to ask, ‘How could you possibly know that?’ crossed his face, but his wariness gradually shifted toward curiosity.

“So, don’t worry about your friends. We should concentrate on finding a way out.”

“...What about you, sir? You said you came here to find someone.”

Right. Ho Yuwon.

But another question occurred to me.

If the school doors were locked and everyone was asleep, how had the Counselor managed to leave?

At that exact moment...
