# Chapter 311

My griptok.

It was the final button component of the Dream Incubator.

The main button—the one that corresponded to the Wish Ticket.

“……”

I stared at it blankly.

The button that had lit up gave off a faint glow, almost as if it were tempting me.

Without realizing it, I raised my hand toward it—

“Agent Grapes!!”

“…!!”

I jerked my hand away.

Agent Choi, who was beside me, grabbed my arm and switched off the power connected to the device with his other hand, pulling the cord out.

“You told me yourself. If something goes wrong, pull the plug immediately.”

“…Thank you.”

“What was that just now?”

I looked at the button that had gone dark and murmured as though my soul had left my body.

“I don’t know.”

But—

“It seems I’ve been carrying the most important part of this machine all along.”

“……”

Agent Choi’s pupils widened as he examined the button I had completed.

“A memorial griptok.”

“……”

“But the color…”

I hesitated for a moment before speaking.

“It’s not a memorial griptok.”

“What?”

“The shape and function were exactly like a memorial griptok, so I thought it was one, too, but apparently, it wasn’t.”

“By who.”

“The elder told me.”

“…!”

— *This one is of even higher grade. But it seems to imitate something from our workshop.*

“…I heard that something even more valuable had mimicked the memorial griptok.”

And the original function of this griptok… was the Wish Ticket button of the Cheerful Research Institute’s Dream Incubator.

Still looking at the griptok that had become the button, I reflexively lifted my head.

It wasn’t visible now, but whenever it activated, a floating memo pad would appear near my bedside.

A remnant of the *Darkness Exploration Records* popup store.

[Darkness Exploration Records Real Goods Box]

— *New merchandise usage permission unlocked!*

The griptok had been one of the first-place prizes I won at that popup store.

An item from the real goods box.

*But why… why is that the button to the Cheerful Research Institute’s Dream Incubator?*

My head throbbed.

To sort things out for myself, I started by explaining what the elder who repaired the broken griptok had told me.

How she had looked at this item with pure curiosity, without a hint of hostility.

“……”

Agent Choi listened, his eyes growing thoughtful.

“So you’re saying it’s not cursed or anything… That’s what you want to claim?”

“It’s not a claim—just what I experienced.”

“…All right.”

Agent Choi sat down on the floor, power cord still in hand. He brushed the ashes off the spot in front of him and patted it.

“Seems like we’ve got a lot to talk about.”

….

“Grapes. So, where did you get that thing?”

“You won’t believe it.”

“Yeah? I’ll listen anyway. I’m good at that, you know. It’s one of an ace agent’s virtues.”

I looked at his faint smile… and finally sat across from him.

Then, impulsively, I opened my mouth.

“I came from another world.”

He stopped breathing.

***

…At some point, I had imagined being honest about my situation to someone.

But I knew I’d be treated like a lunatic or like a walking urban legend, so I never said it.

That’s why… this felt strange.

Saying it out loud myself.

…Liberating, but strange.

But it wasn’t an impulsive decision.

“……”

I looked at Agent Choi.

…He definitely had things he wasn’t telling me. Information, circumstances.

*If I want to learn those things, I need to state my own secrets, too.*

If I kept hiding, I’d look even more suspicious. And we’d just sit here doubting each other without ever showing our cards.

That couldn’t continue.

*There was a new clue now.*

I needed information and deductions. I needed someone to talk to, someone to exchange ideas with.

So if I wanted information, I needed to give some as well.

…In a way that would earn Agent Choi’s trust as much as possible.

“…Do you remember the cases from the paranormal disaster ‘Sparkling Dragon Palace,’ where they couldn’t verify some of the children’s identities?”

“…I do.”

Kids who named countries that didn’t exist on Earth, or whose physical features were slightly different, or who gave addresses that didn’t exist in reality.

“Those children weren’t ‘residents’ of this world.”

“……”

“I’m similar.”

A missing person caught in an urban legend.

“I came from another reality—one almost the same, but just slightly different. From a Seoul that’s nearly identical but… not quite.”

I did not say, *I came from a world without urban legends.*

Because I remembered his reaction when I’d talked about cults. It would only undermine the credibility of this conversation.

*But parallel-world legends were already registered in the Darkness Exploration Records.*

So this much explanation should be acceptable.

Then, I poured everything out.

That when I opened my eyes, it was at the Daydream Inc. New Employee Orientation, and I had already been given several pieces of equipment.

“……”

Agent Choi stared quietly at the ash-covered floor for a long moment. Then at last, in a calm voice, he said, “So you’re saying you got caught in a paranormal disaster and dropped here. The world changed suddenly. And you suddenly had equipment.”

“…Yes.”

“And so…”

I heard him inhale.

“So. Your wish was to go home.”

“….”

I forced myself to smile.

“But I couldn’t.”

“….”

“Agent Bronze was right—the Wish Ticket doesn’t work that way.”

“Grapes.”

“But I didn’t expect to find another clue in this machine. …A clue that might explain why I was brought here.”

I looked again at the powered-off incubator.

More precisely—at the Wish Ticket button completed by my griptok.

“….”

The adhesive pad left on my phone could no longer function as a memorial griptok.

Meaning I needed that button-turned-griptok to read the Darkness Exploration Records wiki.

*So someone removed the Dream Incubator’s button, processed it into a memorial griptok… and made it a first-place popup store prize.*

For the person from the world that created the Darkness Exploration Records… to be summoned *into* the Darkness Exploration Records.

…Or for someone born from the Dream Incubator’s “legendless world” to be brought outside.

*Which is it?*

What hint were they trying to give me?

What did they want?

Something was forming—not clear yet, but close.

Only…

“I can’t help thinking this Cheerful Research Institute has done something.”

“You think this research lab brought you here?”

“Given the circumstances… that’s my guess.”

Honestly, I was nearly certain.

Because I had awakened inside this incubator.

“So I want to know more about it. Not just to rescue the people of Segwang Special City… but because it seems tied to me as well.”

“….”

“That’s all.”

Agent Choi was silent.

His eyes shifted between the Dream Incubator and me. His fingers fidgeted on the handle of his ritual blade as if in conflict…

Then he spoke.

“Grapes.”

I looked at him nervously.

“The underground lab—we really tore through it once you disappeared.”

“….”

“…And we found something.”

What?

“Something…”

“But I need you to swear one thing first.”

His sharp gaze locked onto mine.

“You can’t hand this information to Daydream Inc. Got it?”

“…Agent.”

I wrestled with myself—then sighed.

“I have a permanent contract with Daydream Inc. Forever.”

“…!”

He must be remembering too.

The number 130666 wearing a Security Team uniform.

It was a choice made for survival—but the outcome wasn’t pleasant.

“As 130666, if Daydream demands it for the company’s benefit, I probably won’t be able to refuse. So… I might not be able to keep that promise.”

“….”

“But I still want to hear it.”

Agent Choi looked like he’d been punched.

Then he replied.

“…All right.”

And gave a bitter smile.

“Honestly, did you think I’d just let you off easy because you asked nicely?”

“Kind of.”

“You’re unbelievable.”

He pulled something out of his jacket.

“…Because this is the answer.”

A folded piece of paper.

“I restored what I could from a document labeled ‘Experiment Log.’ It was covered in residue, so there weren’t many salvageable pages.”

“…!”

“It must be what you’re looking for. It was in the place you discovered. Here.”

He handed it to me, adding that he had taken it along when grabbing the incubator.

I took it.

【 Ah, like the saying ‘Behind every cloud is a silver lining,’ my friend—it seems the crisis of being discovered by that dusty government agent in the lab wasn’t entirely a bad thing. 】

【 For we have returned like this, haven’t we? 】

Braun’s applause echoed in my skull like tinnitus.

My hands trembled as I opened the document.

It was…

—That contract!!

It was a denunciation of Daydream Inc.

No—it was a document where fury had fossilized into text.

—It wasn’t a signature on a contract handed over by some pharmaceutical executive. No—because of ■■, we had helplessly lost control of our research.

—They stole ■■ from the Dream Incubator. Their promise to become a supply line for dream-solution fluid had been nothing but cheap wordplay, masking their real intent: to tap the incubator for raw solution.

So…

*Daydream approached the Cheerful Research Institute pretending they would supply dream-solution fluid, but after making use of it, they just stole the incubator that produced the potion?*

A very Daydream-like betrayal.

The next lines continued with similar rage against Daydream, until finally the tone became more analytical.

They left the main research building and prepared a separate lab. They would find weaknesses in that pharmaceutical company here.

Then came notes on how Daydream had modified the incubator, reducing it into nothing more than a potion production machine.

Most of the text was too damaged to read, with many blanks.

But then… My eyes caught something.

A line.

A line I *could* read.

Confirmation of a child-use *Paradise Syrup* counterfeit among the potions produced by the company.

—Name: *Wi■h T■ck■■.*

“…!”

Censored, but unmistakable.

*Wish Ticket.*

—  The children’s Paradise Syrup was the original.

—D■graded version suspected due to removal of incubator  ’  s cultivation function and lowering of required solution concentration.

—Examples of degradation:

—Spoken sentence required for activation.

—Degraded worldline ■■ function.

—Compromised wish-fulfillment—a negotiated outcome, not true paradise.

That was all that could be read.

“……”

“How is it?”

I looked up.

Agent Choi glanced at the sheet I was holding and joked faintly, “Feels like an exposé on corporate corruption, right? If the Disaster Management Bureau weren’t under the Ministry of the Environ— …Grapes?”

I snatched the power cord from his hand.

Then plugged it back in.

“You—!”

“Just a moment!”

The Dream Incubator’s buttons lit up again.

As expected…

A display appeared.

The missing amount of solution.

Red text at the bottom:

#rDE 05000000r#

…Five million points?

My mind blanked.

*A-grade solution gave around 150,000.*

How was I supposed to—

*No!*

I could fill it. Why not? Clear 30–40 A-grade missions. It was possible.

After all, at the Segwang Special City subway station—at the Hanbit Library escape—I’d gotten almost A-grade, hadn’t I?

If I just took my time…

….

Unless.

*Unless there was a way to do it all at once.*

Outside that same subway station.

An enormous legend.

Something like… an extinction-class paranormal disaster that consumed an entire city.

And I knew… if you didn’t just escape a legend but resolved it properly, you could extract an even higher-grade dream-solution fluid.

“……”

Meaning… if I ended the Segwang Special City legend:

I could go home.
