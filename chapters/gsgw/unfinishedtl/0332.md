# Chapter 331

Agent Bronze and I were staring at each other as if we’d just been struck by lightning.

The train shelter.

Its true value revealed itself beneath the engineer’s seat.

“An escape talisman...!”

But once the shiver passed, an urgent question flooded my mind.

“Wait a moment. But if you could leave using that talisman from the start, wouldn’t you have escaped long ago?”

“...!”

With agents dying one by one, there had been no reason to hold out. So why—?

—They kept saying there was something else they still needed on this train, and went out searching for it!

Ah.

“So something was missing.”

What was it? What could they have lacked?

At that moment, someone threw open the door outside the engineer’s cabin.

“Roe deer...”

“Ms. San-yang!”

The instant I saw Ms. Go Yeongeun’s face, words spilled out before any explanation about avoiding her or about the current situation.

“We found traces of an escape attempt.”

“...!!”

I hurried everyone into the engineer’s cabin, then watched as Manager Lee Jaheon shut the door and leaned against it as if standing guard.

It seemed the two of them had brought Ms. Go Yeongeun along with Deputy Eun Haje.

The narrow cabin was so packed that it was hard for anyone to sit, but no one cared right now.

“If this train were placed on high-speed rail tracks, could it run?”

“On high-speed tracks?”

Instead of asking **why** , Ms. Go Yeongeun seemed to grasp something instantly and exclaimed in shock.

“Wait—don’t tell me you’re thinking of running a high-speed train with this subway?”

“It wasn’t our idea.”

“What...?”

Her gaze followed mine and Agent Bronze’s, landing on the talisman beneath the fallen chair.

“The agents who made this train shelter seem to have planned from the beginning to use this train as a means of escape.”

“...W-wait, just a second...”

Ms. Go Yeongeun took several deep breaths as if sorting through the flood of information, then spoke quickly but calmly.

“Mr. Roe Deer.”

“Yes?”

“Why on earth did you think I’d know about the compatibility between a subway train and high-speed rail...?”

“......”

I’d received so much informational help from her before that I’d reflexively asked, thinking she might know. If that put pressure on her, I was sorry—

“But I do know.”

“...!”

“I’ve tried to learn as much as I could from my aunt while living here. About subways and rail systems.”

Because we were trapped in the subway.

“And I’ve also heard some ‘rumors’ about the subway.”

I looked at her—the person who had desperately gathered scraps of rumor and information to adapt after being suddenly thrown into an extreme survival situation.

“Then may I ask for your answer?”

“Yes.”

Ms. Go Yeongeun frowned.

“As far as I know... first of all, the track gauge should be the same. So it would physically sit on the tracks without a problem.”

“...!”

“Wait. But subways and high-speed trains operate at totally different speeds—don’t they work differently?”

“They do. The current is different.”

At Deputy Eun Haje’s words, Ms. Go Yeongeun nodded.

“Subways usually use direct current, and high-speed rail uses alternating current. Simply put, high-speed rail uses much higher voltage.”

“So they’re not compatible?”

“I don’t know about other places, but as far as I know, the Segwang Special City subway isn’t.”

“......”

Ms. Go Yeongeun had learned this through conversations with her aunt, who worked in a related field.

“Then the agents probably realized that, too.”

If they’d reached the same conclusion we had—

—They kept saying there was something else they still needed on this train!

“That must have been it.”

“Pardon?”

“The reason the agents who built this shelter didn’t immediately attempt escape, and instead kept searching for supplies.”

Electric current.

Fuel—or power—to make the train run.

And that was—

“Will-o’-the-wisp.”

I turned to Agent Bronze.

“If they had a will-o’-the-wisp, wouldn’t it be possible?”

If they used a supernatural entity as the power source instead of electricity.

“It’s possible.”

“...!!”

Yes. Then everything connected.

“But at the time, the agents on this train didn’t have a will-o’-the-wisp.”

If they had, they would’ve left traces—like at the Hanbit Library—that only agents could recognize...

“So that’s why they kept going back out to the stations.”

“...To look for someone who had one!”

“Yes.”

I nodded.

“They must have been searching for other agents who entered carrying a will-o’-the-wisp.”

But instead of finding anyone, they were worn down until eventually...

......

We naturally swallowed the rest of the sentence.

“...Then if we obtain a will-o’-the-wisp, we could try this escape method immediately.”

For a moment, hope flickered in Ms. Go Yeongeun’s eyes.

“But wait! If it were really possible to escape just by physically running out like that, wouldn’t lots of people who were already outside the subway have escaped? But they didn’t.”

Having had hope crushed again and again, she tried to assess the situation as calmly as possible.

“You’re right.”

“Or maybe it **is** possible, but everyone outside is already dead, so no one even tried.”

“......”

“Well, just my guess.”

Deputy Eun Haje’s words were hopeful for us—and utterly despairing for Segwang Special City.

But I had another doubt.

“The blockade of Segwang Special City isn’t just about preventing ghost stories from escaping outward.”

It was a sealing ritual that required the sacrifice of the city’s residents. So of course civilians couldn’t easily escape.

Could we really escape via high-speed rail? How much had the shelter agents actually anticipated and prepared for?

“For now... we need to bring Agent Choi and Agent Haegum here.”

I wanted to hear the opinions of veteran agents familiar with Segwang Special City.

If someone died because we rushed this, there’d be no going back.

I nodded.

“It won’t be too late to try after preparing and checking everything thoroughly.”

“...Yes.”

Ms. Go Yeongeun’s face settled into calm.

As if trying not to get her hopes up again, she deliberately suppressed her expression.

From outside, faintly, we could hear the lively voices of the train shelter residents.

“......”

“......”

“Um, but...”

Ms. Go Yeongeun’s face grew oddly uncomfortable.

“Mr. Roe Deer. That thing you said a few days ago—about there being more rabbit enthusiasts on the train... You weren’t talking about that ceremony happening outside right now, were you?”

Ah.

“It... wasn’t intentional.”

Her expression grew even more awkward.

“You mean you can **accidentally** predict an increase in rabbit doll enthusiasts?”

“......”

“......”

“I’m sorry. I was trying to find time to explore the engineer’s cabin... I’ll clean it up.”

The stares were piercing.

Receiving the gazes of the shelter residents—and Manager Lee Jaheon (why?)—I quietly bowed my head.

Ms. Go Yeongeun’s awkward voice followed.

“Still, I think this is the first time I’ve seen everyone in this shelter having fun together. It’s kind of... like a World Cup atmosphere.”

Yes...

“Thank goodness...”

I was just letting out a sigh of relief when—

“But don’t you feel like it’s been strangely quiet for a while now?”

“Huh?”

“I mean, I know it’s really noisy outside... but somehow, it feels strange.”

Murmuring, “Why?” Ms. Go Yeongeun kept glancing toward the cars outside the engineer’s window.

Car No. 1 was mostly empty and quiet, but beyond that, the other cars were noisy.

“What do you mean?”

“What kind of quiet is that?”

“I don’t know. It just feels... empty?”

Empty?

“As if something that was there is missing?”

But in a train, something you’d notice **audibly** being gone would be—

......

Ah.

There was no announcement playing.

“......”

“How long have we been talking?”

“Five minutes? Ten...”

“Eleven minutes and twelve seconds.”

Wait.

Hearing Manager Lee Jaheon’s voice, I slowly turned my head...

“The interval between stations on the Segwang subway is—”

“About eight to ten minutes...”

An announcement should have played at least once by now.

“And if I include the time I spent talking with Agent Bronze about the talisman...”

This was definitely abnormal.

“The train isn’t arriving at a station...”

......

......!!

“Agent!”

Almost simultaneously with me, Agent Bronze flipped the chair back over and checked the talisman. The ash-water strokes—

They were burning, black and seething.

“......”

Wait—wait!

“Is this... did the talisman activate just now?”

“Yes??”

“Why all of a sudden?!”

“Agent Bronze, does this item activate when discovered by others?”

“No! If the talisman were used that way, it couldn’t look like this—”

But Agent Bronze’s face turned deathly pale.

“...But it **is** in effect.”

Damn it!

I immediately pressed my face to the window at the front of the control cabin.

More precisely, I slammed my forehead against the glass, trying to look back at the sections we’d already passed.

“It’s unfamiliar.”

I’d seen the outside environment of a running subway several times before, but this—

It was darker than before.

“......”

I pulled out my smartphone, turned on the flashlight, and pressed it to the glass, trying to see behind the train.

The light was useless. The rear was invisible.

But I caught a glimpse of a passing sign.

Safety First During Maintenance

Two-Person Teams Required

This wasn’t an officially operating subway line.

A maintenance zone.

A line leading outward.

“The train... is running straight.”

The tracks were no longer curving in a loop.

Without realizing it, we had already passed it.

The track switch.

The talisman had changed the train’s destination by triggering the switch through some coincidental accident.

“...The train has left the subway loop line.”

“Wait.”

“Then the destination is—”

Manager Lee Jaheon slowly lifted his head and declared:

“The altitude is increasing.”

“...!”

We were heading above ground.

“From now on, do not stare too intently ahead. We are leaving the underground.”

“......”

“This team is entering unexplored darkness.”

And outside this subway lay the space of an extinction-class supernatural disaster.

The train was running toward the outside high-speed rail.

“We have to stop the train.”

“Mr. Roe Deer...!”

“We’re not ready yet!”

There was no time to persuade anyone. Damn it! I rushed toward the control panel to hit the emergency stop—

“No! The talisman is already in effect. Interfering could cause it to malfunction in an even more unpredictable direction!”

Damn it!

“Agent, even if this doesn’t go wrong and we escape safely, it’s still a problem!”

If—if this train really was a way to escape Segwang Special City, and even if this escape succeeded—

Those two would die.

They were already dead.

Once outside, they’d only become something that moved while dead, tainted by ghost stories.

“No.”

An alternative!

I shook off Agent Bronze’s hand gripping my shoulder and grabbed the side door of the engineer’s cabin.

“Hey!”

“Agent! Deputy! I’ll open the door—get off! Get off and walk down along the tracks!”

It wouldn’t open. Damn it! I frantically scanned the cabin. Door release mechanism—where is it?!

“Where is it?!”

There were no obvious hints for a layperson. Damn it, damn it—

“Manager! Just break the door lock—”

“Stop.”

“......”

“If you try to save the two of us and wreck the train, we’ll all die. Come on, Roe Deer.”

I turned.

Deputy Eun Haje was smiling bitterly.

“...It’s already too late. Look.”

Ah.

I turned my head.

Bright light was pouring in through the engineer’s window.

The surface.

Segwang Special City appeared.
