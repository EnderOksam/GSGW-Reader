# Chapter 327

A few days after Kim Soleum’s “Grand Plan to Dominate the Train Shelter”  was launched.

“Damn it.”

A train resident was irritably tossing a magazine aside in Car No. 2.

The cover featured a stylish celebrity, but the magazine was already ragged and torn.

‘So damn bored.’

Today was another unbearably dull day.

Originally, before “everything happened,” he had been a senior in college. The stress of job hunting had pushed him to his limits, and he’d even thought that maybe the world ending wouldn’t be so bad.

But now that it really had ended, he missed that ordinary life like crazy.

At least back then, he could watch short videos.

‘This sucks.’

He got up and looked around, trying to convince himself that he was still living in the top 1% among the people in this city.

The comfortable Car No. 2.

Inferior Cars No. 3 and 4.

Even worse conditions in the rear cars.

And outside the train, monsters roamed freely.

‘I’m a winner.’

If you stayed on this train long enough, a way out would eventually appear. He’d heard that the person who built this shelter had said, “There will be a way to leave later.”

And since he was in the front cars, he’d surely be included in the priority group when that time came.

Safe.

—“You guys just found the train a little earlier than us! It’s not like this train belongs to you!”

The person who’d said that had been expelled—even back when there was still room in the shelter.

They’d been a huge nuisance, so it was only natural. Honestly, it had felt pretty satisfying.

‘If you’re going to cling to a train someone else claimed first, you should at least be polite, right? We’re the ones being generous here.’

This former “college student,” who quickly forgot that he himself had gotten in thanks to seniors already being here, left Car No. 2 and started walking.

He was so bored that he just wanted to find something to look at.

That was when he saw it.

Between Cars No. 4 and 5.

In the narrow boundary separating the front and rear cars, people were gathered, laughing and whispering.

‘Huh?’

Usually, people in the front cars subtly looked down on those in the rear.

The college student didn’t consciously realize it, but it was closer to squeezing out dopamine from a sense of superiority while trapped in fear and boredom.

But now, that feeling seemed strangely weaker.

Why?

‘That…’

A high school student from the rear cars was chatting cheerfully with a middle-aged woman from the front.

They showed each other something, bowed slightly, and the student returned to the rear cars.

The woman, meanwhile, composed her face as if hiding something and walked away…

‘What did they exchange?’

The college student immediately followed her and spoke up.

“Ma’am, what were you talking about with someone from the back?”

“Huh? Oh, nothing—just asking how they were doing…”

As she tried to brush it off, he clearly caught the fleeting emotions in her eyes.

Superiority.

The pride of knowing something.

Pleasure.

They surfaced briefly—then were forced back down.

‘What?’

His mood soured instantly.

At the same time, anxiety crept in.

‘Something’s going on.’

If the hierarchy on the train was about to change, shouldn’t he get involved fast?

If he missed the trend and fell behind, he might end up being sent outside to scavenge for food—dangerous day labor.

Or worse, pushed aside during an escape.

“Excuse me, ma’am.”

In the end, he lowered his posture more than he ever had before, sticking close and pestering her with flattery.

The woman kept insisting, “There’s nothing like that,” but eventually, with a look of reluctant generosity, she whispered,

“Go check Car No. 7.”

And so, the college student stepped into the rear cars.

**Clatter.**

The door slid open, revealing Car No. 7.

It was a place for trading supplies with outsiders, so people usually avoided it unless necessary. Fights had broken out here before—people had even died.

But today, it was chaos for a different reason.

“Line up!”

“Hey, move it already!”

In a corner—the narrowest, most insignificant spot where goods were usually piled—more than ten people were crowded together.

Among them were several familiar faces from the front cars.

‘What the hell…?’

Rear-car residents coming forward was tacitly discouraged, but front-car residents often came to the back under various excuses.

Still, he’d never seen this many before.

‘What are they selling?’

He tried to push forward, but wary of the front-car people’s reactions, he ended up lining up instead.

And only after several long, nerve-wracking minutes did he finally see the “goods.”

[Snacks for sale]

The shabby sign, made from reused cardboard, said as much—but the items on display were dazzlingly refined.

Chocolate bars, cookie sets, popcorn… even salads with fresh green vegetables and hot dogs.

‘Huh…??’

Every surface was decorated with colorful, theme-park-like illustrations.

“Where did all this come from?”

When the college student demanded an answer, the vendor shrugged.

Wearing glasses, with messy hair obscuring their face.

“Th-the home shopping thing… at that station… it’s new… I just happened to go…”

Apparently, a new place to obtain food had opened at Afternoon Station.

Of course, since being accepted into the train shelter, the college student had never gone down to a station to do “that kind of thing.”

Anyway, the vendor claimed they’d won as the 100th customer and received a massive pile of snacks.

Even items that weren’t sold to regular buyers.

‘Lucky bastard.’

The college student frowned and glanced at them. The vendor bowed even deeper.

‘Can’t even make eye contact?’

It was amazing someone this pathetic had survived this long. If they stepped off the train, they’d probably look even worse.

‘Trying to worm your way into being a train resident with this stuff?’

Then they should’ve offered everything “for the sake of the shelter.” Selling things like this was audacious.

But judging by the timid wording on the sign, they hadn’t thought that far.

[Accepting lodging passes]

‘Hmph.’

Seems like they were just aiming to stay a little longer in Car No. 8.

Short-sighted. Timid.

‘Total pushover.’

Strong against the weak, the college student spoke casually.

“What’s your name?”

“Uh, I… please call me Grapes.”

Hiding their real name, too?

Anyway, eyeing the snacks greedily, he said, “I’ll pick some out and bring you a lodging pass later. Got it?”

“Huh…? Th-that’s a bit…”

“I’m saying I’ll give you one, so why not? What, don’t trust me? If you don’t trust train residents, why are you selling here?”

The vendor fidgeted, but eventually nodded.

“Two snack packs for one lodging pass…”

“Yeah, yeah.”

Brushing it off, the college student quickly picked his snacks.

Even though food supplies weren’t scarce in the front cars, flavor was a luxury.

There were limits to what could be brought back.

Not to mention the weird, barely-edible stuff or rough, raw vegetables people often dragged in.

For people used to that, new snacks were more stimulating than food itself.

There were keychains and magnets too, but he didn’t even glance at them.

Chocolate bars and popcorn.

After shoving them into his padded jacket, he quickly procured a lodging pass.

Getting one for free from the rear cars—who avoided conflict with the front—was easy.

He returned to Grapes’s stall and held it out.

“Here.”

“Th-thank you…”

As Grapes bowed with both hands to take it—

“No, if you’re thankful, give me a freebie.”

“Huh?”

The college student snatched the pass back and grabbed a handful of candy from the stall.

“One of these should do.”

“N-no!”

As he smiled in triumph at their weak resistance—

“Stop it.”

“Yeah. What’s your problem?”

…People in line muttered, stopping him.

Some were even from the front cars.

‘…Shit.’

What’s with this atmosphere?

People always did this kind of thing.

But in the end, reading the room, he put the candy back.

Just as irritation toward Grapes began to rise—

“Um… if you buy snacks right now, we’re giving out raffle tickets…”

“Raffle?”

“Yes. Here’s the list…”

Grapes flipped the sign to reveal the prize list.

7th – 1 candy

6th – Perfect button

5th – Popcorn (small) x1

4th – Perfect magnet

3rd – Cookie box

2nd – Perfect keychain

And lavishly decorated at the top—

1st – Perfect plush doll

‘You’ve got to be kidding.’

What’s with this “perfect” series?

Probably dumping unsellable non-snack prizes.

“Want to try?”

“Yeah. Sure.”

Praying for snacks, he drew a ticket.

But—

“Congratulations! Second prize…!”

2nd – Perfect keychain

In his hand sat a golden keychain.

‘You’ve got to be fucking kidding me.’

A useless-looking metal keychain engraved with a pink rabbit doll.

But—

“Oh my!”

“That’s…”

Whispers rose again behind him.

Even Grapes stared at him with shock and renewed admiration.

As if seeing someone incredible.

“C-congratulations…”

Huh?

Turning around, he noticed several people in line looking at him with longing eyes.

Is this… better than it looks?

‘Then I should keep it.’

He shoved the keychain into his pocket and returned to Car No. 2.

After that, he spent his time secretly planning how to eat a chocolate bar without anyone noticing, nearly forgetting about the golden keychain.

But that night—

He dreamed.

“Hahaha!”

A dream where he spent a joyful day at a magnificent golden amusement park.

The resort was classical yet luxurious, unbelievably fun.

Some weird things wandered around—kind of childish—but that’s just how theme parks are.

He roamed the golden park. The dream was blurry in places, but the joy was vivid.

Then—

**Clink.**

Something impossibly real appeared before him.

A carriage-shaped snack cart.

♬♬♩♪♬♬♩♪♪♬—

From the soft pink-and-gold stall, something waved at him.

As he approached, it extended a round, fluffy paw.

Inside it—

A cookie.

That alone was crystal clear.

A sugar cookie, delicately decorated with chocolate lines forming a pink rabbit doll.

He reached out—

And as he grabbed the fluffy hand offering the cookie—

He woke up.

“…!”

No theme park. No music. No dopamine.

Just the dim, cramped train car again.

‘W-what?’

Gasping, unable to distinguish dream from reality, he jolted upright.

Something fell from his pocket.

**Thunk.**

The golden keychain.

The pink rabbit smiled up at him.

‘……’

It was the same rabbit from the cookie in his dream.

“…!!”

Heart pounding, he got up.

‘That was what they were selling.’

He sprinted to Car No. 7.

**Clatter!**

He threw the door open—but the car was quiet, no market in session.

Grapes sat beside their closed stall, eyes shut, sleeping.

Before he could even call out—

They snapped awake.

“You’ve come.”

“…!”

Grapes felt… different.

The cringing meekness was gone.

They smiled with confidence—almost reverence.

‘What the hell?’

Before he could speak, Grapes offered him a lodging pass.

“Please use this to buy snacks.”

“….”

“And then, would you draw a raffle ticket?”

Like a sacred ritual rather than commerce.

“…Yes.”

Nervous for no reason, as if facing something monumental, he hurriedly picked two cookie sets.

Then reached into the raffle box again.

This time, the slip seemed to cling to his fingers.

Like fate.

He pulled it out with trembling hands.

1st prize.

“See! I knew it!”

Voices burst out beside him in awe.

“I could tell from your last visit! It felt like the Cotton Body was guiding you…!”

“…Cotton Body?”

“Have you seen them? That fluffy…”

Wait.

Fluffy?

“You had that… dream too?”

“Of course!”

Their voice dropped shyly.

“Though not often. I heard a high school student in Car No. 5 dreams almost every day…”

Lightning struck his mind.

‘This was it…!’

The secret the front and rear cars shared.

The dream amusement park.

“Usually it doesn’t happen right away—but if you carry an item related to the Cotton Body, sometimes…”

“Ah.”

Feigning casual concern, he said, “I dreamed immediately after getting it.”

“Incredible…”

Superiority swelled in his chest.

At the same time, he felt kinship with the once-pathetic vendor.

They shared something special.

His heart raced.

Today, he might be reborn—chosen.

“Then this win must truly be the Cotton Body’s will.”

Grapes nodded and carefully lifted something from a box beside them.

Placed gently on the softest cloth—

Slightly larger than a palm—

1st prize – Perfect plush doll.

A perfect plush doll.

“Behold.”

It was revealed.

A luxurious rabbit plush—fluffy and soft.

Button-like black eyes, a velvety nose, a plump pale-pink body.

“The most ideal form of the Cotton Body.”

Drunk on the sensation that something enormous was unfolding around him, the college student stared in awe.

And Grapes, gazing at him in admiration—

‘Dopamine jackpot.’

Kim Soleum, who had spent days searching for someone gullible yet morally questionable enough to deceive without guilt, silently cheered.

But his face still wore an ingratiating smile.

Did you know?

The amusement park in the dreams isn’t real—

It’s advertising.

‘More accurately, ads played in dreams via merchandise.’

Even though the merch was designed with minimal functionality to avoid contamination, people experienced it far more vividly than expected.

Maybe using Braun—the TV-show host—helped.

Either way, he’d successfully checked every box of a “classic cult recruitment process.”

Perfect execution.

There was just one difference.

‘This train was already basically a cult.’

Especially the front cars’ chosen-people mentality.

He was simply overwriting one elitism with another.

On a larger scale.

Look—this guy had once bullied an old man from Car No. 6 into scavenging at Afternoon Station, resulting in his death, and now—

[ Friend!  ]

He was grabbing for Braun.

[Good heavens!]

“L-let me serve you!”

Kim Soleum blurts it out, lifting Braun with both hands like a Lion King scene.

Braun had been seconds away from incinerating the would-be cult leader.

“Moving the holy image is the duty of someone like me! For the chosen one, merely being here has meaning!”

[It certainly has meaning—to offend my eyes.]

‘I’m sorry…’

Kim Soleum felt like he was bathing in cold sweat.

The college student, oblivious to this exchange, simply nodded, intoxicated by being “chosen.”

“So… what do you want from me?”

Casual speech already.

Kim Soleum hesitated.

This was supposed to cause chaos to access the driver’s cab—but he couldn’t let the shelter suffer.

‘I gave a guy like this a pseudo–cult leader role… I need to rein him in.’

So he added a different direction.

“You’ll be told in your dreams.”

That night, the college student dreamed of dancing in the theme park, chanting, **“Be kind to everyone.”**

Later, Kim Soleum heard him say with misty eyes, “The Cotton Body emphasizes character…”

‘Phew.’

That should do.

Kim Soleum decided.

And a week later—

Just as he hoped, chaos erupted before the driver’s cab as the Rabbit Doll Cult gathered—But first, a declaration was read aloud.

“Everyone has the right to their own rabbit doll!! Remodel the train!”

‘…!?’

That was not the slogan he expected.

And—

“Yeah! Do it!”

Standing at the very front was Deputy Eun Haje…!
