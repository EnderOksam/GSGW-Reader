# Chapter 339

I watched the scene with my breath held.

Eun Haje’s colleagues were trying CPR.

“R-Reporter...”

The assistant manager’s body shook as they worked on her.

Perhaps realizing there was no hope, they lifted the body, sobbing, looking around in panic, and then headed up toward the concourse...

“Agent.”

“......”

“Is... that someone we’re supposed to rescue?”

I nodded without thinking.

“Then I’ll come with you!”

Ryu Jaekwan, a high school student who had been about to board the train, turned his body and started moving away from the train toward the concourse.

Wait—

The platform screen doors are closing...

I turned my head.

Behind me, the train doors were shutting.

Through the window, I caught a glimpse of Ho Yuwon’s face—looking at me with an unreadable expression.

The train departed.

Wooooo—

“......”

It left.

I gasped for breath, then moved after Ryu Jaekwan, who was already running up the stairs.

Dodging evacuees sitting on the steps sobbing or muttering to themselves, we went up—and there I saw figures holding a body, utterly at a loss.

They were in such a state of panic that they didn’t even realize they were being followed, despite Ryu Jaekwan openly chasing after them.

“Excuse me.”

I spoke to them directly. They flinched and turned toward me.

“Y-Yes??”

Seeing their eyes filled with both fear and caution, I chose my words carefully.

The form of address...

—Reporter!

“By any chance... is the person you’re carrying Reporter Eun Haje?”

“...!”

The color drained further from their tear-streaked faces.

“Are you someone who knows our reporter?”

“Yes. We worked together until recently...”

I trailed off.

Standing beside me, Ryu Jaekwan took a small, sharp breath when he looked at Eun Haje’s condition.

‘He’s realized it.’

That she’s a corpse.

I didn’t need to make anything up. Looking at Eun Haje’s pale face, I spoke.

“What happened? What on earth occurred?”

That was the trigger. As if a dam broke, testimony poured out of them.

“We—we were on our way to City Hall.”

“...!”

“Yes! There was news of a terrorist attack at City Hall, so we went straight there to cover it—but on the way, the traffic suddenly stopped right in front of us.”

They said the cars ahead all came to a halt on the road, as if possessed.

Car horns blared. People got out, shouting, asking what was going on...

“We had a bad feeling! I mean, they said ‘terrorism.’ We thought it was probably just a mistaken emergency alert, but once that happened, it felt real.”

“......”

“Reporter Eun said to zoom in with the camera and just take one shot of City Hall—if nothing showed up, we’d turn back. She said you only live once.”

I didn’t know whether to laugh or cry.

“So we got out of the car while filming... we got out, and...”

“...Were people marching?”

“...!! Yes! You saw it too?!”

“F***, it was like zombies...”

They spoke like they were screaming, hands shaking, faces pale.

I took a deep breath.

Then my gaze fell on the camera bag hanging at someone’s waist.

“You said you filmed it—do you still have the footage? ...I think I’ll need to tell the family.”

“Ah—ah... yes!”

One of them turned on the camera and showed me.

The footage was shaking.

—Hey, just run!

...Eun Haje’s voice.

The road pulled farther away on screen.

Emergency alert sounds blaring everywhere, silhouettes of passersby flashing past.

—What’s wrong with them??

—I just saw the emergency alert... huh? Huh??

“When the emergency alert went off, we saw people just... joining the march. Like they were possessed...”

—Guys. Let’s all turn off our phones.

“She said it felt wrong, told us to turn them all off...”

“Did you?”

“Yes. We held the camera backward and ran, filming what was happening behind us. But then... hic...”

Their eyes twisted, tears pouring again.

“Someone who was trying to escape in their car suddenly swerved hard onto the sidewalk.”

“...!!”

I stared at the screen.

—Huh? Huhhh?!

—Aah!! Aaaaaaaaah!! Aah!

—Go!

A deafening crash.

The camera jolted violently. A body struck by the car, bending and slamming into the ground, passed through the frame.

“Reporter Eun pushed us out of the way. Then she—she got hit.”

“......”

“There was so much blood...”

I looked again at Eun Haje.

They had tied her waist with emergency padding to stop the bleeding, but the abdominal wound was so horrific it was hard to look at.

It seemed likely her organs had been crushed, causing shock.

‘...Assistant Manager.’

I breathed deeply, trying to steady myself.

“You... must have been close to her.”

“......”

I nodded slowly.

Nearby, I saw the group mindlessly replaying the footage as if trying to forget reality—and Ryu Jaekwan watching it with them.

Then—

“That is...”

“Huh? What—oh?”

A puzzled voice.

“There’s something at City Hall.”

...!

I reflexively looked back at the camera.

“What? Look.”

“No—here. What is that on the building?”

The paused, blurry frame—silhouettes smeared by motion—still showed it clearly.

In the upper left corner of the frame, Sekwang City Hall.

“...A balloon?”

Near the top windows of the building, something bulged outward.

A pale, swollen, round shape.

Strange rainbow-like colors shimmered across it.

“Looks like it ate a ghost.”

“Even if the color fits, what is that...? Don’t tell me that’s the biochemical terrorist agent? In balloon form?”

Their speculation sounded plausible.

But to me, it didn’t look like a balloon.

Rather—

‘A cocoon.’

Or—

‘...An egg.’

It looked as if something was growing, using the building as a nest...

‘...What on earth is growing there?’

I couldn’t take my eyes off the footage—

“Agent.”

A hand suddenly grabbed my shoulder.

I turned to see Ryu Jaekwan’s face, deathly pale.

And then I realized.

“Mm-mm-mm-mm-mm...”

Everyone watching the video was softly humming.

Chills ran through my entire body.

‘Damn it.’

“Thank you. I’ve seen enough.”

I quickly moved as if wiping the camera clean and covered the screen.

The humming stopped.

“...Ah.”

“No, no—it’s nothing.”

The reporters answered awkwardly, only then seeming to come back to themselves.

They didn’t notice anything strange.

Do you know what’s scarier?

When Ryu Jaekwan grabbed my shoulder, I was humming too...

‘I even ate the fruit the counselor gave me.’

And still I was affected.

‘...No matter how I think about it, the counselor probably won’t even make it to a proper resolution attempt.’

But the train was already gone, and I had chosen Eun Haje’s body.

I felt like I was bathing in cold sweat.

“...So this is how the reporter ended up like this.”

“Yes...”

While their attention was on the body, I swallowed and quickly reached out to the camera, deleting the footage.

They might curse me later—but being in a state where they **could**  curse would already be a blessing.

“Um... do you know where the reporter’s family lives? The body...”

“......”

They couldn’t bring themselves to continue.

“If you’re okay with it, I’ll take care of the body and deliver it to her family.”

“...!!”

“Why...?”

I spoke as calmly as possible—

Looking down at the unfamiliar body of Eun Haje.

“Because I know where
      h er family is. And you all seem injured. Please take care of your own safety.”

“Ah...”

Their expressions changed.

After showing the footage, crying, and pouring everything out, their rationality seemed to return.

Carrying a body was overwhelming. Leaving it behind was unthinkable.

And then someone appeared, offering to take responsibility.

They glanced at my clothes.

Since I’d worked with a social affairs reporter like Eun Haje, they probably assumed I was in a related field. A flicker of relief crossed their faces.

“Th-thank you.”

“Please... take care of her.”

After bowing several times, they gathered their cameras and left together.

They said going back to the station was impossible—they had to go home.

And so Eun Haje’s body was left here.

“......”

“......”

I looked at Ryu Jaekwan.

“...I’m sorry.”

“Huh?”

“I rushed over without properly assessing the situation and gave up the train. I interfered with your plan.”

What are you talking about?

“And in the first place... because you delayed to rescue me, this happened—”

“No.”

I grabbed his head.

“Absolutely not. Don’t think like that. Okay?”

“......”

I forced myself to add, half-joking,

“Otherwise it’d mean it’s my fault for not saving her.”

“Th—!”

“You don’t think that, right? I feel the same about you. In disasters, we don’t blame individuals who did their best.”

“...Yes.”

Ryu Jaekwan nodded. His expression eased slightly.

But the sight of a corpse made his face pale again.

Honestly, I wanted to break down crying too.

‘...What if she’s really dead?’

I didn’t know the principles, the mechanism—how any of this worked.

And that uncertainty was terrifying.

If—

If she’s really dead.

‘...Is this really the end?’

It didn’t feel real.

In the noisy concourse, Ryu Jaekwan and I stood frozen, staring at the body...

‘A way to save her.’

Even if I carry her around in the tattoo, a corpse is still a corpse.

If I died right now, too, would I wake up again on the morning of May 4th, Disaster Day of Sekwang Special City?

But what if I’m an anomaly—appearing only this time, and disappearing in the next May 4th?

Or what if my memories vanish completely?

‘...Then it’s over.’

Cold sweat dripped down.

Is there another way?

I kept staring at Eun Haje’s body.

Near her chest was what looked like an ID badge.

Broadcasting station ID.

‘...The broadcasting station.’

Blood Broadcasting Station...

Twilight Station.

Delusion Home Shopping.

And the thing that replaced that home shopping space—

‘...Flower Golden Resort Pop-up.’

......

......!

‘Braun.’

I swallowed.

‘Could the subway-station urban legends I experienced in Sekwang Special City and this place... be the same space?’

[Hm. Someone might say a calf and chateaubriand are the same thing too. Isn’t it all a matter of environment and conditions?]

A chilling metaphor—but I understood the ambiguity.

In the end, it’s defined by the observer.

And such criteria often form important rules in urban legends...

‘Then.’

It was worth trying.

“Jaegwan.”

I moved Eun Haje’s body to a corner with Ryu Jaekwan.

And then I began to act.

“What... are you doing?”

“...You could call it first aid.”

My hands were firm, but my mind was a mess.

Isn’t this wrong?

But isn’t this better than truly dying?

If the day really does repeat anyway...

Questions and fear swirled endlessly, but my hands kept moving desperately.

And then, after a moment—

The dead reporter Eun Haje opened her eyes.

“...!!”

“Hoo...”

Like someone waking from deep sleep, she sat up.

Ryu Jaekwan stared blankly.

At the reporter who moved as if alive.

Frowning, alert, Eun Haje looked at me.

“Who the hell are you?”

Perfectly normal.

...I shoved the ‘Flower Golden Resort Employment Contract’ I’d hidden behind my back back into my tattoo.

It worked.

“So you’re saying I revived? Not three days, not three hours—thirty minutes?”

“...You regained consciousness from a suspended state.”

“Then why doesn’t it hurt?”

“Adrenaline.”

“Well, that’s at least a scientific explanation.”

Assistant Manager Eun—no, ‘Reporter’ Eun Haje—looked between me and Ryu Jaekwan with a scowl, as if craving a cigarette.

She looked incredibly natural. Human.

So Ryu Jaekwan kept staring back and forth at us, utterly confused.

And I thought of the sloppy contract I’d written...

A day-labor contract.

Yes.

I added a clause to Eun Haje’s ‘Delusion Home Shopping Day-Labor Contract.’

Employment as an employee of the Cheerful Theme Park.

**The employee is transferred under Flower Golden Resort.**

Just before that, I’d thought of the ‘Mr. Heoun’s’ who worked at the pop-up store.

Especially the deceased Jang Heoun.

‘...When I pulled him into Sekwang Special City’s subway station, he suddenly came to his senses.’

As if the contaminated urban-legend persona had never existed—only the resort employee remained.

‘And this station is Twilight Station, where that pop-up store is.’

The place where my rented ‘Cheerful Theme Park’ space existed.

Maybe that’s why I thought it might work again—so I tried reviving Eun Haje as ‘my employee’...

And it really worked.

If only the ‘resort employee’ persona had revived, I planned to erase the clause immediately.

Thankfully, that wasn’t necessary.

‘Thank goodness...’

But can I really call this fortunate?

How is this possible? I don’t know the cause.

And is it right... to save her like this?

Should I have let her die here, so I could save her later?

I don’t know.

‘No.’

Being alive matters.

Being able to talk like this matters!

Swallowing the surge of anxiety and impatience, I stopped Eun Haje’s excessive movements.

“Don’t move too violently. Your wounds could worsen.”

“Wounds? Hm...”

She looked down at her abdomen, expression strange.

The traces of massive blood loss were unmistakable.

I changed the subject to draw her gaze away.

“I stopped the bleeding, and your life isn’t in danger. But the situation’s urgent—let’s move. You need to leave this city—”

“Hm?”

Eun Haje laughed.

“No. Why should I trust you and follow?”

“...!”

“And where did my team run off to, leaving me here with some stranger and a kid?”

Before I could answer, a sharper question followed.

“And did we know each other?”

“...You probably don’t recognize me.”

“Then it’s one-sided. You look like someone seeing an acquaintance.”

“...!”

“Haha. Thought so. So you were just checking on a collapsed stranger...”

She rested her chin on her hand.

“So why should I travel with someone I don’t know?”

“......”

“I can tell you don’t mean harm—but it’s a terror situation, and I got hit by a car.”

She waved her hand dismissively.

“You don’t travel with suspicious people. Well—good luck.”

I realized—

‘...She won’t be easy to persuade.’

If I said I was an agent, she’d probably scoff.

This version of ‘Reporter’ Eun Haje was sharper, more confident, less restrained than the assistant manager I knew.

Her eyes gleamed even in this chaotic concourse.

Then—

“...What if I get you out of this mess?”

“Well.”

“What if I give you a scoop?”

Her eyes changed.

Now we were speaking the same language.

“What kind of scoop?”

“About the terrorism.”

I said quietly.

“Reporter. I’m going to uncover the cause and identity of this attack.”

Eun Haje’s eyes lit up. She extended her hand.

“Deal.”

“......”

“So we’re in the same line of work. Pleasure.”

I shook his hand.

‘...she doesn’t fully trust me.’

It was purely a journalist’s gaze.

Still, getting her to come along was something.

Then Ryu Jaekwan whispered, “Agent... are we taking the subway to Sekwang Station?”

But Eun Haje cut in. “Sekwang Station? The subway’s still running?”

“Well...”

“It was only running in one direction.”

“...That’s strange. In terror incidents, trains usually skip all stops or shut down entirely. No notice from control?”

“There were station staff on the opposite platform.”

“Hm. So they haven’t completely abandoned it.”

After glancing around, Eun Haje grinned and stood fully upright.

“Either way, operating this strangely means there’s something going on. Let’s ride.”

“...!”

“You said Sekwang Station, right?”

I hesitated briefly.

We’d already lost a lot of time. I couldn’t guarantee it was safe to move outside anymore.

I had to recalculate.

‘Then.’

Moving inside the subway and exiting when crowds pass might not be a bad idea.

The subway is relatively safe.

And if we gather information at Sekwang Station, few are as capable as Eun Haje in that direction.

“...All right. We’ll go to Sekwang Station together.”

“Okay.”

Seeing my former boss speak so briskly, I smiled faintly.

Even now, she felt reliable.

And so, boarding the subway one step late, we headed to Sekwang Station.

To trace the counselor’s trail.
