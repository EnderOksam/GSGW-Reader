# Chapter 316

*Thump. Thump.*

The sound of my heartbeat pounded violently inside my head.

I placed my hand over the tattoo near my heart.

The contamination I had forced in and organized there.

The remnants of ghost stories I had separated from myself.

“Roe Deer is an empty shell? What do you mean the real one is inside? Are you talking about the contamination…?”

Voices protesting from the jury box. And soon, several lights in the jury seats turned off.

**If you continue causing disruption, you will be removed.**

“……”

I heard the sound of someone grinding their teeth, brief and sharp—then the jury box went quiet.

But Juror No. 1 was still staring at me.

An agent with no visible face.

**Candidate No. 202■-370614.**

**Kim Soleum.**

I caught my breath and lifted my head.

**At the request of Juror No. 1, we will proceed with additional questioning.**

**Step forward and answer.**

Step forward?

“Ugh.”

Something tore outward from my chest, a sensation brutally crushing my facial features from within.

I staggered and tried to step back, but soon—

“Did you call for me?”

**[ Over here ]**

Are **we** answering?

[What is this? Oh dear, could someone bring in the cameras? Interrogations are always so stimulating; the viewers will love it!]

—  It’s not a school, but… I recognize the outfits from somewhere.

Contaminations.

My contaminations burst out and began answering on their own. I couldn’t stop them. What I had stuffed into the tattoo spewed out like vomit, flooding over me and overflowing uncontrollably, smothering everything however it pleased…!

*Put them back in—!*

I tried to force them back into the tattoo, but the truth revealed cannot be hidden in a courtroom. No…!

**Based on the weight of “motive-less murder,” we ask:**

**Candidate, have you ever taken the life of an innocent third party by your own will?**

[ **Over here** ]

[Absolutely not! I have gloriously participated in producing a talk show that makes innocent viewers’ hearts flutter, but that’s all!]

Welcome to Looky Mart! Need help checking out?

“Those are difficult words for the children to understand! Say, ‘Have you ever killed someone for no reason?’ Use that sentence instead!”

Mindless answers with no logic or emotion—a non-human response, obsessive mutterings that made no sense poured out. None of those were really me, but at least none of them claimed I had harmed someone.

Thank god.

Relief—followed by nausea.

The courtroom was silent. Only the sound of the contaminations babbling chaotically on the platform echoed inside the vast space…

**Statement acknowledged.**

Good.

Good—this was the end!

**Next question.**

Shut up!

**Then…**

**Candidate, can you swear you will not harm the life of an innocent third party for your own benefit?**

Of course!

Of course I swe—

[ **Impossible** ]

My heart sank.

—Games, by nature, begin with harming someone else for your own advantage. At school, we survive by guiding other students toward sacrifice.

That’s—

[Ladies and gentlemen of the jury—this phrase, “innocent third party,” what do you think of it? Perhaps it exists in law books.]

[But what about reality?]

Shut up.

[An action I take to survive today might, unintentionally, endanger someone else’s life!]

[But isn’t that just life?]

No!

I’ve never thought like that. That’s contamination—distorted thinking created by ghost stories.

[Even if someone else suffers, you don’t have to take responsibility for everything! No one can predict all outcomes of their actions. If something bad happens, it’s not automatically their fault.]

[So, “I won’t harm an innocent third party for my own benefit?” Who can make such a broad, meaningless vow?]

That— that’s…

If it’s scary…

Maybe I can’t…

“…….”

I can’t guarantee it.

The voice echoed weakly.

The comfortable tone of a children’s amusement park mascot.

A sound of uncertainty, resignation, honesty…

A strangely familiar emotion.

[Life under extreme circumstances is a tightrope walk. You sigh with relief every time you pull off one more performance without falling. You can’t look too far ahead, or take on too much responsibility.]

[For a program to survive, it needs to be more thrilling, fun, shocking.]

To survive…

[Is it so wrong to be a little selfish? A captivating show needs some self-centeredness.]

“…….”

I listened to the contaminations speaking with my mouth.

No.

I listened to the voice coming from inside me.

When had it started?

At some point, I stopped feeling irritation or anger toward people who were no longer useful in the ghost stories.

I stopped making self-centered decisions like letting hopeless trolls die while saving only the salvageable ones.

I stopped celebrating when meeting someone easy to manipulate, whispering thank-you’s through guilt.

I stopped deceiving or frightening others to control the mood—unless I was sure they were villains.

All the silent screaming I did while begging everyone to cooperate, the efforts to survive together—

Gone.

“…….”

I looked at my hands.

They were human hands.

The same hands I desperately wished for during the time I was melted into a pulp as 130666.

But am I really the old Kim Soleum?

I tried to live like a human.

Smooth, clean, proper decisions—only those.

But is that truly me?

Assistant Manager Eun Haje and Agent Bronze made completely different arguments from the jury box.

Their judgments based on personal experience, personal selfishness, personal protrusions.

I had those, too.

But at some point, they vanished.

And now I understood why.

… *I thought they were contamination.*

I had labeled all the “parts of me that overlapped with ghost stories” as contamination.

Because they were frightening.

Because if they were contamination, then maybe my mind—after my body had broken into something inhuman—would collapse again.

So I shoved all of that into the tattoo along with the contamination and sealed it away.

Because I was afraid.

“…….”

I remembered the conversation I had with Braun on my first day in Fox Counseling Room.

*—I like who I am now.*

*—I don’t like it when my judgment suddenly flips like turning a page.*

I feared contamination because I thought it meant losing myself.

So I rushed to cut it out… only to cut myself out in the process.

“…….”

I raised my hand.

“I… want to speak to Juror No. 1.”

**Permission granted.**

I lifted my head and looked at the faceless agent.

“You were right.”

I passed my distorted desires, my twisted thoughts, my extreme emotions, and all abnormal judgments to compartmentalized personalities inside me.

“The unpleasant desires, the extreme thoughts, the twisted logic inside me…”

I said, “They’re all mine.”

The contaminations had burst out exactly as they were.

“Some of the things I just said were impulsive and inappropriate, but…”

I took a deep breath.

“…I *have* thought them before.”

The incident at Hanbit Library resurfaced.

Every page of a book is still the book itself.

I remembered the advice I heard at the Cosmic Mall:

*—Don’t destroy the contamination inside you.*

*That’s why I chose the tattoo.*

I placed my hand on the tattoo and recalled the Moonlight Tattoo’s description…

*—To govern oneself!*

The most primitive and important task. But when one’s mind is full of identities and worries, it’s difficult to gather them into one.

Attach index tags to each part.

So you can organize and piece them together whenever needed.

Index tags.

Organizing and assembling.

Not cramming and locking them away—

indexing and arranging them.

“…….”

I placed my hand over the tattoo.

And I realized the circular-band tattoo could rotate along its central axis…

*Ah.*

…Now I understood why the tattoo parlor discouraged me from covering it with another tattoo.

I wasn’t properly *using* it.

“I acknowledge it.”

The organized contaminations began to rotate.

Re-categorizing what was truly foreign, truly infection-level identities—those moved to the outer ring.

Everything that was originally mine, even if distorted through contamination—those moved closer to my heart.

A rush of discomfort hit me. Anxiety, selfishness, impulse, aggression, destruction, deceit, theatricality, superiority, cowardice, curiosity, excitement….

Extreme, sharp emotions collided inside me, flooding back vividly.

But soon they became familiar.

Because they were mine from the beginning.

“…….”

I lowered my hand.

The impulsiveness, weakness, and oddities that burst out earlier were still in my mind.

But it was fine.

They were supposed to be there.

“All of that is me.”

“…….”

*Juror No. 1, speak.*

Juror No. 1 raised his hand again and looked at me.

**I confirm the candidate’s statement as valid.**

**I recognize the author as a whole, unified state suitable for weighing on the Scales of Evil.**

He smiled.

And now I could see.

Under the lighting, I recognized the contours of his face.

It was—

**I recommend extracting his heart once more to weigh it.**

My own face.

Agent Grapes, wearing glasses.

**Candidate No. 202■-370614.**

**Place your heart on the Scales of Evil again.**

“……”

**Candidate Kim Soleum.**

“Yes.”

I took a breath.

Then I tore my gaze away from Juror No. 1 and quickly retrieved my silver heart from the scale.

I pushed it back into my chest… and then sliced it open again, retrieving it.

Over the tattoo.

“……”

This time, the heart wasn’t pale like before.

It was still silver—but heavier, denser.

*It looks heavier than before.*

But I placed it on the scale.

The counterweight labeled “motive-less murder” shook violently.

“—!”

I almost screamed.

The massive gray balance swayed… then slowly tilted to one side.

“……”

The weight of sin fell downward.

*Passed.*

My heart hovered gently above the scale.

Lower than before, but stable—definitive.

**Candidate 202■-370614 is deemed a citizen, not subject to conscription, under regulations of the Paranormal Disaster Management Agency.**

The stares from the jury faded.

**Additionally, we acknowledge the validity of the candidate’s statement that the Segwang Special City branch no longer officially exists, and we declare the termination of rescue and selection operations.**

*Bang. Bang.*

The gavel struck.

All spotlights vanished.

Darkness vanished as well.

A blank white space remained.

“Agent Grapes!”

Agents leaped down from the jury box toward me.

【 Ah, it seems the show has ended. Everyone is leaving their assigned seats. 】

Yeah.

I looked around as the courtroom faded.

The jurors disappeared one by one.

…They might have been the last lingering resentment of dead agents, or something imitating them, but I hoped they would rest peacefully now.

*If possible, I should confirm later…*

“Grapes, retrieval!”

Ah.

I picked up my heart from the scale and shoved it back into my chest.

At that moment, I saw something move under the Scales of Evil.

“……?”

A thread dyed in five colors.

It curled up and rolled into Agent Choi’s arms.

Wait.

*Was that guy planning to tamper with the scale if necessary?*

As if he were a shady fishmonger cheating customers.

It was absurd—but also a little funny.

…And I was grateful.

But at the same time, I realized Agent Choi, too, had been staring at Juror No. 1.

“Agent.”

I approached and whispered softly.

“Did you keep looking at Juror No. 1 because you recognized who he was?”

“…Who he was?”

“…Agent Grapes.”

My agent identity must have been acknowledged and seated there, too.

Interrogating oneself is a cliché in this type of ghost story.

But Choi’s face froze.

“Agent?”

“…You saw Grapes’s face there?”

He laughed stiffly, sweat running down his cheek.

“To me, it looked like my own face.”

“……!”

I immediately whipped my head toward Juror No. 1.

He was staring at me from nearby… with Agent Grapes’s face.

“…Who are you?”

The mouth opened.

*This is a leftover projection.*

In the next instant… the courtroom was blown away.

As I burned white and my consciousness faded, I saw the face and outfit of the agent in Juror No. 1 shift… revealing its true form as if a disguise had been exposed.

In Juror No. 1’s seat… Director Ho was smiling.
