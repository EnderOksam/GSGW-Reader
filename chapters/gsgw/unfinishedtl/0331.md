# Chapter 330

Inside the engine room.

The conductor, pale-faced, kept flicking his gaze between the agent uniform I was wearing and the noisy chaos outside, then began to spill everything he knew in a halting rush.

About this train.

“You already know… you already know this, don’t you? That subway trains were strangely safe to begin with….”

It seemed that almost everyone had realized from the very first day of the disaster that **“once you got on the train, even injured people would be fine again.”**

“People shoved to get on, fought over seats… fights broke out, people died—it was absolute chaos.”

“So was this train already occupied like that from the beginning as well?”

“No! This train just suddenly appeared!”

…!

“A train that hadn’t existed before suddenly started running the next day, pulled into the station, and the doors opened!”

I could picture the scene clearly.

Survivors who had barely lived, unable to board any train, gathered on what little safe platform remained—then a newly empty train appeared, its doors opening.

“…And Agent Chogae and the other agents were already on board.”

“T-That’s right.”

The conductor rolled his eyes, then forced himself into a more authoritative tone.

“But I recognized this train. This—it was a broken train!”

“…!”

“You know how it is. When a train runs out of juice during service, you don’t always haul it back to the depot. Sometimes you shunt it off onto a side line. After operations end for the day, trains get parked on hidden sidings near stations. That’s probably where this one was.”

“…….”

“I don’t know how they repaired it, but they just boldly brought it out and started taking people who were left in the station….”

They must have used supernatural equipment or abilities to restore the broken train’s function.

Given that everything had already been swallowed by the paranormal, there would’ve been no reason to hesitate.

“As for me… yes. They asked if anyone had experience driving trains, so, well. I helped Agent Chogae a lot. Hahaha….”

So this man hadn’t even been the original engineer of the train.

**That’s why he was called a conductor.**

“Subways are mostly automated these days, sure, but still—having a professional makes a huge difference, doesn’t it?”

“Yes. I understand Agent Chogae’s decision.”

But my eyes went to the paper stuck beside the control cabin—dozens of names, all crossed out with X’s.

“I imagine his expectations of what that professional would do after he disappeared were… rather different.”

“No—Agent, Agent! You really have to understand this. Living for years with this many people—do you have any idea how grueling that is?”

The conductor spoke desperately.

“The agents, you see—they’d go out to the station to get supplies, and then one by one they wouldn’t come back! That’s because they kept trying to take everyone who came to the train with them. With food and everything already scarce, trying to gather enough for the numbers—that’s what led to it!”

“…Is that how you see it?”

“Of course! You know how it is—when people are all together, you have to cut away what needs cutting, throw away what needs throwing away, or the system won’t run!”

“…….”

“It was all—everything was done to maintain this shelter! According to Agent Chogae’s wishes! I was just trying to keep it going, and I got overwhelmed and made mistakes!”

I didn’t feel anger surge up.

Just a strange feeling.

This man, after all, was himself someone who had been discarded by the outside world under that exact logic of **“throw away what needs throwing away.”**

And now he was using it to justify himself.

And thinking that agents had died one by one trying to save this kind of person left me with an odd, hollow fatigue.

… **This probably isn’t for me to judge.**

The people who had lived in the train shelter should be the ones to deal with him.

So instead of showing hostility, I resumed gathering information.

“Tell me more about the time Agent Chogae went missing. I heard he went out to get food.”

“What? No, there’s some misunderstanding here. I never drove the agent out!”

Hmm?

I stayed silent on purpose. The conductor, reading too much into it, rushed into further excuses.

“You mean, why he went out when food wasn’t actually short, right?”

[He’s confessing on his own.]

“Absolutely not! I never did anything to chase him out. He just kept getting off on his own—said something like the train needed something more?”

…‘ **The train needs something more.’**

“What exactly did he say it needed?”

“He didn’t really explain. Just smiled and told me to wait and see. There was nothing to smile about, but he kept doing it—got on my nerves….”

I cut him off.

“Did Agent Chogae leave any specific words behind? Anything he emphasized, or said before leaving.”

“…Well, he tried to give the people on the train hope. Stuff like—one day we’d be able to escape, that he was looking for a way….”

I could see him roll his eyes, thinking it all trite.

That alone told me enough.

… **That agent must have suffered immensely.**

Disaster Management agents—especially because they were agents—would have known better than anyone.

Beneath the surface, under a city where countless civilians had died, trapped inside a subway urban legend, with no idea how long they’d be stuck with survivors…

**At some point, he must have realized they were sealed in.**

An agent versed in rituals or occult matters might even have grasped, however faintly, signs of human sacrifice.

But he couldn’t show it.

With little time left, yet still an overwhelming number of survivors for any individual to bear, keeping everyone calm and preventing the shelter from collapsing into chaos would never have been easy.

“…….”

“Oh—r-right! Agent Chogae left something behind! His belongings—probably the only keepsake left. Over there….”

“Don’t move.”

The conductor started to rise, reading the room, but froze again when I grabbed his shoulder.

Keeping one hand firmly pressed there, I opened the place he pointed to.

A small drawer beside the control cabin.

When I opened it, a single small item emerged from the dusty emptiness.

“…….”

A five-colored escape cord.

Standard issue for agents—useless in this place.

It was neatly tied like a shoelace, dust settled thick on top.

…The ends were slightly worn.

As if someone had often rubbed it, seeking peace of mind.

“…….”

A desire to leave.

And… it felt like a cord of hope, a declaration that someday he would leave.

That’s when I realized.

**He must have been planning something.**

He must have been planning something beyond mere survival.

Even if the odds were slim, perhaps some attempt to enable rescues from outside.

Like the agents at Segwang High.

If so—then where?

“Well, I’ve answered everything now, Agent….”

“Shh.”

I kept surveying the engine room.

If the train needed something, it would naturally be here—its core.

But would agents really have tampered with such complex machinery without a specialist?

If so, then where…?

—Sir.

—What if you think less about the facility, and more about the users?

Users.

A place the agent himself accessed, used, and protected daily.

Yet somewhere others wouldn’t notice, or accidentally touch—even within the engine room.

“…!”

I turned to the conductor.

The moment our eyes met, he flinched. When I reached out with my free hand, he panicked.

“H-Hey—what—?!”

I lifted the conductor up—

And moved him aside.

Then flipped over the chair he’d been sitting on.

The seat of the **engineer.**

“…!!”

A short while later.

“E-Everyone!”

The conductor burst out of the engine room, both hands raised, shouting.

“I was wrong! The rabbit doll is the perfect friend of humanity! Of course I approve the train renovation!”

“Waaah!!”

Dopamine at its peak.

The train filled with a victorious euphoria, like winning a sports match. Swept up in the narrative, people carried the conductor toward the leader of the Rabbit Doll Cult.

Naturally, the crowd surged, then ebbed—people flowing toward Car No. 3.

And among those quietly following the tide was someone else.

Lee Jaheon.

His build stood out enough that anyone who knew him spotted him instantly, even in the chaos.

“Section Chief?”

“Assistant Manager Peregrine Falcon.”

Eun Haje approached with a familiar smile and whispered.

With rabbit badges pinned on, even outsiders were enjoying a temporary social pass.

She spoke lightly, deliberately not mentioning her own death.

“I heard you worked part-time at a talk-show urban legend. What did you do?”

“Audience guidance, guest service, staff assistance, and porter duties.”

“…All four at once? Wait—didn’t someone keep coming over while you were doing one job, sighing or yelling, and making you do another? Right?”

Lee Jaheon answered flatly.

“Yes.”

“Ahahaha!”

Eun Haje burst out laughing.

But given the situation, she couldn’t laugh long.

She spotted a confused companion wandering nearby.

“Ah—there he is.”

Agent Bronze, muttering “Pink…?” while staring at the Rabbit Doll Cult and the plush doll enshrined in the central velvet case.

He’d spent the last few days visiting where Jang Heo-un—Hwagal Agent—was, under the pretext of getting food, and now couldn’t adjust to the suddenly transformed train madhouse.

He’d been so focused on managing corpses outside the train to prevent decay that he’d nearly forgotten the bitterness of it all.

And now he’d finally found someone he knew.

“…!”

Agent Bronze rushed over, blurting out the main issue.

He didn’t know why that lizard staff member was here—but that wasn’t urgent right now.

“Isn’t that the doll Agent Grapes used to carry?!”

“No.”

“Then that’s a reli—”

“It’s a replica.”

“…….”

Agent Bronze was momentarily speechless, then forced out a reply.

“Then… was this Agent Grapes’s doing?”

“Yes.”

“…!! Have you seen where they are? We have to go find them immedia—”

Lee Jaheon grabbed Agent Bronze by the nape and lifted him.

“?!”

To fulfill both requests.

And so, a little later—

**Knock, knock.**

With a short knock, the engine room door opened again, and Lee Jaheon pushed the person he’d brought inside.

“…Agent Grapes.”

“Agent Bronze.”

Agent Bronze hurried forward.

“What on earth did you do?! Don’t tell me you were trying to get into the engine room—”

He stopped.

Kim Soleum was staring at him, tense, anxious, yet hopeful, lips twitching.

“Agent, could you check something for us?”

“Huh?”

“Here—”

Kim Soleum flipped over the seat in front of the control panel.

“…There’s a talisman here.”

“……!”

“It looks like something the agents who turned this train into a shelter attached. I’m not an expert on talismans, so I called you….”

“Please wait a moment.”

Agent Bronze halted everything he was about to say.

Then he looked again at the markings beneath the overturned chair.

Straight lines.

Blocked symbols.

Repetition and confinement.

Clearly drawn by mixing burned ashes with a viscous liquid—ritual elements scraped together with difficulty in an environment lacking everything.

But it was a talisman.

And he had seen agents try to use similar talismans for temporary containment—back at the high school in Segwang Special City.

Agent Bronze’s blue eyes gleamed with terrifying focus as he deciphered its meaning.

“…….”

“…….”

Only after half an hour did he straighten up.

“…What kind of talisman is it?”

He carefully returned the chair to its original position before answering.

“This is not a warding talisman.”

“What?”

“It’s not meant to repel evil. On the contrary—it’s meant to draw something in.”

…!

“A fortune talisman. One that attracts luck, fate, circumstances.”

“…Like a good-luck charm for exams?”

“Yes. But it’s a different type.”

He explained calmly.

“This is—what you’d call a fate talisman.”

…Fate?

“So this train is a talisman meant to meet something?”

“Not someone… but something  , it seems.”

Something.

“In other words, that talisman was made to ensure this train would certainly meet something. And if supernatural rules are applied…”

Agent Bronze declared, “To meet it, the train itself will move.”

……!

“Yes. If used properly, the entire train will relocate somewhere.”

“…! Could it move completely outside Segwang Special City?”

“That seems unlikely.”

He frowned.

“You can’t inscribe lines that make it abandon its role as a train. So—it would be moved to a place where it can still function as a train.”

“…….”

“That’s my interpretation.”

A place where the train can fulfill its role.

“…Then it would be on the tracks.”

Kim Soleum’s mind raced. He hurriedly checked the monitor beside the control cabin—

The train’s route map.

“ If this talisman is used properly, does it send us to the tracks on the opposite side?  ”

Up until now, the opposite tracks had been out of service. But since there was a hint over there, perhaps that agent had wanted to make the train run in the opposite direction….

“ Then what’s the order?  ”

Kim Soleum hurriedly recalled the sequence of stations according to the Segwang Subway Line map.

Segwang Subway Line Map

He carefully read through the stations again, tracing them in reverse order. But then….

“……”

As He did, Kim Soleum suddenly realized yet another question.

Because the station names had been revealed one by one, he had never gone over them all in his head like this before—and so he hadn’t noticed it.

A faint sense of unease he had simply brushed aside.

“ The subway stations of Segwang Special City… they’re all named after times of day.  ”

They were strange names.

Before, it probably hadn’t been such an unintuitive system. The stations would have been named according to their areas and characteristics.

But after being contaminated by the Disaster Day incident, the subway itself had mutated into a supernatural phenomenon, and the station names must have changed into these.

…And yet.

“ Then why is Segwang Station still just Segwang Station?  ”

It was strange.

Did that mean Segwang Station alone hadn’t been contaminated?

How could that be?

The suicide-spot urban legend known as the “Forest Path of the Final Moments,” attached to Segwang Station, was horrifyingly contaminated. He hadn’t even dared to try to resolve it so far—only to run away.

So why was only the station name intact?

“ That’s because…  ”

……

Segwang Station.

Segwang Special City.

……!!

“Agent Grapes?”

“Agent Bronze.”

Kim Soleum turned toward Agent Bronze.

An uncanny light flashed in his eyes.

“I think I’ve figured out where Agent Chogae intended to send this train.”

“……!”

“It’s here.”

Kim Soleum pointed to a station on the map.

“Segwang Station… you mean?”

“Yes.”

Kim Soleum nodded.

“But it’s not the tracks we’ve seen so far.”

“That’s because….”

“Agent.”

Kim Soleum’s mind felt as if it were about to burst as the logic snapped into place.

Why hadn’t Segwang Station’s name mutated like the other subway stations?

Simple.

Because it hadn’t been completely contaminated by the subway urban legend.

That “Segwang Station” wasn’t a name used only for the subway!

“There must be a high-speed rail station at Segwang Station.”

“…!!”

That was it.

Kim Soleum recalled Seoul Station, where he had boarded the train bound for Tamna.

Seoul Station also has a subway.

But at the same time, it has something else.

“ High-speed rail…!  ”

Tracks that connect region to region.

“The station that represents this city called ‘Segwang’ is very likely not just a subway station, but also a high-speed rail station…!”

“…Then.”

Agent Bronze looked down at the route map.

“If this train were placed not over there, but onto the tracks of the high-speed rail….”

The destination of those tracks does not circulate within Segwang Special City like this.

“It goes outside.”

“……!”

“The agents were planning to ride this train and escape beyond Segwang Special City!”

Agent Bronze realizes it again.

Here is Agent Grapes, who can procure fuel for the train.

And if someone like himself—well versed in talismans and incantations—is here as well….

“We can try it now.”

The train shelter can be escaped.
