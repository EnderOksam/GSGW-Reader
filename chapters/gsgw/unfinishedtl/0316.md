# Chapter 315

I swallowed.

From the dozens of jury seats on either side of the dark courtroom, the disaster management agents of Segwang Special City stared at me in the state they had been in when they died.

A chilling sensation crawled up my spine.

It wasn’t the look you often see in agents: “steadfast, firm, calm.”

*...It’s a horror story.*

Corrupted.

The real agents... must have already perished across Segwang Special City during the rescue operations.

They were already dead, yet some force held these forms in this court.

For this meaningless process of selecting who could be rescued...

They were staring at me.

**Those who wish to testify about candidate 202■Management 370614, raise your hand and speak.**

Naturally, my companions quickly raised their hands.

But I saw it.

Others had raised their hands too.

Across the dark jury seats, strangely deceased forms were raising their hands.

“......!”

**Juror #5, speak.**

Only the seat numbered lit up in a pinpoint spotlight.

A dismembered body.

From the darkness, the agent, whose organs and limbs remained in geometric fragments, whispered at me:

“The candidate is wanted in a casino.”

...!!

Currently a wanted criminal. Even though they earned many coins there, none of the many addicts in the casino were helped.

I stepped back.

If I rescue gambling addicts and something happens, who will take responsibility? I need to stay rational... it’s right not to rescue... I can’t help it... I turned my head elsewhere.

A strange guilt pressed on my chest.

What is this?

**Juror #17, speak.**

Another rises from the jury seat.

A swollen, pale face.

Eyes pale and unfocused, seeming to look at nothing even as they stare at me.

He had already attempted suicide.

I swallowed.

“In the forest near City Hall, he had hanged himself. Because he had already given up his life, it was more a matter of humane execution than rescue.”

...This.

*This is what I did at the Segwang Special City stations.*

I had no time to stop suicides or handle the bodies... I had to move... Quiet!!

I grounded myself, swallowed, and endured this strange mental shock...

And I recalled a chilling realization.

*They’re testifying about my sins.*

The choices I had made at each station.

All actions that could be judged socially or morally wrong.

*Rebut...*

I should rebut.

But a strange guilt wouldn’t let my mouth move. What is this feeling? These strange thoughts? This fury and grief—where are they coming from...?

**Those who wish to testify about candidate 202■Management 370614, raise your hand and speak.**

Meanwhile, a new juror is selected. Hand raised. Ah...

**Juror #32, speak.**

From the distant jury seats, a dead agent in uniform rose. The figure...

Burned pitch black.

Completely burned, facial features destroyed. Even the uniform was charred, leaving only a crumbling outline...

......

“Ah.”

A chill ran through my chest.

A terrible premonition suffocated me.

Wait, wait...

“Arsonist!!!”

I staggered.

“They had set fire to a residential area. Countless civilians who wanted rescue perished. Dead, because of you, they died... bear the responsibility... die... die... die...”

*Ugh.*

The immense pressure crushed my heart, and the unusual burden and guilt clouded my mind.

I felt like vomiting.

I barely held onto the podium. Thinking of the countless people who had died in the residential area, my heart felt like it was being torn apart, sorrow and resentment stabbing through my head. I had seen that scene until death... uuugh.

Whose memories were these?

“Huk!”

**Those who wish to testify about candidate 202■Management 370614, raise your hand and speak.**

*Hik.*

The pressure eased slightly.

I straightened up. Something hot streamed from my eyes. Wiping it, I realized it wasn’t tears, but blood.

I barely lifted my head, and another hand rose from the jury seats.

Agent Bronze.

**Juror #37, speak.**

“I doubt the subjectivity of the previous testimonies.”

All eyes in the jury turned toward Agent Bronze. But he continued with a pale, dead face.

“To objectively verify the truth of these testimonies,” He looked at me. “I propose that the Scale of Evil be used... as originally intended, hanging ‘unmotivated murder’ as the measure of guilt.”

...!

“If the testimonies are correct, this person committed acts worse than unmotivated murder, and the scale can determine that, right?”

Agent Bronze’s voice echoed in the courtroom.

“Use the Scale of Evil as it was originally intended.”

Its original purpose.

Only those who commit greater sins than killing without reason are marked as evil.

If you prove you are not that evil person, you remain a good citizen.

A normal person, not drafted into a horror tale as a scapegoat.

Eligible for rescue.

“......”

Ah.

“If we judge people the current way, it’s wrong. It’s a corrupted, distorted method that the original management would not have used...”

“No.”

I wiped blood and saliva from my face and rose.

“...Agent Grapes?”

“No, sir...”

I looked at the jurors.

Those mangled forms.

“Even the method you judged as ‘distorted’ was actually what the disaster management had to do.”

“...Huh?”

“According to the original rules for using the Scale of Evil, it would have been impossible to prioritize rescue.”

“......!”

“In a city destroyed by catastrophic disaster, countless people died, resources were limited... and choices had to be made.”

Who to save.

Rescuing gambling addicts could have caused problems... I couldn’t stop suicides or handle bodies... I had to move.

Recalling the countless people who died in residential areas... the traumatic pressure poured onto me.

...That must have been the immense burden and guilt the agents felt before death.

“Should we save the most virtuous, the most useful, the weakest... I’m sure they considered all options.”

“Then.”

Assistant Manager Eun Haje asked, “Who did they choose as rescue targets?”

“......”

“The least dangerous person.”

“...!”

“That was probably considered the way to save the most people.”

One who would not overstep.

In other words, the person least likely to ‘troll’ or act recklessly during this unprecedented disaster.

Someone who wouldn’t endanger others through impulsive actions.

...Someone who would prioritize principle over personal attachment.

“So the opposite side of the scale measures ‘what you love?’ ”

Silence, as if time stopped.

“Yeah... makes sense.”

Assistant Manager Eun Haje nodded.

“Then shouldn’t we just invalidate this trial?”

...!

“No one was saved anyway. This principle was useless.”

The courtroom trembled in silence.

Dozens of pinpoint lights flickered, and the agents’ forms blinked in the darkness...

“Who gave anyone the authority to arbitrarily decide rescue priorities? The Scale of Evil? Wanting to save good people rather than bad is understandable, but turning it into a principle is not something a state agency should do.”

The scale trembled.

A scraping sound echoed where the metal floor met the dais.

“Sure, officials may follow their conscience and choose whom to rescue, but to codify a rule for selection... is wrong.”

**Stop speaking.**

Assistant Manager Eun Haje’s spotlight went off.

“...!”

**The 38th juror had no turn after Juror #37.**

**Juror #37, continue.**

The spotlight returned to Agent Bronze.

He spoke again, as if weighed down by fatigue.

Assistant Manager Eun Haje’s hand motioned subtly, urging him.

But...

“...I have no corrections. Please verify the testimonies with the Scale of Evil.”

“...!”

“Letting each agent decide individually... citizen, your point is often heard, and I’ve been considering it lately. But...”

In the darkness, Agent Bronze glanced at Assistant Manager Eun Haje’s juror seat and spoke calmly.

“If this happens, all responsibility falls on individual agents.”

“......”

Guilt, death.

He looked at me.

“Even if it seems unfair, if necessary, principles are better, and following them may be better for each individual.”

“......”

Agent Bronze had said something similar before.

But this time it carried far deeper meaning.

Words of someone who had experienced much and pondered deeply.

“I understand why the Scale of Evil rules were modified, but in the current situation, it’s still corrupted and useless. I formally recommend returning to the original principle.”

And Agent Bronze stood, unmoving until his words were enacted.

But the trial simply continued.

**Those who wish to testify about candidate 202■Management 370614, raise your hand and speak.**

So.

“I will.”

I raised my hand.

“...!”

I had an idea, based on everything I’d experienced. Sparks seemed to fly in my head.

“May I testify about myself?”

Normally forbidden in criminal court.

A witness is a third party to avoid perjury; the defendant cannot testify.

*But this is not a real criminal trial.*

It’s a selection process, with a corrupted Scale of Evil.

*Even the trial format is only as Assistant Manager Eun Haje requested.*

So if I can persuade this “scale trial” horror, it might be possible.

If my own emotions and self were stirred by it, all the more reason.

“I am the one being selected. As such, I want to testify.”

“......”

**Permission granted.**

“Yes.”

I took a deep breath.

And I spoke.

The sentence I had been recalling.

“Do not rescue me.”

“......!”

“Not just me—do not rescue any of the citizens waiting to be selected here. That is the right thing to do.”

The jury stirred violently. Spotlights flickered. Anger, guilt, despair, fury, hope, empathy, jealousy, and hatred boiled over.

My heart surged so that it felt hot at my nose and mouth.

“Because!”

Through gritted teeth, I continued, “The Segwang Special City disaster management branch no longer exists.”

**【** Oh! **】**

The uproar in the courtroom ceased.

“...You all know the city has been sealed. It’s now inaccessible, and the branch officially no longer exists.” I said with difficulty, “This is an obvious fact. Anyone can deduce it. So...”

“......”

“There is no longer any task to rescue anyone.”

I lifted my head.

“So based solely on the original disaster management principles, not special designation by the branch!”

I shouted.

“If the Scale of Evil can prove only that I am not an evil person...”

I finished weakly.

“Send me away without execution or rescue.”

“......”

“......”

The gaze vanished.

*Click.*

All pinpoint lights returned to normal.

And one more appeared.

A huge ash-colored Scale of Evil was lifted in front of the law dais.

**Candidate 202■Management 370614.**

**Place the weight of sin on the Scale of Evil.**

**Done.**

I stepped off the podium on trembling legs and approached.

Was this right?

*It feels like I’m directly denying all the agents’ desperate efforts...*

No, keep going. Endure.

I saw my companions’ faces as I walked.

Agent Bronze, slightly relieved but tense.

Assistant Manager Eun Haje, lost in thought.

Agent Choi, staring oddly elsewhere...

Passing them all, I stood before the scale.

And suddenly saw the black weight.

A strange weight with the faces of screaming people in bas-relief.

It was the weight labeled “unmotivated murder.” If heavier, the candidate is classified for conscription.

That was the object’s depiction.

I lifted it. Surprisingly light, though it gave an odd unease.

And carefully climbed the dais, placing my silver heart on the opposite side of the scale.

“......”

One side descended.

The weight for “unmotivated murder” was added.

“...!”

It gently lowered, and the scale smoothly balanced.

*Thunk.*

The added weight hit the floor with a light sound.

My silver heart remained on the opposite side, floating gently in the air...

“......”

**Passed.**

Overwhelming relief almost made my legs give way.

*It’s really done.*

Even in this strange state, I managed it. I’m okay. It’s resolved.

*I can leave like this.*

Then I’d be treated as clear, gather information, and take the Dream Essence...

“......”

At that moment.

I saw.

A single hand.

Someone else raising a hand from the jury.

Sitting right in front of the scale.

**Juror #1, speak.**

They stood.

Their appearance was oddly intact.

I recognized the familiar uniform, but even as the spotlight hit, the face was strangely invisible.

Only the silhouette looked familiar...

Their mouth opened.

“I object.”

“...!”

“The candidate’s sin cannot be measured by the Scale of Evil.”

What?

The juror’s unseen gaze was on me.

“They had transferred their own sin elsewhere.”

......What?

“All their flawed desires, thoughts, extreme emotions, abnormal judgments—they had separated and isolated them in other personalities.”

“......”

“The candidate is an empty shell.”

And they pointed at me with a finger.

“No, the real one is inside.”

My tattoo.

I looked down at the “contamination disposal” tattoo on my chest, pale and horrified.
