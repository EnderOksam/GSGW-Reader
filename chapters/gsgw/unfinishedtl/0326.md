# Chapter 325

I looked at Agent Haegum.

The agent’s hand, gripping the hilt of her signed sword tightly, had gone stark white with tension—as if she were trying to process the shock of what I had just said.

I understood.

I hadn’t fully processed it myself yet.

That the Disaster Management Bureau had chosen to offer up all the citizens of Segwang Special City as human sacrifices instead of rescuing them…

I looked down at my own hands.

They weren’t exactly full of color either.

“…Why did you think that?”

“I found records.”

“What kind of records, exactly?”

So I told her.

Rather than explaining the Cheerful Research Institute itself, I focused on the fact that they were testimonies from citizens who had been in Segwang Special City at the time.

“……”

“…Agent. Could this also be a kind of selection process?”

Selection.

The choosing of sacrificial victims carried out by the Scale of Evil.

—To save a large number of innocents, a smaller number who will die efficiently are selected.

Yes.

The reason I had instinctively felt that this was likely the truth the moment I saw the records was because the flow was the same.

“To stop an extinction-class supernatural disaster—one where no method works and the death toll grows exponentially—they use people who were going to die anyway.”

If they tried to rescue the people of Segwang Special City and still failed to control the disaster, everyone would die regardless.

So instead, they ‘used’ them—to prevent the supernatural disaster from spreading further.

[Logical, isn’t it? In times when resources are scarce, such decisions become necessary.]

[Like locking an entire family infected with the plague inside their home in a 14th-century city and letting them starve to death.]

[At least the epidemic wouldn’t spread!]

I lifted my head.

Agent Haegum met my eyes.

Her gaze was dark.

“It’s possible. We’re not a volunteer group—we’re civil servants. If orders come from above, we’re obligated to carry them out. Especially in a national crisis like an extinction-class disaster.”

“……”

“But, Agent Grapes.”

She spoke quietly.

“Do you really think the Bureau’s agents would have made that choice?”

“…!!”

“Based on what you’ve personally seen?”

That was—

……

“No.”

I remembered Team Leader Hong and the Azure Dragon Team members at Segwang High School, wringing out every last idea to save the children.

I remembered my teammates who couldn’t bring themselves to refuse the request to spend just one more day checking if they could rescue the kids left behind in the Shimmering Dragon Palace.

And countless others—agents who vanished into a single line in the Dark Exploration Records…

“I don’t think they would’ve made that choice.”

But—

“Still, a group’s decision is another matter.”

Isn’t that separate from the feelings of its members?

Just like how the agents at the Court of Scales were forced to select people to save while suffering endlessly themselves.

Agent Haegum sighed.

“Yeah. Still, I don’t think even the upper ranks of the Bureau would’ve gone that far…”

“……”

“When the Scale of Evil was first introduced, you wouldn’t believe how many agents opposed it.”

I actually did know.

And that was exactly why it felt even more ominous that it had been carried out anyway.

But listening to her, my mind calmed a little.

‘Am I too steeped in Dark Exploration Records?’

In a world of urban legends, the state sacrificing a million citizens almost feels… plausible.

……

Wait.

**A city-wide ritual anomaly

Government?

A massive human sacrifice ritual??

If this continues, we die—must escape—**

Government.

“Agent… may I ask something?”

I swallowed.

“…What if it wasn’t the Disaster Management Bureau’s decision—but that of an even higher authority?”

“……!”

I’d misidentified the subject.

Thinking about it, that made more sense.

‘In a national crisis, it’s less likely the Bureau alone made the call.’

But Agent Haegum let out a cold, bitter smile.

“I’ll ask again. Do you think the agents would’ve followed that?”

“…!”

“They’re the kind of people who disobey orders just to save one more life. You think they’d comply with that?”

“Is it difficult to carry out such a ritual without agents?”

“Yeah. That’s the problem.”

She glanced at her sword before returning it to her waist.

“Even the Bureau needs specialists to perform sealing rituals. If everyone had risen up and refused, it couldn’t have happened.”

“……”

“If this really occurred—and the agents’ memories were wiped—then someone must have cooperated.”

Cooperated.

“…You mean non-agent officials within the Bureau?”

“Yes.”

……

I muttered, feeling like I’d been punched.

“Then they must’ve deceived the agents to carry it out.”

“Exactly.”

She answered carefully.

“Sealing rituals are fundamentally sacrificial rites. You offer a sacrifice and petition some divine or inexplicable being. The more unnatural the request, the greater and more dangerous the price.”

She seemed about to give an example, then stopped herself.

“At that stage, it’s possible to deceive the agents carrying it out.”

“How?”

“By vaguely lying about what the sacrifice is and saying it’s just a sealing ritual for an extinction-class disaster… or by placing a taboo spell.”

If they were going to erase memories anyway, they could use any number of unethical methods.

“So that means people in the Bureau’s upper ranks actively cooperated and used the agents.”

“That’s right.”

“Do you have any suspects?”

“……”

She stepped closer, her expression serious.

**Smack!**

“…?!”

She smacked me on the head.

“Picking up bad habits from Choi, huh?! Why don’t you just shout that you’re about to do something extremely dangerous—do you think I’d answer that?”

“Ah—!”

“I—I’m sorry!”

Rubbing my throbbing head, I protested,

“But if we know, we can respond!”

“Respond how—are you going back?”

“No, I mean…”

I hesitated, then spoke.

“This is about ending the extinction-class disaster.”

“……!”

“Agent. I’m going to end the disaster in the Special City.”

“…….”

“If there’s anything that could help in that process, I want to know it.”

She didn’t call me crazy.

She didn’t curse me with her eyes either—just looked me over and let out a long sigh.

“Like leader, like team. Truly Black Tortoise Team One.”

I gave an awkward smile.

But it vanished when I remembered Team Leader Hong, bound and corrupted at Segwang High.

‘They didn’t remember in the hospital…’

“Looks like Choi infiltrated and combed through the records, too. If there’s no uproar, they must’ve wiped even the confidential ones clean.”

“……”

That level of thoroughness—burying a million people and cleaning up so perfectly—was chilling.

‘…Wait.’

I turned sharply.

“Agent.”

“Hm?”

“If they realize we know… wouldn’t they respond?”

“……!!”

I stood up abruptly.

“What if they placed a taboo spell that reacts to even mentioning it?”

Her expression changed, too.

“There is.”

Damn it.

I shot to my feet.

‘And of all places, we’re in a Bureau container.’

A mistake.

I glanced instinctively toward the entrance. She did too, summoning will-o’-the-wisps around us.

At that moment, the container door opened.

“……!”

But no one was outside.

An empty street.

No presence.

Which somehow made it worse.

The door had clearly opened.

“……”

“……”

We stood there for a long while, then cautiously exited, still wrapped in ghostly fire.

She deliberately circled the container several times.

**Step. Step.**

Our stiff footsteps echoed.

There was nothing behind us.

Who killed the young lady?

The kite string hanging from the branch over the wall

Strangled her neck

We kept walking.

Nothing behind us.

**Who killed the young lady?

A single tooth of the comb the maid held

Sharpened and stabbed**

We kept walking.

Nothing behind us.

**Who killed the young lady…**

“……”

“……”

It vanished.

I felt the absence disappear.

“…It’s fine. We can go now.”

“……”

“We didn’t use precise terms, so it didn’t trigger. From now on, don’t mention it with proper nouns.”

Don’t say “Segwang Special City” and “human sacrifice” together.

“Yes.”

I nodded immediately.

My neck was drenched in cold sweat.

She sighed deeply.

“I guess there’s no helping it. I should enter that disaster myself.”

What?

“I need to see the situation firsthand. And… yeah. Our team has three days off.”

She checked the shift app on her phone, then smirked.

“Tell Choi to sit this one out. He can cover shifts while I’m gone.”

Ah.

“…So an agent is joining the project.”

“Yes.”

Agent Bronze grabbed his head.

Even though he was already dead and shouldn’t feel headaches, being in the train in his living form meant he clearly did.

Agent Haegum had shown clear interest and eventually took two more personnel with her.

“Who?”

I looked away.

“Deputy Butterfly from the elite team, and my cohort—Chief Pony…”

“……”

Yes.

Deputy Jin Nasol and Chief Kang Yihak.

Upon hearing that two textbook Daydream Company employees were accompanying Haegum, Bronze looked utterly speechless.

“Why…”

“…At Agent Haegum’s request.”

—Give me one cold-blooded bastard and one money-crazed lunatic.

—Pardon?

—That company should make them easy to find.

—Pardon?

I deeply understood his horror.

I was terrified, too.

“…Anyway, understood.”

He steadied himself.

“So why is Agent Grapes visiting this train?”

Simple.

‘I think there’s something else hidden here.’

Given the human sacrifice clues, it made sense.

Between the note hidden by the self-proclaimed Lee Kang-heon and the fact that this was a shelter where a Blue Dragon Team agent stayed almost until the end, there had to be something.

“With Deputy Eun Haje and you already here, it’s easier to infiltrate now.”

I’d search this train thoroughly.

But blurting out “the government sacrificed a million citizens” to Bronze would be insane, so—

I smiled.

“I’m going to settle in on this train.”

“…Excuse me?”

“I’m tired of station visits. I want to rest.”

“…Excuse me?”

“Please take care of me.”

He looked stunned. Sorry—but this was the best option.

—That must’ve been a difficult decision. Counselor.

It was.

But now that I’d decided, I’d—

“…?!”

—Did I startle you? Please check your pocket.

Pocket?

I shoved my hand into my jacket.

A small silver coin glittered there.

A Cheerful Coin.

Minted from the rusted silver powder I’d collected.

I didn’t want to leave it as dust, so I used my inventory tattoo to have it compressed into a theme-park-style coin.

[Show business, my goodness.]

From within it, the voice of Juror Number One echoed.

[Let’s throw it away, my friend!]
