# Chapter 326

Unfortunately, Braun
's immediate and quick-witted request was rejected.

Before throwing away a talking coin, shouldn’t we at least hear the full story…?

And then, the “Coin Made from Juror No. 1’s Powder,” which had suddenly found its voice, calmly explained the situation.

**“I’m not really sure myself.”**

Right…

**“I felt a strange surge of strength, and suddenly my consciousness returned. I became aware of myself again.”**

The rusted silver coin glinted.

Each time it spoke, it seemed as though the rust was gradually flaking away.

**“Still, it’s nice to be able to have a conversation again like this. I’m glad.”**

Juror No. 1—what remained of Hoyuwon, left behind at Morning Station—had regained a sense of self and was speaking to me once more…

I steadied my almost-leaping heart and reasoned through the throbbing in my head.

‘Was it a mistake to turn the remaining powder into a Cheerful Themepark Coin…?’

Or perhaps it was because we had entered the Segwang Special City subway again.

‘…In any case, I’m glad to meet you, too.’

**“Thank you.”**

Laughter came from the coin.

‘How much do you remember about yourself?’

**“I remember everything up until the day of the disaster. Please feel free to ask.”**

…!

‘Then… who are you?’

**“I am a psychological counselor who tries to comfort and encourage the people who gathered in this city.”**

**“My dream is for their exhaustion and wounds to heal, so that this city can become a more beautiful and livable place.”**

‘What is your relationship with the Disaster Management Bureau?’

**“I’m acquainted with the agents there. You could say we help each other out.”**

The voice was gentle and warm.

Like the fox counselor I met at the Fox Counseling Office.

**“I don’t know how long we’ll be able to keep talking, but until then, I look forward to working with you.”**

So this was Hoyuwon from before Segwang Special City descended into chaos…

Either way, it was an astonishing situation.

And for someone currently tucked into the pocket on my front , it seemed to be a profoundly shocking event…

[Yes, friend  . I knew this day would come. Ah, an unstoppable flow in the entertainment business. The undeniable truth that everyone loves a fresh face!]

There was the exaggerated sound of someone wiping tears with an extremely luxurious handkerchief—though it sounded more like delicately polishing a TV screen…

[This cotton-stuffed body of mine will be cast aside now. To think you’d befriend a scrap of a coin instead of your one and only friend who has given Mr. Roe Deer full support…]

The situation was escalating on its own.

If I listened absentmindedly, I might reflexively shout, “That can’t be!” and naturally fling the coin out of the subway…

This is insane…

[Mr. Roe Deer, what are you thinking so hard about?]

‘Um.’

I spoke with the uneasy feeling of breaking into a cold sweat.

‘…What if we think of it as a guest joining us?’

[I will fire them.]

Yeah…

[Ha-ha! Oh, friend  , you’re tense. You needn’t be. Everything I said was merely playful advice!]

The host’s soothing voice suddenly dropped sharply.

[Surely my friend wouldn’t betray matters of contract and trust? You personally summoned this
Braun here as a friend, and you already know deep down that this position cannot be replaced…]

Terrifying. Truly.

[Isn’t that right?]

‘Of course. Naturally.’

Friendly-friend horror stories are scary.

I replied with a smile, feeling cold sweat pour down my back.

‘But we need information. We need to talk.’

[Hmm…]

‘It’s small and allows real-time communication—if we focus only on functional traits, it’s hard to compare it to you, Braun. By that logic, a smartphone would count, too.’

I hastily added before something like, “Really? Then let’s keep a smartphone around, too, friend!  ” could slip out.

‘It won’t overlap with your position.’

Soon, a slightly displeased but accepting voice responded.

[A sincere statement, Mr. Roe Deer.]

Phew.

[Very well. But do not keep it beside me. A plague, honestly.]

In the end, I took out the silver coin and put it into my pants pocket as far away from Braun as possible. The “counselor” residing within didn’t seem to mind much…

**“You’re very eloquent, sir.”**

Thank you…

The counselor seemed rather amused, but soon muttered thoughtfully while observing the surroundings.

**“This is… inside a subway train.”**

It felt like a jolt of clarity.

Right. Juror No. 1 appeared to have been from the Segwang Special City Disaster Management Bureau.

‘This is said to be a shelter created by a Blue Dragon Team agent. …Do you know anything related to that?’

After a moment of careful thought, a voice replied.

**“My memories after the day of the disaster aren’t very intact, but I can make a guess.”**

**“If an agent made it, perhaps they were trying to make the train itself safe.”**

The train itself?

‘Then there should be traces left somewhere that could affect the entire train…’

When it came to the train’s core facilities, only one thing came to mind.

‘The driver’s cab.’

The very front car of the train.

A zone no different from a system interface.

The problem, however, was that this train shelter was divided into factions between the front cars and the rear cars.

And the front cars were hostile and exclusionary toward outsiders… with a strange sense of superiority, even chosenness.

‘Come to think of it, maybe that’s because the driver’s cab is there.’

…Hmm.

Leaving Agent Bronze to rest despite his ongoing headache, I went to meet someone else.

“Ms. Goral.”

“Mr. Roe Deer…”

Go Yeongeun-ssi looked healthier than last time, thankfully.

She seemed to study my complexion, then lowered her voice and asked, “Did something bad happen?”

“Pardon? Ah, well—both good and bad things happened. Personally.”

Getting my body back was good; human sacrifice was bad. That sort of thing.

But unexpectedly, Ms. Go let out a sigh of relief at my response.

“That’s a relief.”

“Huh?”

“You didn’t look too well, but you seem more at ease now.”

“…Thank you.”

After sharing a brief smile with her, I turned serious and got to the point.

“For the time being… it would be better if people from the train shelter avoided going near the casino.”

“What?”

Though startled at first, she quickly sensed the mood, stiffened, and asked, “I wasn’t planning on going anyway, but… how long should we avoid it?”

“You’ll probably receive word within a few days.”

I recalled, “They said they’d send a signal once things are wrapped up.”

Agent Haegum raised her head.

Before her stood a door embedded in the subway, brazenly resembling the entrance to an illegal gambling den.

‘Brings back memories.’

Gambling den horror stories tended to be extreme.

Either a dazzling, luxurious casino—or a run-down, obviously dangerous den.

This one leaned toward the latter, but Haegum didn’t need to speculate. She’d already heard everything from someone with firsthand experience.

Behind her followed two employees from Daydream.

“How does it look, Team Leader?”

The young woman in a pony mask spoke politely; she looked affable, but desire overflowed from her eyes.

Greed for wealth!

“Ah, it’s my first time meeting a senior like you!”

At her overly friendly greeting, Haegum’s face—beneath it—was covered by a Daydream-style mask.

A white owl mask.

She’d considered a Hahoe mask—specifically the Jungtal—but decided it was better not to reveal her agent identity unnecessarily.

From behind this unfamiliar mask, she examined the next person.

“Are we entering now?”

The one wearing a butterfly mask was impeccably dressed—razor-sharp, without a single crease. Gleaming nails, polished luxury shoes.

Yet beneath the flamboyant mask, the eyes visible on the tall, slender frame were icy cold, tinged with faint irritation.

If she hadn’t come as the temporary team leader, there would have been a power struggle from the start.

‘Mm. Good.’

She looked utterly merciless.

More importantly, she was familiar.

Haegum felt a faint sense of sentimentality, knowing the butterfly employee—whom she’d once locked in a glass prison—had been promoted to the elite team.

Concealing one’s identity had advantages like this.

“Let’s enter.”

Haegum immediately took out a pouch. A pile of Body Casino coins she’d received from Agent Grapes.

Funds for this expedition.

After distributing the shining coins one by one, she led them into the Body Casino.

“So that’s the mission funding?”

The pony mask’s eyes sparkled at the heavy pouch.

“Haha, of course, I know we’ll be playing as a team, but…”

“No. We won’t.”

“…What?”

Smiling, Haegum divided the coins into three on the spot.

“Here.”

“…!”

“Spend them however you want.”

Soon, each of them held their own stake.

Haegum looked at the two through the gaps of her mask.

“But if you go into debt, I won’t save you.”

“Understood!”

As if that wasn’t obvious!

The pony mask nearly shot beams of light from her eyes as she accepted the money, then glanced slyly at the butterfly deputy.

“Deputy, perhaps—”

“Get lost.”

“Yes, ma’am!”

And so the group split up.

Kang Yihak ran toward the slot machines, while Jin Nasol strode straight into the dealer room.

Haegum watched them act as they pleased, wearing a faintly amused smile.

At that very moment, Kim Soleum was recoiling in shock at the sight of a talking coin on the train.

‘Phew.’

They must have entered the casino by now.

If it was Agent Haegum, she’d be handling things well. I’d even passed along the advice to make sure she had a way to escape the casino if needed.

I should focus on the train.

Which is why I was currently talking to the person in front of me.

Go Yeongeun’s aunt—the so-called “station attendant.”

“You want to settle on the train?”

“Yes.”

“…Hmm. That won’t be easy.”

I knew it would be difficult to accept more residents, with two already taken in.

“Don’t worry. I’m not asking to be accepted as a shelter resident. I just want long-term lodging in Car No. 8.”

When I added that I would officially purchase a lodging pass, the attendant nodded.

“That doesn’t really require my special approval.”

“There’s just one thing I’d like to request… Is it possible to purchase additional privileges?”

“What kind of privileges?”

“I’d like to sell supplies in Car No. 7 as well.”

“Hmm…”

After a brief consideration, the attendant nodded.

“Very well, for now. Let’s discuss it again after reviewing the items.”

“Thank you.”

Done.

‘I already have the items in mind.’

I nodded as well, then added the decisive remark.

“However, when I stay long-term, I think I’ll arrive looking different from now.”

“…You mean a disguise?”

“Rather than a disguise, think of it as a different identity. And from that point on…”

People remember impressions more than details.

No one in the front cars would remember my face clearly. It wasn’t as if they had clairvoyance—they’d remember clothes or masks at most.

In that sense, removing the mask, messing up my hair, wearing glasses, and hunching my posture would make me hard to recognize.

“So please call me Grapes.”

…It’s been a while.

Agent Grapes.

I immediately went down to the platform and started changing clothes.

[So what bold choice will you make this time, friend  ?]

[One of our splendid train episodes comes to mind. My friend played the role of cult leader most daringly and magnificently…]

[How about it? Will you once again reveal the truth to those seeking a sign of faith in an extreme environment?]

Hmm…

Just as I was choosing my words—

**“Sir Doll, have you ever considered that your way of speaking might be placing excessive pressure on the other party?”**

Wait. Hold on.

[Oh.]

[Do go on.]

**“Your friend likely has a way of life they want as well. Constantly reading your mood might not be truly satisfying for them.”**

**“And perhaps you yourself aren’t entirely comfortable with this atmosphere either.”**

[Good heavens… I feel as though I’ve appeared on a cheap therapy program.]

Please stop.

I quickly cut in, feeling like I might break into a cold sweat.

‘Um… thank you for your concern. But actually, the approach I had in mind from the start is quite similar to what Braun suggested.’

[Ah, I see!]

This wasn’t empty flattery meant to soothe the horror-story host—it was genuine.

‘In an isolated, small society like this, nothing wields influence as easily as a cult.’

I’d even tried it once before on the Tamra-bound train.

But the conditions were different this time, so as an outsider, I planned a different approach.

‘But I’m not going to be the cult leader.’

[Hmm?]

‘I’ll be the sycophant.’

A suffocating silence followed.

“What?”

‘The number-one worshipper.’

[Mr. Roe, whose sycophant do you intend to be? This is unbelievable—my friend in a worshipping role. Then the cult leader must be an extraordinary being indeed…]

‘You.’

[Oh.]

And thus, I began secretly spreading the doctrine of Rabbit Doll Worship throughout the train.
