# Chapter 342

*Honestly, I couldn’t be sure.*

*Had Manager Lee Jaheon really been swept into this Day of Disaster as just another civilian, like me?*

*I had encountered the two people stored inside my tattoos in different ways, but Manager Lee Jaheon... had definitely died before I did.*

*I remember the sight clearly—the remaining upper half of Manager Lee Jaheon’s body, the one that pulled me out from beneath a pile of corpses, dropping heavily onto the concrete floor with a dull thud.*

That memory always brought a wave of anxious uncertainty whenever I recalled it, and I had forced myself to assume, “He must still be here.”

*And he really was.*

*I just never expected to meet him like this.*

*“...??”*

*I looked at the tall man in a white lab coat standing among the corpses.*

*Now that I looked closely, I could probably recognize him by his build alone.*

*That much made sense—but still, but still...*

*Why was Lee Jaheon a Cheerful Researcher?*

*‘And why does he not have a face...?!’*

*For a moment, shock froze my thoughts—but then I realized what I had to do.*

*A way to access the Cheerful Research Institute hidden inside the machine room.*

*I had to persuade this man.*

*“Are you an employee of the Cheerful Research Institute?”*

*“Yes.”*

*Manager Lee Jaheon—no, Researcher Lee Jaheon—turned his attention toward me. Come to think of it, does he have eyes? Or—no—there’s nothing—nothing at all—*

*‘Ha...’*

*I lowered my gaze to around his throat and steadied my rough breathing.*

*Then, with great effort, I spoke solemnly.*

*“My companions went inside already. Given the chaos, it looked like they were trying to deal with the situation.”*

*Setting aside the fact that he was Manager Lee Jaheon, this was simply a case of running into a related party in front of a secret passage leading into the Cheerful Research Institute.*

*If I wanted to enter the machine room without trouble...*

*‘I have to gamble.’*

*I took something out from my tattoo.*

*An ID card.*

*Name: ■ Heoun

Sekwang Transportation Corporation*

*It was Heoun’s ID—the one I had found in the station office.*

*I cleverly covered the bloodstained name area with my thumb and showed it from a reasonable distance.*

*“He gave this to me and said it was his access card. Is this the right place to go in? I have something urgent to tell Researcher Heoun—about this disaster.”*

*I was impersonating an acquaintance of a staff member.*

*And to keep him from questioning me further, I deliberately rushed my words, glancing around as if fascinated by the tracks across from us and the surroundings.*

*“But is it really okay for the path to the lab to be in such an open place?”*

*“Yes.”*

*“...Does that mean other people can’t see it, even though it’s here?”*

*“Yes.”*

*“So you’re using some kind of supernatural principle. I’m curious what kind.”*

*“I see.”*

*Researcher Lee Jaheon stepped closer. I fully covered the ID with one hand to hide how unnaturally old it looked and took a small step back.*

*Standing in front of the door, the researcher spoke.*

*“This door is a type of darkness. Those who do not understand its function cannot perceive anyone interacting with it.”*

*...I see.*

*“I suppose that’s why you could just hand out the key card so openly.”*

*Researcher Lee Jaheon didn’t reply.*

*I took the initiative.*

*“Then do I just tap the card here?”*

*Before an answer could come, I pressed the card key against the machine room panel.*

Beep.

*With a short sound, it unlocked.*

*A cool, exhilarating shiver ran from my fingertips all the way up to my head.*

*I immediately reached for the machine room door—*

Thump.

*Researcher Lee Jaheon grabbed the handle first.*

*“Please step back.”*

*“......”*

*That was it.*

*He didn’t ask who I was, or what relationship I had with Researcher Heoun.*

*‘Either he finds me suspicious, or he really thinks I’m a related party...’*

*At the very least, I wanted to read how a Cheerful Research Institute researcher would react to seeing an agent from the Disaster Management Bureau.*

*But there was nothing.*

*No sign at all.*

That was *his natural disposition—but with no face, it felt even more unreadable.*

*‘...It seems like other people can see his face, though.’*

*I checked the expressions of Student Ryu Jaegwan and Reporter Eun Haje.*

*One was watching the researcher with tense focus, bracing for whatever might happen.*

*The other had wide, sparkling eyes.*

*It seemed she was no longer admiring his looks, but rather growing intrigued by the situation itself.*

*Phew.*

*Meanwhile, Researcher Lee Jaheon opened the door.*

*Whoosh.*

*The air of a well-maintained office flowed out through the doorway and brushed against my nose.*

*Strangely, it was slightly sweet.*

*“Follow me.”*

*“......”*

*I tried to step inside after Researcher Lee Jaheon as he entered the machine room.*

*“You two should stay—”*

*I stopped.*

*Both of them wore expressions that clearly said they had no intention of staying behind.*

*And thinking about it, it was probably better for me to keep them with me rather than leave them among corpses and refugees.*

*Worst case, I could put them into my tattoo.*

*“...Let’s move quietly. We shouldn’t cause trouble for the researcher.”*

*“Yes.”*

*“Of course.”*

*Leaving behind the two who nodded vigorously, I cautiously looked down the corridor again.*

*And I was left speechless.*

*The very same office corridor layout I had seen beneath Daydream headquarters spread out before my eyes.*

*Not the burned-out corridor—but a vivid, living office hallway.*

*Yet it felt like another world entirely.*

*“Kyaaahahahaha...!”*

*Every corridor was filled with toys and decorations.*

*Colorful fireworks burst from golden frames on the walls, and laughter echoed through the air.*

*Multicolored confetti melted away the moment it touched the floor, while finely crafted wind-up dolls and small toy trains ran across it.*

*Whoooosh!*

*From the toy trains, instead of steam, pale pink and light blue, Milky Way–like clouds burst out and vanished into the air.*

*“Ah.”*

*So this is what it originally looked like.*

*Red and blue velvet curtains fluttered here and there, and carpets covered the floors.*

*The overwhelming sight made my thoughts freeze for a moment.*

*And then...*

*On the wall in the middle of the corridor hung a gleaming brass nameplate.*

Cheerful Research Institute!  For all the children of the world,

we research safe enjoyment.

*I realized it naturally.

That this place was the very core level of the Cheerful Research Institute.*

*“......”*

*I looked beneath the nameplate.

Below it, embossed reliefs of stuffed animal figures could be seen.

‘A rabbit, a fox, ...a dragon?’*

*I felt the urge to look more closely, but Researcher Lee Jaheon’s footsteps continued forward without hesitation,

into a maze-like corridor.*

*In the end, I tore my gaze away and continued following Researcher Lee Jaheon.*

*“Ah...!”*

*High school student Ryu Jaegwan ducked to avoid a burst of fireworks, then carefully followed along while avoiding a clockwork toy soldier coming toward him.

Reporter Eun Haje lifted her eyebrows at every step, pulled out a notebook from somewhere, and furiously scribbled something down.*

*That literally “cheerful” scene continued, and just as my mood was starting to lift in a strange way, a sharp voice cut through the cheerful laughter of children.*

*The voices of angry adults.

Confusion, rage, rough emotions.*

*“...!”*

*Researcher Lee Jaheon did not stop walking.

I briefly spotted an antique elevator at the far end opposite us, but an A4 sheet with “FIRE! OUT OF ORDER!” scrawled on it was taped above the doors.*

*Researcher Lee Jaheon changed direction.*

*The deeper we went into the corridor after her, the louder and clearer the voices became...*

*Two people were arguing.

And one of them was—*

*— You’re saying it’s not there?*

*It was a familiar voice.

That flustered tone was unmistakably—*

*‘Ho Yuwon.’*

*Click.*

*At last, Researcher Lee Jaheon stopped in front of an office and pulled the door open.*

*Room 1004.*

*The door with the nameplate swung wide open, revealing the interior.*

*The first thing that caught my eye was a dream incubator.*

*In front of the machine, completely empty of any solution, two figures were standing and arguing.*

*“What do you mean it’s not there—what on earth are you saying...”*

*Then, startled by the door opening, they turned toward us.*

*I recognized the pale face of the counseling teacher among them.*

*And then—*

*“Researcher Lee Heoun.”*

*...!*

*“They said they came looking for you.”*

*“Pardon? In a situation where the entire institute is evacuating, what do you mean—”*

*The researcher standing in front of the counseling teacher, with long dark-brown hair tied into a ponytail, met my eyes.*

*I stared at them as if struck by lightning.*

*A person I had only encountered through traces until now—someone presumed to be dead.*

*“Do we... know each other, Agent?”*

*A researcher of the Cheerful Research Institute.*

*A living Lee Heoun stood before my eyes.*

*‘My God.’*

*I had hoped I might encounter a living researcher on the day of the disaster, but now that it was happening, my hands were trembling.*

*The clue was right in front of me.*

*“I’ve never seen you before. How did you enter the institute? And why—”*

*When that person noticed the bloodstains on the body of Reporter Eun Haje standing behind me, their face turned deathly pale.*

*Their speech, build, behavior—everything—*

*【 Good heavens, they look exactly like my friend’s resort employee—a doppelgänger! 】*

*...was terrifyingly similar to Jang Heoun.*

*A chill ran down my spine, but I looked at them calmly.*

*Their expression showed confusion and wariness, but surprisingly not hostility.

Even as they immediately identified me as an agent from my attire.*

*‘...Because of the counseling teacher?’*

*It was easy enough to infer, given that they were already meeting one-on-one with a cooperative spirit.*

*‘...If that’s the case.’*

*It might be easier to shock them instead.*

*I pulled out my glass hand cannon and aimed it at them.*

*Lee Heoun’s expression changed.*

*“Th—!”*

*“Researcher.”*

*“...Yes?”*

“I came here after receiving a tip from an insider that the cause of this massive supernatural disaster is the Cheerf
ul *Research Institute.”*

*Lee Heoun’s face turned stark white.*

*“Who on earth—no, that’s not it, we—”*

*“You’re saying you’re not involved? That can’t be true.”*

*I needed to press them and extract information...!*

*Gesturing toward the empty dream incubator, I spoke.*

*“Isn’t all of this happening because of the children’s Paradise Syrup you were researching?”*

*“......!”*

*“An astronomical number of civilians are dying outside. Answer honestly. Is the Cheerful Research Institute the cause of this disaster?”*

*“No!”*

*The answer burst out as if vomited.*

*“That’s not what we research. Someone tampered with it!”*

*“Then what were you researching?”*

*“That’s...”*

*Seeing the researcher trail off, I crossed my arms and said,*

*“How to create a world without urban legends?”*

*“...!”*

*The researcher’s face froze.*

*Then, slowly—very slowly—they looked at me.*

*“...If you know about the children’s Paradise Syrup, then you must know that as well.”*

*They slumped their shoulders as if resigned.*

*Then they stepped toward a framed picture hanging in the office.*

*It was a photograph of stuffed animals.*

*Behind a deer doll, an enormous black shadow with a strange silhouette loomed...*

*Good Friend*

*“This world is far too dangerous. Urban legends, darkness, supernatural disasters—these strange phenomena, called by many names, have only become more frequent in modern times.”*

*Researcher Lee Heoun traced the black shadow with their hand.*

*Their eyes shone with gentle sincerity.*

*“Dangerous places—places that are not reality—are being projected.”*

*“......”*

*“This institute has been researching ways to process those phenomena as safely and enjoyably as possible.”*

*“...!”*

*‘Right.’*

*Toys.

Board games.

Children’s shops.*

*All the inventions and products created by the Cheerful Research Institute took the form of children’s entertainment.*

*Even the syrups they produced.*

*“Children’s products are safer and more harmless than adult ones. Because urban legends are influenced by perception and rules, we tried to interpret everything through a children’s lens...”*

*As if censoring content by target age group.*

*Like “Children’s Shakespeare” or “Children’s Romance of the Three Kingdoms.”*

*‘The Cheerful Research Institute has been trying to censor urban legends.’*

*Everything clicked into place in my mind, sending a faint but thrilling shiver through me.*

*Then Lee Heoun’s gaze shifted to my chest.*

*“...Like your doll, Agent.”*

*“......!”*

*“That’s a Good Friend, right? ...Its form is a bit different. It must be an improved version made by another department.”*

*【 Oh dear, this cotton-stuffed body is a limited edition handmade piece my friend personally produced at the resort. One might say it’s a rare artisan work! ...Ah, my friend! 】*

*I reflexively stroked Braun and gently pushed him into my front pocket.*

*Watching that with somewhat warm eyes, Researcher Lee Heoun prompted me to speak.*

*“But that wasn’t the end, was it? You also...”*

*I looked at the dream incubator.*

*“You began using the Dreamwake Solution.”*

*“...That’s correct.”*

*Lee Heoun’s voice lowered.*

*“We realized that this alone wasn’t enough. No matter how much we tried to make children’s products, the fundamental danger didn’t disappear. Urban legends are still urban legends.”*

*“...So you began researching a way to make the phenomena themselves... disappear?”*

*“Yes.”*

*Lee Heoun’s speech quickened.*

*“But it was impossible. You can’t use mystical power to erase mystery—just like you can’t cut off your left hand with your left hand.”*

*“......”*

*“So we came up with a new idea. ‘What if... we created an entirely new world without urban legends?’”*

*Their words sped up further.*

*“World creation. It sounds absurd, but when you think in children’s terms, it’s not impossible. Children imagining their own little worlds, like imaginary friends, is quite common. But even that ran into obstacles...”*

*“...To create a real world, you would have needed materials.”*

*“Exactly.”*

*My heart began to pound ominously.*

*“Following a proposal from a certain spirit, we partnered with a company and began extracting power from supernatural phenomena. A mysterious golden light—imagination and unknown force. That...”*

*Lee Heoun placed a hand on the dream incubator.*

*Their voice grew dreamy, almost dazed.*

*“We decided to call it the Dreamwake Solution.”*

*“......”*

*I looked at the incubator’s emoticon buttons.*

*“After several trial and errors, we developed a syrup capable of manipulating reality. And one of those was the children’s Paradise Syrup.”*

*But there was no slot for a children’s Paradise Syrup button on the machine.*

*The center of the bottom row was smoothly sealed, while an angel-ringed emoticon was mixed among the others on the top row.*

*“...At first, it was just a potion that let you have wonderful daydreams.”*

*Lee Heoun gently touched the button, affection evident.*

*“But at one researcher’s suggestion, we used that potion as a base and developed another one—a potion to create a ‘world without urban legends.’”*

*I recalled the note I had seen at the burned-out Cheerful Research Institute in the subway.*

*For this invitation research

Use children’s Paradise Syrup

→ my opinion!*

*...Wait.*

*“Then why was it called ‘invitation research’?”*

*“Pardon?”*

*“Why was it an invitation? Why did it involve inviting someone?”*

*At my question, Lee Heoun hesitated, as if about to make excuses, but finally spoke as though giving everything up.*

*“...Because we realized a way to create a world very naturally. By inserting it as a premise.”*

*“A premise?”*

*“Like this. If we invite Cinderella, then a world where there is a fairy godmother and magical glass slippers must exist.”*

*Wait.*

*“If we invite the wicked gray wolf, then a world exists where Little Red Riding Hood’s grandmother is eaten by the wolf. ...So if.”*

*Wait—*

*“If we invite a person who lives in a place without urban legends.”*

*“......”*

*“Then a place without urban legends must exist.”*

*I froze.*

*“That’s what we’ve been researching all this time. And today, we attempted the invitation.”*

*I understood.*

*The one they invited was me.*

The supernatural disaster in Sekwang Special City occurred as part of the ritual to invite Kim Soleum—from a world without urban legends.
