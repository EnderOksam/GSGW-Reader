# Chapter 337

**Why is Agent Bronze... wearing Sekwang High’s uniform, and looking so young?**

“......”

The insanity of the situation nearly left me stunned, but I forced myself to collect my thoughts and assess things immediately.

Agent Bronze looked even slightly younger than his appearance as a high schooler in the urban legend **‘In the Black Shade.’**

And it wasn’t just the uniform that had changed.

His expression and overall presence were unmistakably youthful.

“Uh...”

And he didn’t recognize me.

‘...Has he been assimilated into the Sekwang Special City urban legend?’

Like me—assigned an appropriate role as a citizen of Sekwang Special City on May 4th.

...Wait.

If everyone else who fell here is in this same state, then how am I supposed to get them out?

How do I convince them?

The relief of finding a companion was brief; another urgent dilemma immediately followed.

I desperately turned my thoughts over and over. While I did, the high schooler Ryu Jaekwan—who had been running behind me—caught his breath and chose his words.

“About the bicycle earlier... are you okay?”

“I’m fine.”

I looked at his almost boyish face.

There was confusion and concern mixed in, but considering he’d just witnessed something straight out of an urban legend becoming reality, he was remarkably calm.

‘...He said the first time Agent Bronze got caught up in an urban legend and was rescued by the Disaster Management Bureau was when he was a student.’

Could he have been reflected into an even earlier point than that? No—what mattered now was how to move together.

Pretending to look over his uniform as if checking for a name tag, I asked,

“More importantly... are you a student?”

“Yes.”

“Is your destination Sekwang Technical High School, by any chance? That looks like their uniform.”

“Yes, it is, but...”

“That works out. I’m heading that way too.”

I moved toward the shared bike rack and began smashing the locks with a rock.

“...!”

“Renting shared bikes with a smartphone is probably difficult now. This’ll be faster than wasting time trying.”

“......”

“Did you see people swelling up on the way here? Or disaster alerts?”

“...Yes.”

“From now on, only listen to the alert sound. Don’t read the messages. And if you see people walking together along the roadside, never approach them.”

One bike’s lock finally broke, but I didn’t stop—I smashed the next one too.

Then I pulled a bike free and held it out to him.

“Can you ride?”

Ryu Jaekwan stared at me with trembling eyes, then nodded firmly.

I rode in front, leading the way.

Behind me, high schooler Ryu Jaekwan followed, riding the shared bike I’d freed.

Judging by how tightly he clenched his teeth and kept up, it seemed Agent Bronze had shown promise in bike riding from the start...

Skillfully avoiding the sounds of disaster alerts and screams echoing from all directions, I kept detouring toward Sekwang Technical High School.

But those warning signs would disappear soon enough.

...Once there were no survivors left nearby, the alerts would naturally stop.

‘We have to move fast.’

My mind raced as I planned our route—and then I spotted something.

A convenience store.

“Wait a moment.”

“Yes?”

Making sure Ryu Jaekwan was steady, I stopped briefly, rushed inside, and swept items into my arms.

‘Food first.’

I didn’t take too much—mobility was essential.

Instead, after some thought, I grabbed two sturdy long umbrellas and a tool kit.

While paying, I spoke to the part-time clerk at the counter, who’d been glancing at a book with earbuds in.

“Get out of here quickly.”

“Pardon?”

“There’s a biochemical terror incident spreading near City Hall. Run to a subway station as fast as you can.”

“!!”

Only then did the clerk check their phone—and immediately started frantically scrolling with a panicked face.

As I left, I glimpsed the clerk hurriedly gathering supplies, just like I had...

Outside, I turned to Ryu Jaekwan, who was standing awkwardly beside his bike.

“Open your bag.”

“Pardon?”

I put two small bottles of water and a calorie bar into his school bag, then handed him an umbrella and strapped it to the bike.

“We don’t know how things will go. It’s better to be prepared. Use the umbrella to shove things away if you have to.”

“Th-thank you.”

He gripped his bag, hesitating.

“I’ll pay you back.”

“This isn’t the time to take money from a student. Get on the bike. We’re heading for Sekwang High, fast.”

“But... yes.”

He looked full of questions, but wiped his sweat and grabbed the bike again, clearly understanding the seriousness of the situation.

Still, he eventually asked,

“How do you... know all this so well?”

“......I’m used to it.”

“Pardon?”

I changed the subject.

“More importantly, school’s already started. Why are you heading there now?”

“I had to stop by the bank, so I’m late.”

“Did you contact your family?”

“...It’s fine.”

That careful answer told me his assigned backstory here was likely close to reality.

—His parents divorced when he was young, and after his mother passed away, he grew up in an orphanage.

...Let’s not push further.

“Understood.”

I rode on quietly.

Probably because of his build, Ryu Jaekwan kept up without trouble.

‘Good.’

Soon, the wall of Sekwang Technical High School came into view.

“Hoo...”

The area around the school wasn’t chaotic yet.

But from the opposite wall and nearby apartment complexes, disaster alert sounds carried over—

Along with screams.

“......”

“......”

We approached the main gate with extreme caution.

I could feel his eyes studying me.

“Why... are you coming to this school?”

“There’s someone I need to find.”

I answered without hesitation, scanning the area as I entered the grounds.

After parking my bike in an inconspicuous corner, Ryu Jaekwan did the same and followed me.

He was still holding the umbrella, just like I was.

It was oddly commendable—but my tension only increased.

‘...No security guard?’

Strange.

We crept toward the main entrance, careful even with our footsteps.

Ryu Jaekwan grabbed the door handle and pulled—

But it didn’t open properly. Something felt jammed.

‘Hmm?’

He hesitated, then pulled harder—

“Wait.”

“Yes?”

“Come this way.”

Something felt off.

I stopped him, left the door untouched, and cautiously called him over to a window.

From there, we could see into the first-floor hallway.

Soft-colored curtains hung inside, but there was a gap.

“...Hoo.”

I looked through first, then stepped aside for him.

His questioning expression froze into pale shock the moment he looked inside.

“...Why?”

Just as I thought.

Students lay asleep along the hallway floor.

It was the same peaceful ending scene I’d seen after clearing **‘In the Black Shade.’**

Paper talismans were stuck in the gaps of classroom doors and windows, filling me with an eerie sense of aftertaste.

‘So this is how Team Leader Hong’s barrier manifested...’

Keeping children asleep even through the repeating Disaster Day.

That suspended state was probably treated as a form of “death,” isolating them from the extinction-class disaster spread by the alerts—like the Snow White’s apple I’d eaten.

Which meant the school doors couldn’t be opened.

Even if they could, they shouldn’t be.

But for Ryu Jaekwan, seeing students collapsed in the corridor was clearly horrifying.

“What... is this...?”

I quietly turned to him.

“Does anything come to mind?”

“...Come to mind?”

“I mean from seeing this. Especially—”

I pointed at the talismans.

The symbols Agent Bronze himself had once drawn.

“Those talismans.”

“......”

For a brief moment, a strange light flashed in his eyes as he traced the patterns.

But it quickly faded into confusion and worry, returning to the gaze of an ordinary teenager.

When he looked back at me, there was tension—wariness.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about.”

“......”

Fair enough. It took me over six hundred loops to escape—expecting him to snap out of it from a few words was unrealistic.

If anything, I probably looked suspicious, pointing at talismans and talking nonsense.

‘Don’t rush.’

Then—

“There’s no need to worry.”

I smiled at him.

“Those are protective talismans. As long as no one removes them, this school is safe from what’s happening outside.”

A flicker of **How would you know that?** crossed his face—but it turned more curious than wary.

“So don’t worry about your friends. Let’s think about how to get out of here.”

“...Then what will you do, sir? You said someone you need to meet is here.”

Yes. Ho Yuwon.

But a doubt surfaced.

With the doors sealed and everyone asleep, how would the counselor get out?

At that moment—

Both of our disaster alerts went off simultaneously.

“...!”

I turned reflexively.

...Near the gate.

From the shadow of the school building’s corner, a smiling security guard stood holding a phone—and looking at us.

Then he slowly stepped out of the shade and began walking toward us.

Frozen, I stared.

And realized—

There wasn’t just one guard.

Another upper body was fused behind him, with an extra lower body trailing like a tail.

They were merged.

“...!”

And that wasn’t all.

...Beyond the gate, people were visible.

Residents from nearby apartments, wearing calm expressions.

Some of the flow of people walking along the main road had reached right outside the gate...

‘Damn it.’

The guards were crossing the field.

The alerts kept ringing. The exit was gone.

I slapped my own hand away as it reached for my phone—and knocked Ryu Jaekwan’s phone down too.

‘We can’t leave through the front.’

Waiting for the counselor... has to be abandoned, for now.

“Student.”

“......”

“When I say go, grab your bike and head for the back gate. Do not—under any circumstances—run into the crowd at the front.”

He nodded slowly.

Cold sweat trickled down my chin.

Which meant—

‘...I have to lure them.’

I hated it, but there was no choice.

I focused on the guards rather than the crowd.

‘I can’t let them into the building.’

If my presence had caused this abnormal draw, it would be the worst-case scenario.

Suppressing a chill, I swallowed and prepared to sprint toward the gate—

But then.

[Oh? A visitor, I see. My friend!]

Someone moved toward the gate before I did.

The silhouette was instantly familiar.

The counselor.

The person I’d been waiting for looked briefly startled—then regained composure and ran.

“...!!”

He headed straight for the gate despite the crowd outside, but stopped short when he saw the guards.

And the moment the **guards** noticed him—

...the counselor calmly walked toward them.

‘No.’

A chill shot down my spine.

‘Is he insane?!’

I clenched my teeth, ran for my bike, and shouted,

“Go! Back gate, now!”

“...!”

Then I mounted the bike and charged toward the gate at full speed.

The wheels rattled against the ground.

‘Please!’

Trust the Disaster Management Bureau bike training!

But then—

The counselor thrust his hand into the guards.

“...!”

It was as if he were rummaging through the mutated flesh of the guard’s head, searching for something. The guard swayed.

I realized something I’d known but never fully **felt** .

The counselor wasn’t human.

A cooperative spirit.

Meaning—that, too, was an urban legend.

I reflexively braked.

The being before me examined the guard’s insides with serious, almost academic focus.

But—

“......!”

The counselor flinched.

Something was wrong. He tried to pull his hand out.

It wouldn’t come free.

Instead, his arm was sucked deeper into the guard’s distorted torso. My phone blared its alert. The world warped around him—my head flooded with bizarre sensations, blue forests, feverish madness—

“Hng!”

I snapped back to myself.

The counselor had nearly pulled his arm free—but what emerged was—

Melting.

“...!!”

The guard slowly reached out, as if to grab him.

‘Damn it...!’

After a moment’s hesitation, I rode forward, teeth clenched.

With an internal scream, I snatched the counselor away from the guards.

“...!”

The recoil of the bike tore his arm free.

A sharp pressure split my head; blood dripped from my nose.

The bike nearly toppled under his weight, but I brute-forced the balance and pedaled again.

My head spun.

[An acrobatic performance worthy of praise, Mr. Roe!]

The counselor was slung behind me like baggage, carefully keeping his infected arm from touching my back.

‘...Ha.’

After nearly crashing three times, I finally shook the guards.

My hands were slick with sweat.

For the first time, I found myself desperately missing Agent Choi and Agent Bronze—people who were actually better at riding than me.

‘Please.’

Cold sweat streamed down my spine as I pedaled like mad.

A shaken voice came from behind.

“You are...”

“Later!”

We cut across the field, joined up with Ryu Jaekwan at the back gate, and barely managed to escape.

Toward a direction where no alert sounds followed.

With both hands occupied, I couldn’t accidentally read the messages—thankfully.

The counselor I’d grabbed stayed silent behind me.

“Huff!”

I finally stopped near the wooded hill behind the school.

Far from City Hall, with few people around, there were no visible infection sources.

And—

“Are you alright?”

“......”

A warm hand rested on my shoulder.

I turned.

The counselor, holding me with his uninfected hand, looked at me with concern.

Ho Yuwon.

A face—and presence—so calm and gentle it felt unfamiliar.

“You’re an agent, right? Thank you for helping me. Are you injured? Ah—doesn’t look like it. That’s a relief.”

Director Ho’s true form.

The very target trapped in Sekwang Special City was standing before me.

“Cooperative spirit.”

“You can just call me a counselor.”

“...Counselor.”

I swallowed.

I really didn’t want to say this, but—

“Please give me your arm.”

“...Pardon?”

“Your arm. Bite down.”

It clearly needed to be amputated.

But he smiled faintly and tried to stop me.

“Contamination only applies to citizens. I should be fi—”

Then his expression stiffened as he looked at his arm again.

It was collapsing, losing form.

“...Begin.”

“......”

He stopped protesting.

I took a tool from the kit and began the procedure—

“Student.”

“......”

“Please turn around.”

But Ryu Jaekwan sat beside me, pale.

“I’ll help.”

“No.”

“Step back.”

Yet he stubbornly stayed, handing me the saw from the kit.

His hand trembled.

He probably couldn’t fully understand what was happening—yet his eyes held stubborn resolve.

Holding back a sigh, I began.

**Squelch.**

The counselor’s face remained calm.

Strangely so—no pain, no reaction.

Below the collapsed elbow, the arm simply fell away.

As if it had never been there.

Erased.

“......”

“Thank you.”

Perhaps because he wasn’t human.

I nodded silently, suppressing the chill creeping up my arm.

The counselor looked at the missing limb with unsettling calm—but urgency colored his voice.

“May I ask one more favor? There’s somewhere I must go to end this disaster. Something I need to retrieve.”

“......”

“If you can bike me as far as the station, I’ll attempt termination with the Black Tortoise Team while you carry out rescues.”

But I knew.

Every effort the counselor and the agents made would fail.

Trapped inside, endlessly repeating Disaster Day.

“......”

My mood sank.

Noticing, the counselor forced a joking tone.

“You’re wearing my jacket, after all. I was hoping you’d hear me out.”

“...!”

“You found it in the branch lounge, didn’t you?”

I looked down.

—Juror No. 1’s jacket.

Ho Yuwon’s Disaster Management Bureau jacket was still draped over me.

He’d recognized it.

‘...Then.’

I smiled awkwardly.

“It is yours. I was on leave and grabbed it in a hurry. Sorry.”

A faint smile eased his anxious face.

“It’s fine. Are you with the Azure Dragon Team?”

Then—

“...Did you say agent?”

Both of us turned.

Ryu Jaekwan.

His voice trembled, but it was controlled—trying to suppress fear and confusion.

And there was curiosity.

“What kind of... agent are you?”

“......”

That was when I realized.

How to bring him along.

I spoke slowly.

“You saw it earlier—the alerts, the monsters, the strange phenomena. Like urban legends.”

He nodded.

“These are disasters known as supernatural phenomena. I belong to a government agency that handles them—the Supernatural Disaster Management Bureau.”

“...!”

“I came here because there are people who must be rescued.”

I looked at him—his eyes unknowingly shining.

“Do you know who one of them is?”

“...The counselor?”

“No.”

I smiled at him.

“It’s you.”

“...!”

“Ryu Jaekwan.”

Hearing his own name from a stranger who’d never been told it, his eyes wavered.

“I’m glad we found you quickly. So—”

I grasped his shoulder.

“Until you escape this disaster safely, I’ll be accompanying you.”
