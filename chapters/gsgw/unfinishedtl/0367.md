# Chapter 366

I lifted my head.

Water was rising in the resort corridor.

Slightly murky—not entirely clear like the disinfected water in a theme park pool.

Like rainwater.

Rumble…

Water poured in, waves crashing.

Strangely, I saw that the thick swell did not enter the open control room. It only surged and flowed along the dark corridor of the Flower Golden Resort.

That heaving scene.

And then—

Good child.

I approached the water’s edge in the corridor.

And since I couldn’t think of a more fitting title, I called out:

“…Elder?”

Come here.

The rainwater shimmered, gathering an outline and splashing up into the empty air of the corridor.

As if beckoning.

“……”

I placed my hand into the current.

My cloth hand soaked through, growing heavy as it was drawn downward into the water.

Strangely deep.

“…!!”

Splash!

And just like that, I sank.

My breath… my breathing wasn’t troubled.

It was calm.

Ah.

I lowered my head.

In my arms, I saw the scale shimmering even in the opaque water—then melting away…

And then—

Rumble…

The current began to flow, heavy and fierce, toward the far end of the corridor.

…!

The scenery of the corridor blurred and changed rapidly.

Thanks to the water enveloping me, I didn’t collide with anything. Though submerged and unable to see clearly, I witnessed it.

The Daydream character!

The dragon mascot of Daydream, still clutching the office scissors, was swallowed by the massive wave it encountered, its cotton body drenched and dragged under.

Ah.

In the rushing water, I saw its outline.

The murky golden-black liquid covering it was washed away.

The grotesquely stitched form of the mascot was fully revealed. It spotted me in the water and seemed to raise the scissors—

But a stronger current swept it away first.

A wave.

Within it, the figure came apart.

The worm-like things writhing between the mascot’s seams scattered as well.

Down into the deep water.

The remaining fragments of the Daydream character settled like sediment over time, like sinking to the seabed—fading beneath the corridor floor.

I watched the overwhelming sight in silence.

Rumble…

The water kept flowing.

It carried me past the corridor and finally into the massive lobby of the old Flower Golden Resort.

Waves already shimmered there as well.

The lobby doors, unable to withstand the pressure, burst open, and the current flowed onward again.

Carrying me along its path…

Now.

Here.

…Outside the resort.

To the gate.

“……”

Carefully, I stepped out of the water.

I walked to the gate and looked beyond the yellow zone.

Where the blue zone had been.

In my mental world, that place…

“……”

The Blue Dream Water Park was now submerged.

All its various pools, wave pools, lazy rivers, and artificially constructed basins were swallowed by a vast water surface.

The sea.

It had become the sea…

“……”

A strange swell rose in my chest.

Cutting out a piece of the sea and stuffing it into a pool.

Adding preservatives so it wouldn’t rot even when stagnant.

Why did all of that feel so cruel…?

I didn’t know.

Whose emotion it was, either.

I’m fine  .

“…!”

I lowered my head.

The “current” rippled as it spoke.

Here.

“……”

I looked.

At the gate leading out.

If I left through this gate, I could exit my mental world.

Then I could wake up.

Thanks to “someone inside the blue mascot” answering my call, I had avoided being killed as the Good Friend persona.

But.

…If I leave, Director Cheong will just try another method.

So…

Are you worried?

…!

“Yes. I am worried.”

The current rippled.

It’s okay.

I’ll help.

I knew what that meant.

It meant… it would confront Director Cheong.

That had been something I half-expected when I used the scale.

Wouldn’t the “Lord of the Deep Sea” have a conceptual advantage over Kkangcheol, symbol of fire and drought?

In ghost stories, that kind of thing matters.

But… is it really okay?

I knew I should nod immediately.

No matter what Director Cheong had done when she stuffed that dragon into the blue mascot, no matter what would happen if they faced off—

It was offering help.

This wasn’t a situation where I could afford to worry about a ghost story. At this rate, my ego would collapse and I’d be permanently locked underground with the Security Team.

It wouldn’t be strange to panic.

I knew that.

But…

……

“Thank you. But… I think it might be too dangerous.”

Smiling faintly, I crouched and looked at the flowing water.

“Director Cheong has already harmed you once. There’s no guarantee she couldn’t do it again.”

Yes.

Saying it aloud felt right.

“I’d appreciate it if you would lend only the power you can, without revealing yourself….”

But—

No.

“…Pardon?”

I’m already here.

“What!?”

I’m here.

It meant it had already taken advantage of closing hours, answered my call, and was arriving at the rooftop of Segwang City Hall—

W-wait!

“It’s dangerous!”

And if it did that, it might not even be able to return to being a mascot!

Without a manager to operate in its stead like I had, that was practically suicide. Mascots weren’t meant to leave the theme park. That identity was both a shackle and an authority. If it lost that…

“…You might disappear.”

It’s okay.

The current rippled as if patting me.

It’s okay.

“……”

No.

That wasn’t a confident assurance—it was comfort.

And then—

I want to.

A firm declaration that this was the dragon’s own will.

Not just to help me, but because it had its own unresolved cause and effect with Director Cheong.

“……”

If so.

“Then I’ll support you as best as I can, so that doesn’t happen.”

I explained the method.

The current seemed to listen carefully, and beneath it the great dragon agreed.

“…Thank you.”

And then—

Now.

Go.

The current nudged me like a hand.

‘Ah.’

I lost my balance and fell—

Through the exit gate.

***

“Grapes!!”

I opened my eyes.

Drip.

Raindrops fell onto my face.

Just like in my mental world, rain soaked my body.

I reflexively looked up.

The massive eye that had appeared in the sky was gone.

Director Cheong’s eye.

Heavy rainclouds, swollen with water, covered it.

Ah.

“Are you conscious? Hurry!”

Someone pulled me up.

I turned my head—blue jackets, not just a lizard’s face—

Wait.

Agents?

Had they already arrived?

I scrambled to my feet.

Ugh!

My arm was broken. …Coincidentally, the same arm torn in the mental world.

Did I get caught again on the emergency stairs and dragged into the sky… then fall back onto the rooftop?

Gritting my teeth, I looked around.

Among those surrounding me, I saw someone unexpected.

“Agent Chogae?”

“Oh! The agent from the subway!”

The lion mask bobbed.

“Kkangcheol seems weakened by the Azure Dragon’s energy. We must use this chance—”

You.

“……”

From the sky.

A massive, dull voice boomed.

Dodging.

You have a talent for making things troublesome.

I swallowed.

The agents skillfully supported me, moving me back without looking directly at the sky, senses sharpened.

That is not a very obedient attitude.

“Director of the pharmaceutical company, have you ever heard of a Ministry of Employment and Labor audit?”

“Agent Choi!”

“Nice word choice! For a moment I thought I’d been thrown back to the 1980s!”

It seems the soldiers of the state have grown accustomed to speaking carelessly.

Entering where they have no permission.

“Hmm? Since when did the Republic’s special city become Daydream’s corporate town?”

I saw cold sweat trickling down Agent Choi’s neck.

What are they doing?

Now I noticed—the agents were preparing something.

Agent Chogae shook his lion mask as if to draw attention.

Do you think you can rely on these rainclouds?

Waiting for the Dragon King of the East Sea to come with lightning?

“You know your myths well, Kkangcheol! Better run with your tail between your legs—”

Unfortunately, that one has been unemployed for quite some time.

“…What?”

Oh dear. It seems you haven’t heard the news these past few years.

After losing his job, he struggled with applications… until he finally took the position I arranged.

Even if it meant wearing a mascot costume and entertaining in a vulgar trade.

What choice did I have but to help?

Insult after insult poured down.

Agent Chogae froze.

The lion mask trembled.

Though now he won’t be able to leave that tiny amusement park.

He is treated according to his abilities.

“Y-you…!”

Go on. Call him loudly again. Summon the Azure Dragon… what was it, your ceremonial nonsense?

…….

“No need to call.”

I grabbed Agent Chogae’s shoulder.

“Agent.”

Because—

“He is already here. …Below us.”

I pointed down from the rooftop.

Clank.

On the roadside below the old Segwang Special City, a wide fire hydrant burst open in all directions.

And then—

Rumble…

Water surged upward.

“……!!”

From the rooftop, it looked like a painting.

In an instant, it spread along the streets—the water of Blue Dream Water Park unfolding across the ground.

Neatly finished tiles lined the outer edges where the water pooled.

Blue Dream Water Park tiles.

Yes.

There is already a second theme park branch I expanded underground there…

—Since the infrastructure exists, why not temporarily open Blue Dream Water Park next to it? The cost wouldn’t be high.

—Like a pop-up.

And so we did.

Following a proven method, the pool filled the subway maintenance tracks like a sea, overflowing above ground, briefly revealing the dreamlike theme park.

It would vanish once people returned to Segwang and daily life resumed.

A short illusion.

A fragment of Blue Dream Water Park.

But that was enough.

This is…

“Director.”

I spoke.

“He has come for you.”

From within the water of the water park, a massive shape rose.

“…!!”

The current.

Rippling and dancing upward into the air, it did not possess a fixed form.

Yet it projected the symbolic appearance imagined by civilization and story.

The serpentine body of a snake, rabbit’s eyes, ox’s ears, eagle’s claws, tiger’s paws, and—

Deer antlers.

All wrapped in cloud-like fins and winding whiskers as it rose into the sky.

Ascension.

“Kkangcheol… is fire and drought.”

“……”

“Behold the waterway that drives out fire and drought.”

A trembling voice rang out.

“The procession of our lord.”

The dragon’s form encircled City Hall and cast its shadow over the rooftop.

Up close, I could see—

Parts of its form were broken. Missing scales. Pale, blurred claws. Marks of cruel torture, as if flesh had been brined in saltwater.

And yet—

It was overwhelmingly powerful and numinous.

Beautiful.

If I looked too closely, I felt as though I might want to gouge out my eyes and offer them.

“Don’t stare too closely! You might not withstand it!”

At Agent Haegum’s warning, I gasped and averted my gaze.

Even so, its silhouette and presence filled the rooftop with both pressure and strange exhilaration.

The Azure Dragon.

The massive figure flashed within the storm clouds.

Then—

A gaze.

Lightning split the sky.

From beyond the clouds, a faint voice spoke.

In the Five Elements, water overcomes fire…

Fire and drought collapse before the arrival of the East Sea Dragon King who brings rain.

Yes.

And yet—

Did you truly think so?

The enormous voice carried something disturbingly familiar.

A trace of laughter.

You are… terribly old-fashioned.

The sky opened.

A colossal head made entirely of eyes.

Where a head should be, a vast crystalline eyeball shimmered, transparent, filled with dozens of pupils rotating endlessly.

Each pupil a power.

Sulfur. Flood. Hell.

Disaster.

Disaster!

“Not that.”

“Not—not—not—”

A corporate executive’s tone drifted from the heavens.

Yes. No longer that.

This is all self-improvement.

When one holds an executive position for years, must one not evolve?

These are simply the laws of the world.

Did you truly believe everything would remain unchanged?

The rain stopped.

Clouds tore apart.

The dragon-shaped current convulsed, its incomplete form revealed, yet still resisting.

The wicked gaze descended.

Dozens of eyes.

Time flows. Mountains and rivers change.

Did you believe I would remain the same?

I had miscalculated.

Time was on Cheong Dallae’s side. Over years, she had stolen powers from countless urban legends.

Fire and drought were only a fragment now.

But even so—

“Even as time flows and mountains change… some things remain.”

…!

Mist filled the rooftop.

No—clouds.

The dragon’s power shielding us briefly from the profane gaze.

Agent Chogae raised his hand, pointing at the calamity.

“Even after all those years, that being’s nature hasn’t changed. Greedy. Cruel. Harmful to people.”

“……”

“Unchanged.”

I realized it.

“Even in folklore, there’s no error.”

Kkangcheol tales evolved—bringing storms, hail, disasters beyond fire and drought.

“But one thing never changes.”

I saw it clearly.

“It ruins farming.”

That was the core.

“Kkangcheol… is still Kkangcheol.”

“…Yes! That’s it! Still a disaster!”

Agent Chogae looked at me.

“Our ancestors devised ways to confront such supernatural disasters.”

He smiled.

“Do you know the method?”

I did.

“…Driving out Kkangcheol.”

The prototype of rain-invoking rites.

Driving out the calamity that ruins crops.

“That’s right!”

Clapping.

“Do you know how?”

“I do.”

Then hesitated.

“In theory.”

“That’s enough.”

Agent Choi patted my back and took the nearly broken bell from my waist.

“Let’s do it together.”

I lifted my head.

“Let’s make it lively!”

The sharp clang of a small gong rang across the rooftop.

Driving out Kkangcheol.

The rain ritual began.
