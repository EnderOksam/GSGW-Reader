# Chapter 324

I almost stumbled.

I reflexively stiffened and looked back up the stairs of Morning Station, but nothing had changed.

“Ho Yu-won.”

Truly, an agent of Segwang Special City wearing the face of that Daydream director was standing in the old subway space.

Juror No. 1 from the Scale Tribunal.

—That one is an empty shell. The real thing is inside.

—He separated and isolated his distorted desires, warped thoughts, extreme emotions, and all abnormal judgments, handing them over to other personalities within himself.

The juror who had interrogated me.

And so—

—We have verified the validity of the candidate’s testimony.

—We recognize him as a single, complete state that can be weighed on the Scale of Malice.

The someone with Ho Yu-won’s face who had ultimately guided me back into being human.

Though I thought it might be an inappropriate form of address, I opened my mouth anyway…

“…Agent?”

Juror No. 1 looked at me, smiled gently, and disappeared into the shadows inside the waiting hall.

“…!”

I instinctively followed him up into the hall.

But the old waiting hall was dark and completely empty.

[That’s one hell of a poltergeist phenomenon!]

I swallowed.

[Two paths lie before you, Mr. Roe Deer. “To chase, or not to chase—that is the question!”]

“…I have to go.”

[Oh!]

I stepped onto the stairs.

“To end this city’s urban legend, I need to know what kind of legend it is in the first place.”

What exactly is the urban legend that has seized Segwang Special City outside this subway station?

Are there rules, or a method of response  ?

“If anyone would know, it would be a Disaster Management Agency agent—and Ho Yu-won.”

It was practically a direct line of inquiry placed right in front of me.

In the end, I stepped into the waiting hall above the Morning Station stairs.

“……”

Overall, it wasn’t much different from the waiting halls of other stations where the legends had “disappeared,” but it was more devastated.

Meaning there were more traces of people here and there.

“Looks like more people tried to flee here….”

A dry ruin where even filth had dried up completely—an abandoned, desolate cityscape….

And then—

[Look over there, friend  !]

The sign for Exit 2, labeled “Segwang District Court.”

Juror No. 1, wearing Ho Yu-won’s form, stood in front of it.

He was staring at the sign, so I could only see his back.

“Phew.”

“…The Scale Tribunal is gone, but you’re still here.”

Juror No. 1 turned to face me.

“Are the other jurors still here as well?”

He gave an ambiguous smile, frowned as if thinking, then pointed somewhere with his finger.

At Exit 2, its shutter pulled down.

“…Outside?”

“You mean they’re outside this subway station?”

Juror No. 1 slowly nodded.

I sank into an even stranger mood.

“…I know a Catastrophe-Class disaster occurred here years ago. Outside, what exactly is happening right now?”

There was no answer.

“Should I try a different angle?”

I spoke bluntly.

“I met your other self.”

Juror No. 1’s smile dimmed noticeably.

“He desperately wants to reclaim himself, trapped here. That’s why he sent me to Segwang Special City. …Do you feel the same, Juror?”

I pressed again.

“Don’t you want to leave here too, Agent—like your other self?”

……

Juror No. 1 opened his mouth.

A foggy, indistinct voice reached me…

—I am not an agent.

Not an agent?

I unconsciously looked Juror No. 1 over again—from the identical face of Ho Yu-won to the uniform I’d barely scrutinized.

And then I realized.

“Ah.”

What I’d thought was an agent uniform was subtly different in detail from a real one.

What was different was—

“There’s no affiliation.”

Where an insignia should have been was a small, crudely stitched felt badge, like something a child made.

…It depicted a fox.

Something about that ill-fitting detail naturally brought will-o’-the-wisps to mind—

A gentle supernatural being.

“You’re not an agent… You’re a cooperative spirit of the Disaster Management Agency.”

Juror No. 1 quietly looked at me, then spoke.

—I am only a projection left behind here.

—Not the real thing.

And starting from his limbs, his body began to crumble like sand.

“…!”

He looked as though he had no strength left to endure.

Juror No. 1 glanced at his hand, already missing an index finger, smiled bitterly, and spoke again.

With each word, his body collapsed faster.

—There is still time. Go back from here. You don’t need to answer anyone’s demands.

Then he looked at me with faint curiosity and warmth.

—And please continue your counseling regularly.

—That’s what I stayed behind to tell you.

“Wait—”

—Do not go to the opposite platform.

“…!”

With those words, the figure of Ho Yu-won vanished completely.

All that remained was white ash—and an old “agent-uniform-shaped outer garment” lying carelessly on the floor.

“……”

I picked it up.

Just in case, I emptied a bottle of mineral water and gathered as much of the white ash into it as I could.

“…The opposite platform.”

As far as I knew, there was only one meaningful facility over there.

The Cheerful Research Institute.

[Hmm. Final advice at the end of one’s life—emotional, but not necessarily the truth…. I’d like to hear your decision, Mr. Roe Deer.]

I replied calmly.

“Let’s go.”

[As expected!]

“If he truly wanted me not to go, he wouldn’t have given me a hint like ‘the opposite platform.’”

This was, in a way… a warning to steel myself for something brutally difficult ahead.

“Still, be ready to run if things go south.”

Braun, however, seemed excited.

[You understand the flow of the episode, my friend! Truly someone who proves their background through action….]

…That level of enthusiasm is actually kind of scary.

“Phew.”

After storing Juror No. 1’s “agent uniform” and the ash inside my wrist, I moved on.

And on the opposite platform, just like at other stations, I opened the door of the Cheerful Research Institute disguised as a “machine room”—

—and froze.

The Cheerful Research Institute facility at Morning Station was barely burned.

“…!”

There was some scorching, but an office corridor that was almost intact came into view.

Had the sprinklers worked in time? Or was this floor far from where the fire broke out…?

Either way, one thing mattered.

“Clues!”

[Oh, a space where survivors may have remained until the very end!]

I hurriedly opened office doors and searched everywhere.

And then I realized.

“…Braun.”

[My friend calls for Braun.]

“This isn’t an office.”

Almost every room beyond the doors was empty.

No burn marks—yet no desks, PCs, or chairs.

[What does that imply?]

I moved forward.

In the corner of a room lay a round object…

A ball of light-green yarn.

On the band wrapped around it, the gold-stamped logo of the urban legend “Cheerful Research Institute’s Toymaker” glittered.

“This floor was probably used as a warehouse by the Cheerful Research Institute.”

[Hoho.]

Boxes can be moved—that’s why there were no traces.

When the fire broke out, they must have used items to move everything stored here out at once.

“And when those items spread into the world… various urban legends emerged….”

Thinking that way, everything lined up.

I checked a few more places.

All empty.

[What a shame. It would’ve been fun if this were a treasure hunt.]

But Braun’s disappointment was premature.

I found something in the room right in front of the emergency exit.

[Hmm. An ordinary desk.]

It was the only desk.

“Probably for a warehouse manager.”

But what mattered came next.

On the bookshelf lay a USB drive.

It bore the Cheerful Research Institute’s logo—a comical red rabbit.

“Of course.”

I grabbed it immediately.

I could check it right here.

“I brought a laptop.”

[Oh!]

Exploration gear based on past experience.

I’d planned to give it to the researchers, but using it here was fine.

I quickly pulled the laptop from my inventory tattoo, booted it up, and plugged in the USB.

As expected, there was no Wi-Fi—but the USB read successfully, appearing as a removable disk…

Password: ___________

Ah.

[Oh dear, the road is blocked. How are you supposed to find a password in a research institute that left no traces at all?]

“……”

[ Friend  ?]

I read the text displayed as the password hint.

Hint: Our first beginning

Password: ___________

[Surely they didn’t do something as stupid as setting the founding year as the password?]

Normally I’d have thought the same and even choked on disbelief—but my eyes were elsewhere.

The number of slots in the password field.

Password: ___________

“…Eleven characters.”

Alphabetic or numeric—maybe symbols—but that made a date unlikely.

“The Cheerful Research Institute’s first beginning….”

Their first invention?

Trying to recall their first product from darkness-exploration records was difficult. Surely it wasn’t the order of urban-legend registration.

“Do I have to list all their products?”

Most had long, flamboyant names—hard to compress into eleven letters…

……

…!

[Oh, my friend has started typing.]

There is one.

Eleven characters.

Something they used even to summon me here.

Among their inventions, one I still keep close to this day.

Password: ckrgksclsrn

( “  착한친구  ,” “Good Friend”  )

I pressed Enter.

The lock released, and the disk opened.

“I cracked it.”

Shock raced through me, and then I hurriedly checked the files.

[Last Message.txt]

A Notepad file. When I double-clicked it, I saw fragments—hurried notes, not even full sentences.

Fire outbreak

Evacuating to subway station

Valuables -> fire hydrant

Fire hydrant.

I instinctively looked up, ran into the corridor, and opened the fire hydrant there.

Feeling around inside, I tore it open—and found a safe hidden in a concealed compartment.

“…!”

[You’ve found treasure. Congratulations!]

I shoved the entire thing into my wrist inventory. …It felt like capacity was getting tight.

“Still—jackpot.”

Valuables from the Cheerful Research Institute’s warehouse.

Wiping sweat, slightly exhilarated, I continued reading the memo.

Confirm…: gov… response plan (urgent)

Which meant—

……

……

……

……

……

Ah.

“Haegum-unnie, I’m heading out now~”

“Yeah, yeah.”

Agent Haegum yawned and chuckled as she watched her team member leave the container.

A recently reinstated member—someone she’d recruited to fill Go-myeong’s vacancy.

No matter what, it never quite felt normal.

“Phew.”

At least there was hope Go-myeong might return someday—that helped.

Haegum was polishing her sword, getting ready to clock out—

“Agent.”

**Bang.**

The container door flew open, and someone stood there staring at her.

A pale-faced person.

Agent Grapes, Kim Soleum.

“You—!”

Haegum brightened for a split second, then her expression hardened as she recalled his situation.

And the lack of any reason for him to suddenly appear.

“…Are you really okay?”

“Yes.”

The answer was firm, but his complexion wasn’t.

“There’s been no new anomaly with the agents.”

“Alright. Sit down first.”

Following her guidance—half invitation, half insistence—Agent Grapes dropped into a chair and spoke immediately.

“I came because I have something to tell you about the Special City.”

“Yeah?”

Though she answered casually, Haegum was puzzled.

“Why come to me?”

Wasn’t he closer to the two from Black Tortoise Team 1?

Even Haegum herself had tangled with Kim Soleum often enough to feel some attachment—but since he was from another team, a junior would naturally keep distance.

Especially given his spy background.

Still, if he came, there had to be a reason.

Haegum waited.

After a long silence, Agent Grapes spoke.

“The sealed Special City.”

“Alright. Did you find something new?”

“…About how it was sealed.”

The method?

“The Agency erased the city’s very existence, didn’t it?”

“From what we can tell, yes.”

Cognitive erasure.

Making people no longer recognize or remember the city’s existence.

“Do you know what kind of ritual was used for that seal?”

“…Roughly.”

Haegum’s gaze darkened, then deepened.

“But it’s not something I can easily talk about. It’s not even my specialty.”

That was more in Choi’s wheelhouse.

And if he had any sense, he wouldn’t babble about it either.

“Those rituals usually demand a huge price and are extremely dangerous. People don’t mention them lightly.”

“I know.”

Agent Grapes’s face remained pale.

“Then… have you ever thought about how the price for sealing the Special City was paid?”

Haegum suppressed a sigh.

“At the very least, they would’ve tried to avoid harming innocent civilians. Maybe not in a clean way—something like the Selection Scale—but still.”

“…….”

“…Agent Grapes?”

“Agent.”

Kim Soleum lifted his head.

Recalling the final contents of the USB he’d read.

**A city-wide occult anomaly**

**Government?**

**A massive human-sacrifice ritual??**

**At this rate, everyone dies—we must leave at all costs—**

“I think… to seal it, the Agency offered all the citizens of Segwang Special City as human sacrifices….”

“……!!”
