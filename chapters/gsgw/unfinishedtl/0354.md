# Chapter 353

*It was past midnight.*

*Only two days remained until the day of the disaster.*

*Agent Choi and I sat inside the hideout beside Agent Bronze, who was still asleep, each of us organizing information in silence.*

*Nothing had changed from a few days ago, when I first woke up.*

*The silence stretched.*

*—The blockade will be lifted that evening, around the hour of the dog… If we want to act before then, the morning of that day is the absolute limit.*

*There really wasn’t much time left.*

*Which meant we couldn’t keep going like this.*

*I let out a breath and put down what I’d been looking at.*

*“Agent.”*

*“……”*

*“What are you hiding?”*

*“Hiding what?”*

*“I know there’s something you’re not telling me. …We can’t afford to spend time like this.”*

*I looked at him.*

*“This is an extinction-class supernatural disaster. Even if we share everything we know and plan together, there’s no guarantee the attempt will succeed.”*

*“……”*

*“And on top of that, the termination method I’m proposing hasn’t been definitively proven either. There will be gaps. If the people entering the disaster aren’t perfectly in sync, that’s dangerous.”*

*“…Yeah. You understand that well.”*

*Without lifting his eyes from what he was reading, Agent Choi spoke casually.*

*“Grapes.”*

*“……”*

*“The method you’re trying is nonsense.”*

*…….*

*“What?”*

*“I said it’s nonsense.”*

Thud.

Agent Choi dropped the researchers’ Potion Manufacturing Device Repair Log *onto the floor.*

*“I memorized all of it. I also talked to the researchers you siphoned off.”*

*“Th-that’s—”*

*“But no matter how I look at it, there’s no line of reasoning that shows this device can terminate a Special City–level supernatural disaster.”*

*“……”*

*“You knew it, too, didn’t you? Your plan isn’t just flawed—it’s a gamble.”*

*“It’s not that bad.”*

*“Oh yes, it is. …You’re really putting that much faith in the note Lee Kangheon left behind?”*

*“……”*

*“That wasn’t a clue you uncovered through supernatural analysis. It’s just a note someone left. And you’re trusting it?”*

*“I thought the circumstances made it worth trying.”*

*“No.”*

*Agent Choi’s eyes, sunken, fixed on me.*

*“This isn’t ‘trying.’ This is experimentation.”*

*“…!”*

*“ ‘Maybe it’s real, so I’ll risk my life and try it alone.’ That’s what this is, isn’t it?”*

*“Agent, you too—”*

*I forced my anger down as I spoke.*

*“You’ve tried escape methods based on small clues before, haven’t you? While rescuing people.”*

*“Of course I have. But willingly walking into a death trap is different! I don’t do that.”*

*“…….”*

*No—you do.*

*If the flow hadn’t changed when I cleared Lookie Mart, you would’ve gone up to the fourth floor and disappeared.*

*But I couldn’t say that. The words jammed in my throat.*

*And then—*

*“I’m not walking into a death trap.”*

*“Yeah? Then let me ask you. You said people go insane when they hear that music box—so how were you planning to prevent that? You didn’t seem to be gathering any counter-items.”*

*“I told you. For some reason, I’m not strongly affected by that mental contamination—”*

*“…….”*

*“Agent.”*

*I realized it then.*

*“You never believed me in the first place.”*

*—So if we manage to terminate the disaster right before the blockade is lifted… there’s a chance the citizens could survive.*

*That hadn’t been agreement.*

*From the beginning, he never intended to cooperate with my attempt.*

*He didn’t believe what I was saying.*

*“Suggesting we try termination… was just a lie to reassure me.”*

*“It wasn’t a lie.”*

*Agent Choi stood up.*

*“I was planning to try a different method.”*

*“What method? If you had one, shouldn’t you have told me from the start—”*

*Ah.*

*“You never planned to act together with me.”*

*“……”*

*“Thinking about it, it’d be easy. You tell me we’re entering the Special City, then just put me to sleep instead of letting me enter.”*

*“Grapes.”*

*“That’s why you’ve gone along with everything I wanted until now, letting me think I was in control.”*

*“……”*

*“A different method? If you try instead of me, what changes?”*

*“It changes everything.”*

*Agent Choi looked at me with a tired face, rubbed his face, then stood in front of me.*

*And in a calm voice, he said, “…Soleum. At the very least, I won’t try a method that might be a trap. I’ll choose one where the outcome can be predicted as much as possible.”*

*“I’ve already neutralized the trap.”*

*“And if there’s another one? What if the note itself is the trap? You think no one else would’ve thought of what you did?”*

*“Even then, we agreed it wouldn’t make things worse!”*

*“Right! Even if it’s a trap, it won’t make things worse—except for the person who dies or ends up worse than dead!”*

*“Stop!!”*

*Both Agent Choi and I turned away, breathing hard.*

*Agent Haegum, who had just entered the hideout, shouted with a stiff expression.*

*“There are only two days left before this country might collapse, and you’re fighting? Do you really have time to spare?”*

*“…The country won’t collapse. Disaster Management will reinstate the blockade.”*

*The words spilled out of me like vomit.*

*“But Segwang Special City will stay trapped in an urban legend where citizens die over and over every day. That’s—”*

*“Agent Grapes.”*

*Agent Haegum looked straight at me.*

*In her eyes was calm concern.*

*“Where is that impatience coming from?”*

*“……”*

*“If you’d heard that story from Yeongeun, I’d understand. But you don’t have any special ties to that city.”*

*“……”*

*“Could it be… that emotion came from the director of the Fox Counseling Office? The so-called spiritual being of the Special City?”*

*I hesitated.*

*“…I may have been influenced. But that’s not all.”*

*Keeping things vague was becoming unbearable.*

*…Maybe it was better to be honest.*

*I’d never done that before. Maybe if I laid everything out now, something would change.*

*After hesitating, I finally spoke, almost retching the words out.*

*“I… want to terminate this darkness—no, this disaster.”*

*“There must be a personal reason. Why?”*

*“…….”*

*Ha.*

*“I need dream-liquid.”*

*I clenched both fists and lowered my gaze.*

*Maybe this time—*

*“The solution used to create wish-granting rights in Daydream. I’ve… really, this time… found a way to use it properly.”*

*“……”*

“Not the dangerous Wish Tickets the company hands out. The original version. The one Cheerful Research Institute originally developed. If you consider the organization’s purpose, it really will *grant a wish—”*

*“……Agent Grapes.”*

*“It’s true. The Wish Tickets produced in Daydream were distorted. This time, it’ll be the real thing.”*

*And then Agent Haegum—*

*patted my back.*

*Like she was calming someone who wasn’t in their right mind.*

*…….*

*“You don’t believe me.”*

*I sank.*

*“I figured. That’s why I didn’t say anything. But—”*

*“Kim Soleum.”*

*Agent Choi sat down in front of me.*

*And in a firm, pleading voice, he said, “You can’t go home.”*

*“…….”*

*Calm down.*

*“You’ve experienced it, too. Don’t believe talk about returning to the past. Okay? But—”*

*He didn’t understand.*

*Of course, he didn’t. No rational person would believe I truly had a home in another world. I knew that. And he kept talking—*

The living have to live on.

Don’t cling to the past. Move forward.

*I knew it was meant as advice, as tough love.*

*I knew that.*

*But—*

*…….*

What do you *know?*

*“…!”*

*I barely stopped myself from grabbing Agent Choi by the collar.*

*But the words were already out.*

*“I can go back! I’m not from this world!”*

*“What?”*

*“This whole situation—this place itself—is an urban legend to me, a supernatural disaster! I—”*

*“…….”*

*“…….”*

*“What… do you mean by that?”*

*Ah.*

*I stepped back.*

*“I’m sorry.”*

*“Grapes—Kim Soleum…!”*

*I backed away, left the hideout, and ran, searching for a door.*

*“Kim Soleum!”*

*I hung a nameplate on what could barely be called a door—an abandoned shipping container—and crossed into the Fox Counseling Office—*

*Then stopped.*

*“…….”*

*This wouldn’t do.*

*Don’t get overwhelmed and run away again. Avoidance only leads to more avoidance.*

*…….*

*Right.*

*“Agent.”*

*I turned back, head bowed, took a deep breath, and spoke.*

*“I know it must sound frustrating to you. I know what I’m saying doesn’t make sense.”*

*I saw Agent Choi stop.*

*“And if you already have a more ‘reliable’ method in mind, I know my attempt might look like smashing a rock with an egg.”*

*“……”*

*“But honestly, I also know that your so-called ‘reliable method’ is a crazy one where you shoulder everything alone.”*

*“You—”*

*“So let’s make it fair.”*

*I looked back at them.*

*“I’m going to do my absolute best, too.”*

*“…….”*

*“If you decide you want to cooperate… then please come to the counseling office.”*

*I opened the door and crossed over.*

Click.

*“…….”*

*“…Sir Roe Deer?”*

*I let out a deep sigh.*

*Then I sat down on the counseling room floor and pulled the Dream Collector out of my tattoo.*

*It was intact.*

I can collect.

*…How would Agent Choi respond?*

*Even if he didn’t cooperate, there were alternatives.*

I can proceed as planned.

*Entry, movement, backup—this counseling office could handle all of that.*

*“Hoo.”*

*I looked up at Ho Yuwon, sitting at the waiting room table.*

*“What did you talk about with Agent Choi yesterday?”*

*“Huh?”*

*“Did he ask you to put me to sleep secretly instead of letting me enter the Special City when my turn comes?”*

*“…….”*

*He answered.*

*“Yes.”*

*…….*

*“He said he wanted to try a more humane and safer way to block the disaster while saving the citizens of Segwang Special City.”*

*As expected.*

He planned to interfere with the sealing ritual…!

*He was preparing to intervene in the Disaster Management Bureau’s sealing ritual that would restart as soon as the blockade was lifted.*

*That explained why he’d been tracking the ritual site nonstop these past days.*

*And of course, it wouldn’t be a normal method.*

How could one person possibly bear a seal maintained by sacrificing hundreds of thousands—maybe millions—of people every day?

*…and my approach—trying to terminate the disaster before the blockade lifted—had just been something he pretended to support so he could stop me.*

…….

*Did Agent Haegum know too?*

*My head went cold.*

*My mouth dried from anxiety.*

*“So… he said he’d stop my entry and just put me to sleep?”*

*“He said he’d think about it.”*

*“…….”*

*“But since time is short, he said he’d need to decide today or tomorrow.”*

*A subtle smile appeared on Ho Yuwon’s face.*

*“He really doesn’t like me, but he asked quite earnestly.”*

*“…I see.”*

*Between the shock, something faint and aching welled up and vanished.*

*I knew he was acting out of concern for me.*

Ha.

*If so, maybe it was better that things turned out like this before it went that far.*

At least my head is clearer now.

*And, honestly, I knew some of what Agent Choi said made sense.*

… I barely considered the possibility of failure.

*I’d only thought about how to make this method succeed.*

*Thinking about failure only made me anxious—and there was no alternative anyway.*

*Right now, I was fixated on a single possibility.*

*Because that outcome was so ideal, I’d been interpreting everything in a biased way to support it.*

*…But if I stepped back—*

*The thing that bothered me the most was this.*

*“Counselor Ho Yuwon.”*

*“Yes?”*

*“Why hasn’t Director Cheong stormed in here and attacked us already?”*

*This.*

*She had nearly captured me when I escaped Segwang Special City.*

*Which meant she had a rough idea of how much I’d figured out.*

*Given her personality, would she really just set a trap with the music box in the potion device and leave me alone?*

*Was that reasonable?*

*“If my plan really works, wouldn’t she try to shut the whole project down—even destroy this counseling office?”*

*But Ho Yuwon, who’d looked slightly surprised, thought for a moment and shook his head.*

*“No. I don’t think so.”*

*“Why?”*

*“Well… because I’m a director of Daydream, and this project falls under my jurisdiction.”*

*……!*

*“I doubt Director Cheong respects you just because you’re also a director.”*

*“True. It’s more about following the pharmaceutical company’s rules.”*

*A faintly sinister glint crossed Ho Yuwon’s eyes.*

*“A director can’t attack another director—especially one managing an active, productive project—without justification.”*

*“…….”*

*“And it would be even worse for an executive to sabotage their own company’s functioning department with brute force.”*

*“…So she can attack through corporate politics, but not personally?”*

*“Exactly.”*

*I realized something.*

*Director Cheong had attacked Ho Yuwon through performance pressure while he was excavating Segwang Special City—but she’d never tried to kill him outright.*

*Likewise, if she really wanted me back under her direct control, she could’ve tried to abduct me physically while I was out on project business.*

*But she hadn’t.*

*“If there’s one thing that being truly reckless, indulgent, and cruel still prioritizes…”*

*It was—*

*“Daydream Corporation itself.”*

*Director Cheong always talked about “my company.”*

*“…It might be the only rule she actually follows.”*

*“Possibly.”*

*I nodded, feeling strange.*

*…My mind settled.*

She probably has more prepared moves.

*And most importantly, my employment contract was still in her hands.*

*But I’d prepared contingencies at the resort for the worst case.*

*I wouldn’t be completely helpless.*

*And I absolutely wouldn’t let Agent Choi interfere with the sealing ritual.*

If he does, he’ll be the one who dies.

*Or worse.*

*Trying to shoulder that kind of burden—just thinking about it made my spine chill.*

*Trying my method first, where even the executor might survive, was the more rational choice.*

*Still, I didn’t know if that would convince him.*

*And remembering the things I’d blurted out about myself made a low groan escape me.*

*“…….”*

*“Sir Roe Deer. Why don’t you get some sleep?”*

*“What?”*

*“You haven’t slept properly for about two days. At this rate, you’ll die on the street from a stupid mistake before you even attempt termination.”*

*“…….”*

*“Shouldn’t you rest first? You’ll need a clear head to make decisions.”*

*“…What if the agents move me while I’m asleep?”*

*“That’s a hurtful thing to say.”*

*He blinked exaggeratedly, almost obnoxiously.*

*“You can just ask me not to let that happen. I’m also your employee, remember?”*

*Ah.*

*“You remember, right? I signed a contract, too.”*

*I did.*

*“I promise. I won’t hand over your identity or interfere with your sleep without consent.”*

*“…All right.”*

*I finally nodded.*

*I went into the long-term inpatient room and lay down.*

*“Good night, Sir Roe Deer.”*

*I fell asleep.*

*…and when I opened my eyes again, I saw someone standing by my bed, looking down at me.*

*“Hellooo!”*

*A familiar face I hadn’t seen in a long time.*

*I spoke in a hoarse voice.*

*“…Miss Dolphin.”*

*“It’s been sooo long, right?”*

*She smiled brightly, her curly white hair sparkling in the sunlight filtering through the counseling room curtains.*

*“I heard you’re entering the darkness to terminate it this time!”*

*“…….”*

*“I’ll support you all the way.”*

*I nodded and shook her hand.*

*“Thank you.”*

*…and until the moment I re-entered the Segwang Special City subway—*

Agent Choi never came to the counseling office.
