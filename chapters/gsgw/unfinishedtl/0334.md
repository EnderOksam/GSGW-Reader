# Chapter 333

Today, Sekwang Special City had exceptionally good weather.

“Whew.”

Stepping outside Hanbit Department Store, I took a deep breath in and let it out.

There was no yellow dust, and it wasn’t as hot as expected—completely pleasant.

The people walking around nearby all seemed cheerful, too. I smiled as I walked along the sidewalk, dodging a broadcasting station vehicle driving past.

“Why do I feel so good today?”

My mind felt calm, like I’d just returned home after a long trip and finally gotten some proper rest.

Maybe it was because I’d just come out of a department store.

Or maybe because the air here was cleaner than Seoul’s...

“Hm. Maybe it’s because it’s the day before Children’s Day.”

I checked the date on my smartphone.

Monday, May 4

It was Monday after the weekend, but knowing I could rest again tomorrow made me happy.

Maybe that was why I felt so good.

...Even though I still had to go to work now.

“Business lunch...”

Well, come to think of it, it was better than a dinner engagement. Ugh.

But then—why did I take a day off?

It wasn’t like I was taking a long break starting from the weekend. Using leave just to visit a department store felt irrational no matter how I thought about it...

“......”

Doesn’t feel that important!

The meeting probably got scheduled after I’d already planned my day off.

Yeah. What matters is what I need to do now.

“Let’s go entertain clients...”

Holding back a sigh, I started walking.

I had some time, and the weather was nice, so instead of taking the subway, I decided to walk—to the lunch meeting spot.

“Mr. Kim Soleum, I always say this, but you’re really a decent person.”

“Haha, no, Section Chief Park, it’s just that you’re kind enough to see me in a good light.”

The business lunch itself wasn’t particularly difficult.

Since it wasn’t a dinner meeting, there was no drinking involved, which made it easier, and we were already acquainted.

No matter where you went, platforms always had the upper hand, but when company sizes weren’t too mismatched, things stayed polite enough.

Moments like this made me grateful I’d landed a job at a major corporation.

That didn’t mean the other party wasn’t still in a superior position, though.

“So even if you study hard and get into a good place, you still end up doing such unproductive things. This country, really—”

“Haha...”

Making money isn’t easy...

He probably just liked talking to polite young people, which was why someone as junior as me got sent out.

“Just don’t step on a landmine... whew.”

I desperately steered the conversation into softer, more personal topics.

Thankfully, once we finished eating and stepped outside, we found something else to talk about.

“My kid goes to school here.”

“Oh, is your child’s school right there?”

I smiled as I looked at the high school building he pointed to.

Sekwang Technical High School.

It seemed close to a Meister high school—one that received extensive city-level support for technical education.

Then his finger pointed past the school, toward a massive glass building.

“You see that? That’s a library. I think it really helps the academic atmosphere, so it makes me happy.”

“An environment for studying really matters. You sent them somewhere good.”

When superiors or important people brag about their kids, you nod enthusiastically.

I reacted with appropriate enthusiasm while looking at the enormous library beyond the school.

Usually, after sweating through that kind of response, I’d get something back—

— **Excellent response, my friend!**

“......?”

Had someone said something like that?

It felt like a pleasant voice I should remember. One I’d heard often, somewhere...

“...Maybe on TV?”

“......”

“Mr. Kim Soleum?”

“...Yes! Section Chief!”

Oops—business meeting.

I quickly dropped the thought and smiled. I could look it up later.

“So your child must be in class right now.”

“That’s right! The school’s quiet as a mouse—proper atmosphere, don’t you think?”

“Haha.”

That was when it happened.

Someone burst out of the school’s front gate, running.

“Hm?”

The person rushing out wore neat casual clothes, not a uniform.

“Good-looking. A student teacher?”

“That could be.”

But as the person ran past on the other side of the crosswalk, I caught a glimpse of a cute name tag on their chest:

‘ Counseling Teacher’

Probably something a student had made.

I’d heard that schools these days had professional counselors—he must be one of them.

“Taking the subway, huh. No car?”

“Haha...”

He really liked talking about other people.

“Oh. That taxi—I called it. Well then, I’ll head off now, Mr. Soleum.”

“Yes! Take care!”

I saw him off as he got into the taxi.

As I did, I happened to glance again toward the subway station, where the counselor was still running in the same direction.

...He looked familiar.

I felt like I’d seen that back somewhere before—

A sharp pain jolted through my hand.

“...!”

Startled, I pulled my hand out of my pocket.

“Static electricity?”

But it hurt too much for that.

I pulled out the only metal object I had.

A silver coin.

At some point, it had become blackened and scorched.

“What is this?”

I rubbed it with my fingers, but it didn’t help.

“...Is it made of something bad?”

I considered throwing it away. Just then, the silver surface reflected sunlight.

It was blinding.

I reflexively lifted my head—and saw sunlight reflecting off a window.

The windows of Sekwang Technical High School.

“......”

Feeling strange, I walked closer to the school.

As if possessed, I approached the wall.

“Hey!”

I looked up.

From an outer window on the third floor, someone was calling out to me.

A student with dyed hair and piercings, wearing a loosely worn uniform, waved down at me.

What?

“Down there!”

I looked down.

There was a note on the ground near the wall.

It looked like the student had dropped it by accident.

“Could you pick it up? Just crumple it and throw it back.”

“Oh, sure.”

Probably a note passed between friends during class. Before tossing it back, I casually read it.

**Just stay here.**

“......”

I folded it very small so it wouldn’t drift, then tossed it back through the window.

“Thank you.”

Despite his appearance, the student bowed politely, snatched the note, and disappeared.

I stood there blankly in front of the school wall until I finished my Americano.

I felt strange.

I went home.

“Whew!”

My studio apartment was a clean two-room layout—living room and bedroom neatly divided.

Even after taking the bus home, I still had time.

“Let’s enjoy my day off.”

I ordered chicken, sat by the living room window, cracked it open slightly, and enjoyed the familiar comfort.

“Ha.”

The breeze was cool.

“Living alone isn’t so bad.”

My parents lived in Bundang, and my younger sibling ran a dessert shop nearby that was famous locally.

I lived alone in Seoul—

...No, I’d been assigned to Sekwang Special City  , living here.

“Well, I visit on weekends.”

Sometimes when my sibling got busy with trendy desserts, I’d get drafted as extra labor.

— **Wow, Kim Soleum, you’re really good at this. Just quit your company job and come work here.**

And when I smacked them, they’d yell **“Canceled! Canceled! Hyung!”** Feels like yesterday.

Come to think of it—it really was yesterday. I definitely went down over the weekend.

“It’s spacious. Easier to breathe.”

Now that I thought about it, my apartment—

Sekwang wasn’t as insane as Seoul when it came to housing prices. I could’ve rented something bigger. Why did I choose this place?

...Probably to save money.

Shrugging, I took off my suit jacket and reached for the shower—

My hand brushed against something soft.

“......”

I reached into my pocket and pulled it out.

A pink rabbit plush.

The silver coin was one thing, but I had no idea where **this** came from.

If I had to guess, I probably bought it at the department store.

“Why did I even go there today and take leave?”

No idea. Impulse?

The plush looked very high-end—soft, delicate, almost unsettlingly detailed, yet strangely endearing.

I turned it over.

On the tag were ornate golden letters I couldn’t read—not Korean.

I pressed the rabbit’s belly.

No sound.

“...Seriously.”

What was I expecting? Squeeze toys with sounds went out of fashion ages ago.

“Sorry. That was stupid.”

I pressed its nose. Smooth.

After some thought, I washed my hands again, made a small spot on the side table with a cushion and blanket, and placed the rabbit there.

Under the soft lamp light, its black nose gleamed.

“...Comfortable?”

Of course, there was no answer.

Feeling oddly awkward, I fled to the shower.

After washing up and receiving my chicken, I sat in front of the TV—

Well, more like an OTT streaming service.

“Alright.”

It was a mystery thriller.

As long as I skipped the more brutal scenes, it was watchable. No ghosts, after all.

But strangely, it felt harder to watch than before.

Why?

Every time limbs were severed, or someone died in another’s place, my mood soured rapidly.

They weren’t even showing the cuts directly.

“What’s wrong with me?”

“I thought I felt great today.”

Maybe the client meeting drained me and canceled out the good weather.

I turned off the service.

Then, earlier than usual, I lay in bed and turned on my phone.

“......”

There was something I usually watched at times like this... what was it?

“Shorts?”

But it didn’t feel familiar.

I scrolled aimlessly, felt no comfort or dopamine, and turned the phone off.

Then I turned my head—and froze.

A giant human-shaped black shadow loomed on the wall.

“Ah!”

...But it was just the rabbit plush’s shadow on the side table.

“Ha.”

Embarrassing—no one else here.

Calming my pounding heart, I looked at the shadow again.

Streetlights and headlights from outside made it flicker, almost as if it were moving—

But that was all.

The doll didn’t move.

Of course not.

“......”

Let’s sleep.

I shrugged, buried my head in the pillow, oddly chewing on a lingering sense of regret—

And fell asleep.

May 4 ended.

“......”

I woke up in the first-floor lobby of a department store.

“Hm?”

I must’ve dozed off while waiting for something.

What... was I waiting for?

“Don’t know.”

Not important. Let’s go home.

“Whew.”

I put my smartphone in my pocket and stood up.

Something dropped from my front pocket.

“...A doll?”

A rabbit plush.

Did I buy this?

Digging through my pocket, I found—besides my phone—a scorched silver, button-like coin.

“...Merch?”

Did I buy this, too?

But it looked too worn for that.

“Don’t really know.”

I shrugged and put the coin back.

I must’ve gotten it somewhere.

Holding the plush, I headed toward the lobby doors—

Out of Hanbit Department Store,

into Sekwang Special City.
