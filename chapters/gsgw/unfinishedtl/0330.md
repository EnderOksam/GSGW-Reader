# Chapter 329

A suffocating silence fell.

In front of me stood the lizard.

Someone who thought of me as a front man for a cult... well, given the situation, that wasn’t exactly wrong.

“......”

“......”

This won’t do!

“Hyung~”

I broke into a wide smile and, once again playing the shameless sycophant, rubbed my hands together as I spoke.

“I’m sorry. You just look so much like our Section Chief... would it be okay if I call you ‘Chief’? Nice to meet you. Since we’ve crossed paths like this, you said you were curious about the Being of Stuffed Cotton?”

“No—”

“What a wonderful thought! This way, please!”

And like some street-snatching cult recruiter, I dragged the lizard off between Car 2 and Car 3—

[Friend!]

Aaaah!

“Wait, wait a second...!”

I pretended to enshrine Braun in the velvet case, swapped him with another doll, and slipped the real “Good Friend” into my pocket.

[My stand-in, you say? Oh, I hope that cotton doll enjoys this excessive honor to the fullest!]

Phew.

Once we were more than one car away from Car 1, the dense crowd thinned out. Occasionally, shelter residents who couldn’t sleep and were debating whether to join the dopamine party in the front cars glanced at us curiously, but once we slipped into the space between cars, they didn’t follow and disappeared.

**Clatter.**

The moment I entered the narrow passage between Cars 2 and 3 and shut the door, I collapsed into a seat.

“...Chief.”

I’m exhausted...

“Mr. Grapes.”

“When it’s just us, you can call me Roe Deer...”

Let’s please stop with the Grapeseed-oil-sounding nickname...

“Yes.”

Anyway, embarrassment aside, it was good to see him.

“Did you enter Sekwang City this time? I mean—uh—what I’m asking is why you entered.”

“Yes.”

And he explained.

In short, after I’d been stuck on this train for several days without coming out, Director Ho apparently went wild and decided another team needed to be sent in to extract information.

“I was instructed to confirm what the previous entry team was doing in the exploration zone and report back.”

Mm. That made sense.

It was rare for someone to stay inside Sekwang Special City this long. Of course they’d be curious.

“So you came straight to the train?”

“No. I first visited Midnight Station to assess the situation.”

Ah!

“You went to the Body Casino.”

Come to think of it, Officer Haegum said she’d contact me once things were wrapped up—hadn’t heard yet.

“Were there any anomalies?”

“Yes. The Body Casino has suspended operations.”

“...!”

Jackpot.

At the same time, a voice in my head shouted, **I knew it! Of course!**

‘From the moment Officer Haegum confidently requested two people to take with her, I knew something was up.’

As expected, entrusting the casino to her was the right call! I smiled triumphantly and prepared to listen carefully.

I figured they’d cleaned the place out so thoroughly the casino couldn’t take it and shut itself down—

“They received an order to suspend operations.”

......?

“Who... issued that order?”

“I don’t know.”

I nearly went speechless.

“Could you explain the circumstances in more detail, so we can make an inference?”

“Yes. The three agents deployed entered the Body Casino and, acting separately, continued gambling for half a day.”

Chief Lee Jaheon continued in his flat, uninflected tone, but the content itself sounded like a deranged gambling-movie script.

“They were caught trying to tamper with a slot machine?”

“One of them stabbed another player’s hand with a knife while watching a dealer and a guest play?”

“...They encouraged gambling addicts to bet coins on who would win or lose, like sports betting, and skimmed a commission... is that what you’re saying?”

“...And when an addict shoved them and tried to take over the table, claiming he had ‘good luck’—yes, I understand. The survival status of that addict is unknown...”

I could feel the subject of those sentences.

‘Kang Yihak... Deputy Jin Nasol...’

And then it clicked.

Officer Haegum...!

‘She brought the others along to draw aggro, didn’t she?’

A money-crazed person and someone utterly devoid of mercy—both perfect catalysts for explosive interactions with a casino.

So what had Officer Haegum been aiming for amid that chaos...?

The lizard chief promptly supplied the answer.

“At the same time, the team leader discovered blueprints inside the casino.”

Blueprints?

“They refer to the layout of the casino that operated near that station before it turned into darkness.”

...!

—The ■■ City Royal Casino at Midnight Station is no longer for foreigners only. Any passenger may enjoy the machines at any time.

That was the phrasing, wasn’t it?

‘Then that’s definitely a clue.’

Extracting information directly from the origin of the urban legend.

“Were they trying to use the old blueprints to locate a hidden area in the Body Casino?”

“Yes.”

Oh!

“Then it seems you found a breakthrough through that hidden space.”

Chief Lee replied expressionlessly.

“They used it as evidence to report illegal operations.”

“......”

“Operating a VIP-only secret gambling area in an unregistered space is illegal under current law.”

Ah.

“The exploration agent emphasized that, since the casino is located within a national facility, these violations warranted a shutdown, presented their public servant ID, and stated that an order of suspension was possible.”

Officer Haegum...!

‘So that’s where she unleashed her gambler instincts...!’

And it worked beautifully.

“The entities appearing to be casino dealers reacted with confusion, then expelled everyone and closed the casino.”

“......”

“Afterward, I personally confirmed a sign reading ‘Under Renovation’ posted at the casino entrance.”

They shut down to avoid an audit...

A strategy that pierced straight through the rule-bound nature of urban legends.

‘And that’s such a civil-servant way of thinking...’

I stared, mouth agape, in awe. This was the kind of exploration that would be recorded as an irregular in the Dark Exploration logs.

Wait.

“So she’s the one who issued the suspension.”

“Yes.”

“Then why did you say you don’t know who did it?”

“We were unable to confirm the team leader’s precise identity.”

Ah......

I nodded, savoring the familiar lizard flavor of that answer.

“?”

In any case, the exploration members all exited the casino safely, but Officer Haegum stayed behind at the station to observe whether any further anomalies appeared.

‘...She was probably planning to call me only after confirming it was completely secure.’

And likely to buy more time to explore Sekwang City’s underground in greater depth.

After about two days of observation, she left, asking Chief Lee—who appeared at the station—to pass along her message.

Thus, the entire team returned to reality.

“Among them, Assistant Manager Pony is currently hospitalized in the Fox Counseling Ward receiving contamination treatment.”

“......”

That person seems to undergo contamination treatment every time money is involved... must be my imagination......

Anyway, that meant Midnight Station—the former location of the Body Casino—was now empty.

‘I need to go there soon.’

Relief settled into my chest as things fell into place. I nodded sincerely.

“Thank you for telling me.”

“Yes.”

Just as the mood began to warm—

“Roe Deer.”

“Yes?”

Chief Lee was staring at the pink rabbit-ear headband that had slipped halfway out of my pocket when I sat down.

Cold sweat beaded.

“The ‘perfect rabbit doll’ the protesters on the train are talking about... is that this?”

Well... more or less...

[Oh, friend. If the story’s finished, shall we return to the stage? Surely the audience is waiting for us.]

[And please don’t allow this crude knife-wielder a chance to touch my cotton-filled body. His gaze is lecherous.]

But instead of grabbing the rabbit ears, Chief Lee said flatly,

“Evacuate.”

“What?”

“The current train situation is dangerous. Evacuate outside first, then re-enter.”

“Wait.”

I pressed my fingers to my forehead.

“It looks dangerous because it’s noisy, but it’s not actually that dangerous...”

It’s basically... a rabbit-doll enthusiast parade...

Just as I was thinking that soberly—

A gentle voice spoke from my pocket.

—Sir. If you don’t mind, may I join the conversation?

Hm?

I took out a silver coin from my pocket. It was the coin containing the former persona of Ho Yuwon—the “Counselor,” the First Juror.

I looked at the lizard.

“...Can you hear this?”

“Yes.”

I see...

Then it should be fine.

“Go ahead.”

—Thank you.

The warm voice addressed me.

—I’ve been closely observing your actions over the past few days.

—You were trying to create a fun atmosphere, like a festival, while minimizing harm to people, weren’t you?

Well... the fun part wasn’t intentional, but...

Still, I nodded. Minimizing harm while causing a disturbance was my goal.

—That’s a commendable intention.

—But sir, have you ever heard this? Before modern times, the most popular and common form of public festival was execution.

......!

[Good heavens, do they think my friend doesn’t know that? How awkward. When the foremost expert on the subject is standing right beside them at all times.]

[The masses always crave stimulation and pleasure. Why would executions be any different? My friend surely already knows this...]

—Of course. That was merely an opening—to introduce the important role such execution-festivals played.

—Namely, community cohesion.

I instinctively glanced through the window between cars.

Shelter residents were gathered, chatting with excited or cheerful expressions.

Tension. Excitement. Focus.

Dopamine.

Cohesion.

—In a boring, stifling, isolated daily life, people easily immerse themselves when an entertaining stimulus appears.

—And those who share the same experience form tighter bonds.

“......”

—Then they begin to want more intense, more aggressive experiences. Enjoyment just for us  .

—For example... the punishment of a bad person who is not us.

A person marked with a rabbit-doll symbol shook hands proudly with members of the Rabbit Doll Cult, stroking their precious dregs.

Then they raised a middle finger toward the driver’s cabin. People tried to stop them, but soon laughed together.

—Even if it looks joyful on the surface, please don’t forget that it always carries the risk of escalating to extremes.

“......”

[Oh, I can’t listen to this any longer.]

Sharp yet elegant rhetoric poured out like cream, accompanied by a sigh.

[Come now. Let us ask the so-called Counselor—this lingering trace of a plague.]

[Is it truly counselor-like to instill guilt in a client over something that hasn’t even happened yet? I think not!]

—...!

[And you push overly pessimistic claims as if they were established truth. But isn’t it precisely danger that makes content thrilling and enjoyable?]

[An entertainer must be able to walk the tightrope between danger and stimulation. The pros and cons are two sides of the same coin!]

I felt gloved hands pat my shoulders in encouragement.

[If permitted, I shall speak as a professional. Friend! This Brown disagrees with the Counselor. You are providing the public with extremely enjoyable entertainment.]

[Who knows? Had you not done this, perhaps they would have chosen something truly archaic—like real executions—as entertainment!]

—That’s a statement based on the values you pursue professionally, Mr. Host, so it’s understandable.

—But I hope the sir will also reflect on my words when he has the chance.

[Persistent, aren’t you!]

Let’s please stop.

“Anyway...”

I rubbed my forehead.

“I understand what you’re trying to say. I’ll be careful.”

[Oh, friend!]

I chose an effective method and ended up with a cult; since it turned unexpectedly silly and heartwarming, I’d nearly let my guard down.

‘Right.’

Doing this purely for fun...

That might actually be more dangerous.

“So, Counselor, you’re saying there’s no guarantee how this atmosphere will develop?”

—My current diagnosis is that there’s a high probability it won’t last long.

That made sense.

In an isolated survival shelter, it’s far too easy for ideas and beliefs to become distorted.

Hm.

“Understood. Then that settles it.”

—Pardon?

“I started this situation, so I’ll take responsibility and make sure it ends as gently and positively as possible.”

That was the plan from the start—just sooner now.

So.

I declared solemnly.

“I’ll ride the momentum while it’s still funny and deal with it quickly.”

The Counselor fell silent, then asked,

—Deal with... what?

“The conductor.”

—What?

[Oh!]

Now that I had help, I could push the plan all the way.

Just wait, driver’s cabin.

I’m coming in—right now.

—Sir?

Thank you for the advice, Counselor.

And then I saw the cheat code.

“Chief. Would you be able to accompany me for a moment?”

I returned to Car 1 with Chief Lee Jaheon.

Fortunately, after I insisted again, he didn’t kick me off the train—he agreed to help me with today’s task only.

“Distributor!”

On Chief Lee’s chest, just like everyone else’s, was a rabbit-shaped badge cut from gold tape...

“He’s staying in Car 8 and said he was deeply moved after seeing the perfect form of the Being of Stuffed Cotton!”

“Oh!”

Thankfully, there was still no hostility or exclusion toward outsiders.

‘No telling what happens after tonight, though.’

Right.

While this whole “Have you accepted the Rabbit Lord?”  atmosphere was still friendly, I’d wrap things up peacefully and steer them back into harmless rabbit-fan territory before they fully evolved into a deranged cult.

Steeling myself, I stepped to the front of Car 1.

Then, as if there were nowhere else to sit, I sat down with my back against the door to the driver’s cabin.

Waiting for the signal—

“Everyone! The Leader is about to share the dream he had!”

“Oh!”

People flocked toward the Leader, who had just woken up near Car 2 to share his amusement-park dream story.

‘I put in a particularly fun advertisement tonight.’

I’d added souvenir-shop shopping.

And with the shopping dopamine amping everyone up, a rabbit-goods lottery began nearby using the raffle box I’d prepared in advance.

“Everyone, take one!”

People in Car 1 surged toward the extra dopamine. Even those still wary watched intently for different reasons.

And then, for a brief moment, an artificial situation formed—something normally impossible in the train shelter.

All eyes temporarily left the driver’s cabin.

“......”

Chief Lee quietly reached behind him and grabbed the cabin’s lock.

No special unlocking technique was needed.

Because the lizard chief behaved exactly like a lizard chief.

**Crack.**

With a tiny sound, the door opened.

‘Phew.’

I silently thanked him with a glance and nod, then slipped carefully into the cabin.

And quickly closed the door.

Chief Lee would guard the outside—so I could work without worry...

“Hrrrng...”

The conductor was asleep.

One hand gripped a pole, the other held a can of preserved food.

In front of the control panel was a crumpled notebook page plastered with names—each crossed out with an X.

Judging by it, it was a list of people he’d expelled for displeasing him.

...Most of them were probably dead.

‘Thanks a lot.’

That only sharpened my resolve.

The cabin smelled stale and musty, like poor ventilation.

‘I can’t let him wake up and scream.’

I approached carefully, knocked the pole away, and grabbed his neck—

Covering his mouth at the same time.

“...!!”

“Shh.”

I whispered to the conductor, who jolted awake and thrashed in terror.

“Please be quiet. I’m holding a knife. I don’t want to use it to silence someone.”

The knife part was a lie.

“~!!”

But he didn’t know that. He nodded frantically, but I didn’t trust him.

“You hear that outside, right?”

I let him listen to the cheers, laughter, and shouts beyond the door.

After giving him time to fully register it, I spoke.

“They’re all excited right now... they might even find it entertaining if they saw you die.”

“...!”

Of course, I had no intention of actually doing that.

But based on the Counselor’s analysis, it was a disturbingly plausible outcome—and one that could spiral into a genuine cult-style execution.

‘That would be instant radicalization.’

In short, what I was doing was actually better for his life expectancy.

But it was important that he didn’t realize that.

“Will you stay quiet?”

“Mmgh.”

He nodded desperately. Sweat, saliva, and tears smeared my hand.

“All right. But promise me. I don’t want to use more force than necessary.”

“Mmgh.”

Only after confirming his compliance did I release him.

Then I stared into his trembling eyes.

“Tell me everything you know about this train.”

His eyes quivered.

I’d thought carefully about this.

‘What can I say to extract information quickly, one-on-one, without pointless arguing?’

I already had the most efficient, comprehensive line prepared.

This.

“...!!”

Before him, I produced a garment as if by magic from my wrist and put it on.

The Disaster Management Bureau jacket.

An agent uniform left behind by the First Juror, with a fox patch.

Then, in a calm voice, I said, “I know that Agent Chogae built this train.”

Watching the conductor’s eyes fly open at the sight of the uniform, I continued.

“So answer honestly. We will find a way out of here.”

And thus, the human-shaped information thresher began to turn.
