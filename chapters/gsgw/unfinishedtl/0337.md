# Chapter 336

“Was that a disaster alert just now?”

“They say it’s terrorism—terrorism!”

May 4th.

Disaster Day of Segwang Special City.

A moment from the past.

Near Hanbit Department Store, where I had rushed out that morning, on Segwang City’s Byeonhwaga Street—it wasn’t chaos yet.

But it was ominous.

At the very brink of something enormous breaking loose, standing at its starting point, I smelled fear—an unsettling sensation.

Tension.

The atmosphere shaped by the disaster alert reading **‘Biochemical terror attack near City Hall.’**

“Look up nearby shelters.”

“Junseong, stop looking at that and let’s just go home. Hurry!”

“Are we just taking the subway? What do we do?”

“Don’t you hear noise coming from over there? Someone’s screaming. There—over there...”

At those last words, I reflexively turned my head.

In the distance, only the upper part visible, stood a rectangular glass building.

Six hundred thirteen days of repeatedly living through a peaceful May 4th in Segwang Special City told me exactly what it was.

Segwang City Hall.

—Class had just started when a disaster alert suddenly came in. They said a terror attack happened at City Hall.

But the City Hall building looked perfectly fine.

It appeared clean and intact, as if nothing like a terror attack had occurred. Sunlight glittered off the glass surface.

That was why pedestrians kept glancing toward City Hall. They looked confused.

“Was the disaster alert just an error?”

“Hey, they said biochemical terror. Let’s not get close, at least...”

But—

Thud.

“...Huh?”

I felt it.

A strange vibration coming up from the ground.

Thud.

At short intervals, a sound like impacts—like raindrops rattling rapidly against something.

“What is that?”

The murmuring of the crowd grew louder as others noticed it too.

But the sound itself grew louder.

Thud.

The next moment—

“Uh—uh?”

“Hey, that’s...”

The pedestrians realized what the sound was.

Footsteps.

The sound of countless people walking.

Thud.

[Oh, look over there, my friend. We’ll need a close-up.]

I turned my head.

From the direction of City Hall, a group of people was rounding the crosswalk and walking toward us.

They weren’t running away from something.

They weren’t screaming.

Even so, a large crowd naturally drew attention, and pedestrians whispered and speculated as they watched.

“What is it?”

“Looks like they’re blocking off the street.”

“It must be because of the terror attack.”

“Of course the police would be there.”

“I saw fire trucks on SNS.”

Voices like that echoed everywhere.

I swallowed hard and stared at the approaching group.

And then—

“Huh?”

A sense of wrongness crept in.

They all wore calm expressions, walking silently, not talking to one another.

“...This is strange.”

Their clothing varied wildly. Restaurant staff, office workers, people in tracksuits, café uniforms... all of them walking side by side on the sidewalk without anxiety, without urgency, without hesitation.

“Hey, hey, look at that.”

“What’s wrong with them?”

But the unease didn’t end there.

As the stream of people kept pouring onto the sidewalk, new outfits appeared among them—

“......”

“...Firefighters?”

Firefighters.

They were walking calmly in their red turnout gear.

Even police officers followed behind them.

After that came paramedics.

All of them wore faint smiles.

Behind them, there were injured people.

People who should have been loaded into ambulances were walking among ordinary pedestrians...

Those who had rushed to the terror scene, gathered there in an emergency, were now marching exactly as they had been.

“......”

“Hey... this is wrong.”

Humans feel fear even from a sense of wrongness.

People in the busy district near the department store finally began to hesitate, backing away, or quickly lifting their phones to record videos.

‘Damn it.’

I slowly started backing away.

My heart pounded.

And then—

Beeeeeeep!

Disaster alert sounds began ringing out from all directions.

With trembling hands, I checked my phone.

A chill ran straight down my spine.

[Segwang Special City]

Alert lifted

Not terrorism

Not a weapon

Not an explosion

Please remain calm

Let’s go together

And then—

“Uh... uh?”

As the alert sounds faded, another sound emerged.

Humming.

A small humming melody softly vibrated through the air.

‘Where is that coming from?’

Then I realized.

Everyone’s mouths.

Everyone reading the disaster alert was humming.

Including me.

A melody that felt vaguely familiar grew clearer and clearer from a blurred, faint sound—

‘Fuck!’

I slapped my own face with my phone.

“...!”

As dull pain snapped my mind back—

“Aaaah!”

I immediately turned and ran.

Behind me, screams were followed by grotesque sounds.

“You—what are you doing?? Uh, ugh...”

“Why are you running!”

“Oh my god!”

The distant shouts were now right behind me.

The sound of flesh squelching together, grinding noises, wailing mixed with footsteps...

“Sw—swollen... hiiii!”

The hair on the back of my neck stood on end.

‘Don’t look back.’

I have to get away. I have to get away...!!

Survival instinct shoved me forward. I ran without even breathing.

For a fleeting moment, reflected in the department store glass to my left, a child holding their parents’ hands stared at me with curious eyes.

My breath caught in my throat.

Beeeeeeep!

[Segwang Special City]

Not a disaster alert

Join the casual walk event

Let’s walk together

To paradise

Alerts rang out everywhere. I didn’t read further and shoved my phone away.

No—why did I even pull it out to read it while running like this—wait.

...Why do I see the department store entrance again?

And then I realized.

I was running the wrong way.

I had turned around again and was now running toward City Hall.

...the department store’s glass doors were on my right.

“Uh—uh?”

“Let’s go forward.”

“Yoonjin! Yoonjin—!”

I stopped. I steadied myself after nearly falling and staggered backward.

I tried to lower my head so I wouldn’t look ahead—but I glimpsed it.

...The legs of someone who had joined the group from City Hall were swelling.

A slow, steady humming was coming out of my mouth—

‘No.’

Someone shoved past me and ran forward.

“Hng!”

The humming from my mouth stopped abruptly. Fear, confusion, panic spread like an infection. I made a split-second decision.

‘I have to get away.’

But if I couldn’t just run in the opposite direction of that procession—

‘I need to leave the street!’

I sharply changed direction and ran back into the department store.

“Aaaah!”

“Ah!”

“Close the doors!”

Behind me, I heard the glass doors slam shut—and something splattering heavily against the glass.

Without hesitation, I sprinted like mad across the entire first floor of the department store, heading straight for the opposite exit.

“Ah!”

“What is that?!”

People who didn’t yet grasp the seriousness glanced over—then screamed as they noticed the chaos in the lobby.

Leaving behind the sound of disaster alerts echoing through the lobby, I kept running.

Soon, an outdoor freight parking lot came into view.

“Huff...!”

I burst out of the department store.

Under the blue sky and sunlight, the parking lot looked peaceful—but screams and rupturing sounds echoed from somewhere beyond.

I looked at my now-silent phone.

‘...Is it that if you get within a certain radius of that phenomenon, you start hearing it?’

Damn it. Damn it!

Why did I drop into the morning of Disaster Day?

My head spun.

‘What am I supposed to do?’

Segwang Special City is doomed now—how do I survive? No, first I need to find Section Chief Lee Jaheon, and the two people who were inside the tattoo...

[Now, now, my friend. The more you rush, the more you waste. Let’s not waste your precious ideas and thoughts. Let’s think efficiently...]

[Why don’t we start by organizing what Mr. Roe knows?]

...What I know?

‘That’s it!’

It felt like sparks flew in my head.

I know this city.

Its geography, locations, transportation—things I naturally learned from repeating a peaceful May 4th over six hundred times.

And the last several months spent exploring the underground of Segwang Special City after it became a catastrophe-level disaster.

I know what exists where in this city, and how its landmarks change after Disaster Day.

Reasoning backward—

‘I know everything—what’s where, and what characteristics each place has...!’

...Then I can at least try something based on that.

“Hoo.”

I straightened up.

First—

‘Find people.’

Find the people who fell with me into the heart of Segwang Special City after leaving the train.

‘There’s a high chance they’re trapped in this past too.’

...I had to think that way.

And this place was already a disaster.

If this insane May 4th was endlessly repeating in Segwang Special City, I had to find a way out—no matter what.

Explore and escape.

“...Let’s go.”

I decided.

Taking a deep breath, I stepped forward.

After some thought, I didn’t turn off my phone. It also served as an alert that the ‘disaster phenomenon’ was nearby.

‘First, I need to get away from Segwang City Hall—the apparent origin of the disaster.’

And there was at least one place where I knew the disaster was temporally delayed.

‘...Segwang Technical High School.’

—The teachers suddenly collected all our phones. They unplugged all the TV cables.

—Then they told us to close our eyes, cover our ears, and lie still.

At least until they did all that, the students were fine.

‘...They said they played classical music.’

And they told them to close their eyes and cover their ears...

Only now did I begin to vaguely understand what that might have meant.

And—

—One of the teachers tried to go outside.

A guidance counselor.

I might be able to meet Ho Yuwon as well.

“......”

[You’ve chosen a destination, my friend.]

‘The bus won’t work.’

Following main roads would be stupid, and by now the roads would be complete chaos.

And the subway—who knows when the entrances might be blocked...

‘Wait.’

As I looked around the public outdoor parking lot connected to the department store’s freight area, I noticed something.

—Bicycle rack.

In one corner, seven or eight bicycles were locked up...

And so, shortly after—

‘Sorry...!’

I was riding a bicycle.

I picked the oldest, dustiest one—the one whose owner clearly hadn’t come back for it. At least I wouldn’t be leaving someone devastated after getting their escape bike stolen!

‘I should stick to alleys.’

Avoiding people, I began riding toward the high school through side streets and between shops instead of main roads.

“What is it? What’s happening?”

“Hey, go into the café street!”

“What are those people...”

The bicycle skillfully squeezed through narrow passages, weaving past panicked people.

My bike-riding skills, honed at the Disaster Management Bureau, were shining in a very strange place.

Come to think of it, since I was heading into a supernatural disaster to find people, maybe it was the perfect fit...

“Aaaah!”

I jerked my head up.

Right ahead, people spilled out of an alley next to a convenience store and tumbled right in front of my bike.

Damn it!

I barely leapt over them, nearly crashing.

Five or six people had fallen. Regaining my balance, I shouted,

“Are you okay?!”

It looked like someone had rounded a corner at a run and knocked into others, causing them to pile up.

One of the fallen people got up without answering and ran off.

I shouted after them,

“Go away from City Hall! Avoid the main roads!”

“Get up!”

“Eek!”

The others shoved and helped each other up and began running—but one middle-aged man couldn’t get up.

“Ugh...”

A high school student who had been pinned underneath was instead helping the man up.

‘Damn it. Damn it.’

It might be pointless, but I stopped my bike and moved to help. Just as I turned—

Someone was standing in the side alley.

“......”

The shape of a person holding a phone.

Disaster alerts were continuously sounding from the phone in their hand.

A citizen of Segwang Special City, dressed ordinarily, wearing a floral-patterned dress.

But—

‘Ah.’

It was swelling.

I heard humming.

The melody grew more distinct.

It staggered toward us, humming softly...

The disaster alert rang on my phone too.

‘Ha.’

My scalp prickled. I got off the saddle and shoved the bike toward the alley where it stood.

“Go!”

The middle-aged man, finally on his feet with the student’s help, took off running.

I wanted to scream and run too.

‘It’s already infiltrating the alleys.’

At least there weren’t many yet.

‘Get out fast.’

Gritting my teeth, I started running again—planning to go on foot for now and grab a bike later if possible.

But then I saw it.

The high school student who had helped the man was heading toward the direction where I’d pushed the bike.

“...!”

I immediately grabbed the student and kicked the bike away again.

I clamped down on the humming trying to rise from my mouth, turned, and ran.

The student stumbled along, then started running on their own.

And when I was sure the disaster alerts were no longer sounding—

“Huff...”

I finally stopped after spotting a rack with several shared bicycles.

I turned toward the student who was still following me.

“Are you oka—”

And froze.

“......”

The uniform the student was wearing—it was familiar.

‘Segwang High...?’

I reflexively raised my head.

And stopped completely.

“Huff... y-yes, thank you...”

Ryu Jaegwan.

Agent Bronze was panting, in the form of a high school student.
