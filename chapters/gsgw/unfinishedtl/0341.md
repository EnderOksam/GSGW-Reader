# Chapter 340

We managed to board the train heading for Sekwang Metro about twenty minutes later.

[Good grief, at this rate I’ll be trampled to death! What a filthy spectacle!]

We had to let several trains go by.

“I almost got crushed trying to get on.”

It was the point when people who had suffered serious injuries while fleeing began desperately refusing to get off the trains.

“They realized that as long as they stayed on the train, their bodies were intact.”

Between those trying to board and those shoving them away, the platform descended into chaos.

...In fact, I saw people get crushed to death.

“...Hoo.”

Watching the horrific scene unfold beyond any chance of intervention, I clenched my teeth and focused on keeping the two of them safe.

“Please come this way.”

Instead of forcing it, we moved calmly to the very end of the platform, where there were relatively fewer people, and attempted to board from there.

The doors are opening.

“As expected.”

People in panic rushed along the same routes like a lemming effect, but the very last car—avoiding that flow—was comparatively rational.

...For now.

I shoved the two of them inside and managed to stand clustered right in front of the doors.

The screen doors are closing...

Done.

“Hoo.”

The relief and tension twisted my insides to the point it almost hurt.

But Reporter Eun Haje raised one eyebrow as she looked at me.

“You’re pretty capable. Calm, too.”

“Thank you.”

“There’s mayhem outside, but people here aren’t in terrible shape. From what I overheard on the way, it sounds like a zombie virus outbreak.”

I replied evenly.

“The underground is relatively safe.”

“Is it? That’s strange. With biochemical terrorism, toxic substances usually settle, so underground should be more dangerous, shouldn’t it?”

Sharp.

“And... how does that beanpole over there know things like that?”

“......”

I checked our surroundings.

With the noise this loud, no one seemed to have the spare attention to eavesdrop. Everyone was lost in their own pain and desperation—trying to contact someone, clinging to acquaintances in fear, or staring outside.

I spoke.

“...Please think of me as someone affiliated with disaster situations like this.”

“Does that have anything to do with being called an ‘agent’?”

“......”

So she heard.

“Yes.”

I briefly explained the Disaster Management Bureau to the reporter. Her eyes sparkled as she listened.

“Even if it sounds far-fetched, it fits the situation well enough.”

And when I finished saying that there were people to rescue and escape, and that it was already underway, she laughed heartily.

“Alright. ‘Agent,’ then you’ll need to write a report too, won’t you?”

“Possibly.”

“Then you’ll need someone to record what you did, right?”

She pointed to herself with a grin.

“I’d be perfect.”

“......”

“Few people are better than me at tracking conspiracies and incidents. I’ll write it very objectively—with proper analysis and interpretation.”

It really just sounds like you want to publish something...

“...Well, fine.”

I suppressed a sigh and nodded.

“I’d appreciate that.”

“Okay. Good choice.”

Reporter Eun Haje winked.

But in truth, she wouldn’t be able to write that article as a reporter anyway.

What was here wasn’t the real reporter, but only a proxy—her appearance fixed to match the Disaster Day of Sekwang Special City after being contaminated.

...Actually, that was why I tried using the resort contract, invoking an ‘employer’s order’ to revert her to her original form.

It didn’t work.

All I’d done was add a single line about a resort contract to the existing agreement. I couldn’t exert any more authority than that.

If it had worked, she would’ve changed into her resort uniform and her injuries would’ve healed—but it didn’t work at all.

Meaning my abilities as a resort manager.

“So Sekwang Special City blocks the influence of other urban legends.”

I’d assumed it was sealed off until now, but perhaps it was the nature of the disaster itself, originating at City Hall...

......

Wait.

The train shakes.

“Huh?”

The car is tilting.

“Huh—huhhh?!”

“Wha—?!”

“Mother of—!”

People grabbed the handholds, but those who couldn’t—or lost their grip—slid and spilled violently to one side.

The opposite side of where we were standing.

To the right.

“Aaaaah!”

My blood ran cold.

I immediately hooked my arm around the pole and reached for the other two. Reporter Eun Haje and student Ryu Jaegwan, who were already holding another pole, crossed hands and grabbed my arm and shoulder.

We were almost suspended in midair, dangling crookedly against the left-side doors.

“What is it?! What’s happening?!”

“H-hhkk!”

The train was still moving—terrifyingly unstable.

“Agent... there’s a strange sound...”

It wasn’t the friction noise of a curve.

It was...

Something breaking.

“Ah.”

A train running overloaded with passengers.

Unable to bear the weight, it derailed—

And was overturning.

Kiiiiiiiiing—!

“Aaaagh!”

“Help us!”

Screams came from the front cars. The train kept barreling forward without slowing, rocking violently—

Then began to completely topple.

“Aaaaaah!”

The pole we were holding rose toward the ceiling. People collapsed downward like dumped cargo, piling up with the sound of bones snapping.

Those crushed at the very bottom were already pulp.

We were being buried.

The train was still moving, the body jolting.

“No—!”

If I let go, I’d be swept away.

“Calm down, calm down—fuck!”

Gritting my teeth, I prepared to land as carefully as possible.

“Before it flips completely, I need to slide down to the floor.”

I’d slide down the pole.

The hands gripping me were slick with tension and cold sweat. I sent them first, then drew a breath and pulled my arm to slide down last.

And then I saw it.

Outside the window, a blue light flickered.

“...!”

A dokkaebi.

For a split second I blanked out, then snapped back to my senses and turned toward the device mounted beside the door.

“Emergency release...!”

I operated the manual mechanism, making the door capable of opening.

“Hurry!”

I climbed the pole again and reached for the door—

“Agent, step on me...!”

“...!”

Using high schooler Ryu Jaegwan’s shoulder as a foothold, I launched myself more easily and grabbed the door.

I cracked it open slightly.

Whoosh.

A wavering light slipped through the gap.

The blue traces of dokkaebi fire lingered near the door.

It looked like a rescue attempt to anyone else...

“...A trace?”

There was no expression or personality in the dokkaebi fire.

Just a mass of energy that had lost its purpose.

“Then...!”

I released my grip on the pole.

“...!”

Barely hanging onto the ceiling-mounted door with one hand, I thrust my other arm through the gap—

And grabbed the trace of the dokkaebi fire.

Then hurled it downward beneath the train.

Whoom.

The sacred flame of a cooperative spirit—recognizing my former status as an agent—flowed through my hand and connected beneath the subway wheels.

A flash of blue light—

And with a grinding sound, the train flipped back.

“...!!”

“Urrgh—!”

Not too abruptly, the train rotated ninety degrees back in the opposite direction. The door I was holding shut on its own. The sound of wheels spinning freely echoed—

Click.

The sound of rails and wheels locking together.

“......”

The train is departing...

Outside the windows, the scenery began to move smoothly once more...

“...Ha.”

Clinging to the door, I let out a long breath.

“Are you okay?”

“Huh... more or less?”

“Yes—yes!”

We were safe. Relief made my heartbeat thunder in my head.

“Man... that tall guy’s flying around.”

I gave a weak smile at Reporter Eun Haje’s amazement.

But the inside of the car already turned to hell.

“Hhngh...”

“Minjeong? Minjeong!”

The sounds of corpses crushed to death, and people screaming with broken bones, filled the space.

Right beside us—close enough to collide bodies.

The packed train now had corpses filling it as well.

“Damn it.”

I reflexively pulled the high schooler away, closer to the door.

The reporter, on the other hand, was carefully observing the carnage.

My hair stood on end, but I too rose on trembling legs and looked at the mass of people.

It was horrifying... but after seeing so much death all day, I felt a strange, dry exhaustion instead.

I staggered forward and began pulling survivors out of the pile. Once the interior was somewhat cleared, I withdrew my hands—

“Wow, thank you. Unknown agent. We survived thanks to you.”

...!!

I whipped my head around.

Beyond the crowd, in the front cars, several new figures had appeared inside the train.

Figures tending to the crushed and injured.

They wore blue jackets very similar to mine.

Ropes, glass handguns,

...Traces of blue dokkaebi fire.

And—

“If this hadn’t been handled before the next train came, it would’ve been a real disaster.”

Beside me, a person wearing a traditional lion mask babbled casually.

On the blue jacket beneath the mask, letters were visible.

Supernatural Disaster Management Bureau.

Disaster Management agents.

“...!!”

The Disaster Day of Sekwang Special City.

The agents dispatched that day had appeared right before my eyes.

The one wearing the lion mask looked at me—the one who’d helped reconnect the train to the rails.

Then, sounding slightly anxious yet cheerful, he asked:

“You even cleaned up this car already? You handled that dokkaebi fire really well earlier—what branch are you from, Agent?”

......

“I’m not from the Sekwang branch.”

“Ohhh, I see.”

The eyes inside the playful yet fierce lion mask seemed to glow yellow as he looked at my jacket.

“...But that uniform doesn’t look official. Seems like a knockoff.”

“That’s right.”

I answered deliberately awkwardly.

“I’m on break, so I didn’t have my uniform. I borrowed this from a local cooperative spirit in a hurry.”

“Hah. Living with gums when you’ve got no teeth—now that’s a real field agent answer...”

“Ha ha.”

He laughed heartily at my wry smile.

I quickly scanned his condition...

“...Male? Female?”

I couldn’t even tell.

Like a Daydream mask, it obscured perception, making their appearance and traits hard to grasp.

Even the voice—high or low—I couldn’t tell. The playful aura of the lion mask flattened all individuality.

“...Or maybe it’s the dokkaebi’s power.”

Sure enough, he brought up the dokkaebi with a slightly troubled tone.

“Do you also travel with a dokkaebi fire? One of my partner fires suddenly evaporated this morning. Left only afterimages.”

Of course it did.

The real dokkaebi fires would’ve all been evacuated before the sealing ritual.

They wouldn’t exist inside the May 4th Sekwang Special City trapped on Disaster Day.

But I didn’t show it.

“...I’m on leave, so I don’t have any dokkaebi or equipment. But if that’s true, it’s no ordinary situation.”

“Yeah... Normally that thing sticks to me like glutinous rice cake.”

The lion mask scratched at itself.

“What’s weird is—there are traces left. Like its ego vanished, but something like a backup battery remains.”

“......”

That meant—

“Could the power of the dokkaebi fire used on Disaster Day still be trapped in here?”

Just like at Sekwang High School.

“Did you hear anything else when you borrowed that uniform from the cooperative spirit?”

“Nothing in particular.”

Instead, I relayed what the “counselor” had said—that they were heading to Sekwang Station to end the disaster.

“I see. Hmm...”

The lion-mask agent turned away.

I swallowed as I watched agents in the front cars calming people and separating bodies as best they could.

And my companions...

“So you’re both from the same Disaster Bureau, but you don’t know each other? Different departments and regional branches, I take it?”

“Hahaha. Yes. That’s right.”

The lion mask brushed off the reporter’s question easily, then suddenly ruffled Ryu Jaegwan’s hair and said with a smile:

“You must be rescue requesters? Azure Dragon Team?”

I smiled bitterly.

And before I realized it, I answered:

“We were actually from the Black Tortoise Team. But we have no equipment... so we’re rescuing whoever we can.”

“Haha! Impressive, fellow agent.”

He dug through his pocket and pulled something out.

A shimmering jade-colored firearm made of glass.

A glass handgun.

“I’ll give this to you. I brought a spare.”

“...Thank you.”

“No, really. I’m the one who owes you. I almost died trying to reset a train using just dokkaebi afterimages.”

I quickly tucked the glass handgun into my coat. Watching this, the lion-mask agent nodded.

“So you’re getting off at Sekwang Station. You’ll check on the cooperative spirits and reroute the rescue path.”

“Yes.”

“Then...”

He hesitated slightly, surveying the carnage and the condition of the civilians.

He’d been cheerful throughout our talk, but now anxiety and unease briefly surfaced beneath the surface.

Lowering his voice so my companions couldn’t hear, he whispered:

“Hey. I’ve got a bad feeling. I smelled that nasty bastard here.”

“‘That bastard’...?”

“Daydream’s director. That fraud.”

......!

Which director was he talking about?

“And ‘that bastard’...”

Also known as Gangcheori.

The fire-breathing evil imugi yokai... wasn’t it?

[Hmm, a folktale popular in my friend’s homeland! Classic or outdated, depending. Though if someone’s been famous since the 17th century, you could call it a classic.]

Right—but Daydream has someone like that?

More importantly—

“You can smell that...?”

“I’m not wearing this hot, heavy mask for nothing. It’s got sacred effects.”

“...!”

The lion mask swayed as the yellow eyes rolled playfully.

“Especially for detecting wicked strong ones or unfair contracts—this thing’s amazing! Better than a bloodhound!”

I see...

But an even more important realization hit first.

The Cheerful Research Institute was destroyed because of Daydream.

Then there’s a high chance Daydream is involved in the Sekwang Special City incident as well...!

Maybe even right now.

I asked urgently:

“Do you think Daydream is deeply involved in this disaster?”

“Most likely. And there’s something else bothering me.”

What?

I leaned in closer at his gesture.

In an even quieter voice, he said:

“That smell—it’s coming from you too.”

I froze.

“Agent. Did you make some kind of contract with a Daydream Director?”

“......”

“Are you impersonating an agent... causing chaos?”
