# Chapter 348

I wanted to blurt out, “*What do you mean?*”

But in truth, my mind had already understood what Agent H *aegum was saying.*

*It felt like something in my brain had burst from the shock.*

...The blockade ritual is collapsing?

*So the disaster of Sekwang Special City... will be completely unleashed?*

*And that—*

In one week...

*An overwhelming sense of terror surged up reflexively.*

Having just experienced the reality *of that “disaster” firsthand, my mind erupted into a near-physical panic, sharp and prickling.*

Then...

*It’s the end.*

What happens now?

*Even though I knew the horrific truth behind the blockade, even though I had seen the suffering of Sekwang’s citizens with my own eyes, my face must have gone deathly pale.*

*Because in a few days, that scene would spread across the entire country.*

*Helplessly—no, wait.*

*I turned to Agent Haegum.*

*There was no way I was the only one feeling this fear.*

*“Does the Bureau... not know?”*

*If they did, there was no way they would’ve wasted the past 300 days. They would have devised countermeasures somehow.*

The problem was, if they had *a plan, these two agents wouldn’t be talking about an extinction-class disaster in one week with this kind of tone.*

...Are they keeping it secret?

*Agent Haegum frowned slightly.*

“You know this. There are barely any agents or officials in the Bureau who even know *about that Special City.”*

......!

*That’s right. Everyone’s memories had been wiped, the city rendered cognitively unrecognizable.*

*In the process, the Azure Dragon Team had been erased entirely, the organization reorganized as if it had always been that way.*

*But—*

“Still, someone must *know.”*

*“......”*

*Looking into Haegum’s eyes, I realized who she meant.*

The one who led the blockade ritual.

*The one who pushed the horrifying idea of endlessly repeating May 4th—using the deaths of countless people as fuel to sustain the blockade.*

*Someone at the very top had to know.*

*“...But from what Choi and I have been able to find out, there hasn’t been any unusual movement inside the Bureau over the past few months. If they were taking action, it should’ve affected the blockade ritual—but there’s been nothing.”*

*“That can’t be...”*

*Wait.*

*A horrifying possibility flashed through my mind.*

*If they weren’t acting now, then—*

*“...Are they waiting until the blockade disappears completely?”*

*“......”*

*“And after it vanishes... are they planning to perform the ritual again?”*

*“Yes.”*

*“...!”*

*“If they have to blockade it anyway, it’s a proven method. They’re more likely to choose that than some uncertain reinforcement.”*

*Damn.*

*“The problem is... where they’ll get the ‘offerings’ this time.”*

*Haegum seemed tense just thinking about that unknown.*

*But I already knew the answer.*

*...They’d simply re-offer the citizens of Sekwang Special City once they were freed.*

This is insane.

*A chill ran up my scalp.*

*I spoke urgently.*

*I had to say this—*

*“Agent, have you... talked to the other agent about the blockade ritual?”*

*About the human sacrifices.*

*I tried to keep it vague, but the moment I met Agent Choi’s eyes, I knew.*

*“Why beat around the bush, Grape. I already heard.”*

......!

*His bitter smile came with a dry, exhausted voice.*

*But there was little anger or confusion.*

*...He had already turned it over countless times.*

*“Oh. Worried about being tracked? Then it’s fine to talk here. This place is right under the Tiger General’s sanctuary—no one can follow us.”*

*Then he added quietly,*

*“...And the Bureau doesn’t know this place exists.”*

*“......”*

*“I tried everything—pulling out living citizens while maintaining the existing blockade ritual—but I just couldn’t interfere with the ritual itself.”*

*His blue eyes met mine.*

*“Not even now. With seven days left.”*

*“......”*

*His mind must have been on the verge of breaking.*

*A missing junior and a former spy still unconscious after an extinction-class disaster.*

*A workplace he’d risked his life for hiding a grotesque secret.*

*Director Ho, unpredictable as ever.*

*Looking into those hollow eyes—eyes that had clearly never rested despite his leave—my chest went cold.*

*The crisis was right in front of us.*

I’m losing it.

*A storm of unresolved matters crashed through my head: Director Ho, the security team, the researchers at Twilight Station, Blood Broadcasting Station’s rent, the lizard body stored at Manager Lee Jaheon’s residence...*

*The suffocating void of 293 lost days.*

*But none of it would matter if we couldn’t stop the extinction-class disaster coming in one week.*

*And yet... re-sealing Sekwang Special City—*

*I couldn’t bring myself to accept that again.*

*Hell for someone either way.*

...How much does Director Ho know by now?

*No. Focus.*

*If we couldn’t change what was coming, we had to move faster.*

*We had to deal with this—somehow.*

*“Agent Grape, careful—”*

*“I’m fine.”*

*I stood up without support. My legs shook.*

*My mind cleared.*

*The first thing we needed—*

*“I want to meet Ms. Yeongeun. Agent Mint.”*

*The event that triggered the blockade’s collapse.*

*We needed context—from the person who had been the core during the train shelter escape.*

*Fast.*

*****

*Go Yeongeun arrived at Agent Choi’s hideout under Agent Haegum’s guidance less than half a day later.*

*“Soleum!”*

*She looked completely fine.*

*Gone was the haggard padded jacket from the Sekwang subway. Instead, she looked neat and composed, dressed casually just like when I first saw her at the company.*

*...Which also meant all her limbs were there.*

—What about the body parts you lost at the Body Casino?

—I got them back after escaping.

—...!

—Yeah.

*“You really woke up. I’m so, so relieved...”*

—Less than two weeks after escaping, she called Choi and asked about the ‘dying to escape’ method we used to enter Sekwang again.

—As soon as she recovered, she went right back in. And she retrieved every lost body part from the Body Casino after it reopened.

—Only the ring finger was left. Someone else had already taken it from the pawnshop.

*I saw her brush her face with her left hand—missing the ring finger—as she let out a relieved breath.*

*Then I spoke carefully.*

*“Ms. Yeongeun. I should have come to you myself, but given the situation, I asked the agents to invite you here.”*

*“Of course that’s fine! I mean, you just woke up—your muscles must be completely gone—”*

*She noticed that I was in better shape than expected, a flicker of curiosity crossing her eyes. But instead of asking outright, she stayed silent.*

*...Even there, I felt a strange distortion of time.*

*“Ms. Yeongeun, how have you been? I heard you’ve been entering the Special City subway again.”*

*“I’m... better than before. I’m living with my aunt now. She’s... forgotten everything that happened.”*

*Her voice darkened briefly, then steadied.*

*“Honestly, part of why I want to return as an agent is to avoid memory erasure.”*

*Ah.*

*“You might call it calculating, but... losing my memories feels like losing myself.”*

*She explained that she’d deliberately expressed her desire to return, implicitly delaying memory deletion.*

*“And I brushed off getting my body parts back as an item effect. ...Sounds excessive, right? It’s the Disaster Management Bureau, not some Daydream.”*

*“No. I understand completely.”*

Especially for someone who had almost claimed a wish while not even knowing why *she’d forgotten her family in Sekwang.*

*And then—*

*I couldn’t speak.*

*I’d planned to say this with both agents present so I wouldn’t have to repeat it again.*

Damn it.

*But time was gone. Ruin was coming.*

*“Ms. Yeongeun.”*

*“Yes?”*

*“There’s... good news, and bad news.”*

*Agent Choi’s bitter smile appeared—then vanished as I continued.*

*“The good news is that the others may also wake up safely.”*

*“That’s—that’s wonderful!”*

*Joy sparkled in her voice, then quickly settled.*

*Agent Choi was watching me too.*

*“Deputy Haje, Mr. Ryu Jaekwan... and Manager Lee Jaheon—are they alive? They disappeared from the train, too. We thought they... fell together.”*

*I nodded slowly. A flash of cold fury crossed her face.*

*“...That conductor pushed them, didn’t he?”*

*“......”*

*“I knew it. That insane bastard...”*

*She said she didn’t regret hitting him at all, then steadied her breath.*

*“Still... I’m glad they’re alive. We couldn’t find any trace of the four of you in the subway.”*

*“......”*

*“Then you were on the surface?”*

*“Yes. ...Unexpectedly, people were living aboveground in Sekwang, too.”*

*I met her eyes.*

*“And dying every day.”*

*“...What?”*

*The air turned cold.*

*“...What do you mean?”*

*I told her.*

*About Sekwang’s endlessly repeating May 4th.*

*“...!!”*

*“And... that’s how the disaster is being contained?”*

*“......”*

*“By repeating a single day over and over, keeping the disaster from leaving the city—people are dying every day?”*

*Her stunned voice slowly filled with emotion.*

*“Then my parents...?”*

*“Ms. Yeongeun—”*

*“It’s been years now... j—”*

*She gasped. I moved to support her, but she pushed my hand away.*

*...!*

*She bowed her head, silent for a long while.*

*Then a whisper.*

*“...This is real? This is reality? It really... happened?”*

*“It might only be my deduction—”*

*“You’ve always been right! Every deduction you made was right! Why say that now...!”*

*She took another breath.*

*A stabbing silence sank into the cave.*

*Her clenched fist was shaking, white with strain.*

*Agent Choi rose quietly and moved beside her. He and Haegum both looked pale—but hid their shock before her.*

*I barely caught my breath and asked softly, “...You knew?”*

*That she knew.*

*“You mean whether I know the blockade lifts in seven days?”*

*......!*

*“Of course I do. My ability to hear ‘rumors about the Special City’ didn’t disappear after I got out. That’s why I kept entering the subway in the first place.”*

*Her voice cracked.*

*“But then I thought—”*

*“...Yes.”*

*“If we just let the blockade lift... maybe it’ll happen while my parents are still alive?”*

*...!*

*“May 4th keeps repeating. If the timing’s right—if it lifts while lots of people are still alive—wouldn’t they get fixed in that state? Alive?”*

*【 Oh, an interesting hypothesis! 】*

*My blood ran cold.*

*It felt too convenient. Too hopeful. But I couldn’t refute it outright.*

*“...Yeongeun. In that scenario, the entire country would collapse within days—”*

*“Then we just run. Anywhere. The other side of the planet might be safe...! And—”*

*Her voice was ragged, yet icy.*

*“Why should I care about that?”*

*“...!”*

*“Let them handle it themselves—s-sorry, I—”*

*She clenched her teeth.*

*“No. I’m not sorry at all. ...I only apologize for snapping at you.”*

*“......”*

*Agent Haegum lowered her weapon and gently patted Yeongeun’s back.*

*“Honestly, you don’t even need to apologize. I feel the same way...”*

*“......”*

*I knew she was crying silently—not from sadness, but from pain and rage.*

*Suppressed fury, like that of a guidance counselor pushed too far.*

*I swallowed my own rising emotions.*

...Is there really no way?

*Was her idea nothing more than a personal hope?*

*Or—*

*......*

*Wait.*

*“What if we end it first?”*

*“...What?”*

“If we end the disaster before *the blockade lifts... it might work.”*

*“Agent Grape!”*

*“Wait.”*

*Agent Choi looked at me sharply.*

*Something strange flickered in his eyes.*

*“So if the disaster ends right before the blockade collapses... the citizens might survive.”*

*“Yes.”*

*A spark ignited in my mind.*

*I knew how to leave the subway now.*

Follow the maintenance tracks.

*If I stepped onto Sekwang’s surface on the rewound morning—*

*At a very early point, before the city was fully contaminated—*

Attempting termination itself... might be possible.

And I already had a method *I’d been preparing.*

—If we somehow steal back the dream incubator copied by Daydream Co.

—Install it in the remaining Cheerful Research Instit ute facility—

*The potion machine I’d taken from Daydream.*

*And the researchers entrusted with it.*

*......*

*My experience of May 4th aligned with the note in my memory.*

*It was hazy—but—*

*“Even if the chances are slim, shouldn’t we try?”*

*I looked up.*

*Yeongeun steadied her breath.*

*Conflict flashed in her eyes—calculation battling against the fear of hope.*

*“Ms. Yeongeun.”*

*“......”*

*“Have you ever been to Twilight Station underground? There’s a resort pop-up there, and—”*

*“You mean Mr. Heoun, right?”*

*“Yes. And there should’ve been Daydream researchers there—”*

*But her expression answered me before she spoke.*

*“They weren’t... there?”*

*“No.”*

*......!!*

*“Wait.”*

*Her calm voice came just before my blood froze.*

*“I’ve heard a rumor that Director Ho secured the Daydream employees who went missing in the subway.”*

*...!*

*“They’re probably being held somewhere in the subway as part of the current project.”*

*......*

*“...Do we need them to attempt termination?”*

*I nodded.*

*“Then... let’s go meet him.”*

*“...!”*

*“Before it’s too late. Let’s go see Director Ho.”*

D-6 until the Day of Disaster.
