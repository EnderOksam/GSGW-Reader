# Chapter 338

A mountain path in Sekwang Special City.

Ryu Jaekwan panted as he looked at the person running ahead of him.

The person riding the bicycle—no, the **agent** —was guiding him with astonishing speed and decisiveness.

“Huff...”

They were literally riding through the mountain.

More precisely, they were using a well-maintained walking trail to avoid densely populated areas, cutting through the mountain to escape the residential district.

[Mountain Trail & Rest Area]

The path was just barely maintained enough that a mountain bike wasn’t strictly necessary.

Behind the leading agent, the school counselor was perched almost as if laid across the bike, while Ryu Jaekwan clung desperately to the unfamiliar bicycle as he followed.

The agent occasionally checked his own condition but never slowed down. His determination to escape danger as quickly as possible was palpable.

Even in this lie-filled, chilling, and urgent situation straight out of a disaster movie, he remained calm and resolute.

And yet—

“Jaegwan, be careful!”

How did he even know his name?

He was an orphan with no connections.

Then why is he trying to save him?

Something between hope and fear churned in his chest. Amid the terror of this extreme, non-everyday situation where he might die, a strange thrill crept in.

The longing of adolescence.

The excitement of being given a special role.

But the situation was far too urgent for such sentiments to fully surface.

**Beeeeep!**

“...!”

“We’re turning!”

The moment the disaster alert rang again, the bike ahead sharply swerved.

Ryu Jaekwan hurriedly followed.

But a chill ran through him as he realized he’d almost let go of the handlebars to grab his phone and check the message.

That impulse was growing stronger...

How long had they been riding?

“Let’s... huff... rest for a moment over there.”

They stopped in front of a rest-area sign.

Ryu Jaekwan reflexively scanned the area.

The rest area halfway up the mountain was a cabin, with a small clearing around it enclosed by a fence.

In one corner, water pooled beneath a faucet shaped like a wooden sculpture, nestled between rocks like a tiny spring.

[Comma Spring (Washing Prohibited)]

Following the agent, Ryu Jaekwan dismounted and pushed his bike toward the cabin entrance—

Then he heard the counselor whisper softly.

“There are other people inside the cabin.”

...!

“They’re watching us from the window. ...I don’t think their intentions are good. Don’t look.”

Ryu Jaekwan almost turned his head toward the window. Cold sweat slid down his temples.

...it felt as though leering eyes were scanning their bikes, their builds, their luggage.

Then, beside him, a calm voice catching its breath spoke.

“We weren’t planning to go inside anyway... If we need to move quickly, indoors isn’t ideal. Let’s sit outside briefly and then go. There’s a spring here.”

At the agent’s steady voice, the teenager’s bristling tension eased.

“Are you okay with that, Jaegwan?”

“...Yes.”

The adults checked Ryu Jaekwan’s condition with faint smiles, then soon began speaking with hardened expressions.

“It’s only been an hour since the disaster started, and people are already thinking that way...”

“When it comes to survival, no one’s immune. Anyone could tell immediately this wasn’t normal. It’s only natural.”

They were clearly talking about the “people with bad intentions” inside the cabin.

Ryu Jaekwan swallowed.

But the agent, when he looked back at him, seemed unfazed, simply patting the space beside him as if to reassure him.

His eyes among his black hair were calm.

As though he’d experienced things like this countless times before.

Unaware of the inner screams the agent might have been suppressing, Ryu Jaekwan felt a small sense of relief and sat beside him.

Catching their breath, they exchanged information.

“The alert earlier probably wasn’t on the mountain trail—it was likely down below. We should be safe here for a while. We don’t know when it might happen again, so let’s hydrate and get some nutrition now.”

The agent pulled out an empty plastic bottle from his backpack, filled it at the spring, and handed it to the counselor.

The counselor initially refused, but eventually took a sip or two and accepted a candy as well.

Then the agent asked him,

“You said you’re going against the flow.”

“Yes.”

“You mentioned there’s a way to end this disaster... so exactly where are you trying to go by subway?”

“To Sekwang Station.”

At that, agitation flickered across the agent’s otherwise calm face.

Why?

“Sir. The origin of this disaster is Sekwang City Hall. That’s right near Sekwang Station.”

Ah.

“What you’re saying is that you’re going to walk straight into the heart of a fire—without a manual, without basic equipment, in a newly erupted large-scale disaster.”

The agent paused, swallowed, and continued.

Ryu Jaekwan had the strange impression that he was deliberately building tension.

“And this isn’t an ordinary disaster. At minimum, it’s a wave-class event—at worst...”

“I know.”

The counselor’s voice sounded slightly anxious.

“That’s why we need to hurry. And don’t worry. I have my own methods.”

Then, as if to show them something, the counselor suddenly held out an empty candy wrapper.

He clenched his hand tightly so the contents couldn’t be seen, then opened it.

**Sparkle.**

Small red beads spilled into his palm from inside the wrapper.

“...!!”

“Looks like magic, doesn’t it?”

They were tiny, ripe red berries.

“They’re the fruit of a plant called fox beads, Jaegwan.”

The counselor spoke gently, holding the berries and smiling with a hint of playfulness.

“If you eat this, you’ll be fine for a while. You won’t be tempted by disaster alerts. ...I can’t promise complete safety, so we still need to leave this place as quickly as possible.”

As he spoke, his eyes briefly blurred as if gazing at the empty space where his right hand—melted away and severed—had been.

But he soon smiled again and offered the berries.

“Go on.”

Ryu Jaekwan glanced at the agent. When the agent nodded, he took the berries.

He’d already thought it earlier—when the counselor had shoved his hand into the guard-turned-monster, and when that hand had been cut off—

This counselor wasn’t an ordinary human...

......

Wait—had there even been a counselor at his school before?

...Had they been like **this** ?

A faint sense of unease rose, but his young, restless mind quickly brushed it aside.

Because the counselor continued speaking.

“So you two eat these and head somewhere safe.”

“......”

“Just taking me as far as the station is already more than enough. Agent... you should rescue those who want to be rescued.”

The agent fell silent.

Ryu Jaekwan watched his face.

He sensed intense conflict flashing across it—then—

“Thank you.”

A calm voice spoke.

“Jaegwan.”

“...Yes?”

“We’ll take him to the station, then continue along the mountain path. There should be a river ahead—fewer people, relatively safer.”

The agent looked him in the eye and spoke gently.

“We’ll use that route to leave the city. I’ll stay with you until you get out of this disaster safely, so don’t worry.”

“......”

In that moment, Ryu Jaekwan realized something.

This agent had just given something up—for him.

“...Then, are you leaving this place with me too?”

“For now, that’s the plan.”

Ryu Jaekwan spoke.

“That’s a lie, isn’t it?”

“......!”

“It looks like you’ll get me out, then come back here... Didn’t you say there were others you needed to rescue?”

“......”

“I want to come with you.”

“What?”

Ryu Jaekwan spoke firmly.

“You don’t need to send only me outside. That’s a waste of precious time.”

“...!!”

Clearly, that struck a nerve.

As the agent’s expression changed, Ryu Jaekwan oddly felt relieved.

Resolve outweighed fear.

“This is an urgent situation. You don’t need to look after me separately while rescuing others. I’m... strong, and I’ll do whatever you tell me without complaint.”

“No—”

The agent’s face hardened, then he let out a sigh. The high schooler’s heart skipped, but the agent looked at him with eyes that said **I knew it** .

...How?

“First—”

Just as the agent began to speak—

“Hey!”

...!

They turned toward the unfamiliar voice.

...A pair of eyes appeared through the half-open cabin door.

As Ryu Jaekwan sprang to his feet in fright—

“Aren’t you coming in?”

The door opened wider, revealing a middle-aged man in ordinary hiking clothes.

“Things outside don’t look normal. It’s better to be somewhere safer, even if just a little.”

Ah.

**He was trying to help.**

The high schooler felt a momentary relief—

—but the two adults subtly moved him behind them, taking a guarded stance.

‘...!’

“It’s alright. We’re heading out again.”

“Heading out? Didn’t something happen at City Hall? You going to a shelter?”

The man glanced sideways.

“That thing would be fast, but still, shouldn’t you eat something first? Take a cup noodle or two. We’ve got some inside.”

But though he spoke kindly, his eyes lingered on the bicycles with greed.

Ryu Jaekwan realized it then.

They were after the bikes.

When roads are blocked, there’s no better means of transport.

Just as that realization struck and he was about to speak—

“Agent...”

“Agent?”

The middle-aged man’s eyes swept over the agent’s jacket. When he realized it was a uniform, his expression changed.

“Hey—are you a civil servant?”

Crap.

“Police? Military?”

Inside the cabin, people stirred and murmured.

Ryu Jaekwan clenched his teeth—but the agent smiled.

“Hmm. Something like that, but... we’re not on duty. No official backing, either.”

He patted Ryu Jaekwan’s back.

“I’m just traveling with my younger brother.”

...!

“We’re not heading to a shelter. We’re leaving the city.”

“Wait a second—”

“The situation isn’t good. You’d be better off leaving the city too, heading where there are fewer people. We’ll be going now.”

“Me too.”

The two adults slowly backed away, then mounted their bikes.

“Jaegwan!”

Without hesitation, Ryu Jaekwan grabbed his bike and pedaled like mad.

“Hey!”

He glanced back—people were bursting out of the cabin holding trekking poles and wire cords.

A chill ran through him, and he pedaled harder.

Soon, the footsteps behind them faded.

“Jaegwan, the berries!”

“Ah—!”

His heart pounding, Ryu Jaekwan shoved the nearly crushed berries into his mouth.

Strangely, amid the fear and revulsion, he felt a faint sense of exhilaration.

Belonging.

Damn, that was close.

I clicked my tongue and pumped my legs harder.

‘They were totally about to tie us up and steal the bikes.’

[This afternoon, a B-grade thriller movie almost got screened. How about the title “The Cabin Murder Case”? Predictable and vulgar—perfectly fitting!]

Hahaha...

Whew.

From behind me, the counselor spoke.

“You’re a flexible agent. Some agents I know might have tried to persuade those people to evacuate as well.”

Ah.

“Recognizing the limits of what you can do is sometimes necessary. I decided to rescue only those within my reach.”

“...I see.”

“Are you disappointed?”

“No.”

The counselor looked faintly impressed.

“I’m glad you’ve found a way not to destroy your mental health.”

“......”

Just what kind of experiences had made this person trigger the Disaster Management Bureau’s selection like that...?

‘This is insane.’

Was it really right to take him to the subway station?

It wasn’t just bitterness—it felt close to despair. I swallowed a sigh and pedaled again.

The counselor clinging behind me was strangely light, and the bike sped toward the subway station.

Jui Station

The name was different from the mutated subway stations I’d experienced before.

But I could guess.

‘...Broadcast station.’

Near the station stood a massive local broadcasting building.

Afternoon Station (Blood Broadcasting Station)

A subway station connected to the Sekwang Special City Local Broadcasting Network.

Because of that connection, a temporary stage was sometimes set up in the concourse for civic choirs or orchestras.

I clearly remembered reading about it on the wiki.

‘This place is almost the exact opposite side from Sekwang Station.’

Thankfully, because of the distance, chaos hadn’t yet reached the level of the disaster’s epicenter. Those “entities” walking from City Hall along the main roads hadn’t arrived yet.

‘Good call taking the mountain shortcut by bike.’

But the area was already desolate, as if everyone had evacuated.

There were barely any pedestrians—just a few people hurrying along as if fleeing.

“......”

I looked at Ryu Jaekwan, who wore a stubborn **I’m not leaving alone** expression, and suppressed a sigh.

This one’s a headache too.

‘Let’s think while escorting the counselor into the station.’

Inside the subway, at least, the outside disaster’s influence shouldn’t reach. Sekwang Station itself would likely still be relatively intact.

‘It might even be safer than when I visited.’

Whether we could escape the city at all was another question—gathering more information wouldn’t hurt.

I forced down my anxiety.

‘Whew.’

I stopped at the station entrance and stored both bicycles inside my tattoo.

“...!!”

“Shh.”

The high schooler—Agent Bronze—nodded vigorously, eyes wide.

After checking our surroundings, I led us down through Exit 3.

Thankfully, it wasn’t sealed yet.

“......”

Entering the subway again felt strange.

And inside—

“What do we do?!”

“My mom won’t answer her phone...”

“Aaaah!”

I realized where all the people missing from the streets had gone.

“We’re all dead! It’s a virus attack!”

“All the paramedics are dead? Is this real?”

“This video—look at it! Is this real? Is City Hall really like this?”

Citizens had flooded the concourse, turning it into a de facto shelter.

Combined with panicked testimony from people fleeing other stations, it was complete chaos.

Rumors spread wildly—biochemical terrorism, everyone already infected, war breaking out, nuclear missiles about to launch...

In one corner, people were fighting viciously while others tried to stop them.

Elsewhere, someone wandered barefoot, muttering to themselves...

‘Damn it.’

A truly spine-chilling scene.

We hurriedly tried to avoid the crowd and head down to the platform.

“What’s your plan?”

“I was thinking of taking the outer loop—”

But the platform for the outer loop was already blocked.

...Just like when I’d explored the subway stations before.

[Hoho, so it was already impossible from day one!]

“Service suspended! Turn back!”

Station staff blocked the way, controlling the crowd. Damn it!

We rushed to the opposite platform.

“It’s a loop line anyway—taking the other direction should be fine. It’ll be a bit inconvenient, but...”

“......”

I realized something.

The inner loop would actually reach Sekwang Station slightly faster.

Fewer stations.

So why insist on the opposite direction...?

I glanced toward the machinery room on the sealed platform across the tracks.

“Is the place you’re trying to go... a facility on the opposite platform?”

“......!”

The Cheerful Research Institute.

For a moment, something eerie and uncanny flickered in the counselor’s eyes as he stared at me.

An observing gaze.

“...Why do you think that?”

“......”

“What kind of facility do you think is there...?”

At that moment—

**The train is now arriving.**

“...!”

A train entered the platform, wind rushing as it pulled in and stopped behind the screen doors.

Then—

**The screen doors open.**

Some of the people packed inside spilled out as if shoved. Others struggled not to get off but were knocked over near the doors.

“Aaaah!”

“Help!”

As soon as they disembarked, several people collapsed, battered and bleeding.

Some clutched each other and cried; others barely managed to move.

Utter chaos.

‘We’re supposed to board in this mess?’

“Sir!”

Thankfully, the counselor’s strange demeanor vanished completely as he pushed through the crowd toward the train. I clenched my teeth, torn about whether to follow.

‘Should I... check the Cheerful Research Institute instead?’

What could it possibly look like now?

I glanced at the opposite platform, then at Ryu Jaekwan...

In the brief moment the doors were open, I agonized rapidly—

“Ghk...”

...I saw another group disembarking from the train.

Among them—

A familiar face.

Cold, pale, sharply defined.

Deputy Eun Haje.

“...!!”

I froze.

“Agent...!”

Eun Haje—hair longer than I remembered, tightly tied back—was being carried out of the train, unconscious, on someone’s back.

“Reporter!”

“What do we do...?”

The faces of what seemed to be coworkers were smeared with blood and sweat.

And then I understood.

“Huuh...”

She wasn’t unconscious.

She was dead.

Eun Haje’s life had already ended.
