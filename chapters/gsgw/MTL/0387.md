# Chapter 386

*‘Was lasting just two weeks at this miserable company before quitting really too much to ask…?’*

<br>

**[EMERGENCY ALERT!]**

<br>

*‘How did everything go to hell before I'd even lasted a few days?!’*

*‘Ugh!’*

Fear could wait. I shot upright out of bed like someone who’d just gotten an emergency alert saying their apartment was on fire, then snatched up everything I’d stashed beneath the bed in an instant.

*‘Good thing I picked up a backpack from the commissary yesterday!’*

Bang!

I even crammed one of the towels from the bathroom and a bottle of water into my backpack before bolting into the living room.

“……”

“……”

“Oh, for goodness' sake.”

Why was everyone so calm?

“Oh? You came running out too after hearing the alarm?”

The workers sitting in the living room looked completely unfazed.

At most, they just looked mildly annoyed at having been woken up.

<br>

**[EMERGENCY ALERT!]**

<br>

Seeing the (probably) dumbfounded look on my face, one of them waved a hand dismissively.

“Ah, it's fine. It's fine. Happens every now and then.”

“…Excuse me?”

“I mean, they really ought to fix it. We've all got work in the morning, and they're out here waking everyone up.”

...A malfunction?

“W-Wait... h-how do you know it's a malfunction?”

“Hm? Didn't the person in charge tell you on your first day? This facility's old. Since it used to be a coal mine, the old evacuation alarm sometimes goes off on its own. They told us to just ignore it....”

Ah.

“Why’re you acting like you’ve never heard that before?”

Damn it.

“I-I don’t know. Maybe they never told the Z workers about that…”

“Ahhh.”

The worker glanced at the Z999 sticker still stuck to my neck after my sudden promotion, then gave a casual nod.

He’d probably assumed I’d already been too far gone to remember something like that and decided not to question it. Phew.

“Anyway, the facilities here are old, so false alarms happen every now and then. Just stay in the dorm.”

“Ugh... There goes my sleep.”

“It happened last time too, didn't it? They let us sleep through the morning and work in the afternoon instead, so it'll probably be the same this time.”

The workers continued chatting as if nothing was wrong, even over the blaring siren.

Looking around, I spotted Assistant Manager Jin Nasol sitting near the entrance, wearing a thoroughly unamused expression. I quietly made my way over and sat down nearby.

“Um... did you already know it was a false alarm?”

“No.”

“……Would you like some water?”

“No.”

Hmm. Seems like she was irritated about losing sleep…

Just as I was thinking that, she spoke in a low, quiet voice.

“It doesn't make sense.”

“…What?”

“This company of all places would leave a malfunctioning emergency alarm unfixed?”

“…!”

I finally understood the source of the uneasiness that had been bothering me this whole time.

<br>

**[EMERGENCY ALERT!]**

**[An emergency has occurred within the facility! All employees still inside the facility, evacuate through the designated escape routes immediately!]**

<br>

Right.

Daydream was a company that managed ghost stories.

No matter how poorly they treated their workers, would they really leave a basic security issue like this unresolved?

Without any reason?

*‘…That couldn’t be.’*

……

<br>

**[EMERGENCY ALERT!]**

<br>

“Should we prepare ourselves?”

“Of course we should.”

<br>

**[An emergency has occurred within the facility!]**

<br>

I adjusted my grip on my backpack.

Then I sat still, forcing myself to stay alert.

<br>

**[All employees still inside the facility, evacuate through the designated escape routes…]**

<br>

Click.

…The alarm stopped.

People slowly began getting to their feet, ready to head back to their rooms.

“Oh, it's over.”

“Let's go back.”

“I'm so damn sleepy.”

But neither Jin Nasol nor I moved. We stayed where we were, waiting.

<br>

Dingdong—

<br>

“Hm?”

“Who is it?”

The workers who had already gone back to their rooms peeked their heads out at the sound of the doorbell.

A flat voice came from the other side of the door.

<br>

—Hello, this is Security. I’m here to check the alarm malfunction. Please open the door.

<br>

The workers sighed.

“Ugh…”

“Seriously? What a pain.”

One of them started slowly making his way over to the door.

“Wait.”

“What?”

I stopped him with one hand.

Then I stepped up to the entrance myself and spoke toward the locked door.

“Um… you usually don’t talk… but you’re being pretty talkative today.”

<br>

—Excuse me?

<br>

“Isn’t that right? You normally don’t say a single word…”

<br>

—……

<br>

“Hey, why are you saying things that’ll provoke him? You’re just making things more troublesome—”

“Gah!”

Jin Nasol covered the mouth of the worker who was about to step forward.

From the other side of the door came a tired voice, somewhere between disbelief and irritation.

<br>

—If there’s no need, I don’t talk. Right now, I’m asking you to open the door because I need to inspect the alarm malfunction.

<br>

“Ah… I see.”

<br>

—Yes. If you delay things even one more time, you’ll be penalized—

<br>

“But… there’s no reason for us to open it for you, is there?”

<br>

—What did you say?

<br>

“You can open the automatic door from outside with your device. You opened it that way this morning too.”

Silence fell.

“So why are you asking us to open it for you now?”

……

……

<br>

—Ha.

<br>

Biiiiip!

<br>

‘…!’

The door opened automatically, just like it had that morning.

Beyond it…

“Line up. I need to do a headcount.”

There really was someone standing outside in a Security Team uniform.

……

What?

“Seriously…”

“Why the hell are you making trouble for everyone, you bastard.”

“No, he’s gotten so full of himself just because he moved up here. He’s become impossible to deal with. We can’t handle him anymore.”

The workers from my room each threw in their own complaints about me while sucking up to the security staff.

One of them even shoved me forward.

“Hey, move back!”

“S-Sorry!”

The open hostility and contempt were obvious.

I could tell that the little shoves and occasional jabs from those who still saw me as a Z999 worker were just them taking out their irritation and anger on me, but I didn’t fight back.

I simply stepped away and stood where they told me to.

 Beside me, Jin Nasol quietly observed the situation.

“……”

*‘What is this?’*

Something was definitely wrong, but everything was proceeding peacefully.

Even the security staff seemed to be the real thing.

I watched the Security Team member carefully as they approached, checking each person in front of me.

There was nothing strange about their uniform or behaviour…

*‘Ah.’*

However, there was one thing that did catch my eye.

*‘…A string?’*

A white cord was sticking out from the back of the Security Team member’s full-body uniform, as if it hadn’t been properly tied.

It was the kind of thing someone could easily miss. After all, it was in a place they wouldn’t be able to see themselves. But…

*‘……Isn’t it a little too long?’*

The cord stretched on.

And on.

Past the entrance. Beyond the door. All the way down the hallway.

“……”

The headcount continued.

“Are there any personnel who are speaking strangely, or hiding somewhere and refusing to come out? Anyone trying not to show themselves?”

“Huh? No, no! There’s no one like that!”

“Ah, that guy does stutter though! Hahaha!”

The worker who pointed at me turned around, clearing my line of sight and making the cord easier to see.

……

“Did you see it?”

“Yes.”

The thing I had thought was a string…

was pulsing.

<br>

**Squelch.**

<br>

‘Shit.’

Cold sweat broke out across my body as I quietly slipped out of line—

“Ah~ That bastard again!”

Damn it.

“Stand in a single line. The headcount isn’t finished yet.”

“…Security staff.”

I steadied my breathing.

“What’s that cord attached to your back?”

“Excuse me?”

The security staff’s head turned.

“The thing attached to your waist.”

“……”

“It’s squirming.”

His head slowly turned back forward.

“Stop saying unnecessary things and stand in line.”

“Yeah! Just hurry up and get back in li—”

“Standinliiinecheckheadcountstandinline.”

“……Huh?”

“Standinlliiiineaahaahahhhthisiswrongthisiswrong”

<br>

It collapses.

<br>

Splat.

As if everything inside the full-body uniform had melted into something limp and shapeless, the security staff’s form gave way, collapsing and dropping downward with a wet, squelching sound.

“U-Uaaaahhh!”

“Eek!”

<br>

**Squelch.**

<br>

By now, the cord looked more like a hose.

Bloated to the point it seemed ready to burst, it pulsed as it drew everything in, almost as if it were swallowing.

The security worker’s body slowly deflated.

<br>

**Squelch.**

<br>

The Security Team uniform remained behind like an empty shell, the contents inside having been pulled away.

Like it was being drunk.

<br>

**…Gulp.**

<br>

We’re screwed.

“Get out!”

I ran out of the dorm with Assistant Manager Jin Nasol.

“W-Wait, together!”

“AAAAH! What is this?!”

“Thisiswrongthisiswrongthisiswrong”

As we raced down the hallway, I caught glimpses through the open doors on either side. Every other room had a staff member head-counting those inside. A few were already convulsing, while others had realised something was wrong and were slowly backing away.

“Hey! Get away from it!”

“A-Aaaah!”

Screams. Chaos.

And all of it…

<br>

**Squelch.**

<br>

Was connected to the long white strand stretching through the hallway…

*‘What the hell.’*

What was this?

My stomach churned. I avoided the cord and ran as fast as I could.

The anomaly was spreading rapidly from person to person.

An infection? Was it an infection?

*‘The people too… I need to avoid them…!’*

Think. A sealed-off place with no one inside…

There!

“The medical room!”

The moment I turned the corner, the medical room came into view. I grabbed the door and yanked it open, rushing inside. Assistant Manager Jin Nasol followed after me, and just as I was about to close the door—

“P-Please…!”

“Ahh!”

A few of the workers from our dorm desperately forced their way in behind us.

*‘Damn it!’*

Looks like they escaped here with us!

“This is…!”

“It’s faster if we just let them in!”

They were from the same dorm anyway. If they were infected, there was a good chance we were already done for too.

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol frowned in irritation, but even so, she grabbed the two workers who were already half-stuck in the doorway and roughly dragged them inside.

At the same time, I managed to shut the door.

<br>

Bang!

<br>

“…Phew.”

Cold sweat ran down my face.

…Atleast for now, that hell was shut out.

“W-What was that just now…?”

“There was something weird attached to its back!”

“M-Mom…”

I hurriedly checked the floor first. Clean. There wasn’t a cord, nor even a thin string.

*‘Ha.’*

…for the time being, we were safe.

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol, who had immediately raided the medical room for painkillers, walked over while swallowing two capsules at once.

“Infection-type darkness?”

“Infection-type, yes.”

There was no explanation beyond that.

Zombies, Vampires, Parasites—various types of ghost stories flashed through my mind.

“What’s the source?”

“I can’t tell.”

That was the biggest problem.

We still had no idea what was causing people to end up like that.

The only clue we had was…

“That white cord from earlier. Didn’t it look like a hose? Or maybe a large straw?”

“It did.”

Just as I thought.

“Do you think that’s what’s spreading it?”

“I can’t say for certain.”

“Neither can I.”

Whatever it was, what we’d seen looked like the result of an ‘infection.’

So what could have caused it?

Something that sucks everything dry…

“…It kind of reminds me of mosquitoes.”

“A mosquito?”

“Yes. When mosquitoes feed, they inject proteins through their saliva to make it easier to drink blood.”

That can cause the itching afterward, but that wasn’t the important part.

It was the way they fed.

“So, you’re saying it’s making people easier to consume?”

“…more or less.”

Wow. What a disturbing conversation to be having…

But talking things through with Assistant Manager Jin Nasol was the right choice. Thinking about it logically really did help keep me grounded.

*‘A mosquito…’*

“Then our first priority should be protecting ourselves.”

I ripped open every box of medical supplies and bandages in the room, wrapping up my exposed arms and legs.

Basic precautions.

If we wanted to prevent infection, the best thing we could do was avoid leaving any skin exposed.

“W-Wait, what are you doing?”

“Given what we’ve seen, I think it’s safer to cover up. You should do the same. Hurry!”

But the two who had followed us in only clumsily copied what I did before lazily stopping halfway.

Apparently, the crazy thug and his lackey weren’t exactly a reliable duo after all. Damn it!

“Make sure you cover yourselves properly!”

All I could do was hope they realised the situation soon. Though, honestly, I didn’t even know what this thing was, let alone how we were supposed to escape.

*‘What am I missing?’*

As I hurriedly wrapped the bandages around myself, my mind sifted through everything I’d seen so far.

What had I encountered along the way? Was there anything unusual?

*‘…Hold on.’*

Wait.

There definitely was something.

Not something I saw.

Something I heard.

<br>

>[EMERGENCY ALERT!]

>[An emergency has occurred within the facility! All employees still inside the facility, evacuate through the designated escape routes immediately!]

<br>

After all, the ‘emergency’ mentioned in that announcement had been genuine.

If that was the case…

……!

“The emergency alert… it was an actual evacuation order.”

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol scoffed.

“It’s probably just part of an experiment. Do you really think this company created an ‘escape route’ for the safety of disposable trash like us?”

“No.”

It was just that…

“The announcement… it probably wasn’t meant for the workers.”

“……”

Without the slightest hesitation, Assistant Manager Jin Nasol continued.

“Because workers aren’t employees?”

“Yes.”

That was right.

<br>

>[An emergency has occurred within the facility! All employees still inside the facility, evacuate through the designated escape routes immediately!]

<br>

Wasn’t that broadcast meant for the researchers and other permanent staff assigned here?

So that if something like a containment failure happened, they could sacrifice the workers as bait and let the ‘real employees’ escape.

“And the announcements for the researchers that had been playing endlessly until now…”

“It’s stopped.”

The facility was eerily quiet.

“……”

*‘Seriously…’*

The thought made my stomach turn all over again.

*’So all these day laborers are just… being locked in here alive?’*

These bastards are completely insane…

*‘No. Stay calm.’*

Getting angry or letting my emotions take over wouldn’t help. If I lost my head and failed to escape because of it, that would be the end.

Still… if there was one upside to all this…

“…Then there’s a very good chance an ‘employees-only escape route’ exists.”

“So? They would’ve made sure workers couldn’t get to it. We don’t even know where it is.”

“……”

“Judging by how they’re treated, even the Security Team are expendable. But what, you want us to go back out there and rob whatever’s left in their pockets?”

“No.”

That was far too dangerous.

And besides…

Something had just come back to me.

Somewhere workers weren’t allowed to enter.

<br>

>AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY

<br>

“……Assistant Manager.”

I finished wrapping the bandages around myself and looked up.

“The odds are fifty-fifty, but… I think there’s something worth trying.”

“And?”

“There’s one thing we’ll need first.”

“What is it?”

“The prize you won.”

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol looked down at the black prayer beads in her hand—the silver-adorned rosary.

“And one more thing.”

I’d already used mine, but…

“You still have your pass for the Off-Duty Zone, right?”

<br>

<p align="center">* * * <p align="left">

<br>

The plan itself was simple.

Go to the Off-Duty Zone and try opening the door marked ‘AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY.’

And the way to determine whether it would work—whether this was actually worth trying…

“You want me to use this.”

“Yes.”

This much, I was certain of.

The wiki had a record of that silver-adorned rosary’s winner, along with its effects.

<br>

When faced with a dangerous choice, it reveals the path that would be most beneficial to its wearer.

<br>

Fortunately, Assistant Manager Jin Nasol already knew how the rosary worked, so she didn’t need much convincing.

Over the past few days, I had subtly set the mood and repeatedly pretended to speculate about the rosary’s ability, slipping hints to her…

*‘Good thing I didn’t put it off…’*

Once again, even the smallest actions could make the difference between life and death.

I swallowed and watched her.

The rosary’s wearer raised her hand.

<br>

In a situation with multiple possible choices, reflecting the rosary’s silver ornament in the light and observing its shadow causes a wavering reflection to appear on the ground, indicating the way forward.

<br>

The rosary in her hand caught the ceiling light, its silver ornament glinting.

Then, a single hazy streak appeared on the floor.

‘…!!’

It stretched…beyond the door.

“……”

“So It’s confirmed.”

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol retrieved the rosary.

Among the beads, one caught my eye as it slowly burned white.

<br>

Upon use, one bead of the rosary turns white and loses its luster.

<br>

It was exactly as described.

*‘…Though, to be fair, that item does have a fatal flaw.’*

Namely…

If the wearer is placed in what you could call a situation where ‘death would be preferable’, it guides them toward death.

*‘But that’s not the situation we’re in now. We’re trying to find an escape route, and it’s leading us to one—which means that doesn’t apply.’*

I let out a breath.

…Just as I thought. We had no choice but to leave.

“Alright. If everyone’s ready, let’s go.”

The moment Jin Nasol finished putting it away and reached for the door—

“Don’t open it!”

“…!”

A panicked voice came from behind.

“There’s something strange outside! What if it comes in!?”

“Yeah, it’s obvious! This is some ghost’s doing! This place already feels haunted enough—what kind of disaster are you trying to cause?!”

“Shut up.”

“…!”

“Keep talking and I’ll leave you here after breaking the lock.”

And just like that, both of them fell silent.

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol gestured toward me with her chin.

“You open it.”

“…If we take them with us, you’re going to say they’ll just get in the way, right?”

“If you already know the answer, why ask?”

Yep.

Still, for the sake of my conscience, I made one last attempt.

“Please think about it one more time. Later on, you might end up thinking that leaving with us earlier would have been better than staying here.”

“What are you talking about?!”

“If you’re leaving, at least lock the door properly behind you!”

They weren’t going to listen. …There was no helping it.

“Then I’ll open it.”

After taking a deep breath, I carefully unlatched the door.

…Click.

Through the narrow gap, I caught sight of the white cord. It had stopped moving sometime during all this.

*‘…No. At this point, it looks more like a bundle of hoses.’*

Thickened to an unnatural size, it no longer resembled anything that could be mistaken for a simple cord.

I carefully closed the door again, then followed Jin Nasol down the hallway, swallowing my unease.

“……”

“……”

Silence.

The hallway was quiet enough to hear a pin drop.

Only the dim ceiling lights and, occasionally, the bright glow spilling from the open break room doors flickered in the darkness.

It seemed like it had already infected everything it could.

We moved cautiously through the empty corridors, relying on our flimsy protection in a place where every sign of human presence had vanished….

Then—

“Is… is anyone there?”

…!

“Someone… please help me! I’m trapped in here!”

A shout echoed from the far end of the silent hallway.

That voice…

“Oh god… what do I do…”

…I recognised it.

It belonged to one of the older workers who had taken me around the Off-Duty Zone.

……Ha.

“Assistant Manager.”

“Don’t say anything unnecessary.”

“That person knows the Off-Duty Zone well.”

“So?”

“We don’t know the layout of that area. If we want to find the door to the authorized personnel section, we need information.”

“…Twenty seconds.”

Even with her irritation, her decision was immediate.

“Confirm what you need within that time. Don’t do anything stupid that wastes it.”

“Yes.”

Fortunately, the call had come from the direction of the Off-Duty Zone.

While Jin Nasol kept watch, I used the opportunity to quickly investigate the room it was coming from.

…Several bodies immediately caught my eye, collapsed with nothing left but their shrivelled skin.

*‘Haa.’*

Aside from that, the rest of the room was empty. It seemed everyone else had escaped…

“Agh…”

The voice was coming from inside the wardrobe.

“Are you alright?”

“Yes! Oh, thank goodness, I’m in here! My word… thank the heavens…”

“Can you get out?”

“Ah, no, I had a large clay jar stored in this wardrobe… I don’t even know how I ended up inside. But now that I’m trying to get out, the lid won’t open…”

A large clay jar.

*‘It’s completely sealed, so the cord probably couldn’t have reached him.’*

Just in case, I braced myself to run as i carefully opened the wardrobe.

Sure enough, the voice was coming from inside a jar barely large enough for one person to squeeze into.

“Wait… are you that young man from before? The one those people were giving a hard time to?”

“Yes, that’s me.”

I lifted the lid.

“My goodness, what a coincidence!”

<br>

There was no one inside.

<br>

Instead, there was only liquid.

A sticky red-and-yellow mucus, melting and swirling in a viscous whirlpool…

“Ah, it’s hard to move! What should I do?”

It spoke.

“……”

“Young man?”

It had already melted.

My heart was pounding. Even my mind was throbbing, having once again learned that fear pushed beyond its limit could drive a person to this state.

But……

I put the cover back on.

“You seem to be in no condition to walk. I’ll carry you along with the jar.”

“Ah, thank you, young man!”

I lifted the jar… no, I carried an entire ‘worker’ with me.

Jin Nasol gave me an approving look—for a completely different reason.

“It might actually come in handy. You carry him.”

“…Yes.”

I stood up.

“Let’s go.”

<br>

To the Off-Duty Zone.
