# Chapter 388

The Personnel-Only door we’d finally found and opened in the Off-Duty Zone while being chased by the hoses.

“……”

“……”

Beyond it was darkness.

The place was dimly lit by nothing more than the faint green glow of emergency lights. Construction materials were piled up haphazardly, giving it the unmistakable feeling of a building still under renovation…

“…It really is a bathhouse.”

It was.

We’d stepped into what seemed to be somewhere between the changing room and the baths.
I swallowed as I looked at the faded, weathered ‘Namhae Hot Spring’ sign, then continued forward.

My heart was still pounding.

*‘The hoses…’*

At least for now, they weren’t coming through the door.

Phew.

“Let’s head farther inside. If we can find an emergency exit, we might be able to get out.”

“……”

Jin Nasol trudged ahead.

As I passed beneath the glow of an emergency light, I caught sight of a notice.

<br>

@c@ Benefits of a Half Bath@c@

@c@ Relieves fatigue, eases muscle pain@c@

@c@ Thank you for all your hard work in exploration and research! ^^@c@

<br>

*‘Exploration and Research?’*

Since it specifically mentioned exploration and research, it seemed this bathhouse had originally been intended for employees, not workers.

*‘But why is it inside the workers’ Off-Duty Zone?’*

I’d expected the two areas to be kept strictly separate, so that was surprising. Or maybe the Off-Duty Zone had originally been for employees?

*‘Whatever the case, at least we managed to get away from those white hoses for now…’*

…

Whenever I thought about the fermentation jar, a strange feeling settled in my chest.

The mix of emotions left my legs trembling, and I had to keep forcing strength into them just to stay on my feet.

All sorts of thoughts ran through my mind.

“……”

I could feel Jin Nasol walking beside me with her teeth clenched…

She was pressing hard against the hand where I’d just cut off her finger.

“Are you alright?”

“Do I look alright?”

“No.”

I shook my head.

“I meant to ask what I could do to help.”

“Hey.”

She’d probably say something like, ‘Hey. What exactly do you think you can do?’

But when Assistant Manager Jin Nasol looked at me, she spoke in a hoarse, strained voice instead.

“If I melt any further, just leave me behind.”

“…!”

“The jar’s gone now. What are you going to do? Besides, you saw it yourself, didn’t you? How the darkness reacts.”

I thought back to the white hoses and cords that had frantically surged toward Assistant Manager Jin Nasol’s severed finger…and toward the liquid spilling from the shattered jar.

“…You mean it’s attracted to liquefied human remains.”

“Yeah. Why do you keep asking about things that are obvious, every single time!”

“……”

“No, seriously, why are you always walking around with that stupid grin on your face? It’s irritating. We’re stuck in this godforsaken hellhole, so what the hell is there to smile about? You smile while you’re eating, you smile at a bed, what the hell is wrong with you?”

Words that sounded like insults just kept pouring out.

*‘No…’*

It seemed that the pain and the hopelessness of the situation were making her act impulsively, just like she had at the mineral spring.

It was honestly terrifying, but… I had to stay calm. I couldn’t panic.

“Well… um.”

I gathered my thoughts before speaking slowly.

“When things are good, when I have the chance to enjoy something, I try to enjoy it while I can and make an effort to relax. That way, I don’t fall apart even when things get difficult.”

“……”

That’s right.

“At least that’s been my experience.”

A few faces and memories drifted through my mind.

The people who’d made it possible for me to grow attached to this objectively horrible world, and the people who’d seen me off.

“So you’re saying you look for little comforts even in a shit situation, just so you can rationalize it?”

“No. It’s just that even if the situation is a complete mess, I still acknowledge the moments that are good when they come.”

I carefully finished speaking.

Then I borrowed a little of Assistant Manager Jin Nasol’s way of speaking.

“I think that’s the healthiest and most efficient way.”

“……”

She continued on in silence.

I quietly matched her pace. Our footsteps echoed through the abandoned bathhouse…

“…I said something pointless.”

“…!”

“It wasn’t something we needed in this situation. I only made things harder.”

“It wasn’t that bad.”

But she didn’t even pay attention to what I said.

“I can’t get my words out like I used to. You know that too, don’t you? Because I became a piece of trash.”

Jin Nasol poured more painkillers into her mouth.

This time, I couldn’t hold back and asked.

“Are you… suffering from constant pain?”

“Yeah.”

A brief silence passed.

Jin Nasol tapped her temple with the hand that had been holding the painkillers.

“I had a piece of metal lodged in my brain from an accident. The thalamus. It’s the part that keeps stimulating pain signals.”

……!!

“Did you join the company to have it removed?”

“I already did.”

“……Excuse me?”

“I had it removed, and this damn pain still won’t go away.”

Crack. The empty painkiller packet crumpled in Jin Nasol’s hand, the veins in it standing out.

“Phantom pain or whatever they call it, even doctors don’t really know the cause. No matter what they do, it only works temporarily. When you take painkillers until your tolerance gets so high they’re basically just a placebo, it can drive a person insane, you know?”

“So that’s… the reason you joined Daydream.”

Because there was no practical way to get rid of the pain.

“Yeah. Daydream’s Dreamscape Painkillers are pretty good. If I take one, I can manage for about two weeks.”

Her voice softened slightly, as if she were imagining it.

“…Um, how many points do they cost?”

“Five thousand.”

I almost screamed.

A hundred doses of painkillers cost the same as a wish ticket?

*‘Could you even call that a painkiller anymore?’*

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol was only using it as one, but it seemed like it was actually some other kind of potion. No, that wasn’t important. The point was…

…If she used painkillers a hundred times, that meant she was spending enough points to buy a wish ticket just on medication.

*‘If that’s how she was spending her points…’*

While she was in Team A, being under Director Ho’s line probably meant she had access to highly efficient assignments, which she could clear one after another. That must have been how she avoided running a point deficit.

*‘But if Director Ho disappeared in that situation…’*

“…Um, how did the Team A leader handle it?”

“She went missing.”

“…!”

“It wasn’t even that serious of a darkness…it was only C-rank, at best.”

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol’s voice was calm, but the content was anything but.

“She went missing because of a single mistake in the manual. Well, I guess that’s the kind of company it is.”

“……”

Was she really… just missing?

Even if it was ‘that kind of company,’ the circumstances were suspicious. But whatever had really happened, the outcome wouldn’t have been any different for Assistant Manager Jin Nasol.

Her entire network was gone.

*‘Then…’*

If the Team A leader—the one middle manager who could at least have built a new line of connections—had disappeared, and she’d been left adrift within the company until her points ran dry…

“Yeah. Things went bad, but there wasn’t anything else that worked as well as those painkillers. I kept borrowing against my points, and that’s how I ended up stuck here.”

“……”

“That’s what you wanted to know, wasn’t it? How I got this way.”

I could neither confirm nor deny it.

Because it was true—I had asked, even knowing the last three years of Assistant Manager Jin Nasol’s life probably hadn’t been pleasant.

Still.

“So… you’re getting by on ordinary painkillers now.”

After everything she’d been through, the fact that she was still this clear-headed was remarkable.

“Does this look like getting by to you? I’m just enduring it.”

“Once we get out of here, let’s look for an item that helps with pain. You know, like the painkiller item I gave you on the train.”

“Yeah.”

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol’s voice softened a little.

“Enough of the pointless talk.”

There was a bigger problem.

“There’s no emergency exit.”

“……”

We’d been searching all around the bathhouse while we talked, but no matter where we looked, there was nothing that could be called an exit.

We were only wandering endlessly, its cracked tiles stretching on and on.

“…I mean, the inside is in such poor condition that an emergency exit or some other hidden way out might be buried behind all these construction materials.”

“So what, now we’re supposed to search for an exit that might not even exist?”

“I’ll think of another way.”

“Why think? We already have one.”

“…Huh?”

“Your item.”

…!

I pulled the glasses case from my pocket.

The first-place prize from the courage test.

“Most items tend to follow the concept they’re based on. And glasses…”

“Conceptually, they’re something that lets you see what can’t be seen.”

“You know well.”

She was right.

There was a high probability that these glasses had the ability to reveal ‘something that normally couldn’t be seen.’

*‘…If we stay in here too long, those white cords might eventually spread everywhere and make their way in here too.’*

If the Personnel-Only area had been completely safe, the employees wouldn’t have fled in the first place.

*‘In that case, while we still have some time, trying the glasses here…’*

…was a gamble worth taking.

“…If anything goes wrong, please tell me to take the glasses off right away.”

“Okay.”

I took a deep breath and pulled the black-rimmed glasses from the case labeled ‘Clear Water Optical.’

The moment I put them on—

“…!”

“What is it?”

I sucked in a sharp breath.

Beyond the lenses, the bathhouse looked completely intact.

The jade-colored tiles, warm fluorescent lights, and a public sauna that looked comfortable and inviting all came into view.

Though old, the well-maintained employee welfare facility had a familiar warmth that made you want to look around.

“…I can see the bathhouse as it looked when it was operating normally.”

Even the notice we’d seen earlier had returned to its original state.

Is it showing the past?

If so, then at what point in time?

*‘Wait.’*

Either way…

*‘I can use this to figure out the layout of the place!’*

I quickly swept my gaze around as I slowly made my way forward.

Beyond the glasses, the bathhouse was filled with rising steam and every bath brimmed with shimmering water, but there wasn’t a single person in sight.

I alternated between looking through the glasses and at the dilapidated scene beyond them, carefully watching my footing as I surveyed the area.

Here, where steam drifted through the air, everything felt bright and full of life.

It was almost as if I’d stepped into a hidden world beneath the surface.

*‘Wait!’*

I need to focus. First, find the door!

After carefully searching the area, I finally spotted something.

On the wall behind the counter next to the changing room.

Hidden behind a set of shelves… was the outline of a door.

<br>

@c@ EXIT @c@

<br>

*‘Found it.’*

The green emergency light above it was dead, but the sign was unmistakable. An emergency stairwell!

“Assistant Manager, over here!”

Outside the glasses, that section was completely buried beneath broken tiles and construction materials, leaving the wall itself hidden from view.

“Hey, move it.”

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol and I grabbed hold of the tiles and tore them away, revealing the ‘hidden passage’ even in reality.

*‘Good.’*

Now all we had to do was get out of the facility through here…

“Ah.”

<br>

A staircase leading downward came into view.

<br>

“……”

“……”

It’s strange.

To be honest, I’d been almost certain this Namhae Branch facility, run like some kind of forced labor camp, was underground.

There wasn’t a single window, and the interior had the feel of a sealed-off abandoned mine.

I’d even overheard some of the other workers guessing that this place had originally been a coal mine.

*‘But then again, you could recreate that kind of atmosphere indoors anywhere.’*

I couldn’t be certain.

But if this place really was underground…

“This might not be an escape route at all. It could just lead farther into the personnel-only area.”

“So, do you have another idea?”

“No.”

Either way, if we wanted to find a way out, we’d have to search everywhere.

“Then let’s go down.”

“…Alright.”

I cautiously stepped onto the emergency stairwell.

It descended with an almost unnatural straightness, leading directly downward—as though it were guiding us toward some special place.

And unlike before, there was almost no difference between the view through the glasses and the one outside them.

As if everything here had been preserved.

“……”

“……”

At the end of the staircase, we found ourselves facing a single steel door.

There was nothing where the nameplate should have been.

Through the glasses, it was spotless, and the door was firmly shut.

But in reality…

“It’s open.”

It was already ajar by about the width of a finger.

I cautiously grasped the door and peered inside. The glasses seemed to see right through the door as well, revealing what lay beyond…

“…!!”

A vast, circular room.

Dozens of monitors were arranged across the walls in sweeping rows.

At the center stood a massive machine fitted with a keyboard and computer, like some kind of control console, with a chair positioned before it…

One image immediately sprang to mind.

“…It looks like a central control room.”

But in reality, most of the monitors had been smashed, the machine’s buttons were broken, and a thick layer of dust covered everything.

It looked as though it hadn’t been used for a long time. No, it seemed as though there had never been any sign of life here at all.

“This doesn’t seem like a facility that’s currently in use.”

“Yeah.”

But if this really had been a central control room at some point in the past, that was an incredible sign.

There was a high chance we’d be able to see the entire structure of the branch at a glance, including where the escape routes were!

And if I used these glasses, there was also a good chance the monitors would appear fully functional.

*‘This is huge.’*

I swallowed and approached.

“I’ll try turning it on.”

I hurried toward the machine installed in the center and pressed what looked like a red power button—

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol grabbed me and pulled me back.

“Hey. Take off the glasses. Now.”

“…!?”

But instead of asking why, I quickly removed them. After all, I was the one who had asked her to stop me if anything went wrong.

<br>

Wait.

*‘Then what happened just now…?’*

At that moment.

A tremendous pain overwhelmed my eyes.

“……!!”

It felt as if someone were clawing at them with their fingers.

*‘Crazy!’*

Tears streamed down my face. No, they’d probably been flowing from the start—I just hadn’t noticed. What the hell was this? Clenching my teeth, I hurriedly wiped the tears away with the inner fabric near the collar of my work uniform.

It was blood.

“……”

“It’s not getting any worse. Good. From now on, be careful of that side effect.”

“Yes, yes.”

Wow, that’s terrifying.

I endured it for a moment, pressing a hand over my still-throbbing eyes.

<br>

**Black-Rimmed Glasses**

After use, severe eye pain and bloody tears occur. Be mindful of usage duration.

<br>

I guess I can summarize it like this. Hahaha…

And when I opened my eyes, Assistant Manager Jin Nasol was examining the glasses case in my hand.

Her eyes held a slight mix of admiration and desire for the item.

“That’s not too bad. It didn’t even make you go completely blind or anything, right?”

“……Yes.”

The fact that I was starting to understand her unbelievably harsh standards was frightening in itself…

Anyway.

I took a deep breath, cleared my eyes, and looked back at the half-broken control panel in front of me.

“Thank you. Then I’ll try turning it on…”

“Okay.”

I raised my hand and, after taking another deep breath, pressed the red power button.

At that moment.
