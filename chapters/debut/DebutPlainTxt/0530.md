# Chapter 530

Backstage, after the American Music Awards began, the frontman of a band took a deep breath.

"Hoo."

He was scheduled to perform in the upcoming group performance. As a local band member, this was his first time on such a large, major broadcast stage. He had firm faith in his skills, however.

*I need to calm down. I can do this.*

He repeated this to himself and tried to look around with a relaxed expression.

Then, he noticed the boy waiting right next to him.

"......"

The Asian boy with black hair and an indifferent expression had shown considerable skill during rehearsal, so the frontman remembered him. The boy's calm, powerful voice seemed to define him as a singer.

Partly to shake off his own nervousness, the frontman tapped the boy on the shoulder and started a conversation.

"Hey, how're you feeling?"

"Good."

The boy replied shortly in a calm tone.

The frontman noticed the lack of a smile and the boy's unsociable attitude, guessing at the tension he felt.

*Of course, he would be.*

The frontman was considerate of him.

"You should know, it's not embarrassing at all to be nervous before a big stage like this."

"Of course not."

The boy readily nodded his head. Then he added.

"You don't seem nervous."

"Of course."

The frontman felt pleased.

"It's a matter of experience. I've stood in front of quite a few crowds..."

"You look like it."

The boy nodded calmly. The frontman felt the small talk genuinely easing his tension.

He shared stories from a few stages he had experienced, as if giving advice. The boy seemed to listen intently, nodding slightly the whole time.

*He's a decent kid.*

To show that he was an open-minded person, the frontman willingly asked.

"So... where in this country are you based? I'm from the South."

"Cali, if I have to pick."

"Oh, is that your hometown?"

"A member of my band is from San Diego."

Honestly, it was unexpected.

*I thought people from that state didn't know the meaning of the word 'serious'?*

It was a thought full of stereotypes, but the suspicion itself was quite accurate.

*There's no way that kid was born on the Pacific Ocean coast.*

In any case, chattering on about someone's place of origin was rude, so the frontman ended the conversation there.

"Hey, how about we take a picture?"

"Sure."

The boy responded with surprising ease, and they took a few selfies.

"You must have a lot of experience taking pictures with your fans, huh?"

"More than a few."

"......"

The kid seemed quite sincere.

*He's got guts.*

The frontman concluded.

In reality, the person he called a boy was just thinking,*There's no reason to get into a long conversation with this guy,*but what was good was good.

Regardless of the boy's thoughts, the frontman continued the pleasant conversation by himself, eventually arriving at this topic.

"By the way, do you like tacos? I mean, I'm half Latino."

It was a polite probe, indirectly asking about the other's heritage.

The other person responded readily.

"I'm Korean."

The frontman's mind reflexively jumped to a Netplus drama. Fortunately, a musical topic suitable for the situation came to mind one after another.

"Oh, do you listen to KPOP?"

The boy did not answer for a moment.

"Quite a lot."

For the first time, he smiled.

Park Moondae, whom the frontman had mistaken for a boy due to his styling, moved away with a staff member after that response.

It was time to prepare for the stage.

***

In the awards ceremony hall, the host's clear voice filled the splendid venue.

[...And so, we will remember forever!]

It was the introductory comment for the tribute stage.

This stage was for an American rock band that had fiercely dominated the 70s. The band's vocalist, who had been embroiled in controversy until his later years, passed away this year. He became a huge topic of conversation by suddenly donating his entire fortune to society.

As a result, the awards ceremony planned a tribute stage for the deceased.

They selected an extremely emotional and slow-tempo song from the band's hits.

Taking a cue from the deceased's interviews, various rising band vocalists would sing the parts in succession, especially rookies with a strong local color.

The maximization of storytelling!

The stage's purpose was to maximize emotion and empathy. So these rookies, aside from a few who got in through connections, were genuinely outstanding in skill.

Everyone thought the same thing.

*This is the opportunity I've been waiting for.*

It was a rare opportunity that almost never came around at American awards shows, where famous contemporary singers usually performed tribute stages.

They would sing this song, which people all over the world had heard, not as a simple cover but on a major broadcast at an official event.

All the vocalists were fired up with passion and determination.

*I have to prove my talent.*

With such thoughts, the vocalists took the stage with resolute hearts.

VWOOOOM-!

ROAR!

The stage, which they reached via a creaking lift, overflowed with an overwhelming amount of stimulation.

Applause, lights, the MR track, and sound effects violently shook their senses of sight and hearing.

Of course, there was no consideration to give them time to adjust to this huge stage. The song began to play immediately.

DUNG.

The passionate instrumental began with a drum beat.

The vocalist standing on the stage set skillfully kicked things off, even in that situation.

[Not everyone wants

The memory of love]

A refreshing voice raced along, brilliantly using the off-beat rhythm in a way that was hard to believe for someone on a big stage for the first time.

When he threw in an ad-lib during the instrumental break, a cheer erupted from the audience.

*Oh, damn it.*

The frontman who had been talking to Park Moondae almost bit his lip while waiting.

His turn was next.

*Now!*

He confirmed the light on the camera in front of him had turned on, grabbed the mic, and sang.

[I wake at dawn and see the damp bed

Like traces of raindrops

Salty like the sea]

A pleasant voice filled the verse.

[It's like the waves have crashed in

I can barely endure]

*I did it!*

The frontman, who had successfully delivered the high notes with his own vibe, finished his part in a state of euphoria.

Then the camera light went off, and the next person appeared on the big screen.

[I cried away the past

Remembering the good old days.

I don't want to do that anymore]

Another cheer erupted at the rough rocker's voice.

Every following part was unique, with good vocalists shining with their own colors. Since the parts were arranged with harmony in mind, it sounded wonderful even when listened to continuously.

*How well did I do?*

He could not gauge it.

The frontman quickly concluded that it was better to hope the stage itself became famous and decided to just enjoy the moment.

His tension eased, and he noticed a figure on standby on the side stage next to him. It was the boy he had been talking to earlier.

The frontman remembered that the kid's part was in the latter half.

*Does he come on around the 2-minute 40-second mark?*

He recalled, however, that it was not the climax but an ambiguous chorus section before it.

*Good luck, friend!*

Having already finished his part well, he basked in the moment and encouraged the audience's response. When the time came, he shifted his gaze slightly to check on the boy.

Finally, the song was heard.

[Not everyone wants

The memory of love]

A strong, clear voice.

And a brilliant connecting note that dropped into a pleasant, growling-like singing style.

A sharp high note.

*Huh?*

It was a singing style completely different from the calm voice during rehearsal.

His surprise was not just because the boy was singing the song in a completely different way than in rehearsal. Nor was it just the skill and technique.

[The world says

Love is the best.

I'm not saying that's wrong

Oh

I don't want to remember]

On stage.

Having come up from the side stage, he was singing exactly like the original artist.

***

I will be honest.

From the beginning, I had not considered blending in harmoniously with this tribute stage to raise the overall quality.

*Isn't that the job of the person who planned the stage?*

What did they expect when I had zero authority over anything from the arrangement to the part distribution?

I am sorry, but I had no intention of honoring the planning intention of 'a stage where the unique voices of various local bands come together'.

Not when there was a method this effective.

[Oh!]

I checked my own singing on the screen and also checked the number of comments below it.

[12,721 Comments]

Just one hour.

It meant that in the short time since the video was posted, less than an hour, this many people had their mouths wide open.

*Of course, considering the unofficial videos that circulated on social media as soon as the stage ended, it's even more.*

All in all, there was no better bait for drawing attention.

Recreating the original singer's performance all by myself on a tribute stage.

[(2:46) Seriously, who the fuck is this guy?]

*I know, right.*

I smiled and tapped my smartphone.

But let me be clear.

*Simply mimicking him won't get me the effect I want.*

Singing styles change with the trends over time.

Have you ever listened to a song you liked about ten years ago again after a very long time? Then you have probably been surprised by how awkward the singer sounded, how unfamiliar it was.

That is because your mind has already refined the vocals you heard long ago into a modern, cool version.

*I can't have people feeling that sense of disconnect when they watch my stage.*

That would actually lessen the impact.

I do not want to be told I mimicked him. I want to be told that the moment they heard my song, the original singer immediately came to mind.

*The image people have of the original singer's performance.*

The impression they would have felt back in the old days.

My goal was to make them recall that.

So, it was important to find the 'way of singing' that was appropriately adjusted for modern times, the one that existed in people's minds.

*It took a ridiculously long time.*

[Noooope.]

[Sing it stronger! More!]

Let's take this opportunity to thank the American, Cha Eugene.

His honest reactions from a local's perspective were a great help. Though I did have a reality check when he rejected my attempts about ten times.

*Thanks to him, I raised the level of completion as much as I possibly could.*

Given my vocal grade, it was a satisfying result.

[Realizing again that Park Moondae is damn good at singing and smart]

[Wow, I never imagined he'd come on here and kill it lol]

All the way to these kinds of troll-like reactions.

However, it was nothing I could not handle. It is not like there were only one or two bastards who used TeSTAR to stir up trouble.

*I got off easy.*

It was something that would be buried once the comeback promotions progressed further anyway.

Besides, the company was planning to release a fancam soon, so the fans' disappointment could be sufficiently soothed.

There was also a perfectly valid reason that fans could immediately think of so they would not be stressed about why I sang this way.

This was not just a simple cover stage.

If anything, I hope this pro-and-con debate continues for a little longer.

*The other vocalists on this stage will probably benefit from this too.*

Thanks to the attention I drew, the performance definitely became a bigger topic than it would have if it had proceeded according to the original plan.

Of course, if I just left it at that, there was a high probability that it would fizzle out again instead of sucking up all the interest for TeSTAR.

Just like the few times before when we were satisfied with drawing in some new fans with a music video.

But I would not be satisfied with that this time.
