# Chapter 569

"I'm gonna go get some popcorn."

"Resist the urge."

In the dead of night, while the other members slept, three of us from TeSTAR gathered in the dark living room. We used the TV to browse the internet, searching for information.

We were looking into the rookie 'co-ed' idol group that had pushed us aside to take first place on the music charts.

Halo Heim.

*[I'm not even curious who came up with this weird name anymore.]*

"......"

As Cha Eugene, a native speaker, gave his assessment, the co-ed group's debut song started playing on the screen.

*[Now I'm curious about the seat next to me*

*Your Heartbeat*

*Who is it beating for right now]*

The song was up-tempo, catchy, and naturally pleasant. A refreshing, atmospheric melody perfect for a café flowed from the instrumental.

"The song is good."

"I agree."

However, singers released great songs every quarter only to disappear without seeing the light of day. For a co-ed group to hit number one just a week after release, without even a gradual climb up the charts...

"There must be another selling point."

"Yap."

With Lee Sejin and Cha Eugene's unspoken agreement, I continued the search.

It didn't take long. A forty-minute related video with over five million views had popped up right beside the music video. It was their own original content. The thumbnail showed several Halo Heim members in school uniforms, smiling as they faced each other in a crisscross pattern.

*[Are you happy because I'm a transfer student? ❬Heart Heim❭ Ep.2 / Halo Heim]*

"......"

The title's layout felt a bit out of place for an idol's original content. It was, however, a familiar format I had often seen on WeTube.

"......"

I found and played the first episode. A narration began over a shot of a bench on the school's lush, green grounds.

*[Haven't you ever wanted to meet someone who's a perfect match for you?]*

*[Are you the type of person who always clicks on 'the type most compatible with me' whenever you take an MBTI, sentence completion, or any other kind of personality test?]*

*[This is our story, a search for that person.]*

"Ah."

The moment I saw that, I knew what this was. I crossed my arms and groaned inwardly.

*Insane.*

These bastards at the agency had blatantly benchmarked a reality dating show.

Co-ed groups are prone to dating rumors? They had turned that weakness on its head and used it to build the group's entire worldview, telling viewers to create and analyze their own dramas as they pleased.

*[Jihyeon: Hello. I'm Kang Jihyeon, born in Busan.]*

*[Jihyeon: My personality... I think I'm on the quiet side. Even at school... (Edited) Yes. I even deliberately skipped school trips.]*

The video, set in a fictional school, first introduced four female students, each with a different personality. Then, the male members appeared, having 'transferred' to the school.

*[Male Student 1: Nice to meet you. I'm Go Hyeonwoo.]*

Through various team missions with a school theme, the students learned about each other's personalities and behaviors in more detail. They did everything from lab group projects and cooking practice to performance evaluations and even a test of courage.

At the end of each day, everyone anonymously texted one person of the opposite sex and one person of the same sex who they felt they matched with best.

*[Jihyeon: Um... I liked how Hyeonwoo was scared without trying to act tough. It seemed honest and nice. I'll send my text to Hyeonwoo.]*

*[Hyeonwoo: Wasn't Jihyeon really reliable today?]*

The show revealed who got along, who didn't, and who was developing feelings for whom. They even displayed a chart showing the chaotic crisscrossing of 'love sticks' and 'friendship sticks'.

"......"

"......"

Watching the utter chaos, the three of us from TeSTAR were too stunned to react. It was culture shock.

*...It's a blatant dating reality show.*

It was a transparent charade. Still, as I endured the shock and looked closer, I saw it wasn't just chaotic clickbait. The show was meticulously crafted.

For instance, the 'school' setting was handled just like a reality show's backdrop. It created a feeling where everyone knew it was fake but still became moderately invested.

*[Jaebin: This is a school concept, right?]*

*[Hugo: That's why it's fun. I've barely attended a Korean school.]*

They spoke comfortably, not giving the impression they were acting. This was done to make each individual feel much more natural and truthful.

At a decisive moment in every episode, their debut title track would play like an OST.

*[This trembling is still Only in my heart*

*I can act nonchalant]*

The song's highlight melody, which also played right before cutting to the preview, was a perfect fit. They managed to get the song exposure in a very natural way.

*[Jaebin, please, Hugo is a genuine guy. Let's team up with Hugo, give him a chance ㅠㅠ]*

*[Honestly, Hyeonwoo and Jihyeon are the best match. Their personalities fit well. Stop wasting energy on pointless things and just show us the two of them being cute on a mission together.]*

*[Am I the only one who thinks Jihyeon and Hugo are cute? The way they grumble about not getting along is so adorableㅋㅋㅋ]*

└*Ikr, their hate-to-love dynamic is delicious*

Gradually, instead of curses like 'Are you crazy for doing this with idols?', comments from overly invested people received more upvotes and rose to the top. The program was well-produced, making it fun and suspenseful.

And what's more.

"...I heard that if a pair is chosen as the 'best match' by viewer votes, they get to be the center for the next album."

"...Wow~"

Lee Sejin couldn't finish his sentence. I understood. Still, he managed to complete a different one.

"This... No, with this, public opinion would be impossible to manage."

"I suppose so."

It was a method that would make existing idol fans faint. That was probably why the video's views initially came from people who already liked dating reality shows.

The fact that these people were overly invested was an even bigger problem. In episode two, one member was getting horribly flamed. The reason was that they had suddenly and randomly chosen a different person of the opposite sex during the home economics practice.

*[That's not polite, that's really too much]*

*[Wow fr, the type of person you wouldn't even want as a friend.. I guess there's a reason they didn't get a single friendship textㅋ]*

*[They seemed shady in Be My Girls too, as expectedㅋ]*

The malicious comments from people who identified with the 'stolen' partner really crossed the line. On top of that, the show even included unfiltered scenes of members getting on each other's nerves and then flaring up into fights.

*What a spectacle.*

Using kids barely over twenty as punching bags was bullshit that all sorts of broadcasting companies pulled, but for an agency to do this in its own show was fucking next level.

*Isn't this an atmosphere where it's impossible for group fans to even form?*

Even for us, solo stans we gained during ❬Idol Inc.❭ still dominated the atmosphere. The influence of a pre-debut survival show is this strong, so to do something like this with idols who have already debuted...

"Normally, they would self-destruct."

That's how it should have been.

However.

"But it worked."

"......"

This original content, a clever mix of a web drama and a reality show, seemed to be gaining immense immersion and response, mainly from teenagers of the same age group.

The next to join were the viewers of dating programs. These people made up a huge portion of the current viewership.

So what was the benefit of attracting these people?

"It created word-of-mouth."

Posts from overly invested people started popping up all over the internet like mushrooms after rain.

*[Heart Heim is surprisingly fun]*

*[They're going on a courage test and their partner told them to go alone.jpg]*

*[Ah, holy crap, is that Jihyeon the one from Be My Girls?]*

└*Jihyeon debuted?*

Then, hardcore K-pop listeners who had casually watched the minor survival show ❬Be My Girls❭ joined in, and when the global K-pop fans who already like this kind of thing jumped on board...

*The view count explodes.*

Of course, traces of the storm of curses, backlash, and controversy were also starkly visible.

First of all, what about the fandoms of the members who were supposed to debut from ❬Be My Girls❭?

*They must have been dying inside.*

They probably thought their idols were finally debuting, only for the agency to pull this bullshit. They must have been foaming at the mouth. However, they probably couldn't protest too strongly.

*There was already a vote-rigging controversy attached to them.*

Even if they could argue the members were not at fault, the scandal was so huge that they would have been cautious about creating any more trouble. They probably tried to deal with the company quietly but eventually gave up when the public reaction started getting hot and positive.

*And like that, even all the negative chemical reactions are digested as buzz.*

Malicious comments are better than no comments. Because it's still attention. As long as you're confident in the quality of the product you're selling.

*Public opinion can be overturned.*

And the agency had set the stage for just that.

*[What... Ah, so she acted that way because she thought the letter was from Hyeonwooㅠㅠ]*

As the episodes went on, the member who was being flamed had the misunderstandings cleared up, showed a more three-dimensional side, and gradually recovered their public image. The main point-of-view character kept changing, drawing empathy from similar types of people with each episode.

In the end, they managed to appeal to both the romantic and character-based charms of every member.

And just at the moment when the audience's favorability had ripened, Halo Heim's first music show performance blew up. This was their winning move.

*[Wow, they're so damn good]*

*[I acknowledge them as idols...]*

They performed well on stage. The viewers reacted as if they were watching the main characters of their favorite drama perform. The thrill and novelty of seeing imagination become reality created an unexpected synergy.

*[I'm getting emotional. Halo Heim is a real-life dramaㅠㅠ]*

One wrong step and the discomfort would have outweighed the interest, but whether by luck or calculation, they broke through it all, and just like that, the co-ed group had successfully launched.

"......"

In the dark of dawn, in front of the TV in TeSTAR's living room, the three of us were lost in thought. We had just watched the performance of a rookie group that succeeded in an unexpected way.

"OK. I get it."

Cha Eugene was the first to speak, shrugging.

"This team is good on stage, but we're better, so it's fine."

It was a calm statement.

"What about the mixed-gender aspect?"

"Why do you ask?"

"Because there have been almost no successful co-ed groups in Korea since the 2000s."

Cha Eugene replied with a look that said it was obvious.

"Hey, no trend lasts forever."

"...!"

"K-pop groups aren't a tradition either. The trend can change at any time."

That... is true. There was no guarantee that co-ed groups, popular in the 2000s, would never work in the market again. Even if they worked in a completely unexpected form.

"Our group just needs to keep doing what we do well. Got it?"

"Of course~ Got it."

Lee Sejin replied with a laugh. Cha Eugene waved, then strode to the kitchen to return his water cup and came right back.

However, Lee Sejin remained on the sofa, frowning, until Cha Eugene returned. He seemed lost in thought.

"Why."

"No... It's just, the more I think about it, the weirder it is."

Lee Sejin muttered, looking at the turned-off TV.

"This is a method they can't use in the long run."

"......"

"Why?"

"Eugene, you said no trend lasts forever, right? Well, no couple lasts forever either."

"...!"

"I don't know what they'll do from the next album, but it feels like they've burned through their image too quickly. Hmm, it makes you think, 'Wow, that's very experimental~', right?"

It was true. It was nearly impossible to use that kind of promotional method again and achieve the same effect. From TeSTAR's perspective, it seemed like if we just waited, the other side would quickly run out of steam and be unable to hold on.

Of course, you should never make guarantees in this industry.

"For now, let's stay vigilant and think it over."

"Right."

That's how the early morning monitoring session ended. The three of us quietly tidied up the living room and returned to our rooms.

I also lay down on my bed, briefly lost in thought.

*A new method.*

It was a short-lived, extreme promotional method that ground up people's private lives and personalities.

However, another thought occurred to me.

*[Isn't the idol group model we're following now also one with a short lifespan?]*

Even successful idol groups were seen as having an average lifespan of about seven years. With a few exceptions, they would all eventually go their separate ways, focusing on individual activities to find their own paths. This was due to the burden of living together, the strain of high-intensity choreography, and the issue of an appropriate age.

*We and VTIC, who want to keep going for longer, are the strange ones.*

Considering that.

*Is this method also just part of a trend of consuming idols more quickly...?*

"......"

I didn't know. For some reason, I felt dirty.

*It feels different, though.*

Maybe it's because I'm from a different age group, but this felt... somewhat unpleasant. The way that company promoted their co-ed group.

*It encourages consumption in a fucking weird way.*

Especially when I thought of those rookies who gritted their teeth and performed so well on stage.

"......"

I decided to just go to sleep. Instead of worrying about competitors, I should worry about our own group, which has a member going to the military next year, and I shouldn't forget the insane fact that I have to go to the military twice.

*...Hoo.*

However, strangely, even with that thought, my mind didn't clear, and sleep didn't come.

Like a bad premonition.

That I was missing something...

"......"

Just now. What did I list as the reasons for an idol group's short lifespan?

Living together, appropriate age, and...

*High-intensity choreography...*

"......"

Wait.

"...!"

I immediately shot up in bed and grabbed my smartphone. I went online at once, into the undercurrents I hadn't bothered to monitor because there hadn't been any particular controversies.

The moment I looked with awareness, certain phrases jumped out at me.

*[Epic has no choreo either, fuckㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋ]*

*[Hmm... I'm a bit bitter, wondering if Syeombyeol is also going down this path in the end]*

*[Ah, this is why the song shouldn't have done well on the charts. Now Syeombyeol will think this kind of thing works, fuck]*

*[There goes another 'venomous' idol group that grinds out synchronized choreo through practice]*

I felt like I'd been doused with cold water.

*An idol group's peak period is short-lived.*

When that time comes, they get rid of the dorm, individual activities increase, and the proportion of synchronized choreography decreases. Many groups have gone through this.

*This is practically a formula by now.*

It was a fact that all idol fans now knew. Therefore, TeSTAR's latest album was taken as a kind of signal by these people.

An attempt to test the waters, to see if TeSTAR could abandon their existing concept and switch to a more comfortable style.

A sign that they no longer intended to grit their teeth and live fiercely as a peak idol group. A sign that the very reason they originally liked TeSTAR was disappearing and would be replaced.

*[It's not actually a bad thing. Honestly, the boys need to think about their cartilage now, and they'll be going to the military soon.., but what I'm saying is, my heart isn't racing anymore.]*

*[Taking recommendations for rookie idols. Aren't there any groups full of grit?]*

*No.*

Right now, it's just people who deliberately dig into the undercurrents who are making this noise. Most people don't bother to express their dissatisfaction. Because they think it's a natural progression.

*They just... slowly start looking into other idols.*

As activities decrease, their hearts grow distant.

"......"

Cha Eugene would probably say something like this.

*[Let's release a new album quickly. Then there's no problem.]*

He'd mean we should appeal to them that we're still working hard.

*That's the right answer.*

But fuck, the bigger problem is... we can't do that right now.

*We already promoted with Epic!*

The fact that it did so well has actually become a problem! The public's immediate expectation of us has been shaped in that direction. A song that's popular, a little comfortable, and easy to listen to.

But if we suddenly announce a new album in this atmosphere? They would naturally expect another song like that.

*But we prepared this full album to be extremely intense and conceptual.*

Because we stuck to the path of targeting the global fandom and aiming for the Order of Cultural Merit [勳章].

So what happens then?

*[Oh, the song is good]*

*[Hmm, it's a bit different from what I expected, but I'm cheering for you~ Hope TeSTAR succeeds this time too!^^]*

And the moment the song doesn't rank well on the charts...

*It's likely to chip away at both our buzz and our status...*

"......"

I buried my face in my hands.

We're fucked.
