# Chapter 500

Park Moondae orchestrated Spacer's comeback. At first, it seemed like a clever move to exploit the hiatus of top-tier male idol groups. He timed it for just after Cheongryeo's promotions ended and while TeSTAR's own comeback was still in planning.

A few months ago, the news of a 'Spacer comeback announcement' made little splash. This was only natural.

[Spacer, Entering the Music Scene This Fall... "Preparing for an Intense Comeback"]

[Finally ㅠㅠ]

[Looking forward to it, fighting~]

[Who are these guys? lol]

[Intense lol]

Aside from a few malicious comments, most reactions came from existing Spacer fans. With no official announcement, public anticipation was almost nonexistent.

That was all there was to it back then.

As time passed, only Spacer's fans seemed to remember the planned comeback.

Then, one group began a massive comeback promotion.

[Aether, Comeback Announced for October! The Monster Rookies Advance]

It was Aether.

After leaving a spectacular impression with their debut, they were making another comeback within the year to maintain their momentum.

This was also expected.

*They're solidifying their claim on the Rookie of the Year award.*

It also meant they were confident in their song and concept. Park Moondae guessed they had prepared it alongside their debut track.

*I knew they'd come out in the fall. They need time before the year ends for their performance to be reflected in the awards.*

In a way, it was a blessing that TeSTAR's Water Bomb performance had completely buried Aether's appearance on Summer Pool.

Aether's fans quietly let it slide, avoiding any brutal comparisons.

With their status as a major rookie group intact, Aether's agency, Wonder Hall, set a comeback date and announced pre-orders to the media.

The third week of October!

*Good.*

Park Moondae noted it with satisfaction.

A short while later, a series of articles appeared as if on cue.

[Orbit Stars Entertainment, "Spacer's Comeback is in Early October"]

[October Music Scene, a Boy Idol Battle is Coming... "From Spacer to Aether"]

['World Tour KPOP' Spacer's Comeback, Will an October Boy Idol Match Happen?]

Spacer's specific comeback schedule was announced. The articles subtly emphasized that Aether was making a comeback around the same time.

Their comebacks were only two weeks apart.

And Spacer was going first.

This meant Spacer could not use the strategy of hitting Aether's declining chart performance after their peak to create an image of victory.

Instead, the situation was perfect for Aether to easily crush Spacer.

[Is this a clash of the 2nd-tier groups?]

[Aether is totally going to crush them, this is gonna be fun, getting my popcorn.]

[lol What guts does ORBIT have? Is it because they're a small-to-mid-sized company?]

└Looks like they lost their touch after raising TeSTAR tsk tsk

Of course, most netizens interested in idols were far more interested in Aether.

Spacer was no longer a rookie group.

Furthermore, TeSTAR's existence as a superior version in origin and ability had negatively affected Spacer's buzz and image from the start.

It could not be helped.

[I'm already excited to see what kind of god-tier concept Aether will bring next. I've been waiting for a rookie like this on my K-pop journey.]

[Oh, if Aether just puts out a good song this time, they'll be confirmed as a 1st-tier group lol. The bullies who specialize in has-beens lol.]

[lol It'd be hilarious if Spacer hastily tries to go the concept-heavy route only to get pushed aside by Aether. They're TeSTAR's juniors, after all.]

Only low-level, provocative interest maintained their buzz.

Besides, idol-focused communities and social media were already buzzing about another topic.

It was the collaboration stage between Cheongryeo and Park Moondae.

Fan reactions to the unexpected collaboration between two top-tier male idols were intense.

For reference, the reactions were something like this.

[This is dogshit.]

[The muggle X-bastards are comparing them like crazy. This is all because of that crazy bastard Kim Taein who took the money earned from VTIC and dumped it into the Syeombyeol company, calling it an 'investment'. Die.]

[Don't link them, fuck.]

"......"

It was a heavy topic.

Still, the public reaction was good.

People praised both Cheongryeo and Park Moondae and found their unexpected friendship entertaining. The well-produced stage drew great interest and became a huge hit.

The meta aspect of two people from different groups harmoniously singing two songs with completely different concepts seemed especially impressive and fun.

It was so popular it even ranked on WeTube's popular videos list overseas.

[The moment a wish I didn't even know I had came true]

[Ooooh my god they are so cool! 😍]

[Oh my god, when did they do a stage like this? Right, I don't need to know why. I just want to see more stages like this! 🔥]

[VT Star. That's my dream group ☺]

However, as its popularity grew, the core fans on both sides felt their moods plummet. It was inevitable.

The two fandoms had been hostile for years over all sorts of incidents. Now they had to see their group's member laughing with a rival from a show with a deep history of competition.

[Please let's just stop here. If they plan some joint trip or something, I'll kill them.]

[I didn't see ORBIT that way, but a shitty small company is a shitty small company. Why did they have to take an investment from LeTi of all people? Are they crazy?]

Of course, they could not say such things openly. The stage's good reception was an undeniable fact.

So, the prevailing trend was to share cropped GIFs and videos of only one member on social media. They tried to rationalize the situation and move on as positively as possible.

In short, it was a time of provocation and turmoil.

A comeback from Aether and Spacer seemed insignificant in the midst of all that.

To TeSTAR's fandom, it was a minor annoyance. To the public, it was not a big deal.

[If the copycats are giving you a stomachache, let's watch Water Bomb to recover.]

[They'll probably use media play to bash our boys again, it's fucking annoying.]

[I'm just going to focus on Syeombyeol ㅇㅇ]

Thanks to their latest album's massive success and the public recognition from Water Bomb, the fans were not too anxious.

Everyone assumed Spacer and Aether would have their comebacks in that same bland atmosphere.

That was, until Spacer's teaser was released.

[Spacer 'Hello' Teaser]

The teaser itself did not cause a huge stir.

It showed short cuts of the Spacer members enjoying their daily lives, set to a pleasant song.

[So handsome ㅠㅠ]

[Their voices... OMG]

[THIS SONG WILL BE AMAZING]

The song was addictive even on a single, fleeting listen.

Spacer's composer member created the topline, Kim Raebin helped with the arrangement, and Park Moondae verified it with his 'Catchy Ear' trait. The result was sleek and well-polished.

It was an ambitious plan.

However, the general public did not seek out a teaser from a male idol group they were not interested in. Domestic and international fans left the usual comments.

But the teaser was not the only place to hear Spacer's new title track.

A few days later.

[Is this our boys' song?;; (link)]

A fan posted a link on social media.

*What is this?*

Fans who clicked the link without a second thought were soon horrified.

"...!"

They heard the teaser's melody and the familiar voices of the Spacer members.

But the video was not uploaded by their agency, Orbit Stars.

[My own hydration, Delicious Sports Drink!]

[Honey Shake Water]

The ad showed various people drinking a certain beverage refreshingly after a hard day.

The fifteen-second clip was a WeTube ad for a new drink.

Spacer's title track was its background music.

[??]

[What is this?]

It was a CM song.

Spacer had provided a version of their title track with slightly altered lyrics for the commercial. The company was marketing its new product very aggressively.

For free!

Naturally, it was a private agreement.

Park Moondae had nearly used the System to find this company. It had an ad campaign scheduled around Spacer's comeback and was known for aggressive marketing.

*Damn this lack of intel.*

In any case, the working-level staff, with a bonus on the line, succeeded. The contract was signed.

In reality, it was more like a one-sided donation from Orbit Stars Entertainment.

Still, the company might not have signed if they had no expectations for the song, but the name value of Kim Raebin, listed as the arranger, was significant.

*He's the guy who composed an already successful OST.*

The company finally stamped the contract upon hearing that the composer of 'Black Hole', the song from the recent Water Bomb concert, was the main arranger.

And so, Spacer's song now reached smartphone users all over the country.

[Hi, Hi, Hello

Can you hear this voice

If you can, please answer me now]

Park Moondae deliberately logged out of his WeTube account to check the ad's exposure frequency.

*It's considerable.*

He was very satisfied it would broadcast like this for almost a week.

*Our company isn't in a position to aggressively increase TV exposure right now anyway.*

The agency had lost many broadcast appearances after its falling out with T1.

They could manage with TeSTAR, last year's Grand Prize winner, and Mirynay, a female idol with good public appeal, but the number of programs Spacer could appear on was truly small.

*In that case, I'll expose them to the public through other means.*

And so, various versions of the beverage company's ad broadcast on WeTube.

This meant the catchiest part of Spacer's new title track, the chorus, was now stuck in users' minds.

[What song is this?]

└Found it, it's this one (link)

└thx thx

This was a strategy a top-tier group could not use.

Releasing a title track as a commercial song could damage an album's image or a group's brand value.

However, the effect was certain.

As long as the song was good, increasing its exposure was the surest form of promotion.

Moreover, it was an unbiased exposure.

*Even people who automatically skip boy idol songs might react differently if it's first imprinted on them as a CM song.*

The Spacer members had agreed to try it as soon as they heard the plan.

This was especially true since the members had participated in this album more than any other.

Simply put, the Spacer members were desperate.

[Spacer Kwon Heeseung: I just hope even one more person listens to it, really ㅠㅠ]

So nothing held them back.

Mass appeal.

Everything was set up to achieve that one goal.

Furthermore, there was another decisive piece to the plan.

[Easy to watch lol]

[The song is good, enjoyed the video]

Spacer had made their music video clean and simple.

It had no excessive concepts, world-building, or fictional elements, just a neat story.

The music video presented sporty dance scenes on an outdoor basketball court and a story of boys uploading the video to social media. It was not overwhelming.

It maximized visual beauty and created a pleasant feeling.

The low barrier to entry meant fewer people were turned off by the music video.

And so, Spacer's new song began to spread by word of mouth.

[My pick for this year's best commercial CM (subjective, I could be wrong, if you disagree you're right)]

[Did you know this was a Spacer song?]

[Honey Shake? What do I search to find this ad song?]

***

Five days later, Spacer's song slowly climbed the music charts and entered the top 40.

This was their highest rank since their debut album right after ❬Idol Inc.❭.

[Spacer Kwon Heeseung: Aaaaaaaargh]

[Spacer Kwon Heeseung: ㅠㅠㅠㅠㅠWill it go up more? No, I'm satisfied even if it doesn't, but no, should I be more ambitious? What do you think?]

[Calm down]

Park Moondae chuckled at Kwon Heeseung's screaming text.

*It'll be hard for the song to climb much higher if things continue like this.*

There was one more disadvantage to using it as a CM song.

Fatigue builds up.

Watching a forced ad repeatedly can also create antipathy.

*And the effect starts to drop off once people realize it's an idol song.*

From this point on, a chart reversal was mostly up to luck.

And the opposing agency knew all of this.

*Wonder Hall.*

Aether's agency.

They had discovered Cha Eugene's runaway incident in real time and plotted against TeSTAR.

Park Moondae knew someone in his company was leaking information to them.

However, he chose not to catch the leaker yet.

*I'll use it to my advantage just this once.*

He had painstakingly set a trap.

He deliberately scheduled Spacer's comeback two weeks before Aether's to make them look like a tempting target.

He also deliberately left the promotion's weaknesses exposed. This allowed Wonder Hall to obtain the information and made the weakness seem obvious.

"That's right."

[Aether Teaser Release Imminent... The Monster Rookie's Takeover of the Music Scene]

As a result, Aether did not bother to adjust their comeback schedule.

*They're confident they can beat Spacer.*

The weakness seemed clear, and they calculated their own buzz would be enough to push through after their comeback.

*They probably think that for boy idols, building a fandom is more important than mass appeal anyway.*

They might have even thought Spacer's moderate attention made them the perfect target to crush.

But Aether's side had overlooked one thing.

"......"

Park Moondae's side had been planning this scenario for months.

That night, a post quietly appeared on an internet community.

——————————

[Spacer's song is doing well~ But with this title concept and all, am I the only one who's reminded of T-Holic? lol]

——————————

[OMG I thought it was just me! lol Kinda like early T-Holic? The songs from when they were regulars on variety shows lol]

└Right, it's refreshing and not complicated.. The hoodie stage outfits remind me of when Ha Jintae had light brown hair ㅠㅠ

[I get what you mean lol]

[Huh? They're not similar at all, idk]

└Ah, I didn't mean their looks are similar, just that the vibe is similar. The fact that it's not overwhelming.

The post claimed Spacer's music video was subtly reminiscent of T-Holic's golden days.

This opinion met little resistance or sarcasm.

T-Holic's peak was long past, and Spacer did not have an image that warranted significant pushback.

The post simply had the tone of a connoisseur.

[But it's kinda funny, Aether is on the concept-heavy TeSTAR track, while Spacer is on the opposite T-Holic track lol]

└lol K-Pop is so exciting

The post received minor attention and was discussed among a few hardcore idol fans for a day or two.

Then, articles began to appear.

[Spacer, who Resembles T-Holic, and Aether, who Resembles TeSTAR... A Glimpse into the Diverging Agencies]

[Will Spacer Become the Second T-Holic?]

This time, however, Aether's side did not release the articles.

*You think you're the only ones who know how to do media play?*

Park Moondae and Orbit Stars Entertainment had released them.

The articles linked Spacer to T-Holic and Aether to TeSTAR.

This was framing.

It forced a narrative and a structure.

And so, Park Moondae began to reverse-engineer the group positioning Aether had used during their debut.
