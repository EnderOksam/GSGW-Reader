# Chapter 534

TeSTAR's latest comeback music video opened with disjointed, cartoon-like drawings. A crimson circus tent flickered under a midnight light. It was the same eerie place from the teaser where Kim Raebin had removed his rabbit mask and been sucked in.

A faint, echoing melody rang out as if from a memory.

[♬♪♪-♩ ♬♩-♬♪♪]

Brilliant lights flashed. A unique circus melody played on an accordion and harmonica, accompanied by a cheerful drum beat.

The circus performers in animal masks, however, looked exhausted and shabby. They performed perilous acts while bound in prisoners' chains. The audience, visible only as black silhouettes, cackled and clapped.

Dizzying, fragmented images of the contradictory, joyous festival flashed by like a film reel. Then, like an extinguished flame, WHOOSH.

THUD.

Everything vanished in an instant.

A calm, dark silence filled the screen. A dimly lit backstage appeared next. This was not a cartoon but a real video.

——————————

The camera panned slowly across the black screen, then began to close in as if drawn forward.

THUMP. THUMP.

Slow, meaningful, heartbeat-like drumbeats sounded. A person's figure appeared in the center, visible within a faint sliver of light.

It was the silhouette of a circus performer. The performer stood silently, then tore off the old rags and chains.

Underneath, a man appeared in a long coat over a black suit buttoned to his neck, wearing a scarf.

Ryu Chungwoo.

CLICK.

The close-up stopped. A lightbulb flashed above the standing Ryu Chungwoo like lightning.

WOOOO!

A low, howling sound like a ship's horn accompanied a drumbeat that spread out like a war ritual. A sharp, powerful synthesizer rang out precariously.

In time with the sound, Ryu Chungwoo slowly raised his collar to cover his face and pulled his hat down low. He suddenly rushed toward the screen, and the scene transitioned to a performance cut.

[It's now.]

The choreography, like a spreading shadow, exquisitely stole the viewer's gaze, seeming to swallow the camera.

From then on, the music and video surged forward without a break. The camera moved in and out of the complex circus tent with each part change, intercutting shots of the audience being attacked. The progression felt like running through a mysterious space from a first-person perspective, increasing the immersion.

The shaking camera in the darkness met Kim Raebin, who kicked a rabbit mask toward it. This led into his driving first verse.

[The tables have turned

Today, you're the sheep

Run farther, go ahead

How fun, watching you do tricks]

Modern and heavy notes rained down, contrasting with the comical, retro circus music from the start. The music timed perfectly with the performers' movements. A lip-licking sound effect was even used in all the right places.

It felt like an invasion by an advanced civilization.

"...Hoo."

Aether's leader, Nua, replayed the scenes he had watched repeatedly.

He focused on the chorus choreography where they stomped their feet. The choreography's composition and expressiveness polished the image of a 'hunt' to its most attractive extreme. It created a clean sense of pressure. Every limb and joint was perfectly aligned no matter when he paused the video.

[A night of reversal

A time for hunting

(Yeah)

Call up, it's hunting season]

It depicted a Halloween night where circus animals turned the tables and hunted the audience. The overwhelming sound and performance tore apart the joyful, splendid, yet cruel circus atmosphere. The difference in weight class was clear.

The catharsis!

The performers brought the unique anti-hero atmosphere to life with absorbing power and no awkwardness. The members had completely mastered when to exert force and when to hold back. They knew where to place the 'wow' points most effectively and created a whirlwind.

During the final chorus, Cha Eugene stood in the center. He moved with wild energy, flinging his whole body as if commanding a charge. The music stopped as if sucked away, and he struck a perfectly timed pose.

His eyes glinted like an animal's.

[Get close to me]

His command of the craft was overwhelming.

TeSTAR's music video held the tension until the very end. Seon Ahyeon, with a blank white face, threw a circus lightbulb onto the floor. No matter how many times he watched it, the performance was overwhelming.

"......"

Aether's leader put down his notebook with a slightly trembling hand. His vision went dim.

*This is impossible...*

He had tried to hold it back, but hopelessness washed over him. It was the kind of feeling a high school student might get when attempting a doctoral dissertation.

The performance by an opponent with immense talent, years ahead of him, crushed the new pro with enormous pressure.

"......"

When he had seen TeSTAR as just 'respected seniors', he used them as motivation, thinking his group could be like them one day, but the thought of having to be that good right now was suffocating.

It seemed impossible.

"......"

But Aether's leader tried to shake off the feeling. He flipped through his notebook, reviewing keywords he had jotted down for parts he 'wanted to emulate' or 'wanted to express this way'.

*It's slow, but it's not like I'm gaining nothing...!*

He found something new to write down with every viewing. Each analysis helped him concretely grasp 'why TeSTAR's performance is so good'. He clenched his teeth.

He would not give up.

*Even if it's just something similar!*

"Hyung! We want to practice a little more... Yes. Choi Seonsaengnim. If he has time today..."

He asked his manager to schedule additional practice. He had to make it happen, even if it meant sacrificing his already scarce sleep.

*I can do it!*

He would succeed, just as he always had. He firmed his resolve and secretly slid his laptop back under his bed.

The laptop's last screen showed Park Moondae, singing a gentle, off-beat bridge by himself.

***

"Moondae, please close your eyes~ Ah, I'll try my best not to touch your earphones."

"Yes. Thank you."

As I went about my schedule, I recalled what had just happened.

*So...*

I went to take pictures of a dog and ended up getting pestered by a grand senior I never wanted to meet. For the record, that grand senior, T-Holic's Ha Jintae, was talkative until the very end.

[Moondae, let's all three of us, you, me, and Woodan, go for a drink sometime!]

*I have no idea how he came up with such an idea, but I really wanted to decline, and I never wished for this phone call either.*

[I heard that you met Jintae Seonbae.]

*Ah, yes.*

I continued to get my makeup done while taking the call from VTIC's Judan on my Bluetooth earphone.

"Yes, but I apologize, I'm at the salon right now, so my replies might be a little slow."

[That's fine. I'm not the kind of person who's completely inconsiderate of the other party's situation. Besides, my current situation is a bit unique as well.]

*So that's another way of simply saying you're in the military.*

Anyway, I had no reason to object, so I readily agreed.

"You must be going through a lot."

[Yes. It's not like I'm not.]

"......"

I listened to him go on about how 'the military isn't rational, but if you adapt, you can endure it too'.

*Is it a corporal's national rule to change their tune once they get some rank?*

In any case, I didn't have much to say to this guy. I planned to just play along for a bit before hanging up. During our conversation, a somewhat interesting topic came up.

[Jintae Seonbae claims that he gave you some pretty useful advice. Is it okay to treat that as the truth?]

*Hmm...*

I recalled my conversation with the guy from T-Holic and frowned slightly.

*It was advice, I guess.*

T-Holic's Ha Jintae had told me not to underestimate Aether. He said that 'guys who were trainees at large agencies for a long time have tenacity'. When I first heard it, the advice honestly didn't seem very meaningful.

*Is there any successful idol who lacks tenacity?*

It seemed like a virtue all successful people possessed. So I had asked him without thinking.

[What do you mean by tenacity?]

[Hmm. Is that a bit vague? ...Right, do you know how kids from large agencies usually debut?]

[Pardon?]

[They work hard to pass auditions, work hard to get into the debut group, and work hard again to release a debut album, but there's no guarantee when that will happen.]

I understood what he meant by 'tenacity' from his following explanation.

[The timeline for when a debut group will be formed is undecided, so they have to work endlessly for years, and since debuting is like threading a needle, the competition is incredibly fierce.]

It meant aspiring idols knew that debuting from a large agency was unconditionally advantageous, so they clung on for dear life. If you broke through and won, you debuted.

[So, the kids who break through and debut have it ingrained in them that they can do anything if they work hard. They think they achieved everything through their own effort.]

The ones in the debut groups of large agencies are those who have climbed every step without a single setback, whether by luck or skill. In a word, they don't know the taste of failure.

[They unconsciously refuse to admit that they can fail even if they try their best.]

*So... it's more like stubbornness than tenacity.*

Was he glorifying their inability to cut their losses, calling it 'tenacity' because they hadn't learned the right timing? I had reached that conclusion, but the T-Holic member's conclusion was strange.

[So it means they can work hard unconditionally. For a very, very long time, without getting tired. Ugh.]

His words even carried a hint of being fed up, as if he had experienced it himself.

*...No way.*

At that moment, a different group popped into my head, not the Aether example the T-Holic guy had used. It was the idol group that would have been most annoying to T-Holic in their prime, and a group that perfectly fit the 'from a large agency' description.

[Hmm. That's not wrong.]

VTIC.

It seemed that from T-Holic's perspective, VTIC looked that way, so they analyzed Aether through the same lens.

*That's not it.*

VTIC was like that because some out-of-spec psycho had a tight grip on the leader. Aether wasn't that kind of case.

*It seems like something that would only work if there's an extraordinary person on the team.*

...Though the VTIC member himself seemed to agree with it.

[A persuasive analysis. I'm a bit surprised. It's true that I made it to debut without knowing failure. In fact, I'm no different from a flower raised in a greenhouse.]

"...I see."

[Yes, and I'd like to stay in the greenhouse from now on.]

*He's saying that with his own mouth?*

Anyway, I decided to keep T-Holic's advice in mind but not to apply it too much to TeSTAR's situation.

*Everyone's situation is different.*

...And not long after, I would see with my own eyes that 'everyone's situation is different'.

A few days later was the ❬Genuine Match❭ mid-term stage evaluation recording.

[Breaking the silence, once again Gear up

Fill this space with the sound of applause]

Aether performed their stage before TeSTAR.

[Set up]

They performed with every ounce of their strength until the stage ended.

"......"

I could see the amount of practice in their dance break. It had looked polished before, but this was different.

*The quality has changed.*

Before, it felt like they had diligently practiced what someone else choreographed and taught them. Now, it felt like they had practiced to squeeze out their 'utmost'.

*I can feel that they've agonized over how to make an impact as naturally as possible in their own parts.*

Their desperate mindset was palpable. They wanted to show everything they had without a single regret. It was the air of a desperate person. It was the feeling conveyed by someone who knows they can't win but still throws themselves into the fight.

"......"

I clenched my fists.

Certain narratives in this world universally move people's hearts. A situation with a cliff at your back. The stubborn grit of gritting your teeth and trying to make it work on a tightrope. A unique spirit and effort. The will to push through and climb up while bombarded with scrutiny and controversy.

It was an attribute unique to a rising star.

...Whatever the reason for that controversy. Whether they were a genuine prodigy or not. It was the psychology of wanting to cheer for a team as they struggle to climb up as the underdog.

[Underdog.]

It was a position TeSTAR had almost monopolized until they won the Grand Prize.

Moreover, TeSTAR was full of these 'prodigies'. It was an attribute that could hold the public's attention for a very long time by maximizing their narrative through a survival show. However, when the challenger becomes the champion, the challenger's attributes naturally disappear. It is an image that can only be held for a limited time.

*That's not to say any team with a little name recognition can have this.*

Because there is a minimum line.

[You can tell they're good just by looking.]

This was the qualification.

[If they work that hard and are that good, they deserve better treatment.]

To evoke this emotion on a public stage, a team had to at least pass this benchmark for it to take effect.

And until now, no team after TeSTAR had passed it...

[Leap over!]

Right now, on that stage, the guys performing for the mid-term evaluation were just barely crossing that line.

"......"

*Son of a bitch.*

*This is the kind of story survival show producers love the most.*
