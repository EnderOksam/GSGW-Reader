# Chapter 2 - Debut or Die, II

'Idol Joint-Stock Company.'

It was a survival audition show that was popular around this time.

Following the success of the audition, in which hundreds of aspiring idols were gathered and eliminated through voting, other programs began copying the format.

This 'Idol Joint-Stock Company' was also one of many similar programs, but there was a reason why it became particularly famous.

Because it was much more brutally capitalist in its approach.

First, let's look at the promotional slogan from Season 1.

**[Your idol stock grows as much as you invest!]**

In other words, the more money you spent, the more 'stock' you could buy in the program.

The program was in Season 3 this year.

After Season 2 completely collapsed, they treated it as if it had never existed and returned with the new subtitle "Relisted."

Because of this, many people mocked the decision to continue with Season 3 and predicted that it would fail.

...What did I think about it? I hadn't given it any thought because I was busy preparing for the exam.

Anyway, contrary to everyone's expectations, Season 3 became a huge success.

Although there was plenty of controversy and criticism, it became a huge topic of discussion, achieved high viewer ratings, reached 80,000 streams on the music charts, and sold 600,000 albums in its first week.

That's why I'm going to compete on this program, too.

Today, I'm taking the first step toward that goal.

"You're all set! I didn't cut too much off the sides, just like you requested."

"Yes, thank you."

My hairstyle finally suited my face, I thought as I examined my neatly trimmed hair in the mirror.

Maybe it was because I'd been eating and sleeping well for the past few days, but I looked much less haggard and miserable than before. As a result, I was able to trim my thick bangs and reveal my forehead.

To my satisfaction, the middle-aged woman getting a perm beside me showered me with compliments.

"Oh, my! You look so good! You've gotten taller!"

I thanked her and proceeded to the counter to pay with cash.

*It was a relief to find that there was quite a bit of money in his account when I checked it at the bank.*

According to the account history, it was insurance money from his parents, which was probably why he hadn't used it. I felt bad about using it myself.

*...Once I start making money, should I pay back what I used and donate some to a child welfare organization?*

It was a bit bitter. After obtaining a certified copy of the family registry and looking into things on my phone, I realized that this 'Park Moondae' hadn't died on an impulse.

He had moved out of his room, barely getting any of the deposit back because he'd fallen behind on rent, and he'd canceled his phone plan.

He had even done all this on his birthday.

He must have really wanted to end his life.

...Well, I understand how he felt because I'd been like that, too.

*I hope you're in a better place now.*

I paused for a brief moment of silence to remember him. Then I opened the hair salon door right away and walked out. It was close to noon, and the sun pierced my eyes.

There were still a lot of things I needed to get done today.

*First, I need to come up with a real plan.*

I already knew all of the show's special missions, debut members, and theme song.

Of course, I'd forgotten some of the minor details since I'd been busy preparing for the exam, but it was impossible to find a better path to a successful life than this.

Because if I could make it through the second round, I would be able to attract the attention of at least a small or medium-sized agency thanks to the show's success.

However, there was one problem: open auditions for this season had already ended.

Luckily, I knew a useful piece of information, and I planned to use it to my advantage.

My goal was to become a contestant on the survival program even though its open auditions had already ended.

And I planned to do it within a week.

Not at the broadcasting station, but at a nearby noraebang.

* * *

– IJC? Ah, Idol Joint-Stock Company. One of the writers there is a really crazy person.

That was the bullshit some guy in my photography club had said.

He claimed he'd heard it from a relative who worked at a broadcasting station, and I wasn't very fond of him since he was always spreading rumors.

– I heard they're recruiting contestants from karaoke rooms. The broadcasting station—you know, the neighborhood it's in?
– I heard she recruited an ordinary person from a karaoke room, and the PD got angry about it.
– Apparently, she was told she didn't have enough time. Isn't she a complete idiot?

After that, the guy couldn't stop talking about the casting process.

It's common to gossip during a drinking party, but it's rare to find someone who talks about boring topics at a drinking party.

*Who was it that invited this moron?*

*Let's not invite him anymore.*

*Yeah.*

The people around him chatted with sour expressions.

Of course, I didn't believe any of his bullshit either. I was just focused on enjoying the free drinks.

But surprisingly, someone backed up his story.

– The ordinary person who was being cast is Lee Goyoon.
– ...Huh?

Lee Goyoon is a female idol who debuted in season 1. She gained popularity for her bright, cheerful, and bold image with an amusing accent and her pure appearance.

There was a moderate exclamation around.

– Whoa, she's the kid who made her debut, right?
– Yes.
– Oh... how did you know?

The senior in the journalism and broadcasting department spoke calmly.

– That 'stupid bitch' is my older sister.

The guy who had been gossiping was suddenly at a loss for words.

Interestingly, everyone around him began glaring at him with contempt.

– ...Ah, haha. No, that expression is a bit.
– But she wasn't scolded by the PD. Instead, she got a raise.
– ....

The guy didn't say another word after that.

Thanks to her, I was able to enjoy my drink in peace. I sipped Somaek[1]{T/N: Somaek is a beer cocktail made with soju and beer.} while listening to the senior explain how the casting worked.

Anyway, it was quite a fun situation, so it was memorable.

They said that if they couldn't recruit enough contestants through the auditions, they would search karaoke rooms near the broadcasting station for about a week afterward.

– Wouldn't it be better to find people at places like karaoke rooms? They'd be easier to cast, too.

– I heard there are already a lot of aspiring singers there, so they avoid those places.

– I heard that the reason why they want to cast ordinary people is that they need a fresh character, unlike a survival participant.

In other words, they weren't looking for contestants who could potentially debut, but ordinary people who knew nothing about the industry and could add some entertainment to the show.

These days, agencies train their idol trainees to be careful about what they say.

In the end, it meant that the ordinary people they recruited could be used as cannon fodder to make the show more entertaining.

*That's not important to me right now, though.*

What mattered was that there was a good chance the writer would use the same method for Season 3.

*Since it worked before, it would be difficult to abandon the method now.*

Besides, since Season 2 had been a failure, the contestants who auditioned were probably of a lower standard, making the production team more desperate.

I had to think about this logically.

This 'Park Moondae' body is a little scrawny, but he's good-looking and tall.

On top of that, he can sing well, so if I gave the writer a chance to see and hear me sing, there was a good chance she'd cast me.

Besides—

I had to raise my stats by completing the practice missions. Wouldn't I need to be exceptional in some way for the writer to consider me?

I grabbed the microphone, chose a song, and glanced at the door of the private room.

The door was made of glass. It was designed to give customers in their teens and twenties a bright, open atmosphere while also allowing people outside to see inside.

That's exactly why I chose this particular noraebang.

I was waiting for a writer from the broadcasting station.

If she had to go out and recruit ordinary people while preparing for the program, she'd likely choose somewhere close to the broadcasting station and easy to observe.

So this noraebang was the best choice.

Without hesitation, I queued up ten songs and began singing.

It didn't take long to complete my first mission—singing ten songs. Of course, I knew I would achieve it because it was the easiest thing to do.

I invested the point in Vocal again, changing C+ to B-.

I sang the same song again to test the system, and my abilities seemed to have improved. My vocal tone became richer, and my voice grew stronger.

At this rate, I could probably raise my abilities incredibly quickly, but there was something else I needed to focus on first.

"This is my business card."

I didn't expect her to take the bait so quickly.

I'd started hanging around a little earlier than I'd calculated, but I'd unexpectedly hit the timing perfectly.

I took the business card offered by the woman in glasses.

The woman looked tired, but her voice was purposefully bright. She was clearly trying to make a good first impression, which meant I'd probably caught her attention as expected.

The name I was expecting was written on the business card.

The senior from the Department of Journalism and Broadcasting was named 'Ryu Seorin.'

*It seems to be a sister's name just by looking at it.*

"What's your name?"

"My name is Park Moondae."

"You have a unique name, too. Think carefully about what I'm saying. I think you have star quality. Our program is really good at finding people like that."

"...Thank you."

I didn't want to overreact. If I looked too interested, she might realize that I'd intentionally aimed for this situation.

My laid-back attitude seemed to work because the woman continued.

"You're good-looking, and you sing well. I think you'd do really well on our program. Hmm, would you like to come with me and do a screen test today?"

"What?"

She seemed determined to move forward with the casting process immediately.

"Why don't you call your parents? Tell them that you've been scouted for a TV show. They'll be proud of you."

"...I don't have parents."

As soon as I said that, her eyes sparkled with renewed interest. She had probably already recognized the potential for an interesting backstory.

After all, storytelling is an essential part of a survival show, and using a contestant's sad background could even attract criticism for exploiting it. It would be a waste not to use such a story.

However, the writer quickly hid her thoughts and spoke more softly, as if she were sorry.

"Oh my... I'm sorry. I shouldn't have said that."

"That's all right."

"I just mean, think of it as coming to visit a broadcasting station. It's not an opportunity you get every day~."

"...Hmm."

Is it time to start giving in?

I paused, pretending to hesitate, then slowly nodded.

"Oh, you made the right choice! The broadcasting station is right around the corner!"

Things were proceeding much more smoothly than I had expected.
