# Chapter 348 - A Calculated Risk

TeSTAR's new song caused a huge stir from its very first performance.

I decided to see how our performance had been received in the US, where the show had aired.

I entered 'You Got a Chance', the name of the American variety show we'd appeared on, into the search engine.

When I saw the autocomplete adding words like 'who the hell are those guys wearing the jumpers?', I nodded to myself in satisfaction.

*Perfect.*

We'd done precisely one performance on that major entertainment show, which was popular with the American public. We'd made a good impression there.

This was why our first exposure through a major show was so important. There was a reason we'd pulled out all the stops and used every trick in the book.

"The comments asking why we're not an actual band are getting the most likes. I guess we were really convincing when we pretended to play instruments. Everyone's so surprised~" Keun Sejin said.

"Even so, people seem to have caught on to the similar genre we used for the bridge, with the same sound effects and instruments from the movie," Kim Raebin said.

"Oh~ You're right. People don't really seem to have anything bad to say about the song," Keun Sejin said.

"Someone on here is asking who Chungwoo-hyung is. Can I at least answer that?" Cha Eugene asked.

"Let's hold off on that for a bit, Eugene."

Because people associated us with the movie, we were getting all sorts of reactions, both positive and negative. I welcomed all of it. Any buzz was precious.

Naturally, that would draw more people to the music video and bring a huge influx of users to global music streaming sites.

*It's a level of exposure we can't get in Korea anymore.*

We were following the same pattern a group went through when it gained traction through a performance. It seemed to work the same way in every country.

★$
– How were they trained? How can they pull that off? K-pop artists are trained, right?

└ They're from an audition show, which means they're not the beneficiaries/victims of K-pop's soulless factory system!
$★

...In the meantime, some guys skillfully mixed the truth and bullshit in posts that seemed almost promotional. I wasn't sure what effect that would have, but I planned to make use of it too, so I let it pass.

In any case, since the majority liked what they'd seen, reactions in Korea were overwhelmingly positive as well.

★$
– They're so good

– Does it make sense that this was done live without any post-production? Fans must feel full even without eating. It's f*cking amazing

– How did they do that tumbling move after the cannon fired? If it were me, I'd have broken my neck
$★

Even people who would've normally scrolled past, thinking it was just fans hyping them up, were drawn in to see what was going on. This was amazing for us.

And the performance itself was good enough to meet the high expectations of those who clicked.

Because of this, the members who'd practically collapsed after the performance, complaining that they couldn't breathe, abruptly changed their minds.

"We did pretty well. Wow, even our fingers were in sync. Did you see that?" Keun Sejin said.

"Y-yes, e-everyone has great energy... The performance was every bit as good as the song, and the two melded perfectly," Seon Ahyeon said.

"That's right! I was so cool on that stage!" Cha Eugene said.

Cha Eugene's Korean was improving by the day—he was even using the past tense correctly now. It seemed the members had fully accepted the truth after seeing the performance and the internet's response with their own eyes.

*And I think things will only continue like this going forward.*

I nodded in satisfaction.

And the subsequent TNet comeback show and performances on domestic music shows only helped win the public over even more.

*The B-side is doing well too.*

We'd worked hard to develop the concept of a modern noir thriller using a black cat theme, so wasn't it a waste to throw it all away after changing concepts?

We used it for our B-side instead.

Unlike the action-packed, warlike title track, this one had a more graceful feel.

Even those who'd been a little disappointed by the title track's concept liked the B-side. Of course, those who hadn't been disappointed liked it too.

★$
– I love both of them. Love it. Agent TeSTAR and gentlemen TeSTAR in a single promotional cycle! Talk about killer efficiency

– Thank you guys, it's a feast (munching sounds)
$★

Every time we sat down to monitor the reactions, the members' faces seemed to glow from within.

"Our latest performance is getting great reviews too."

"Wow. I was so tired, but I can feel it all evaporating!" Keun Sejin said.

Realistically speaking, it was the best response we could've hoped for.

We'd come from a survival show that had been the subject of much discussion, and our past few years had been eventful, to say the least. It was rare to experience a response that felt like a soothing warm bath rather than scalding or icy water.

Even the comments on articles in the general news section had nothing but good things to say about us.

★$
– This is what enhancing national prestige looks like. Korea's unique *haetae* style~ The great TeSTAR is spreading the word about the *haetae*, those righteous creatures~
$★

In an industry that's always starved for attention, this level of praise was like an addictive drug.

It was exactly this kind of response that gave young people hope and made them dream of becoming idols themselves.

Around this time, articles about our independent label finally began to appear as well.

★$
[TeSTAR's new label ORBIT has been founded... "Will encourage and develop its artists"]
$★

Ryu Chungwoo smiled at the article.

"Orbit, huh?"

"Yes."

I'd named it, though not because I'd particularly wanted to.

*These bastards were trying to give it a name that was way too grand.*

The agency had originally suggested Supernova.

*Why try to draw aggro with the label's name?*

Since we were still affiliated with the agency, I didn't think there was any need to draw too much attention to the label, so I'd made sure we toned things down and chose an appropriate name.

"I think it's perfect. It reminds me of planets and their orbits. It's a great name."

"...Thank you," I said.

Regardless, the fans loved it.

Of course, so did the stock investors.

★$
– These guys are doing very well, what with the Hollywood movie and all. My hopes for them remain high

– TeSTAR is great. Good thing I bought in before the stocks rose any higher. I'm holding on to them until the end of the year ^^
$★

All weekend long, positive translated reactions from overseas audiences were making the rounds, and fans were delighted with the overflowing content.

And the public was already downing the kimchi soup in one shot, with most acting like we'd already secured a spot on the Billboard charts even though the results wouldn't be revealed until next Thursday.

★$
– No, come on. It's too early. They've only appeared on one American variety show...

– I've got a feeling it'll be another career high, but don't let your guard down, guys. We've still got work to do!

– What is this? Why are there so many new TeSTAR translation accounts?
$★

Even the dedicated fans felt burdened by the situation, but as they grew excited too, the internet turned into a mess of happiness, anticipation, worry, and confusion.

Among them, of course, were people speculating about what we'd do next.

★$
– Guess they'll go back to America after they finish the music shows

– ㅇㅇ I saw an article about that. Apparently, they've had quite a few love calls from the US

– ㅋㅋ They drew all sorts of aggro just to get in with Limestone, and now they're doing everything they can to get to America

└ What? Your faves practically offered themselves in a bundle to boost sales, and it had no effect at all. Meanwhile, TeSTAR's getting rave reviews in the US. Feeling inferior much?

└ ㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋ
$★

It had reached the point where trolls couldn't get any traction at all.

Bae Sejin opened his mouth with a trembling voice.

"...I've never seen people be so uniformly friendly before."

"I know, right."

The whole country seemed to love us. It felt like we might become the nation's favorite idols at any moment.

The response to the music video's release had been amazing, and even after a week, there was no sign of it slowing down.

Anyone could see that we were walking down a flower-strewn path.

"Yeah. This is amazing."

"Y-yes..."

However, the smiles on their faces seemed fixed in place, not the relaxed smiles of people who'd reached a happy ending.

Then what expressions did they have?

They looked as though they were about to break into a cold sweat.

It was as though they could see the gates of hell opening on the horizon, their expressions saying, 'What's next?'

It was the kind of expression they wore at the thought of our damned schedule.

I knew the cause... The time was drawing near.

"...But we're going to destroy all that with our own hands soon, right?"

"Yes."

I nodded.

"...Enjoy it while you can."

"Yeah..." Seon Ahyeon said.

They buried their noses in their smartphones again, doing their best to savor the sweetness of public praise while they still could.

And a few days later, that sweetness turned bitter.

* * *

There was still a while to go before the Billboard chart rankings were announced...

On Monday, the beginning of the week, an article appeared.

★$
[TeSTAR shows up on popular American variety show... How long will their incredible success last?]
$★

It was the sort of article everyone would click on, expecting more good news.

They must've been thinking, 'Oh, they're appearing on another American variety show.'

However, if they read the article in full, they'd discover exactly which variety show we were appearing on this time.

★$
⟨Don't Mess It Up⟩
$★

It wasn't very well-known in Korea, but those who knew, knew exactly what kind of show it was.

★$
– Ah... This show's so janky. They do weird challenges and stuff. I don't like it at all...
$★

In other words, it was a variety show that gave guests difficult tasks so they'd fail, or picked on them just to see their reactions. In short, it was a B-list show.

★$
– This isn't a show for popular celebrities. Why are they appearing on it?

– This is going to ruin the high-class image they built up with the movie and their first performance... Ugh...
$★

Ah...

However, TeSTAR would only be appearing in one segment of this show.

Besides, there were usually 10 teams in each segment, and all 10 teams would appear for a total of only 17 minutes.

*It looks like the most ill-informed decision of all time.*

And that wasn't the end of it.

A fairly sophisticated and famous talk show was airing at the same time as this program.

Most idol groups with global popularity, VTIC foremost among them, had performed on that show.

In other words, we could've appeared there, but we'd turned that show down and chosen this cheap B-list program instead.

At this point, it was pretty easy to guess the atmosphere.

★$
– Are they crazy?

– I don't understand why they're appearing here.

– ㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋ I'm dying of frustration right now. I'm going to check it out.
$★

It was also the first time a K-pop idol had appeared on this program.

In the first place, one of the production staff had even made a racist remark in the past and been fired for it. It felt like a truly small-time local TV show.

There was no way they'd treat us nicely.

★$
– Hoo...

– ㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋ
$★

After this news circulated all over the internet, what happened? We checked the results.

"Hmm... It seems quiet."

"Yes."

Everyone had completely ignored the announcement, as if it had never been made at all.

*Just as I thought.*

If everyone admitted that this was an embarrassing mistake on TeSTAR's part, their vicarious satisfaction would vanish, and they'd feel like they'd lost too.

It didn't fit the gukppong mentality they were enjoying, so everyone was pretending they'd never seen the news in the first place.

"The fans seem quiet too."

There was a good reason for that.

*They've decided to wait and see.*

They were all the more upset because TeSTAR had been doing so well recently, but they'd chosen to remain quiet because they didn't want to harm that momentum.

The schedule had already been arranged, and it was an American variety show to boot. A substitute would be hard to find, and making a fuss and potentially getting it canceled wouldn't benefit anyone.

So, overall, the buzz surrounding TeSTAR suddenly died down, along with all the praise and excited reactions, like a candle that had been blown out.

All that remained were videos from the gukppong channels that forced stupid conclusions to console themselves and weren't any help at all.

"......"

"......"

It was rare to disappoint the public without suffering backlash, but I got the feeling it might actually have felt better to be criticized.

Look there—even Bae Sejin, who already knew why we were appearing on the show, was staring sadly at a channel translating overseas reactions. The uploads had stopped.

"......"

"Hyung," I said.

"I know. I know we need to do this!"

Yes. We'd discussed this step in advance.

K-pop was no longer an unfamiliar genre in the US, but a genre with a solid fan base as a subculture.

That was obviously a good thing, but typically, the groups that were already popular were the ones that stayed popular among the people who actively sought out K-pop.

*We even collaborated with the Limestone movie to avoid that.*

So, we couldn't just go along with everyone else. Doing that wouldn't let us take full advantage of the pie we'd secured for ourselves with the Limestone movie.

Bae Sejin seemed to snap out of it and nodded.

"...We just need to follow our plan."

"Correct."

I shrugged and patted Cha Eugene, who was standing next to me, on the shoulder.

"He's got it all figured out. It's his home country, after all. He's American."

"That's right. Trust me! I've got this!"

"I told you I trust you!" Bae Sejin protested.

I left the two of them to talk and turned to Seon Ahyeon, who'd also paused while monitoring the reactions.

Given how much effort he'd put into this promotional cycle, I thought the sudden change in mood would bother him... but to my surprise, he gave us a thumbs up.

"O-of course I trust you, Moondae...!"

"Huh? Oh... Thanks," I said.

"Yeah!"

*Well, okay then...*

*Now for the final check.*

I rolled my shoulders and checked the top comments on our first performance, which were still intact.

Quite a few of them mentioned me.

★$
– I was lying in bed, but as soon as Park Moondae started singing, I sat right up ㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋ Really, he's insane

– His voice sounds like a studio recording. It's so unfair to other groups—this guy's competing with his voice!

└ ㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋ

– Park Moondae is a top-grade main vocalist. My ears are melting
$★

It was a reaction that made investing that extra point worthwhile.

I thought of my status window.

My singing stat... it would be at 'S+' now.

Yes. I added the one remaining stat point to my vocals.

If I'd used the point for my dance stat, which was currently at B+, it would've only gone up to A-. So why would I do that?

It was because I'd seen how the various stat levels actually translated into ability by watching other people in real life.

*S+ rank is basically perfection.*

For example, there was a guy whose Talent stat had finally reached S+ with this album.

★-
[Name: Cha Eugene]

Talent: S+ #g▲g#
-★

Now, people would stare at this bastard even if he were asked to narrate some random song on the street. Even his slightly unpredictable side had become a selling point.

And... There was another guy in a different group with an S+ in a different stat.

I frowned.

★-
[Name: Cheongryeo (Shin Jaehyeon)]

Dance: S+
-★

It was Cheongryeo.

A dance stat of S+ meant he was the perfect center for K-pop choreography.

There was a charisma and power that came from delivering the perfect dance performance with precise strength and nuance.

He'd probably make even a ragtag group of dancers with C or D-rank stats look good if he danced with them.

Considering that the EX rank seemed like something straight out of mythology or legend, S+ was probably the final level of what a human could accomplish, where skill became art.

So, if I wanted to make it impossible for my singing to fail, raising my singing stat to S+ was my best bet.

*Singing talent isn't something I can pass up.*

And... There was one more reason why I didn't put my remaining point into my dance stat.

...Because I was in a group, not by myself.

*There are already so many members who can dance well.*

It wasn't like I was going to be in the center for a dance break or anything, so a B+ was enough for me to keep up with the group's choreography.

*I need to stand out more at what I'm good at.*

Enhancing my own role, even by just a little, would do more to improve the quality of the group as a whole.

And the others had done the same.

I looked around in satisfaction. The guys had finished monitoring the reactions for the day, and just the sight of them was reassuring.

Anyway, it was time to put my S+ vocals to use once again.

*We'll make this recording a success no matter what and then—*

"Wow~ Apparently, one of the production staff posted online that our agency specifically asked to appear on this show. Hey, that's really great, huh?" Keun Sejin said.

I paused. "......"

*Those bastards. I'll deal with you after filming.*
