# The Tour Documentary, I

I put my hands on the table.

It was a small table meant to make the place look like a proper hotel room, but in front of it was a camera as huge as the table.

The production staff standing behind the camera began to speak.

"Please introduce yourself."

"Yes. Hello. I am TeSTAR's Park Moondae."

I nodded at the camera and spoke with a faint smile.

"We're currently... in the midst of TeSTAR's second concert tour."

*And making a concert documentary.*

More specifically, that was what we were actually doing right now.

*Actually, we film plenty of behind-the-scenes content at concerts.*

It gave viewers a sense of closeness to the idols, and fans found behind-the-scenes footage from the concerts they'd attended interesting.

However, it was rare to make a full-fledged documentary like this. It was encouraging that it was being released to the general public instead of being treated as a product only for fans.

It meant that we'd left enough of an impression on popular music for a documentary about us to be meaningful to the public.

*In other words, it's a symbol of moving into a higher class.*

For example, VTIC probably had three or four documentaries made about them by now.

So everyone agreed that we were on the threshold of the heavenly realm, the highest spot an idol could reach.

However, whether everyone liked it was a different question.

*– ...Wouldn't it be uncomfortable? We're going to have cameras following us around for months.*

*– Well, I heard they'd discuss it with us beforehand and film as appropriate.*

*– My everyday life isn't all that interesting, and I'm worried the content won't be interesting either...* Kim Raebin had said.

*– Ah, don't worry~ The professionals will handle it! We don't have to worry too much about being funny. It's a documentary, not a variety show!* Lee Sejin had said. *They'll make sure we look good!*

In the end, after some persuasion and a vote, filming for the documentary began. So far, there had been no major problems.

*It's a little... It seems they're asking rather intrusive questions.*

For example, the production crew was taking things a little too far even now.

@ll@"What part of the tour do you find the most uncomfortable?"@ll@

"Hmm, adjusting to the time difference or figuring out a new concert venue every week... Environmental factors, you know?"

@ll@"I see~ I suppose that would be the case. What about on the more... psychological side of things?"@ll@

"Well, psychologically..."

I could sense the production team's desire to capture something raw.

Everyone behind a camera seemed intent on delving into my state of mind.

After getting us to lower our guard with simple questions to which we'd give polished answers, they'd switch tactics and try to get us to reveal how we really felt.

It was different from the approach of variety show production crews, who were after whatever would grab viewers' attention.

Of course, we could give them answers that sounded quite genuine.

"I think it's hard to accept that factors I failed to control could prevent the audience from enjoying the concert to the fullest."

I paused briefly as if I were thinking, then continued.

"So I think that's what I'm most sensitive about and what worries me most. The overall quality of the concert."

That answer would give them the satisfaction of having uncovered a hidden facet of my personality.

Sure enough, the staff member nodded and moved on to the next question.

@ll@"Then, what does a concert mean to TeSTAR?"@ll@

"Well, seeing the fans up close probably has to be..."

I thought the answers I'd given after that were all suitable for a documentary, too. The edited version should come out looking pretty good.

***

"Thank you."

The interview lasted about an hour. Then the crew left to go work with the next member.

"......"

I lay down on the bed.

While on tour, we were actually less busy than during a promotional cycle.

As we'd grown in experience and popularity, we'd been assigned individual hotel rooms during this tour. Between concerts, we had quite a bit of time to rest and recover our strength for the next performance.

"It's quiet."

I closed my eyes. Perhaps because I'd lived in close quarters with such noisy people for years, the silence felt a little awkward.

"......"

And just before useless thoughts came crashing in...

Suddenly, one of the questions the production team had just asked me echoed in my mind.

*– What does a concert mean to you?*

Then, there was a scene that came to mind.

The first concert of this tour.

The concert at Gocheok Sky Dome in Seoul, our first there in a long time.

*– Waaaaaaa!!*

The crowd's roars. Fireworks. Smoke. Heat.

And pure concentration.

If that psychological exhilaration and overwhelming feeling were the essence of a concert, then the charity concert we'd streamed remotely had been little more than a day of filming on set.

Perhaps because it'd been so long since we'd held a real concert, the air felt so dense that it was as if I'd sunk into the ocean.

The air was different.

Concerts were said to be a good source of income for the company, but from the performer's point of view, it was like transcending to another plane.

Pulling off a concert well was more than worth the mental burden of preparing it, and our group hadn't experienced that in a really long time.

"Hoo."

...It was fortunate that the documentary crew hadn't started trailing us yet when we performed in Seoul.

*Because we all went home crying after that concert.*

We would never have been able to do an interview like that.

The guys who'd thrown their arms around each other's shoulders and sung our debut song in the dorm looked completely and utterly intoxicated as they held back tears. It was obvious the neighbors would've filed noise complaints if the room hadn't been soundproof.

*Good thing Ryu Chungwoo turned down their request to film back then.*

I briefly acknowledged that our leader had made a good call. Then I checked the schedule for our next W Live stream and fell asleep.

...Remembering that there were 34 hours left until the next concert.

*It won't take long.*

Since we were in the US, the venues were smaller than in Korea, but a concert was a concert. The intensity and enjoyment would be similar.

After spending some time at the hotel and rehearsing, we were back onstage.

**[Hello, LA!]**

**[Wooooow!]**

*I guess concerts are still just as special and rewarding.*

However, I felt I'd have to revise my assessment of the documentary crew.

*These guys are taking it too far.*

"Moondae-ssi, are you okay?"

"...I'm okay."

I took off my oxygen mask and stood up. Then a camera hurriedly followed me, and another crew member urgently scribbled something down behind it.

Could they be any more suspicious?

*Are they trying to push a particular story?*

However, you'd have a harder time finding idols who didn't use oxygen masks backstage during concerts. Trying to portray our 'passion' with this footage would only make the documentary corny.

*VTIC has already gotten plenty of mileage out of that.*

But once the concert was over and we'd done a few more interviews, I realized what was going on.

"Moondae-ssi, are you feeling any aftereffects from the car accident?"

"...? The car accident... Mmm, no. I'm perfectly healthy."

They started asking about my physical condition.

*Ah, I see.*

They obviously wanted to portray me as 'a perfectionist idol doing his best despite his injuries'.

A documentary was more fun when it had a story and structure rather than being a dry collection of facts, so I had to give them credit for trying, but...

*This is fraud.*

As far as modern medicine was concerned, I'd completely recovered.

There was a lot of strenuous choreography in this album, so I'd even focused on building muscle. Taking the story in that direction would be a straight-up lie.

I was in excellent physical condition.

*At this rate, we'd be lying to the public. It's at the point where we'd need a lie detector.*

The last straw was an interview about 'anxiety after the car accident'. I answered in a slightly firmer tone.

"I was eager to get back to performing as usual for the fans, since I'd made them worry, but I'm in perfect condition now."

The production team suddenly let out a subtle groan.

*What was this?*

"Um, Moondae-ssi. It seemed like you were moving beyond your limit during the concert."

"...Me?"

*Were they watching a different concert?*

"Yes. Same with the oxygen mask. The other members said so too."

"The members?" I asked.

*What in the world were they talking about?*

Meanwhile, these shameless bastards seemed excited that I was taken aback. *Was it that much fun?*

"They said they could see that you're having a harder time than you used to before the accident. You try not to show it, though, and it makes them sad, Moondae-ssi."

"......"

In an instant, scenes that seemed to support that theory flashed through my head.

*– You... You're already suffering because of that incident. You don't have as much energy as you used to.*

*– You were seriously injured, and all that time you spent recovering in bed must've affected your condition.*

"They think you're struggling with having less energy, so they want you to lean on them more."

No, the only shoulder I'd needed to lean on was Bacchus.

And even without it, I was doing fine now. But the members were apparently comparing me as I was now to the version of me who'd been constantly benefiting from 'Bacchus'.

Apparently, they'd forgotten how unrealistically good my stamina had been back then.

And contrary to my promise, I'd replaced 'Bacchus' with 'Missions Are My Thing', putting an end to the heyday of my physical stamina that I could never get back...

In addition, the system was gone, so I couldn't draw any more characteristics.

*This sucks.*

It was checkmate. I hadn't seen this coming.

I held back a groan and barely managed to open my mouth.

"...I didn't realize the members felt that way."

*I'm not touched, so stop smiling.*

But I'd have to make use of that misunderstanding.

I paused for a moment, then answered slowly.

"I was the one who was hurt the most, so the members have been very concerned about me. I think they said all that because they're still worried about me... I'm grateful they care so much."

...It was partly true, too.

*So take the heartwarming story of friendship between the members and give up on the one about me overcoming injuries.*

But these guys didn't give up.

"Here! Can we get some help?"

"Moo-Moondae, are you okay?"

"...Yes."

*Ha...*

First of all, they persistently filmed me backstage. They seemed especially pleased whenever I was out of breath or someone physically supported me. Their intentions were all too clear.

*They're completely obsessed.*

I thought about giving a serious warning a few times, but gave up.

*Do whatever you want.*

They seemed pretty good at finding what the public would find interesting, so the documentary itself would probably be fact-based and fun to watch.

It'd probably end up planting a distorted image of me in the public's mind, but... Alright, then.

*Struggling with injuries is a common enough behind-the-scenes story.*

Yes, maybe this job starts and ends with deception... I'd just focus on performing as well as I could and earn my money.

*At this rate, the rumors that I'm still suffering from the accident are going to spread all over the world...*

I resigned myself to my fate and focused on the concert.

Apparently, not all of us felt the same way.

"Stop! That's enough!"

"...!"

One of the longer VCRs was playing before the encore when a sudden shout came from backstage.

It was the voice of the guy who was usually the least sensitive among us.

"Eugene?"

"Is that Cha Eugene?"

The guys who'd already changed costumes and were standing by broke into a run, stunned by the unexpected situation.

I didn't like that the cameras were chasing us even now, but the contract included a clause allowing us to delete any footage we found problematic.

*Checking the situation comes first.*

And when we rounded the corner in the hallway, we saw where the shout was coming from.

It was a small backstage room where artists could receive treatment for muscle cramps, joint pain, and the like.

Cha Eugene apparently had been receiving emergency treatment for his shoulder, judging by the patch on it, but there was something more urgent than that.

Cha Eugene seemed to be at odds with the documentary crew.

"...Eugene?"

Cha Eugene glanced back at us, but soon looked away again.

To be precise, he looked at the production team beyond the cameraman.

"Stop that. I don't like it," Cha Eugene said.

"Yes, yes. Sorry."

"Get out. Don't come in here."

And he firmly sent the production team away.

"...Wait a minute."

I sent away the cameraman who'd followed me as well. It annoyed me that they kept the camera trained on the door even as it closed, but Cha Eugene came first.

Ryu Chungwoo turned off his microphone as soon as the door was shut, then spoke to Cha Eugene.

"Eugene. There are cameras. If you have something to say, it won't be too late to say it after the concert is over and the cameras are turned off."

"......"

"Even if there are complaints, let's talk about it after the concert. There are four minutes until our encore... No. There are only three minutes left."

"...Okay. Alright."

Cha Eugene didn't look very happy, but he nodded.

He wasn't the type to let a bad mood get in the way of his performance, so the encore would be fine.

*I'd already been thinking these documentary guys were taking things a little too far.*

Still, I hadn't expected Cha Eugene to react like this.

I had no idea what the hell they'd done to make him react like this. I'd been under the impression that the production team knew where to draw the line.

I held back a sigh and opened the door. Immediately, a camera was shoved in my face.

...This time, even I was a little annoyed. Maybe their enthusiasm was the reason we'd both gotten irritated.

*I'll have to talk to him as soon as it's over.*

But before I had the chance to speak to him, another incident threw me into a panic.
