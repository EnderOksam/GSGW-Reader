# Chapter 303 - ⟨T-Holic's Show Business⟩, III

The performance or skit given as a penalty on ⟨T-Holic's Show Business⟩ was, of course, for laughs.

The idea was to reinterpret or faithfully recreate a famous meme that was either trending or a long-time favorite.

*So far, T-Holic has done half of them. Maybe more, since they often let guests win.*

These guys were so good at making things funny that even when the guests received good coaching or direction, there was a strong perception that T-Holic were the 'originals'.

That was something we had to keep in mind when putting together this performance.

"Next is a draft that incorporates drums and a modified beat."

*Oorurung! ♫*

When the accompaniment kicked in with a perfectly timed rumble like a sports car engine revving, Cha Eugene's eyes flashed.

"This is cool! Let's go with this!"

"Wait a minute."

I stopped Cha Eugene.

"Let's listen to the other ones too."

"Okay, then I'll play the third one."

We needed to avoid making everything look perfect right from the start.

*If we simply perform well, people will say it's not funny.*

What viewers expected was a funny performance, so if we did everything perfectly with no comedy, there was a good chance they'd feel we hadn't delivered.

Even if we poured our heart and soul into making a masterpiece with only a week left, the audience wouldn't appreciate that effort.

*A story like that only works in a survival-show setting.*

That kind of narrative simply doesn’t work on a regular variety show.

However, blindly aiming for laughter was not the best solution.

*Way too many of the previous guests have already managed to be hilarious in these penalties.*

It would be easier to find guests who'd failed to make people laugh. The directors were good at what they did.

If TeSTAR followed that same formula, we'd just be adding to the show's reputation and little else.

It wasn't hard to guess the kind of response the show was aiming for, was it?

*They even gave us a list of recommendations.*

I glanced over the list of candidates for the 'nostalgic anime theme song' the production team had given me.

Motorcycle racing anime, Greek and Roman mythology anime, detective anime...

*What we just heard was the third one, the mystery anime opening...*

Kim Raebin turned it into a polished arrangement with a cool, hard-boiled vibe. In other words, it wasn't funny at all. It was just cool.

The next song was the same.

"The music is great!"

"You're right."

The next was the theme song from the Greek mythology anime, arranged into a smooth, easy-listening indie band track.

And only then did I realize my mistake.

I'd gone about it the wrong way, getting the arrangements ready before deciding on a direction.

*I should've hinted at the direction I wanted him to go in right from the start.*

I'd told Kim Raebin to arrange the anime theme songs to fit a TeSTAR performance, and he'd done exactly that.

They weren't funny at all—they were genuinely sophisticated.

*No, I don't think it would be easy for this guy to make a low-quality, funny arrangement in the first place...*

Of course, he had good musical instincts, so he could probably manage with the right references, but it definitely wasn't his forte.

Especially when he was this excited, saying, “This looks like it'll be fun,” and drafting arrangements for all the candidate songs... There was a good chance he'd get discouraged by what I had to say now.

"How do you like them? They may sound a bit clunky since I haven't done any detailed adjustments or mixing yet, but they've all got the vibe I was going for!"

"Yes. They're definitely easy to listen to."

"Thank you!"

Okay. This guy was really excited right now.

*We don't have much time.*

If I said something unnecessary, Kim Raebin would probably end up wondering, “Funny... music?” And that'd be a headache.

I listened to all three songs Kim Raebin had prepared and fell into thought.

What was the purpose of the video?

*Why were only anime that went viral on WeTube listed as candidates?*

I wanted to stop thinking in terms of generations, but... Anyway, they were targeting Generation Z, who'd seen the viral videos, and Generation M, who'd watched the anime themselves when they were young.

The key was common ground. That was what gave the content the potential to go viral.

*Hmm.*

As I rubbed my chin, Cha Eugene asked without hesitation.

"Moondae-hyung! Do you not like this?"

"...!"

*Damn.*

"...! If you see anything lacking, please point it out. I will correct it!"

Kim Raebin looked startled and stood at attention like a rookie soldier.

*Was I too quiet?*

Well, anyone with even a little tact could tell that I wasn't completely satisfied. I'd let my guard down because it was Kim Raebin.

At times like this, a relaxed reaction was the best way to keep the mood from getting too serious. I shrugged.

I'd just thought of a way to handle this.

"The arrangements are good... But I don't think these are songs everyone knows. Do you guys know these anime?"

"I don't know!"

"I've watched Detective Arthur, but I've only heard of the others."

Cha Eugene asked brightly.

"Do you know them, Moondae-hyung? Have you watched them?"

*...Right.* Park Moondae was only two years older than these guys.

I gave them a sensible answer.

"...I saw them on WeTube."

"Oh."

"I see. For the performance, then... You're very thorough, as usual!"

I decided not to clear up that misunderstanding.

"Anyway, the songs the production team gave us are nice, but they don't really have much to do with us..."

I tossed out the bait.

"Wouldn't it be more fun if we did a song that had something to do with us?"

"Is there a song like that?"

"I like ⟨Billy's Exciting Animal Tales⟩!"

It seemed to be an American cartoon I'd never heard of.

I shook my head and spoke.

"We have to choose an anime that was famous in Korea around the 2000s. That's the penalty condition."

"Boo..."

It was supposed to be a 'nostalgic anime theme song'. In that sense, Cha Eugene's choice didn't fit.

*Maybe this'll do.*

Instead, I showed them a WeTube video of the song I'd just thought of.

Apparently, this one had also become a meme on TicTacToe because of its creative gestures.

"So... How about something like this?"

"...?"

With this, we wouldn't have to make a ridiculous arrangement and risk discouraging Kim Raebin.

*There's no need for us to dress up funnily.*

We could make it funny in a different way.

*The stage isn't funny, but the situation makes it funny.*

The humor would come from the context.

That way, the person arranging it could immerse himself in the work without difficulty, without even realizing what was funny about it.

"This... ah."

As expected, Kim Raebin's expression brightened.

"I see. This could be a worthwhile challenge. Please give me a moment!"

And Kim Raebin put on his headset and got to work on the spot.

*...From scratch without even using the original track?*

Anyway, he was definitely fired up. I nodded.

*I guess that settles it.*

Just then, the last member of our little team, sitting next to me, opened his mouth with a serious expression.

"Hyung."

*Oh, this guy might be a problem.*

I could tell that this clearly wasn’t Cha Eugene’s kind of thing.

"What?"

"Kim Raebin and hyung, do you guys really like this? Really?"

"Yes."

Cha Eugene glanced at the video, then looked at Kim Raebin and finally at me. Then he sighed and spoke in English.

[Oh my god!]

*Well, look at you getting all sassy.*

"This is supposed to be a comedy stage. I'd say it's pretty good in that regard."

"I know! Ugh..."

Cha Eugene smacked his lips a few times, looking put out, but soon shrugged.

That was basically his way of surrendering.

[Hyung. I'm really doing this for the team, and for no other reason. I never thought I'd ever do something like this in my life.]

I chuckled. *Got it, punk.*

"Here's the pitch..."

Kim Raebin carefully adjusted the key and accompaniment, and finished an arrangement 1 minute and 24 seconds long in half a day.

We immediately scheduled an early morning practice.
