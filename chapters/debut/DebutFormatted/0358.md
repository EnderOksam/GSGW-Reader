# Chapter 358 - Muddying the Waters, II

By the end of May, TeSTAR had undoubtedly become a top-tier idol group both domestically and on the global stage.

So what did Park Minha, the leader of Miry-nay, their immediate juniors at the same agency, think of TeSTAR? In truth, she rarely gave them much thought.

*Well... I just think of them as my seniors.*

To begin with, she hadn't seen much of them lately. Even within the company, TeSTAR's new label had been given its own office space, so there were few opportunities for the two groups to run into each other.

But there was one particular way she felt about them.

*...I envy them! I envy them!*

It was rather cheeky of TeSTAR to escape the clutches of the director all by themselves, but in fact, there was no group she envied more or considered a better role model than them.

...And there was a certain sunbaenim who was largely responsible for her thinking that way.

*...The fact that he announced the label at the awards ceremony ahead of time... He must have done that on purpose, right?*

Yes.

This was Park Moondae, TeSTAR's main vocalist, also known as the 'Soft Bean Rice Cake and Apple Rice Cake Puppy.'

Park Minha screamed internally.

*Puppy, my ass!*

He seemed to have complete control over the agency, and he always appeared in the right place at exactly the right time... He must be some kind of genius. Anyway, what did he want?

"......"

*Thinking about it that way, he really is the perfect idol.*

Anyway, he was a little scary.

As a result, she always broke out in a cold sweat whenever she had to stop the other members from trying to become friendly with the successful senior group.

*What if he does something to our members because he doesn't want his own members to have friendships with the opposite sex that could threaten their careers?!*

She didn't know how such a scary person could look so harmless and innocent when the camera was rolling.

*...In reality, he seems a bit difficult to approach.*

Through many years of self-development and careful distribution of stats—though Park Minha knew nothing about the latter—Park Moondae had already become a flawless celebrity. But even now, when she watched videos of him, it gave her chills how perfectly the image of a friendly puppy still suited him.

He was worthy of being a role model.

*...He's amazing. Really.*

So, she made a resolution of her own.

*When contract renewal season comes, we'll make it big!*

*She'd make sure no detail escaped her, so they wouldn't lose out on anything. She would protect the group.*

Park Minha had been deep in thought when the devil himself called her.

And he seemed to have something very significant to say.

*– Do you have any concerns these days?*

"......"

The moment she heard those words, all sorts of disastrous outcomes came to Park Minha's mind.

*Huh? What?*

*W-what is he thinking...?*

So, like a reaction robot, she automatically gave a generic answer.

"We have all the usual concerns. The sort of things everyone worries about. Still, I'm very happy that I can work as an idol and bring happiness to my fans—"

*– Hmm. Yes.*

Park Moondae didn't cut her off. He just listened.

As a result, she quickly ran out of safe things to say.

*Ahh!*

The pressure was on, and she felt like she needed to say something more, but she didn't know what.

*Oh, please!*

*Come on, please! I'm too old for this! You're not my therapist, sunbaenim!*

Fortunately, Park Moondae gave Park Minha a hint before she passed out from stress.

*– Well then, I'm glad. I'm wondering if there's something more specific bothering you.*

"...Well, when you say more specific, what kind of things are you talking about...?"

*– How are things going with the agency these days?*

"The agency?"

*– Yes. After TeSTAR went independent, we haven't been working as closely with the agency, so I'm not as well-informed as I used to be.*

Park Moondae said calmly.

*– But it seems like they still focus on the US. We're also targeting the American market these days, so I was wondering about your group.*

"Ah."

*Ah, that...*

At that moment, Park Minha began to speak almost by reflex.

It was something she had genuinely been concerned about for quite some time. After she'd said her piece, she regretted it and thought, 'I was caught.' But it wasn't a trap Park Moondae had in store for her. In fact, he was about to bring her group an offer that was almost too good to be true.

Of course, that didn't mean Park Moondae hadn't lured her in.

---

*– ...So we're still working hard on our promotions!*

"Hmm."

I combined the information Park Minha had given me about Miry-nay's current situation with what I'd gathered from reading between the lines.

*Miry-nay's current position is... rather uncertain.*

They had hit a plateau.

She was too smart to criticize the agency and had glossed over things to keep everything positive, but she seemed aware that most of the popularity and momentum surrounding their debut had already died out.

It was understandable. As they continued to make music that would be popular overseas and focused their promotions on that, their connection with the Korean public had grown weak.

*But the fandom is okay.*

Because they produced high-quality content themselves and had plenty of financial backing, their fandom continued to grow steadily.

*They've settled into a pattern.*

In short, they had become fandom-type idols whose digital sales had weakened as public interest declined, while their physical album sales continued to rise because of their strong fandom.

*I guess you could say that they've successfully carved out a spot for themselves.*

If they could regain the public's attention just once, things could really work out for them. No doubt she was frustrated that the agency seemed incapable of thinking about anything other than the US.

This was the situation when Youngrin's agency debuted a new female idol group.

The group was already experiencing massive success.

*– Y-yes, there are so many talented artists these days! We've realized we need to work harder too...*

That was ultimately what this meant.

*They feel threatened because they're targeting similar markets.*

They were both trendy female idol groups with global reach, especially in the West.

Two similar images.

The problem wasn't limited to the American market; their similar image also made Miry-nay less distinctive in Korea.

It was usually the newer group that made a stronger impression and eventually took its predecessor's place.

In a sense, it was the perfect time to act before the new group took all the buzz for themselves.

I crossed my arms, holding the phone against my ear with one shoulder.

*Things aren't that bad.*

Miry-nay already had a reputation as a top-tier idol group, along with strong physical album sales.

Powty, the new group, was stronger in terms of overseas digital streaming, even if their album sales weren't as high. Their current popularity came from the rise in digital streaming, even in Korea.

Both groups had their strengths and weaknesses.

*This will make strategizing even easier, since the two groups are so evenly matched.* Few things are more fun than watching evenly matched opponents struggle against one another.

I came to a conclusion. But there was one more hurdle to overcome.

*I don't have the final say in this.* Miry-nay did.

I listened quietly to Park Minha's words, nodding every now and then. By the time she was done, I spoke again.

"Yes. Thank you for telling me. And I have one more question."

*– Yes?*

I asked slowly.

"Would you prefer that more people take an interest in your group even if things get a little... noisy?"

*– ...*

She was silent for a long time.

She probably knew exactly what I meant by "noisy."

*I'm saying that people will criticize her group.*

Of course, the less they were on the public's radar, the less criticism they received. The way they spoke and gestured, plastic surgery suspicions, photos from the past, and so on... I was asking whether she preferred the stability of the status quo or being bothered by all sorts of silly rumors.

*I wouldn't be surprised if she was a little traumatized by the idea of public scrutiny, considering she'd been on* Idol Inc.

Without saying anything more, I waited.

But after a while, a firm voice came from the phone.

*– Of course.*

*Good. She's brave.*

Satisfied, I told her my conclusion.

"How would you feel about the agency doing this?"

*– D-doing what?*

I proceeded to explain.

The very next day, celebrity news articles began pouring out in the dozens.

---

★$
– **[Rookie Powty is on a roll, heading toward global audiences... Will they set a new girl group record?]**

– **[Powty beats Miry-nay and Blue Rain to the top of the search rankings]**

– **[Miry-nay announces a comeback. Are they worried about the popular new group?]**
$★

Some of the articles were more in-your-face than others, but the main message was the same.

*Powty defeated Miry-nay the moment they debuted!*

The articles subtly suggested that Powty would continue to outperform them.

Naturally, this prompted a variety of extreme reactions from readers.

★$
– Fuck, stop tormenting them

– If they're going to do this, please give Youngrin a comeback... They're obsessed with their rookies. These crazy bastards—did they forget who earned all that money so they could move into their own building?

– But objectively, isn't it true their results are better than Miry-nay's? Why all the fuss LOL

└ Get lost, you troll.

└ Okay, you're desperate to believe I'm a troll, huh? T\_T
$★

There was a common undercurrent to all of this.

Everyone naturally assumed that Youngrin's agency was responsible for these cookie-cutter promotional articles.

Targeting an older group with a similar concept through the press had been proven time and again to be an effective way to raise one's own reputation.

Youngrin's agency had already been planning to do the same, since they weren't idiots, and I'd confirmed that this was indeed the case.

An employee from our label contacted the press and was told that Youngrin's agency had already promised them promotional material.

This made things easier.

– *Everything went great. All we had to do was tweak a few words.*

The staff had reported this to me in a slightly excited voice.

*So... we're helping them.*

I nodded.

We were essentially donating to them. Youngrin's agency had been intending to do this anyway, but I'd beaten them to it and given them a little nudge, so to speak.

*They should be grateful to us for this.*

No doubt they had intended to target us rather than Miry-nay, but that was none of my business.

I put down my phone.

All sorts of press agencies were running articles with the same information, and it was making a lot of noise.

"Nice buzz."

Miry-nay's fans would probably be stressed out, but this was better than the group having no coverage at all.

Rivalry tended to be a good source of motivation for everyone involved.

*What's more, Miry-nay is making a comeback soon.*

I hoped this would lead to even more buzz in Korea.

– *Yes! The music is good!*

Park Minha seemed confident.

I grinned and tossed my phone away.

Now all I had to do was wait for the results following Miry-nay's comeback.

*By the time this plan comes to fruition, the agency will release something to the press about our record... I should have the mission results in two weeks at the earliest.*

If nothing went wrong, this latest hurdle would be a thing of the past.

*Neatly done.*

I stood up, stretching lightly.

"Ah."

There was one more thing to take care of. Until the results were out, our group had to remain visually separate from Miry-nay in every way.

To help with that... We needed some kind of independent activity that could generate buzz to help Miry-nay while making it clear we were doing our own thing.

*A popular variety show, obviously.*

Coincidentally, the production team of the variety show that had forced us to sell *hotteok* in America contacted us again.

*Perfect timing.*

---

"Hello~"

"Hello!"

"Oh my, TeSTAR, welcome! Please sit down quickly! Oh my, we're so glad to see you!"

A few days later, we were invited to a meeting about the new variety show.

"First, here's the name..."

We were given some presentation slides, and it seemed they'd already made a logo.

+=
⟨The House Next Door's Grandson⟩
=+

The title alone gave me a hint about what the show would be like.

*It's going to be random as fuck.*

It was obvious that they were going to throw us into some unconventional situations, and I welcomed it.

Several writers we'd grown familiar with excitedly explained the plan to us.

"You'll head down there, do some cooking, grow some vegetables... You know, stuff that's very peaceful and relaxing."

"Ahaha. Yes!"

They were merely laying their trap for us, of course. The members looked at each other and nodded.

*What is this really going to be?*

*I think it's going to be some kind of boot camp.*

*That doesn't sound out of the realm of possibility.*

I wondered if we'd be forced into some ridiculous situation in the countryside again, getting into debt and having to recoup the costs by working on a fishing boat.

But the writers seemed very sure of themselves.

"By the way, we are not baiting you at all! You're going to have some fun with mini-games... In any case, it's not anything you need to be nervous about."

*Right.* I decided I'd pretend to believe them.

"Really?"

"Yes!"

One writer said emphatically. We tried to smile and nod, but the writer added, eyes burning with indignation,

"You can sue us if it turns out otherwise! Really!"

"......"

*Sue you?*

A confused smile appeared on Ryu Chungwoo's face. The other guys had similar expressions.

*Hey, I think they're serious.*

Were they really trying to make a relaxing rural program that would help the tired working adults of the modern era unwind?

"Since you're idols, we can incorporate aspects of your profession and give the show more meaning that way! We can also have you perform for the people living nearby..."

Raebin exclaimed.

"...! I think that would be a *really* meaningful opportunity!"

"Hm, you're right. I like the premise."

Bae Sejin looked a little relieved as he went through the material again, and Seon Ahyeon was writing something down.

Everyone seemed at peace.

"...Yes."

I turned to Keun Sejin.

He was doing his best to maintain a cheerful smile, but the moment our eyes met, his eye twitched.

*Yeah.* We were in trouble.

*Relaxing, my ass!*

The production team had completely lost its touch as far as variety shows were concerned.
