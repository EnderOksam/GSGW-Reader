# The Tour Documentary, III

Cha Eugene was used to conversing in a language other than his mother tongue.

However, that didn't mean he was perfectly fluent. There was always room for misunderstandings.

So, Cha Eugene always tried to be as straightforward as possible.

"They only ask about bad things," he said in Korean.

"The documentary crew?" Park Moondae asked.

"That's right!"

Cha Eugene nodded and put the ice cream in his mouth.

Then he began to grumble to himself in rapid English.

[What damage the car accident left behind, how hard it was to prepare for the concert, whether there were any safety issues or injuries! And they asked if I'd ever become disillusioned with this line of work! It's like a prison guard was interviewing me!]

Park Moondae flexed his fingers in response.

"So, they kept asking you if you felt like things were going badly."

"Yes!" Eugene exclaimed in Korean.

Moondae understood him. That was why Eugene enjoyed talking to him!

"Why do they have to be like this? [They're not giving us any room to show our coolest and best sides. And I'm happy right now!]"

He wanted to share delight, not to show pain or negative energy.

Cha Eugene had a philosophy about his job. He had no intention of living like some celebrities who capitalized on events in their personal lives for fame.

So he crossed his arms over his chest.

Coincidentally, Park Moondae was sitting in the same posture as he listened to him.

"So, I'm not apologizing," he said, switching back to Korean.

It was a dramatic statement, but the pink-haired person on the other side grinned.

"...You must have been pretty hurt by what Lee Sejin said, right?"

That was exactly how Eugene felt.

[Of course I did!]

"Lee Sejin said it for your sake. If all that makes it into the documentary is the scene of you slapping the camera away, it won't look good for you. People will misunderstand."

"It's okay. I don't care," Eugene answered in Korean.

"Then, you should care about the team. Because *we* care about you."

"......"

Park Moondae was trying to express both their concern for the team and their concern for Cha Eugene in a roundabout way.

However, Cha Eugene understood only the latter and nodded right away.

*He's worried about me.*

It was a silver lining of the language barrier.

"And the crew... They probably wanted to show the public that we go through hard times too. Everyone's already seen how popular and amazing we are."

Park Moondae slowly steered the conversation in a different direction.

"Everyone feels pain, and seeing it in someone else sometimes helps people feel a connection. It makes us seem more real."

Cha Eugene respected this clever team member and hung on his every word.

And Park Moondae's words showed that he respected Cha Eugene in return.

"But they can't force us to show our pain if we don't want to."

That was the correct answer.

"Yes."

Cha Eugene growled in English.

[And they are all fucking liars.]

"Liars?"

[I said it very clearly in the first interview. I didn't want to answer any questions about injuries, pain, or personal affairs. I told them to ask our company about those things, not me!]

"...! Hmm."

[They nodded and said they would be careful. But hyung saw it too, right??]

"Yes, I did."

Throughout the concert, he had seen the documentary crew doing their best to capture the painful or unpleasant side of the job and trying to record him showing discomfort.

And they kept talking about the accident without end!

– *No, thank you.*

– *Oh, sorry about that. We'll be careful.*

The production team always promised to be careful.

But they kept trying to ask similar questions whenever there was an opportunity.

That showed how little respect they had for him.

"They did it so many times! Over ten times!"

"......"

Moondae regarded him silently.

Moreover, the worst part was that they switched targets once he'd gotten angry.

– Eugene-ssi has recovered very quickly... Tell me, was there anything in the accident before—

They were clearly intending to ask about Park Moondae when he was in a coma!

*How dare they ask someone about something like that in the middle of a concert without getting permission first?*

It was rude in the extreme!

"They were being so rude. They made a mistake. I stopped them from carrying on with it."

Cha Eugene used Korean again to make a stronger argument.

And Park Moondae seemed to be lost in thought.

"......"

But it didn't take long before the answer came.

It was the answer he had been waiting for.

"You're right."

"...!"

Yes!

"Okay. I understand now."

Park Moondae made it simple and clear.

"Let's go and insist on an apology. They're obviously in the wrong."

"Great!"

Cha Eugene raised his hand for a fist bump, followed by a handshake. Park Moondae thought, 'Do I have to?', but went along with it without showing his reluctance.

*This is the joy of the team!*

Cha Eugene shook hands with him, then laughed cheerfully, flourishing his hand in the air.

Of course, there was work to be done in advance.

"I need to finish this ice cream."

"Okay. Eat up."

Cha Eugene spent the morning relaxing with Park Moondae on the beach.

It was a pretty pleasant way to spend his day off.

***

"Let's buy pizza! The pizza here is delicious!"

"Okay."

With the sun high in the sky, Cha Eugene had fully recovered. He'd been talking about pizza on the way back to the hotel, so we bought some.

*It's done.*

I'd decided to let him do whatever he wanted today, no matter how absurd. He wasn't a guy who needed to lose weight, so a little pizza was fine.

@r@[It's $89.]@r@

[Here you go.]

I paid for the pizza and organized the situation in my head.

As expected, once I'd fed Cha Eugene something sweet on the beach, he'd spilled everything that had happened.

*No wonder Cha Eugene was so pissed off.*

From what I'd heard, the crew had been even ruder to him than they'd been to me.

It seemed that the documentary crew had deliberately tried harder to provoke Cha Eugene.

The reason was apparent.

*It must have been because Cha Eugene seemed to have the most open personality.*

Since he looked the most straightforward and candid among us, he probably seemed like the ideal person to draw unpleasant truths out of.

And then they'd pushed him to get footage they could use for the documentary.

*Terrible...*

I remembered something I'd heard in a photography professor's general education class at university.

*Didn't he say it was better to take pictures of people first and then ask for permission afterward?*

The best photo opportunities disappeared if you stopped to ask permission first. I probably remembered it so clearly because it sparked debate on the university community site.

Anyway, if that attitude wasn't widespread in the industry, then TeSTAR had simply had bad luck with this particular documentary crew.

They didn't seem to know when to stop.

*It's about time we drew a line.*

Just then, Cha Eugene was happily walking along with boxes of pizza in his hands when he asked me a question.

"Hyung! I have a question. How are we going to talk to them?"

"We don't need to talk to them."

I spoke leisurely.

"The company will take care of it. All you have to do is stand by and wait for their apology."

What he'd told me suggested several ways we could turn the tables on the production crew.

"You should go upstairs and eat. I'll make a call."

"Got it!"

As soon as we returned to the hotel, I contacted the company.

Since the recent deal, the management office had been staffed with more responsive people, so we didn't need to worry about our image or take care of things ourselves.

"Hello... Yes. You heard that I found Eugene, right?"

– *Yes, yes! We were informed of Moondae-ssi and Eugene-ssi's whereabouts.*

"Yes. So there are a few things Eugene told me that I wanted to let you know about..."

I neatly summarized what Cha Eugene had told me on the beach, focusing on what I felt was the most important part.

"...That's what they've been doing. The production team shouldn't be doing that, right?"

– *Ah~*

The manager's voice changed slightly.

– *I see what you mean.*

It was a tone that gave off a strange sense of confidence. It meant that he understood.

*Looks like we've finally got a company at our back.*

Until now, I'd thought Ryu Chungwoo should get paid extra for doing all the work of a manager too, so this was a welcome change.

I finished the call and went up to the hotel room.

And the moment I visited Cha Eugene's room to check his condition and get pizza...

"Moondae-hyung!"

"Welcome, Moondae~"

Everyone waved at me, holding slices of pizza.

Apparently, they were waiting in the room just in time for Cha Eugene's return.

"Hyung, come here!"

Cha Eugene sat in the middle, beaming with joy. It seemed he'd already filled them in to some extent, and the air had cleared. If they'd needed an interpreter, Seon Ahyeon had probably done a good job.

"W-we saved some pizza for you...!" Seon Ahyeon said.

"Oh, thank you."

I sat down among the waving guys.

"Did you guys talk it out?"

"Of course~ Oh, Eugene, were you hurt because your hyung told you to apologize without even asking what was going on?" Lee Sejin said.

"Yes!!"

"I said that because I was afraid it would be disastrous if you didn't apologize. Of course I'm on your side, Eugene! That's a given!"

"It's okay! I didn't discuss it with you guys either. Sorry about that!"

"Yes, yes, let's handle it better next time!"

As expected, they reconciled quickly. Bae Sejin seemed to be wondering, 'Is this something we can settle so quickly?' but soon gave up and ate his pizza.

Kim Raebin was the only one who still looked serious.

"Cha Eugene, from now on, be sure to contact us first before leaving. Even if we have a day off, we are on an official schedule, so be careful."

"Yes, okay!"

"...! Uh, right."

Kim Raebin was taken aback by how readily Eugene agreed, but soon smiled to himself, looking proud. *You two are getting along well these days, huh?*

However, Ryu Chungwoo seemed to be deep in thought while watching this scene.

*Hmm.*

Seeing his relaxed expression, it didn't seem like a big deal.

And some really serious consequences were about to befall the production crew.

*I'm looking forward to it.*

I tried to picture the spectacle as I ate my pizza.

And in the evening...

Just before the members' group interview, we overheard the conversation between the company and the production team in the next room.

"We've confirmed that it's a complete breach of contract," someone from the company said.

"No, that is a misunderstanding."

"It's too obvious a violation to call it a misunderstanding. We laid down the rules at the very beginning."

"Hoo, you don't need to be so strict about the filming process—"

"No. The artists themselves told you repeatedly to talk to the company. It's all been recorded, so let's take a look at the footage."

"...!"

Okay. This part was the problem.

Cha Eugene had already brought up the 'company' story. Multiple times too.

The artist himself had refused to comment on certain matters and asked them to talk to the company, but they'd ignored him and kept pressing?

It was a breach of contract because this clause was in the contract.

!pb doc
Should A make a request during the filming regarding sensitive content, B shall discuss the appropriateness of the content anew.
pb!

There was also a compensation clause in case of violation of the clause.

With T1 backing our agency, a firm statement from the company would be enough to leave this production team quaking in their boots. They weren't a major broadcaster, after all.

The guy who had been eavesdropping with me tapped me on the shoulder.

"Hey, Moondae is smart," Lee Sejin said.

"It was nothing."

I shrugged.

Now we just had to get them to delete the footage by threatening to call off the documentary deal if they didn't agree.

"Yes. Please do so."

And it seemed that the threat had worked.

In the end, before we started filming, the production team came to Cha Eugene with an apology that sounded more like an excuse.

"We're really sorry if you were distressed."

*They claimed they weren't trying to gather footage for malicious editing, and that they just wanted to create better, more honest content.*

"...Okay~ But I'm the one being filmed, so please respect my wishes."

Cha Eugene coolly moved on, so the matter didn't become any more serious, but the production crew became more cautious.

It was a benefit to all members.

*This is enough.*

I quickly stopped Cha Eugene from giving me a thumbs-up.

The rest of the documentary filming focused a little more on TeSTAR's professionalism as a group.

And the night after the interview...

TeSTAR left their individual rooms and gathered in one huge hotel room.

"It's so spacious!"

"Oh, this place is nice. Are you paying for it, Chungwoo-hyung?"

"Mhm, only for today."

Ryu Chungwoo, the man who pushed for this, answered as he closed the door.

"The company will take care of the expenses from now on."

"From now... on?"

*What does he mean, from now on?*

"Yes. I was thinking about us spending at least one day a week together like this, in one room, while on tour. What do you all think?"

Ryu Chungwoo had a rather serious face.

"I've been thinking about it. So far on this tour, we've been keeping to our own rooms and hardly doing anything outside our concerts, so we rarely talk to each other."

"That... Yes."

Those who had been living alone for the past few weeks looked blankly at Ryu Chungwoo.

"I think that's partly why the incident with Eugene got so complicated. We didn't have time to find out how everyone was feeling."

Ryu Chungwoo ended his speech with a smile.

"I wish we had time to talk and understand how each other was doing. That's my suggestion, anyway."

I could feel his sincerity. In an instant, several of the guys looked very touched.

"Hyung..."

"Hey, it's so touching!"

"I-I like it...!"

"Uh... I don't mind... if it's just a day."

Even Bae Sejin agreed. Even the guy who'd been ecstatic about having a room to himself was on board, so it was practically a unanimous vote.

"What about Moondae?"

"...Sounds good," I said.

It was a good decision. It could prevent unpredictable outbursts.

I nodded calmly, but suddenly realized the reality.

*Wait, he made it sound nice, but... Isn't this basically a workshop once a week?*

We'd gather together, check in to make sure nobody was getting any strange ideas, and talk about albums, promotions, and concerts.

*Actually, never mind.* The guys were already sprawled out on the floor.

"Our teamwork is absolutely the best. I'm moved," Cha Eugene said.

"It would be nice to bring a beam projector next time," Kim Raebin said.

"Shall we order room service?? Do you want to play cards while eating?"

"......"

Yes, we'd come too far together to treat each other as mere coworkers. 

*This isn't really work, is it? It feels more like a club room.*

I admitted it as I lay back on the sofa. We'd gotten too close for me to think of this as a workshop anymore.

*...No, it's more than that.*

I wouldn't deny it. Even though I was stuck between so many people... It was very comfortable.

I relaxed and thought to myself.

*I suppose we can just focus on the tour for the time being.*

...However, I was overlooking one thing.

Cha Eugene's sensitive footage had been removed thanks to the company's firm action, but the rest, including mine, was left as it was without any corrections requested.

The repercussions of the portrayal of us as 'perfectionist idols doing their best despite the aftereffects of the car accident'... were just around the corner.
