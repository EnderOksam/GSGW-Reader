# The Soundtrack Controversy, I

It was mid-April, and only a few stops remained on the US leg of TeSTAR's tour.

"Let's go to the cinema again!"

"We don't have time."

"Then let's make time!"

*He's being persistent today.*

I looked at Cha Eugene, who kept asking to go to the movies even though we were at the composing camp.

Because of this guy, we'd gone to see ⟨Cosmic Gunner⟩ under cover of night in every city we'd toured so far.

*There have been quite a few posts about people spotting us at the cinema.*

We'd gone so regularly that people started predicting where we'd go next and turning up ahead of time to wait for us. That was why we'd stopped, but now this guy was acting like he had withdrawal symptoms.

"Cha Eugene, it's unreasonable to keep stealing the hyungs' sleep time just because you want to rewatch a cameo that only lasts a few seconds..." Raebin began.

"My voice is in it for way longer than that! And we looked amazing!"

"Haha, that's right. It was amazing to hear our song in the ending credits."

Yeah, none of us had known that our song would be chosen as the main theme song.

I figured the fact that the producer came to our meeting in person last time meant they'd liked the song more than they'd expected.

*Even though those bastards refused to pay us more than the agreed amount.*

I decided to consider the publicity we were getting part of our payment. In any case, the composer himself was very excited.

"Yes! I found it to be a novel experience as well!"

"Yes."

"I wasn't surprised at all. Raebin's song was really good~"

Admiration poured out once again for the guy who was almost single-handedly responsible for this feat. Even Cha Eugene nodded.

"Kim Raebin's song was really amazing."

Kim Raebin solemnly nodded.

"It wasn't as complete as I would have liked given the time constraints, but we made up for that with feedback after filming, so I think it reached a level I'm not ashamed of."

"GOOD~"

It was rare for the two to agree on something like that.

"Th-th-the response is really good. There are a lot of articles coming out... Even in the US."

"Oh~ Really?"

It seemed Seon Ahyeon had even looked up news about our cameo, which was rare for him. Doing something we didn't usually do seemed to have brought us some unexpected results.

I translated the article he showed me into Korean.

"A K-pop boy band composed the main theme song for the recent Limestone movie... The public was surprised to learn the identity of the people in that impressive cameo."

"Oh~"

It was too early to celebrate the articles, since they could say anything. But when I actually searched a few anonymous American online communities, I found posts about us popping up here and there.

*It helps that the movie itself was such a box office hit.*

This was something we couldn't do anything about, but we were lucky.

Anyway, we'd made an impression by appearing in a successful movie, and people were already talking about us.

Even low-quality recordings of TeSTAR's cameo were making the rounds.

The funny thing was that a lot of people who apparently didn't know it was a cameo were saying, 'I thought they were supporting characters, so I was confused when they didn't appear again.'

└ That was a cameo. They're a K-pop group.

└ Are you serious?

Soon, there were actual write-ups explaining who we were.

I remembered the comments on a popular post titled 'The mad lads behind the band in ⟨Cosmic Gunner⟩'.

★$
– *This* is K-pop?

– Is my stereotype the problem, or are these kids the exception?

└ The K-pop fans have shown up, guys lol.
$★

Scrolling further down, I found people even recommending the variety show where we'd sold *hotteok* in the US.

In other words, the reaction overseas was even better than expected.

"The overall response is really good."

"I know."

And here was the guy who was listening carefully to all this while pretending not to be.

He was the other main contributor to this incredible response, alongside Kim Raebin.

I turned my head.

"Hyung, your acting was really great."

"...Ahem."

It was Bae Sejin.

He'd heard this so many times by now that he was only a little embarrassed. His reaction at first had been quite something.

Cha Eugene cheered.

"That's right! Absolutely perfect!"

"Actually, it wasn't perfect. I tried to deliver the lines in a way that fit the outfit and the situation, but my pronunciation wasn't great... Anyway! Thank you! Uh, you helped me a lot with my pronunciation."

"I know!"

Bae Sejin had learned to accept the praise meekly by now. He finally seemed to realize that continuing to deny it would make things awkward.

Seeing so much praise for his acting online had probably helped, too.

I recalled the favorable reactions pouring in on the first day of release.

★$
– I thought he was an actor?

– He's crazy. He's amazing.

– What is Bae Sejin? What is Bae Sejin? He captivated the audience in just 3 seconds. This is what I call Hollywood-level acting.
$★

Even I'd been surprised to see it.

*He asked a lot of questions on set.*

Bae Sejin had even asked Cha Eugene to interpret for him as he tried to understand as much as possible about the script and the situation.

*And the ad-lib he threw in at the end was approved on his first attempt.*

At first, the lines we'd been given for the cameo consisted only of humorous cursing and arguing.

But Bae Sejin discussed it with the crew on set, thinking it might be more fun to add another line at the end. The result was that ad-lib he came up with on the spot.

*[Come and listen to us again sometime.]*

It was a joke he'd made knowing that our song would play again in a fight scene later in the movie.

We hadn't known the complete version of the song, vocals and all, would play over the ending credits, though.

Anyway, his acting was so good that almost everyone who'd seen the movie had nothing but praise for him.

Of course, some people took the opportunity to try to pit us against each other.

★$
– I don't know why he's an idol. Wouldn't it be better for everyone if he just acted? TeSTAR's performances would improve, and Bae Sejin could do what he's actually good at.

└ ㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋ Look at the way he speaks. No thanks.

└ Sejin is a pro-idol and the cutest hamster ever. Get lost.
$★

Naturally, the group's fans immediately blocked the people leaving those comments.

The problem was that this seemed to be the prevailing reaction even among ordinary moviegoers and cinephiles who'd only come to see the movie.

★$
– It's too wasteful to just do a cameo. Build up your filmography quickly.

– There's such a shortage of male actors in their 20s right now. Child actors might be our last hope.
$★

People were predicting and hoping that Bae Sejin would eventually turn to acting after working as an idol for a few years.

*Anyway, being an actor is still considered the better job.*

Maybe it was a matter of career longevity, among other things. I thought about it briefly, then shrugged and moved on.

*For now, I'll just let him enjoy it.*

TeSTAR had really raised its standing by winning so many *daesangs* recently, and public opinion toward us remained balanced.

In the first place, Bae Sejin had only appeared in the movie because TeSTAR had worked on its OST.

That meant nobody was expecting Bae Sejin to quit group activities and start acting 'immediately.'

*Still, if we want to keep doing group activities while doing individual activities, we need to start paying attention to this sort of thing.*

I didn't know what these guys would prioritize when the time came... But it would be best for the group to create an environment where the members could choose.

*It's good to do various activities as a group and produce good results.*

*And there's something about this movie collaboration that people don't know yet, which might help with that.*

Coincidentally, a similar topic had also come up among the members.

"Umm~ Hyung, did they ever say when the entirety of our OST would be released?"

The song we'd made for this movie's OST.

⟨Black Hole⟩.

Even though the official digital version hadn't been released, illegal tracks extracted from the trailers were already circulating. Google was even autocompleting the song title after TeSTAR's name.

However, we didn't have the full rights to the music.

"Well. We haven't been told a specific date... I think it's up to the film company."

I nodded.

"Yes. Because it will be included in their OST album, not ours."

T1 couldn't do anything about it because of the way the contract was arranged.

...And in fact, this was exactly what I'd wanted in the contract.

*If T1 had the rights, they'd have released it in excitement on the very day the movie premiered.*

This kind of thing should be released when interest was at its peak, but T1 didn't have the patience to wait until then. I was sure of it.

Besides, the most important reason was this.

"Limestone will take care of it."

It was the Limestone brand we were talking about.

The name behind many major American superhero films.

*Being included in their OST album is a huge boon for us.*

As soon as our song came out under the familiar Limestone label, instead of under the name of some unfamiliar foreign K-pop group, the barrier to entry disappeared.

The elimination of preconceived notions and prejudices.

It was the best reward.

*This is worth doing.*

In addition, there was some content that would amplify the effect.

*Hmm.*

"An American album? That's amazing."

"Y-y-yeah, that's right. It was also our first film song... You've worked hard, Raebin...!"

"Ahyeon-hyung also did great supporting communication and overseeing our performances!"

Watching the others talk about how rewarding the experience itself had been, I worked hard on calculating our return on investment in my head.

And after a few days...

Under the pretext of thanking the fans for their support, the OST album was released under the Limestone label.

And not only the song, but also a short video was released to the world.

★$
– What is this?

– Huh, fuck
$★

It was a live performance that we filmed on the bar set.

Yeah, the Limestone bastards had decided to release it for publicity instead of including it in a VOD or director's cut.

The day the OST sound source and performance video were released...

On our way back to Korea for a short while, we watched the video on a tablet PC using the in-flight Wi-Fi.

"Wow."

"The editing is really high quality."

It was edited to feel like part of the movie, so the camera work was a little different from how our stage performances were usually filmed. It was unfamiliar, but incredible.

Hands and feet manipulating instruments, chins, back profiles... All those shots were edited together into an artistic scene under colorful neon lights.

The avant-garde outfits and backdrop, the rebellious performance, and the rich vocals. It was a performance that did ⟨Cosmic Gunner⟩ justice, and it was really worth seeing.

It felt natural because there were people who had actually used instruments similar to the bizarre space instruments.

$p
♪ *Hit it like a comet!* ♫
p$

I nodded along to the beat as I watched the low-angle shot of Cha Eugene standing in the center, tossing his microphone from hand to hand.

*They put quite a bit of effort into this.*

It was full of American sensibility, but it was also unique because we'd created such a distinctive image for the band in the movie. There was a definite charm to it.

And not just because we were the ones performing.

It hadn't even been a few hours since its release, but it had already exceeded 20 million views.

At that moment, Cha Eugene blew a whistle.

"The video's ranked #2 in America!"

"Oh~"

"You mean the trending videos tab?"

"That's right!"

That told me where the majority of those views were coming from. Our target market.

*We nailed it.*

Movie or not, we had nothing to complain about if a high-quality video of our performance was getting this many views.

It would take some time to explain that we weren't actually a band, but we could probably handle that by going on a talk show.

*This is really great for us in terms of popularity.*

I was thinking hard about how to maximize its impact when...

Keun Sejin, who was sitting next to me and monitoring the reactions, suddenly thumped me on the shoulder. *What do you want?*

"Park Moondae."

"What?"

"Look at this."

I checked the smartphone he was holding out.

"...!!"

*Plagiarism?*

I immediately looked at Kim Raebin, who was sitting in front of me, but Keun Sejin shook his head and redirected my gaze.

"Keep reading."

"......"

"...??"

*What in the world?*

I hadn't expected our arrangement account to show up here of all places.

I immediately called Kim Raebin over and showed him the article.

"...??"

He made the same stupid face as I did two minutes ago.

I waited patiently for him to react some more, and Kim Raebin eventually shouted,

"...! I see, I think there must've been a slight overlap in some of the samples I'd made before, since I was in such a hurry!"

"Ah."

"Sorry. It's set in the same universe, and I didn't even think of it while working under such time constraints..."

And to sum up the explanation that followed, it was like this.

*I frantically picked through the pre-made beats and samples for the OST because there was no time to make them from scratch, but it seems that they overlapped because it was set in the same universe. It's my negligence! I'm truly sorry! I will reflect deeply and come up with a solution immediately!*

"No, wait."

I tried to calm Kim Raebin down, and he fell silent.

"Moondaemoondae?"
