# Chapter 3 - Initial Evaluation, I

There was nothing special about the broadcasting station visit after the writer practically dragged me there. I only had a simple interview in front of the camera.

They didn't even ask me to do a short dance, which should have been a mere formality. It was also proof that the role I was supposed to play had already been decided.

Well, it doesn't matter. The important thing was that I got my name on the participant list.

However, there were some unexpected elements.

The first filming date was much sooner than I expected.

"...In ten days?"

"That's right. Park Moondae-gun was the last one to join. We don't usually let contestants join this late, but you got really lucky~ Moondae-gun. I think you got this position because you're really well-suited for it."

What a nice way of putting it.

Even though I was perplexed, I thought bitterly.

It didn't cost them anything to put it nicely, so I suppose there was no harm in being polite.

Of course, as soon as they realize there's nothing special about me, they'll toss me aside. I couldn't think of many other industries where people cut ties so quickly.

Besides, expecting an ordinary person to prepare an audition song and be ready for filming within ten days without any guidance was ridiculous. Whether it's a rearrangement or Nabal, they're basically telling us to use the instrumental and get cursed at by the viewers. If the performance wasn't bad enough to attract criticism, I'd simply be edited out.

But I expected this much.

"...Yes. Thank you."

I nodded as if I hadn't noticed anything, but I was still nervous about the filming date that was just around the corner.

Satisfaction flashed across the writer's face.

"We are the ones who are grateful~ Then I will contact you for more details."

It was basically a dismissal. I left the broadcasting station without regret and made plans for the next 10 days.

Since there was so little time to prepare, I'd have to resort to a more extreme method than I'd originally planned.

* * *

After ten extremely busy days, the first day of filming finally arrived.

Like most survival programs, it began with evaluating each participant.

The first round of screening.

Anyone familiar with idol survival programs would know that a contestant's first impression can easily follow them until the end.

So, if a contestant doesn't have anything that stands out, the worst thing they can do is give an average performance. Instead, if the performance completely falls apart and becomes a joke, the production team might use it to create a growth narrative later, depending on the editing. However, if it fails to leave any impression and gets edited out completely, there's nothing that can be done.

Of course, I'm not saying that I aim to be criticized by the viewers.

"Do you remember your number? You're called by the number. Please enter this corridor after you receive instructions~"

The filming began with each participant being filmed as they walked through the set's hallway one by one. It must have been filmed for the teaser, but we'd already spent more than two hours filming a clip that would only last forty seconds. That was probably because there were seventy-seven participants in total.

After I looked around at all the contestants scattered around the empty space that was barely worthy of being called a waiting room, I lost some motivation.

Even with a status window and regression on my side, I realized how embarrassing it was to try to become an idol among all these kids.

"Excuse me, hello."

One of the kids who had been going around striking up conversations approached me as well.

I wasn't sure if he was excited or simply trying to break the ice.

What mattered was that he looked young enough to be a middle school student.

I couldn't believe that, at my age, I was introducing myself to a middle school student just to make connections—.

"Did you apply by yourself?"

"Yes."

"Wow, me too. Oh, by any chance, how old are you...?"

"...Twenty."

It felt strange lying about my age by nine years, but I also felt a little good about it.

"Do you have an agency?"

"No. I don't have one."

"Ah, I see! Usually, some contestants on IJC come from agencies. What company were you with before—"

"I haven't had one since the beginning."

"Oh... I see."

The middle school brat immediately lost interest. After quickly ending the conversation, he went off to talk to someone else.

He already seemed to understand how society worked and wanted to succeed. Of course, he'd have to hide his calculating nature in front of the cameras, but that was none of my business.

In any case, I couldn't believe a middle school student could be that ruthless.

For a moment, I wondered if I'd made a foolish decision because I'd gotten caught up in this bizarre situation. But there was no use crying over spilled milk.

"Let's move the set!"

This was because filming for the main broadcast had finally begun.

I followed the middle and high school students huddled together like meerkats.

My name was called somewhere around the middle of the session.

* * *

"Participant Lee Sejin B...in 15th place! Please take a seat."

"Thank you!"

One of the participants who was being evaluated nodded in response to the MC's words. He was one of four participants being evaluated together because they were from the same agency. Although he was the best of the four, the production team wasn't pleased.

*It's extreme.*

Writer Ryu Seorin, who was spinning her pen, frowned.

Season 3 had received less funding than the previous season. It was very natural. Because Season 2 had been a massive failure, Season 3 had barely been allowed to continue. With a funny title such as 'Re-listed,' the production team was determined to get rid of the shadow of Season 2.

However, no matter how much they tried to compensate through personal connections and planning, some problems were impossible to hide.

One of those problems was the available talent pool.

Many of the adequately trained candidates had already joined other audition programs. They had filled the seventy-seven spots with whoever seemed decent enough, which meant there was a higher proportion of 'good-for-nothing' contestants.

And if there were too many incompetent contestants, there was only so much the editors could do to make the show entertaining.

It was almost impossible to make a talentless person popular on a Korean audition program. Even if they tried to cover up someone's lack of talent, one small mistake would bring a flood of criticism. The problem wasn't simply that someone would be disliked; the criticism would center on words like 'boring' and 'fraudulent.'

Fortunately, they'd secured the top-tier contestants they wanted, but it was still difficult to run the program without a dark horse.

*The same goes for the one coming out now.*

This time, the contestant being evaluated was a complete amateur who had joined only ten days before the first filming. He had been recruited at the last minute to replace a contestant who had been disqualified for drunk driving, so there were no expectations for him.

The contestant had a moderately good voice, wasn't bad-looking, and even had a backstory they could use if necessary. Thanks to that, he'd managed to exceed the relatively low standards she'd set because of the circumstances.

Even with all those qualities combined, he was still only slightly better than an ordinary person recruited to fill a vacancy.

*Well... He could be used for comparison.*

The judges were also chatting with unimpressed expressions after skimming his application, where nothing stood out apart from his family history.

So she felt the same way.

*Yeah, just be really bad. That way, you'll attract people's attention.*

If contestants who merely met the standard kept appearing, viewers would get bored, but a few terrible performances would make the show more entertaining. As the saying goes, even negative attention is still attention.

Since the ordinary person had only ten days to prepare, there was a good chance his performance would be bad enough to be entertaining.

She glanced at the stage with weak expectations.

"Next participant, please enter the stage!"

The ordinary participant then came up to the stage.

Thankfully, he looked better than before, as if he'd found a decent hair salon. The problem was that he gave off a dull, gloomy, and mysterious air.

She had assumed it was because of his family history the last time, but was he really going to act that way in front of all these cameras again?

In reality, it was because he'd seen cameras countless times before, but the writer had no way of knowing that and found him strange.

*...Is he the type who doesn't show his nerves?*

It didn't matter. She could simply portray him as arrogant despite being incompetent. The writer eagerly began planning how to use him.

Meanwhile, Park Moondae stepped onto the stage and listened to the MC.

"Yes, contestant. Please introduce yourself."

"...My name is Park Moondae. Pleased to meet you."

He bowed his head, prompting a few quiet laughs and snorts from the judges' seats.

"Wait, is that all?"

There was a slight sneer in his voice. The judge clearly expected a more detailed introduction, but Park Moondae simply nodded sincerely.

"Yes. I don't have any other experience in this field worth mentioning."

The judges burst into laughter.

"Geez, this one's really something!"

"He's hilarious~."

Several of them whispered, making sure their voices were still picked up by the microphone.

"Oh, looking at his application form, he really doesn't have any experience."

Normally, this would be the time to ask something like, 'Then why did you participate?' However, the judges seemed to think it would be more effective to ask after his performance, so they held back.

This made Seorin think, **There could be some useful footage after all.**

"Well, then. Let's see what you've got."

"Yes."

Despite their malicious interest, the contestant accepted the microphone handed to him by the staff.

Soon, the intro of the song began to play.

Everyone was immediately taken aback.

Why would he choose this song?!
