# Chapter 300 - T-Holic Comeback

T-Holic. The undisputed No. 1 idol group before VTIC rose to prominence.

In short, they were the group that represented the previous idol generation.

"T-this is the guy who sang the first song you covered on the show, right, Moondae?"

"Yes."

'Party in Me', which I sang for the grade evaluation in ⟨Idol Inc.⟩, was a song from one of this group's members' solo albums.

*They're veterans; it's been quite a few years since they were encouraged to go solo.*

Most idols from their generation had already gone their separate ways.

Yet all but one of T-Holic's members had come together again to release an album.

I watched T-Holic's music video begin to play near the bottom of WeTube's trending videos.

Coincidentally, the thumbnail featured the member whose song I'd covered.

"T-Holic sunbaenim has made a comeback~

Wow, it's been a while. I did a lot of covers of their songs in middle school~"

Lee Sejin, sitting in the back of the van, peeked over the seat and spoke knowingly.

That drew even Cha Eugene into the conversation, but...

"Who? Umm... I don't know them."

Maybe because he was American, he didn't seem interested in an idol group that'd been popular more than 10 years ago.

However, Kim Raebin seemed shocked.

"T-Holic sunbaenim... You don't know them?"

"Nope!"

"It's unprofessional to lack such important background knowledge when you're actively promoting as a K-pop idol!"

"...! I know a lot of K-pop songs! I just don't know theirs!"

*Right.* By the time K-pop reached the U.S., they'd been on hiatus for the most part due to military service, so Cha Eugene had a point.

"I know their name! T-Holic sunbaenim!"

"That's not enough!"

*My ears are ringing.*

These days, whenever a topic came up, it set off a chain reaction among the members and things got noisy.

I watched nonchalantly as Bae Sejin, sitting across from me, curled up further into the corner and put in his earphones.

Our promotions were going smoothly, and no one seemed particularly worried or stressed.

That was why even such trivial topics got this much of a reaction.

*That, and we'd gotten more comfortable with each other.*

Watching the guys interrupt and butt into each other's conversations without hesitation, I came to a simple conclusion.

We'd do well on reality shows.

That was my conclusion for the day.

However, T-Holic's comeback had far bigger repercussions than I'd expected.

The first was nostalgia.

They'd managed to harness the power of it.

People in their 20s and 30s remembered their youth and reacted favorably to T-Holic's music.

With almost all the members still together and their first group album in years, nostalgia and admiration made them a hot topic.

Even people who hadn't liked T-Holic back then responded positively, thanks to rose-tinted memories and longing for their youth.

*Of course, none of that would've amounted to anything if the song wasn't good.*

But that brought us to the second factor in T-Holic's favor.

Their sense of humor.

These bastards... They were funny.

From WeTube content about the old days of K-pop to shows on public television.

These guys hit the jackpot on every variety show they appeared on. They weren't afraid to make fools of themselves, but they were skilled enough to know when to stop.

Relaxed and witty.

*Their experience isn't going anywhere.* They were the polar opposite of VTIC, who carefully fine-tuned every piece of media they released.

They aggressively took over content and gained momentum.

Normally, they'd lose their novelty and public popularity after a while, but they had a few strokes of luck on their side.

A few scenes from the broadcast became memes.

Riding that momentum, they even made a WeTube channel for their own variety show.

Despite all the variety show buzz, T-Holic wasn't performing particularly well in digital streaming or album sales.

They were doing just well enough to save face.

In the current inflated K-pop market, their sales were near the bottom of the top tier.

As a result, fans of other idols didn't really see them as competition.

They'd carved out a category separate from other idols.

And riding that momentum, T-Holic's own variety show became a huge hit in a short time.

That was a bad sign.

***

I checked the ratings for T-Holic's new regular variety show on CVN, which they'd earned after their WeTube show became a huge hit.

The first episode always drew a lot of viewers, but the second was what really mattered...

It was a shocking number, on par with CVN's most popular prime-time variety shows.

*That's ridiculous.*

There'd been a lull in popular dramas and variety shows, which helped them do far better than expected.

"Oh~ T-Holic sunbaenims are doing really well."

Keun Sejin, who was waiting with me for the next shoot, shrugged and chimed in.

I had to agree.

"Yeah."

Of course, it didn't seem like it would last long.

*If things go too well too fast, someone will try to bring them down.*

It made for interesting gossip when someone popular came crashing down. The moment the internet found something to criticize, controversy would kill their momentum.

Even without that, popularity born from variety shows didn't last long. So this trend would last... At most three or four months.

*They're not on our level.*

Everyone knew that in terms of sales, it was embarrassing to even put them in the same category as us.

But the timing was the problem.

*We can't afford to lose popularity to them in the third and fourth quarters.*

Although TeSTAR had done well in both digital streaming and album sales, neither was such an overwhelming win that we were guaranteed the grand prize in either category.

*Right now, VTIC and Youngrin are in the lead.*

To win the grand prize under the current evaluation system, split into digitals and albums, the public needed to see us as a 'trend'.

But all of a sudden, these guys were sucking up all the public's attention.

*And these guys are officially classified as idols anyway.*

So, the idols getting the most attention right now were... T-Holic.

*It's dangerous.*

If we lost our status as a 'current trend' apart from our sales results, and TeSTAR's public presence suffered, our chances of winning the grand prize would drop.

I muttered as I turned off my smartphone screen.

"We're going to have to work harder."

"Right?"

Keun Sejin nodded easily.

Since he didn't say something like 'We're already working hard, what are you talking about?', he seemed to have picked up on the situation, too.

"Let's aim for good results by the end of the year~ Fighting!"

Even the guys who didn't know the details seemed motivated by his words.

"Yes! I'll... Try to talk more on variety shows!"

"I've been reminded once again that idols must be well-rounded entertainers, and that we need to develop skills beyond our musical abilities."

In that sense, the guys in this group had something the status window couldn't fully capture in numbers.

Their morale.

Even when things were going this well, they considered all sorts of variables, kept a level head, and maintained the drive to improve.

*Most people would be drunk on their own success by this point.*

We were in the middle of a photo shoot.

"Okay, we'll get started right away, TeSTAR~"

"Yes!"

It wasn't just a regular magazine pictorial.

We'd partnered with a magazine to produce a special issue filled from beginning to end with TeSTAR's photos, interviews, and advertisements.

The magazine, which came with 8 different covers including individual and group shots, had reportedly already surpassed 800,000 pre-orders.

"Oh my God, so pretty~"

"Haha!"

None of the staff were particularly unpleasant during the shoot. They treated us with great respect.

*It's an environment that spoils people openly.*

Especially with most of the members having just entered their 20s, this was the perfect environment for developing bad manners.

There were no consequences for being rude to the people we worked with. Without anything compelling us to be on our best behavior, it was easy to get snappish whenever we felt off.

Before long, our physical condition and mood could dictate how we treated others.

*If we wanted to treat people like X, we could get away with it.*

Yet these guys hadn't changed much from our debut days.

"One more time~"

"Yes!"

We were all huddled together in jeans and T-shirts for a group shot to be used as one of the covers.

As we were asked to change our expressions and poses, Bae Sejin muttered.

"...It reminds me of when we debuted."

"Yeah. Our hair colors are the same as back then."

"I like my red hair!"

The concept of this photo shoot was TeSTAR's history, from our debut to the present.

It was a fitting concept for the group that had released the year's most popular male idol song.

*Has it been that long already?*

I looked back over the years with a strange feeling.

We'd been through so many unbelievable things, yet it felt like we'd been on ⟨Idol Inc.⟩ just yesterday.

And shooting 'Magical Boys' didn't feel like it had been that long ago, either.

Just as the shoot was about to resume, I remembered the compliment I'd deliberately paid Bae Sejin back then.

"Hyung, you've always been good at shoots like this, and you still are."

"Ahem, you guys are doing well too."

Unlike back then, Bae Sejin accepted the compliment without complaint.

*Yeah. At least one thing has changed.*

The distance between us had grown smaller. We'd probably built up trust after everything we'd gone through together.

"......"

"Moondae-ssi, nice smile!"

"Thank you."

Well, now was not the time to indulge in sentimentality.

We only had a few opportunities left to secure the grand prize, but a huge obstacle had suddenly fallen in our path.

So, what was the best way?

First of all, trying to copy their variety show content would be stupid.

*If we put out a similar show right now, we'd just be setting ourselves up for comparison.*

The combination of luck and experience that had led them to their own regular TV show wasn't something we could recreate.

*We need to be a little more crafty...*

*Hmm.*

I mulled it over until the end of the shoot, then came to a conclusion.

*Let's use their advantage to our advantage.*

To catch a tiger, you had to enter its den.

***

T-Holic's show was doing really well.

In other words, their popularity was practically secured by their variety show.

And anyone who appeared on the show could take advantage of its popularity.

Depending on the format, a guest could even outshine the hosts and become the main character of the program.

That was if the episode had something to set it apart from the others.

And if it was entertaining.

*That's our best plan.*

So after discussing it with the group and analyzing the format of T-Holic's show, we picked two members who'd have the strongest impact.

They were perfectly suited to the role we had in mind.

"I'm really looking forward to it!"

Cha Eugene.

"This is my first time being selected for a variety show when so few members are going! I'll do my best!"

Kim Raebin.

And... The one person who could rein these two guys in and wasn't already booked elsewhere.

"Yeah."

Me.

I arrived on the set of T-Holic's ⟨Show Business⟩ with Cha Eugene and Kim Raebin in tow.
