# Chapter 7 - ⟨Shining Star⟩, II

Judge Mewdy was the trainer for the Advanced Vocal.

She gave me a generous score on my first evaluation, and she also said nice things about all the other participants. Perhaps because of that, she tended to be gentle during training as well.

Of course, this doesn't mean that she never gets angry in any situation.

"Wongil-ah, that's not it... Haa."

"...."

The contestant who had kept getting the same part of the song wrong for five days straight hung his head.

He seemed to be trying to hide his irritation rather than any feelings of shame, but it didn't matter.

Because the important thing to remember is that even a good coach will struggle to hide their frustration when watching a participant make so many mistakes.

And here's the main culprit.

"Moondae-ya, do you want to sing this part?"

"Yes."

When the trainer pointed it out, I moved forward, and the electric piano started playing the accompaniment. Then, I started singing the part before the chorus along with the music.

♪♫ *"Thinking of the future, like a shooting star!"* ♫♪
p$

Mewdy nodded, pleased with my clear tone and precise rhythm, while Choi Wongil shifted his feet nervously.

Yes. I was the root of the problem. I'd been able to sing the theme song flawlessly from the very beginning.

It was unavoidable that those who already knew this song would have a different starting point than those who didn't.

I'd heard not just the demo, but the full released version, so I could rely on what I remembered.

Moreover, my voice was perfectly suited to this song, and the trainer was pleased with how I sang it because my vocals sounded clean and clear.

Even now, some people were whispering as they looked at Mewdy's happy face.

"...He's really good."

"I know."

The bar was already high in the advanced class, and having an ordinary guy without any notable training or agency background perform so well probably made things even more stressful for the others.

Especially since singing was the only thing I was particularly good at.

As confirmed in the first evaluation, two participants had A-grade vocals this season. And both had Dance grades of C or lower. As a result, neither of us was really standing out at the moment.

Also, that 'Choi Wongil' participant, who is currently being scolded, was one of those two A-grades.

It's funny that he is the same kid who'd struck up a conversation with me before filming, then decided I wasn't worth being friends with. However, he was actually a first-year high school student, and not a middle schooler at all.

In any case, at his age, with his ego at its peak, the criticism must have been especially hard to take because this was something that he had been hearing from his trainer for several days.

He was compared to the person he ignored as soon as he met him.

"That's right!"

As soon as I finished the pre-chorus, Mewdy tapped the keys in satisfaction. Choi Wongil didn't even look up.

"Wongil, do you understand how I want you to sing this part now?"

"...."

Given his abilities, I don't think it's possible to make that many mistakes. But unfortunately, ever since I'd sung the entire song perfectly on the first day, he'd been desperate to pick a fight with me, and in the end, it seemed like his mind was shattered.

Let's see, this is roughly how the first day seems to have started.

– Wow, you're getting plenty of praise for your singing, at least, Hyung-nim. You should hurry and move up from Beginner Dance!

– I think you fit this pure song well because you're kind of amateurish.

He used to say vague things that got on people's nerves. It was a subtle nuance, but if the opponent got mad, he would be treated like an idiot.

He also made sure to turn his microphone off before saying any of it.

But apparently, those petty insults weren't enough to satisfy him.

– You're very lucky. I'm jealous. It's the most important factor in the entertainment industry. It would have been nice if you could dance with luck, that's too bad. But still, fighting!

– I guess lucky people really do have it better than those who work hard. Most people here are desperate to do well. But you just happened to end up here, and you're already getting such good reviews.

By that point, his insults had become much more blatant.

He was openly harassing me now, and I was beginning to feel uncomfortable simply ignoring him. But I didn't have much choice. I couldn't exactly pretend not to know a song I was already familiar with.

You might not know this, but this is also a matter of life and death for me...

"Calm down, don't be too nervous, and let's try again."

"...Yes."

Choi Wongil tried to start singing again, but then he made another mistake.

That's too bad.

Even until the end of the vocal class that day, I received compliments from the trainer and sharp gazes from several participants, including Choi Wongil.

It had been a while since I'd been on the receiving end of such hostility. It was tiresome, but I didn't particularly mind. If they didn't consider me a threat, they wouldn't bother treating me like this.

Both classes continued much as before while my practice achievements steadily increased. Before long, the final day of training arrived.

On the morning of the last day, the participants in the Advanced Vocal class recorded a song.

Everyone recorded together, and no one had a solo part, so it probably wouldn't matter much.

Instead, I'd focus on the evening's long-awaited ranking evaluation.

It was time for the long-awaited ranking evaluation.

***

The lights on the set were turned on.

"Everyone, how have you been?"

"Ah~ it was hard!"

"It was so intense. I was exhausted... I nearly overslept."

"Was it because you were training the contestants?"

"Of course! Oh, I hope we get to see the results of all our hard work today!"

The judges took their seats and exchanged pleasantries.

Even though they were already done greeting each other in the waiting room in the morning, the conversation was like the show's opening scene.

The place where they came in and sat down was set up to look like a multi-to-one interview room[1]{T/N: 다대일 means the interviewer will be more than 1 person, and they will interview you. (Other interviewees will not be interviewed with you)}.

The set had been used two seasons earlier, with special effects prepared for this occasion.

The judges had been instructed to react with exaggerated surprise, so they pretended to be seeing the set for the first time and spoke naturally.

"Wow, this place... It feels like we're about to conduct a really important interview."

"It's true about the important interview~ It was a Grade Interview!"

"Is this an interview for joining Idol Incorporated?"

As soon as the judges finished speaking, the desk in front of them lit up.

"Whoa!"

The words $a'SHINE YOUR STAR'a$ were written in colorful cursive on the desk.

As they did in other seasons, the judges started to applaud when this season's catchphrase turned out to be grand.

But this time, surprisingly, the wall began to move.

"Huh?!"

"What is this!"

With the skillful reaction of the judges, one wall of the interview room sank into the floor, revealing the vast space beyond it.

As the judges stared around in exaggerated amazement, the emcee's voice rang out from behind them.

"Welcome to the new and improved ⟨Idol Incorporated: Relisted⟩! The first challenge is—a public evaluation!"

"Whoa!"

Behind the judges was a large seating area with audience bleachers. The contestants occupied the seats beneath them.

Unlike the judges, who were putting on a show, the contestants were genuinely horrified to discover that these weren't merely temporary waiting seats.

"All seventy-seven contestants have prepared the same song. Today, they'll perform it in front of one another!"

In fact, the participants could see each other's stages even during the first evaluation.

However, everyone's expressions stiffened. They hadn't expected to perform in front of the very contestants who'd practiced the same song alongside them.

This was because in previous seasons, the evaluations had been held privately in smaller studios.

"The Grade Evaluation order is in reverse order of rank!"

"....!"

"77th place, Chu Seonggu-gun, please come forward!"

The participant who was called to the stage looked like he was half-dead. People looked at him with sympathy and whispered, but Park Moondae didn't say a word.

As with Seon Ahyeon's first ranking evaluation, depending on how the footage was edited, even sympathy could come across as arrogance.

He'd learned this from regularly checking idol fan communities to work out how much to charge for his fancams.

"Yes, thank you for your hard work."

"...Thank you..."

Naturally, the 77th participant missed most of the first verse and completely ruined the evaluation.

"...."

The next contestant, and the one after that, also completely botched their performances in the same cold, tense atmosphere.

After evaluating five or six people in this way, the judges couldn't hide the discomfort on their faces.

It was a cruel and dry arrangement.

However, the production team had nothing to lose.

*If they weren't going to make it anyway, the production crew might as well get some sensational footage out of them to justify their appearance fees.*

They were used as disposable cards.

At the same time, the arrangement made the contestants who could withstand the pressure stand out even more.

For example, contestants who could hold themselves together and deliver a good performance even as the lower-ranked trainees fell apart.

Or the participants who showed significant growth in this short period of time.

The production team probably figured that contestants with little talent had been cast for their looks or fame anyway, so performing early wouldn't make much difference to them.

Interestingly, there was one contestant who fit both descriptions.

"Participant Lee Sejin, you must have worked hard."

"You're doing great!"

"H, h, huh—. T, thank you...."

It was Lee Sejin, who used to be a child actor. Red-faced and out of breath, Lee Sejin nevertheless managed to finish the entire song.

It was hardly an impressive performance, and calling it well-executed would have been a stretch.

But compared to the contestants who'd completely botched their performances around him, he looked considerably better.

Park Moondae had a moment of doubt.

*Is he the Lee Sejin who is debuting?*

Of course, he couldn't say for sure, so he ended his thoughts right away by putting the participant on the watch list.

*I need to stay away from the future drug addict, whoever it turns out to be.*

And the boring middle-class evaluation section followed.

The participants' skills gradually got better, and some of them stood out from time to time.

But hearing the same song over and over again was bound to get tiring.

After the tense mood at the start subsided, everyone showed their skills, and the judges regained their composure and began to feel bored.

"Well..."

"I think it's fine."

When the boredom reached its peak, it was Park Moondae's turn.

Seon Ahyeon held out his fist for Park Moondae to bump, his pale face completely expressionless.

He'd just finished his own evaluation and had done a decent job.

"F, f, fighting...."

"...? Huh, yeah."

*Why is he suddenly so friendly?*

Park Moondae was surprised, but he greeted him back calmly. His instinct to appear friendly won out.

He trudged onto the stage, feeling a strange, unfamiliar sense of excitement.

"22nd place, Park Moondae!"

"Yes."

The judges looked thoroughly weary.

*You're good at singing, but you can't dance.*

After a few days of trying, the choreographer's expectations for Park Moondae disappeared. He even thought bluntly.

*It's getting annoying.*

"We'll begin the grade evaluation."

The boring prelude came out.

Park Moondae began the first move, pulling both arms inward as he spun.

"....?"

The first person to feel out of place was the choreographer.

*His movements are different.*

Until the last face-to-face class just two days ago, Park Moondae had no such thing as a 'dance line.'

Real dancing required knowing exactly when to tense and relax the body, when to apply force and when to release it.

That was what separated merely moving to the rhythm from actually dancing. Park Moondae had clearly seemed capable of only the former.

Or at least, that was what the choreographer had believed until now.

*Why...are you good at dancing?*
