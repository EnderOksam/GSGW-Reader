# Chapter 4 - Initial Evaluation, II

"Huh?"

Several of the judges began whispering to one another.

"T-Holic?"

T-Holic, a famous boy group that won the Daesang award[1]{T/N: The Daesang award is one of the highest achievements a group or artist can achieve.} for three consecutive years, went on hiatus several years ago. This was because most of the members had to enlist in the military.

Instead, the agency promoted the solo career of the youngest main vocalist, who had yet to enlist. He released a mini-album featuring a title track with an addictive chorus.

However, as time passed, people realized that the true value of the album lay not in the title track, but in its B-side. Surprisingly, it climbed to #3 on the music charts.

The song's popularity began with a recommendation from a popular female solo singer before spreading through various entertainment shows.

The B-side track was ⟨PARTY IN ME⟩.

The easy-listening song, which combines rich band instrumentals with sophisticated synths, was frequently performed at college festivals that year and eventually made it into the Top 100.

And without a doubt, the instrumental currently playing in the studio was its intro. It was an iconic intro accompanied by a unique accordion melody.

That was what made the song choice even stranger.

'This... How could he choose this song without knowing what it was?'

The song had a rather ambiguous position. It was neither a major hit from a popular idol group nor a famous song by an established singer. Therefore, whether it was an idol survival show or a singer survival show, it wasn't the kind of song that would come to mind for a first evaluation. The song simply didn't fit either category.

Besides, several years had already passed since the song became popular.

Since she hadn't chosen a song for him because she assumed he'd pick something predictable, she'd ended up in this situation.

Of course, the production team was able to check the sound source submitted by the participants in advance. However, there were 77 participants. The problem was that it wasn't worth the time to check and replace the songs one by one.

'It'll be difficult to compare and edit him with such an unconventional song—.'

The Writer tapped the table irritably with her pen.

Even the judges, surprised by his song choice, waited for the intro to end with a slightly apathetic attitude.

And Park Moondae opened his mouth.

♫ *I remember the 'me' I used to know\
Beyond the shining and dazzling seasons\
Until the days of tiredness and weakness\
Even at the end of the endless days\
The thing that captures the broken me, oh—.\
Only me\
Yeah, It's me* ♪

"...!"

He was good.

The song began on an offbeat, but he came in at exactly the right time and sang the first verse cleanly in a soft voice.

Most people wouldn't notice because it sounded easy on the ears, but the song was actually difficult to sing because of its intricate beats. It was the kind of song that could easily expose an amateur singer.

However, this participant precisely hit every note despite the complex rhythm.

*'Dan, Da-dan–, Ddan, Dan, Da-an–, Da-dan.'*

"...!"

One of the judges jolted in surprise after unconsciously tapping the table. Moondae had entered at exactly the right moment.

That way, even viewers who didn't understand the intricacies of the half-beats would think he was singing well because his notes were perfectly synchronized with the accompaniment[2]{T/N: Accompaniment (伴奏) is to play another instrument next to a song or instrumental music.}.

Besides—.

His vocalization was remarkably good. It wasn't simply loud; his mellow tone resonated with a rich, almost three-dimensional quality.

'Was he like this at karaoke?'

The Writer recalled how he'd sounded when she first met him, but he hadn't been nearly this good. If he'd sung this well when she first met him, she definitely would have filmed a longer interview and helped him choose his song.

'Did he really improve this much in just ten days?'

Regardless of how perplexed the Writer was, the song went through the verse and entered the pre-chorus.

♪ *That's why, I\
I have to memorialize myself\
I must never forget again\
Now, remember\
Until now* ♫

Even as the notes climbed higher, he never sang a single flat note. There was no part where breathing was unnatural.

At this point, the Writer noticed something else: the surprised expressions on the judges' faces.

♪ *I've always lived today\
With an amazing miracle\
Don't forget. Don't erase\
So congratulations\
Memorialize this moment\
That's the party in me\
As if every day is a PARTY, Ooh-let's PARTY* ♫

He hit the high notes with ease, without sounding strained. His voice sounded so effortless and clear that it seemed like he could easily go another octave higher. Rather than shouting, he sang as though he were speaking softly, which suited the song perfectly.

Now, the Writer just wants to laugh more.

'Yeah, that's great... He has a main vocal feeling... Still, we lacked a vocal position, but wow, isn't that a good thing? I have a really great eye. I can't believe this kind of thing rolled in even if I cast him on the street.'

The second verse came out again. The melody was the same as in the first verse, but the rhythm was more complex, and Park Moondae handled it with ease.

After the bridge, his voice rose naturally into the final chorus. His high notes sent pleasant chills down the listeners' spines. He didn't overdo it or rush the performance; it was a superb climax.

And as if talking again, the song ended with the first bar of the verse.

...*I remember the 'me' I used to know*

He maintained a beautiful tone until the very last note.

Park Moondae bowed his head as soon as the music ended. Then he spoke in a dazed manner.

"...Thank you for listening."

There was a brief silence. The Writer gripped her pen tightly and thought, 'Yeah, let's keep him.'

As she mentally readjusted Park Moondae's role, the judges eagerly picked up their microphones.

"Wow, Moondae-ssi!"

"Yes."

"You know you did an amazing job, right?!"

"...I practiced hard so I could sing well."

The other judges then quickly added.

"No, I'm telling you. This is something you're born with. You have real talent."

He was a choreographer known for his blunt attitude, and he was grinning from ear to ear. Since this was a broadcast, some of his praise might have been exaggerated, but he wasn't the type to say things he didn't mean.

Park Moondae bowed his head, screaming with happiness inwardly.

The judges continued to shower him with praise.

Mudie, a female singer who appeared as a vocal coach, seemed especially impressed and showered him with praise.

"First of all, your voice is very refreshing. I can hear it clearly because your vocalization is so good."

"I know, right? It's almost like someone is whispering right next to your ear—"

The others eagerly voiced their agreement. Mudie then nodded and continued in a quick voice.

"Also, I think your voice could suit many genres that are important for an idol, you know? You don't seem to have developed any bad singing habits, so I'm really looking forward to seeing how you develop."

"Thank you."

"Moondae-ssi, you've never worked with an agency before, right? You said you didn't have any experience, and looking at your application, that's certainly true."

"Yes. Not yet..."

Then a young male judge spoke up. He was a famous boy group member whose group had recently lost two members when they chose not to renew their contracts.

"Then did you never take any special lessons?"

"Yes."

"Nothing at all? Not even at an academy?"

Park Moondae silently shook his head. The judge seemed impressed, though his expression remained somewhat skeptical. Mudie then smiled brightly and opened her mouth once again.

"Yeah, it might be better to wait for your big break than to sign with just any entertainment company. I'm sure you'll get an offer from a good agency!"

"Thank you for your kind words."

The Writer suddenly remembered that Mudie's agency was preparing to launch a male idol.

'Now that I think about it, she had a share in her entertainment company.'

She looked at the participant, who was still politely nodding, but he didn't seem particularly impressed by her words. She felt as if... something had shattered.

Obviously, finding a talented contestant was good for the show, but for some reason, she felt unsettled.

Resting her chin on her hand, the Writer stared at Park Moondae's microphone and suddenly realized.

He hadn't actually danced properly!

Of course, Park Moondae also did the same choreography. However, since the song wasn't made for a stage performance, the choreography was limited to simple movements. The backup dancers performed the actual choreography, while the idol mainly moved his hands and feet to the rhythm as he sang.

However, viewers would remember the original performance, complete with its backup dancers. Viewers could simply fill in the missing choreography from their memory of the original performance, so his lackluster movements wouldn't matter much. Especially when he sang so well!

Furthermore, because he chose a song by a top-tier idol, he could rely on his vocal ability and avoid questions like, "Does Moondae-ssi really want to become an idol?"

Ultimately, it was the best song Park Moondae could have chosen in his situation.

'This goes beyond simply having good instincts; he almost seems experienced—'

The Writer felt tricked in some way and looked through Park Moondae with dissatisfied eyes.

And, as if reading the Writer's mind, a judge who had been silently watching the other judges' positive comments lifted the microphone.

She was Youngrin of 'SaintU,' a female idol group that became successful after a fancam of her performing acrobatics in the rain while smiling went viral.

"The choreography you just did wasn't very difficult. Could we see something that shows more of your dancing?"

**The Writer almost clapped with delight.**

* * *

'It's done.'

It was worth singing this song over and over these past few days until I got sick of it because I'd managed to level up significantly.

After spending all my points on improving my vocal ability, the 'Vocal' stat in my status window had risen to A-.

It was the obvious choice. In Korea, whether you're an idol or a singer, you won't get far if you can't sing. If you can't dance, you can make up for it with practice, but if you can't sing, even a pre-recorded track won't completely hide it on stage.

Furthermore, among aspiring idols, there were usually fewer people aiming for the main vocalist position than the main dancer position. So, if I established myself as the main vocalist from the beginning, I'd have a better chance of securing a spot in the debut group.

The next important step was choosing the evaluation song. My strategy seemed to be working, judging by how little negative feedback I'd received.

In other words, I predicted the criticism the judges would make based on my weaknesses and eliminated any songs that could provoke those reactions.

'What's wrong with your dance?'
– Remove any songs that include full choreography.

'You don't even dance?'
– Remove ballads.

'You sing well, but you don't give off an idol-like aura?'
– Remove all songs that aren't performed by idol groups.

'Wasn't it too ambitious to sing this song by yourself?'
– Remove all songs originally sung by three or more people, since I can't rearrange them and don't want to take the risk.

'You'll be compared to the original singer every time!'
– Remove all songs that were released within the last three years.

From the remaining songs, I chose the one with the highest ranking on the music charts.

Hmm, it was a good strategy, now that I think about it.

But there was at least one person who didn't get swept away by the atmosphere when I thought I could get away with it as it is right now.

Then I made eye contact with the judge holding the microphone.

I recognized her.

She was the first idol who made me realize that filming an idol may be profitable. Thanks to her, I was able to pay for my tuition for my first and second semesters. She'd gained a reputation as a hardworking idol thanks to the acrobatic fancam I'd taken of her, so it had been a win-win situation for both of us.

Unfortunately, it didn't seem like our meeting this time would benefit either of us.

"I understand that you've never received formal training. But since this is the first evaluation, shouldn't we at least see whether you have the basics down? If you're participating in an audition program, I'm sure you came prepared with at least one song."

It would be crazy to say, 'I didn't prepare anything' here. It would be better to keep my mouth shut than say something like that.

"Yes. Then is this song possible?"

One of the staff members quickly approached the stage and gestured for me to come over. I approached him and said the song I had in mind.

The camera followed us and even captured the staff's startled reaction.

Seeing this, I figured I'd managed to avoid being completely edited out. My efforts had paid off.

Immediately, a lively melody filled the set.

"...!?"
