# Chapter 337 - The Cameo, I

Let's revisit the role TeSTAR was originally given for the film cameo.

*We'd back up a supporting character as the main character led a large-scale battle, then disappear.*

*It's a perfect setup to pay homage to or parody the original game.*

From what we'd seen of the script, it seemed that a lot of other game characters would make passing appearances, not just us.

*My guess is that it takes place sometime after the midpoint of the movie, right before the climax.*

However, what would happen if we were the only ones who suddenly appeared as game characters, neither actual actors nor CGI characters?

Even if we did well, we'd still look out of place.

If we were even a little clumsy, the moment would be ruined.

*And if we did any worse, we'd be ridiculed on the Internet.*

It was no wonder the director and the people on set didn't like us. This was clearly a choice made by the film company as a deal between two corporate entities.

And the producer sitting in front of us right now was the head of the working-level staff, between the film company and the production team.

He had authority, but in a way, he could sympathize with the people working on set.

But right now, we had the upper hand. He was ready to listen to us.

*Seon Ahyeon set the mood well.*

With tensions this high, he was likely to accept any halfway reasonable proposal.

So I slowly opened my mouth.

First, I'd offer him the carrot before showing him the stick.

"We didn't mean to disrupt the set schedule, either. In the first place, it's impossible because of our tour schedule."

*We're busy too, bastard.*

When the interpretation was delivered, the producer nodded quite seriously. I continued talking.

"So, why don't we just add a simple scene to show TeSTAR in the movie? Our contract has to be fulfilled."

I waited for the interpreter to relay his reply.

"He says he'll look for the most similar scene possible, then arrange a time for you to film."

He'd taken the bait, thinking we were trying to meet him halfway, too.

*I knew you'd do that, but not so fast.*

I stifled a grin.

We couldn't appear in a fight scene looking like ridiculous cosplayers.

I wanted a different image for us.

"Doing that would make things difficult for both sides, so we're willing to make it easier for you."

I was steering the conversation in a specific direction.

I recalled the scenes I had noticed while reading the summary of the scenario on the plane.

And I picked up one of them.

"How about something like this scene instead?"

As soon as we got back to the hotel, I'd approached our staff, who'd been largely ignored while moving around the set.

I asked if any of the scenes I'd picked had been mentioned by the film crew.

And one of them matched.

*– Ah, this...*

*– Did they mention it?*

*– Yes. Well, I think they said this actor would be on set soon?*

That was what this scene was.

The only one that fit our schedule perfectly.

I held out that page of the summary to the producer.

"It's more fun to have artists stay true to their roles. We're idols, so we might look awkward in a large-scale battle scene."

A cut that could be filmed quickly with little disruption to the shooting schedule, while making us look good, too.

I went with this.

"There isn't much you need to do. We'd just say a line and appear in the background, so I think this would be more convenient for you."

The producer listened to the interpreter, and I glanced around a bit.

Since we'd already discussed it at the hotel, none of the others looked particularly surprised.

Cha Eugene, however, who was sitting closer to the producer, raised an eyebrow teasingly. He seemed to have overheard something.

*Oh?*

Cha Eugene smiled. *I could guess what the producer was saying.*

After a while...

"He says he'd like to hear more."

It was actually an OK sign.

*There we go.*

I shrugged.

And three days later, in the afternoon, the cameo shoot went ahead as planned.

* * *

It was a late winter morning.

As always on a Hollywood set, the crew knew exactly what their roles were.

Even if their work meant poor pay, bad treatment and endless drudgery, there was no way around it. If it were only about making a living, they'd have gone to law school instead, and they wouldn't be here.

The same went for the assistant, who was basically the production team's errand girl. The youngest PA, she barely managed to listen to a sudden briefing while checking that the coffee on set was topped up.

"More people are coming in?"

It was a short cameo, but sudden additions like this rarely happened.

When she asked who these 'exceptions' were, a team member answered briefly.

"K-pop stars."

"Ah."

They were the foreigners who had left the set without asking a few days ago.

She didn't know how well-known they were in their own country, but she knew of K-pop and had never heard of them.

*VTIC? That's the only K-pop boy band name I know.*

Thinking of her younger sister who was crazy about K-pop, she frowned slightly, but soon relaxed her expression.

"Certainly, Joanne was rude to them."

The attitude taken by the assistant director and half of the production team at the time was too much.

It was something they'd only do to complete rookie actors. Whether it was okay to treat even rookies that way was a different matter.

"Still, it took guts to just walk off the set without asking."

It wasn't a compliment.

If they couldn't stand waiting in the cold and just walked out of their trailer, they could forget about ever succeeding in Hollywood.

*It's going to be a rough day.*

She tried to guess how badly the group, who had walked off the set the same day they arrived, would mess up her day. Then she sighed.

"Is this an extended shoot?"

"Thirty minutes longer."

"Uh... We should pray that Steve doesn't kill anyone."

"We should pray that it will be short."

The chat ended quickly. She was called away and started running around the set again.

The set and effects were extremely realistic, thanks to the director cutting CG costs as much as possible this time and putting the money into the set instead.

It was a good environment for acting, which was rare in this kind of movie, where actors usually wore nothing but skin-tight green suits.

*Still... What the hell are they going to film here today?*

This scene took place early in the movie, when the protagonist visited the space station for the first time, went into a bar and met a companion. It showcased the massive scale of the movie's universe.

There was no place for K-pop stars wearing eccentric clothes anywhere.

*Well, Joanne, the assistant director, would know.*

Her own job was just to handle the menial work on set. She repressed a sigh as she moved a desk.

And just as she was stacking a few chairs...

She lost her balance.

"Ugh."

She stumbled backwards.

*Oh my god!*

The assistant closed her eyes for a moment, but didn't fall to the floor along with the chairs.

Instead, someone snatched the chairs she was holding from her hands.

"Are you okay?"

"...! Ah, ah... Yes."

"Oh my, you're carrying so much. I see other staff lazing around over there. Shouldn't they be helping?"

It was a lively and relaxed voice.

*Who is it?*

She was puzzled for a moment, but she straightened up first.

And she was surprised by who she saw.

"Everyone has a job to do...!"

"Well, that must be right."

When she looked up, more than ten people were standing in front of her. All Asians, mostly men.

And the handsome, tall boy who'd taken the chairs from her grinned.

"Our job is to appear in a cameo. It's nice to meet you. We're TeSTAR."

"Hello!"

The boys greeting her from behind him seemed about his age and were quite polite. They all had smooth skin and were good-looking, but some were almost too pretty for her. 

*Still, they seem nice.*

All her worries about them making things difficult had vanished like a joke.

"Hello. Yes. Thank you for your help."

She thought of saying something like 'You speak English well', swallowed it, and gave a normal answer.

As if he'd noticed, the pink-haired boy behind him spoke with an odd look in his eyes.

"This guy is from here. California."

"Oh, yeah."

Come to think of it, his accent was obviously Californian, which was why she was even more surprised.

She looked at the boy who helped her once again.

He was muscular and easygoing, with the confident air of someone very successful.

"You can call me Eugene."

"Yes, I see."

"We don't have to eat those delicious sandwiches in the trailer today, do we?"

That way of speaking... He was definitely from California. He had that classic Californian way of making even negative things sound vaguely positive down pat. She managed a smile.

"Perhaps. Let me take you to Joanne... I mean, the assistant director."

"Thank you!"

As she moved to hand them over to the assistant director, she heard a foreign language behind her.

[Hey, Eugene's really smooth, huh?]

[I know!]

She had no idea what they meant, but they sounded cheerful, not offended in the slightest.

*Did they even increase the duration of the cameo?*

What was certain was that the attitude of the assistant director had also changed. Joanne suddenly began to treat them with excessive courtesy.

*What in the world?*

In any case, they quickly took their places on set before the main cast arrived for the day's shoot.

She too moved quickly.

First, she fetched snacks and drinks.

"Where should I bring these after you're done?"

The pink-haired boy glanced at her instead of the staff and replied briefly, pointing up.

*Up?*

"Thank you. Up there, please."

"...?"

She followed his hand and raised her gaze.

The boy continued politely.

"We are going to perform."

*Oh my god.*

Where he'd pointed... It was a corner of the bar, built into the set for the sake of completeness.

A small stage in a pub.

They came as background performance extras in this scene! 

It was absurdly modest. 

*This... Why are they even bothering to do this?*

They were wearing makeup, but she still wasn't sure whether they'd fit the atmosphere of this crazy set.

Even so, preparations for the shoot were steadily progressing, and after a camera test, the boys actually went and stood on the stage at the bar.

*They'll add the music during editing anyway, so they only have to pretend to sing.*

They adjusted the microphones, and one even sat down at the keyboard. There was also a boy who asked about the use of the bizarre space instrument.

And the moment filming actually started, the set was quiet, with nobody expecting much...

*Ooooo~*

The speakers hummed as the pub set went dark, with the mirror ball and nighttime lighting glinting.

A rich voice rang through the microphone, which wasn't meant for actual singing. 

"...!"

The voices were amazing, weaving together in harmony as accompaniment, and the sound seemed to shimmer through the air like lasers or starlight.

But it wasn't elegant. A sound with a texture like randomly drawn crayon strokes jumped out. 

An addictive song with a fast beat suitable for a kitschy space opera. And a rebellious space band singing on a narrow stage.

$p
♪ *Let me swallow it* ♫
p$

The image they painted was absurdly good, blending right into the atmosphere of the grotesque sci-fi backstreet bar.

They had a talent for seizing the moment.

"......"

She began to understand why the boy had left his home state to live in a distant country.

*How could you turn down a chance to work with a team like that?*

She probably would've been stunned to learn they hadn't always been a team, and had originally been a temporary group born from a survival show. 

Anyway, their live performance of the OST, shortened to two minutes, left not only her but also quite a few other crew members in a daze.

As the song built and reached its climax, some of the staff even found themselves moving to the rhythm without realizing it.

*Took.*

And then it was over.

"Thank you."

The relaxed way they bowed and thanked everyone afterward made them seem all the more professional. They really had the air of popular celebrities.

*...It was unexpectedly great.*

Besides, they hadn't looked at the camera even once. Perfect for a movie shoot.

That made her feel all the more disappointed.

*Everything will be edited out anyway...!*

There was almost no chance the full performance would be kept in the movie. It was too out of the blue and had little to do with the plot.

At best, it might be released as behind-the-scenes footage!

She somehow felt that it was all the more unfair.

She could understand why they chose this as their cameo scene, but they'd probably made a mistake because they didn't know much about this field.

"Phew."

It turned out she hadn't needed to worry.

The boys themselves hadn't expected much to come of their performance in the first place.

They weren't hoping their performance would be so good that the whole song would be included in the movie.

They were setting up what would be filmed next.

"Mmm."

"I guess that's it for the performance, right?"

"Looking at the atmosphere, I think so."

Park Moondae stepped away from the microphone and spoke to Bae Sejin as the members waited for the next cut.

"Hyung, now show the acting skills of a genius child actor."

"Go for it!"

"There is no need to feel burdened, but we want you to know our deep faith and trust!" Kim Raebin said.

Bae Sejin nodded solemnly as though he'd never dismissed it as 'just one line of dialogue.'

"...Okay!"

"Oh, oh!"

*Looks like he's immersed in the role.*

That was Park Moondae's assessment.

Bae Sejin refused the water offered by the staff and simply adjusted his outfit, his gaze calm.

A while later, after the actors arrived on set, TeSTAR's 'real' cameo was filmed.

"...!"

The youngest assistant finally understood why the K-pop band had chosen this particular scene.

She was confused at the same time.

*That person... Why is he in a boy band?*
